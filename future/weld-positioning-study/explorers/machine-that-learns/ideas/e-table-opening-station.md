# E — Derek's table opening, as a motorised, observed station

## Picture it (as it now stands)

This is Derek's table opening.

**The collar.** A drop-in collar plate (workspace-as-structure's) sits in a
Ø150–160 opening, with the tube's rim flush with the top. "Fixed" is the collar.

**Below the collar.**
- Four posts carry a shelf on four belt-linked Tr8×2 screws, driven by one
  motor: Z, the first motor.
- LOAD drops the shelf ~70 mm so the tube slides out the front.
- WELD raises it to a per-tube height. The height is measured by the joint
  camera (E-h1) or referenced by work-as-datum's centre plunger (E-h2).
- The existing rotator sits on the shelf.

**Above the collar.**
- A low gantry: Y rails on either side of the opening, and an X beam at
  y ≈ −190 under the gun body.
- The X carriage carries the head: a tilt arc about the hole axis, plus a roll
  yoke with carry-and-locate's open C-ring. The gun sits in the head.
- Y is plan angle. It couples to its belt through a 20–30 N detent with a
  switch (E-y).

**Umbilical.** It passes through a loose ring near the butt to its first hard
clamp, at least 700 mm away (E-c).

**Sensing.**
- A stylus in the gap between the tube and the opening reads the tube's OD at
  the dot's angle and drives X during the weld.
- The joint camera, two PTZ cameras and printed tags on the collar observe.

**Branch E-s** (workspace-as-structure's split mount) replaces the tilt arc
and the gantry. A paddle rides the plate, and a lead screw under the collar at
P3 does the tilt.

## Derek's branch, as he described it

**[Derek, from `context/examples-and-history.md`]**

- A hole in a table. Under it, "something screwed into the bottom of the
  table, maybe metal, maybe motorized, maybe manually height adjusted". This is
  an upside-down box with at least one face open for loading and unloading the
  carbonator tube. It carries the existing rotator on a height-adjustable shelf
  held by four rods or walls at its corners, with no second shelf beneath it.
- Above the hole, "the gun riding a gantry, the entire gantry sliding in one XY
  axis on rails on both sides of the hole, and the gun sliding along the gantry
  in the other XY axis". The gantry is at countertop height and the gun tip is
  at countertop height.
- The gun sits in a custom printed shell, "gripped along its length". It is
  tangent to the circle.
- Why he likes it: it does not solve the XYZ problem, but it shrinks the
  distance from a constructed starting point to the fine-tuned pose.
- His automated vision for the station: motors on X and Y, a motor on Z, a
  motor on the roll, and "another motor controlling the opposite axis of roll
  that is also relevant, the only other axis we'd want to change here". Add a
  couple of software-controlled PTZ cameras, then let an AI iterate for hours,
  days or weeks with the cameras and the laser dot, toward consistent,
  repeatable dry-run results across tubes.

## What workspace-as-structure established, which E stands on

At the scene's opening pose with the rim flush with the top:

- **Where the gun sits.** The gun body sits over solid table on the −Y side,
  70–200 mm up. Only the barrel and nozzle cross the mouth, so a Ø150–160
  opening is enough.
- **Gantry layout.** The X beam can run along X beside the hole at y ≈ −190,
  under the gun body, with Y rails on either side (x ≈ ±205 about the tube
  axis).
- **Tube change.** It is a ~70 mm shelf drop and a slide out through the open
  face; the gun never moves.
- **Shelf structure.** Four posts guide the shelf; four belt-linked Tr8×2
  screws lift it; a lock clamps it.
- **Drop-in collar.** A one-piece collar carries rails and posts, so the wood
  is out of the loop.
- **Tube length.** The shelf references the tube's far end, so tube length
  moves the dot.
- **Breakaway.** They proposed a breakaway detent on Y for a stuck wire.
- **Their allocation.** X precise; Y coarse (it *is* the vertical-axis angle);
  Z on the shelf; angles in a named family of printed pose saddles.

Their version is manual and discrete. E asks what it becomes when it is the
first stage of Derek's automated vision.

## What it becomes

### Derek's five motors are exactly the five that matter

| Derek's motor | Physical axis in E | Welding meaning | Side |
|---|---|---|---|
| X | carriage along the gantry beam | dot across the seam (wall ↔ cap), runout follow, corner scans | gun |
| Y | gantry on its rails | **plan angle**: 0.93° per mm along the tangent, with a small X correction; also the retract axis | gun |
| Z | shelf on four belt-linked screws | height of the corner (per tube); standoff with X; LOAD/WELD | work |
| roll | grip-axis roll: yoke with carry-and-locate's open C-ring | wall/cap split | gun (head on the X carriage) |
| "opposite roll" | tilt on an arc about the hole axis (radial line through the dot) | travel lean | gun (head on the X carriage) |

His list has no vertical-axis motor. That matches the result four explorers
found independently: plan angle is a tangent slide, and his Y axis already is
that control. So E's motor set is my B's motor set, split differently: **Z
moves to the work side, and X/Y become a low gantry.**

That split repairs the objection carry-and-locate raised against B (stages
upstream of the remote centre). The same masses and the same roll/tilt range
give (`table_station.py` §2):

| Nearest gun-side stage | Distance to the dot | Gravity moment on it |
|---|---|---|
| B: Y carriage behind and above the gun | 536 mm | 4.9–5.7 N·m |
| E: X carriage at y ≈ −190, 30 mm above the table | 212 mm | 0.3–1.6 N·m |

The gun's CoM (proxy) sits 132 mm above the table and 133 mm from the tube
axis, almost over the gantry. The roll drive's gravity load stays one-signed
over roll 30–60 and hole dial 20–40 (+0.48…+1.03 N·m for 2 kg, `table_station.py`
§3). A balancer (carry-and-locate's B-f) is therefore welcome but not required
for one-signed drives here.

### The head on the X carriage

- **Tilt about the hole axis.** An arc of R ≈ 200 mm centred on the radial
  line through the dot, standing on the carriage beside the gun body. The table
  limits it from below: at R 200, keeping 25 mm above the top means elevations
  ≥ 9°. The nominal grip-base elevation is 31°, so ±10° of tilt fits.
- **Roll about the grip axis.** A yoke on the arc carriage carries
  carry-and-locate's open C-ring (≥190° sector on three V-rollers). The
  umbilical and conduit drop in sideways; the cable's first clamp rides the arc
  carriage.
- **Moving the head keeps the remote centre.** X moves the head along the hole
  axis itself, and Y along the tangent. In both cases the rotation axes still
  pass through the (moved) dot.
- **Manual first.** Until the head is built, the head is workspace-as-structure's
  pose saddles: discrete, named, printed. The two rolls are the *last* motors
  (see the order below).

### Which axes to motorise first (in this station)

In A the first new motor was X, because A was about runout following. The table
station's defining payoff is different: tube changes without touching the gun.
The largest error it carries is per-tube corner height. OnlineMetals cuts
±3.2 mm, and at the opening pose 1 mm of height moves the dot 0.64 mm across
the seam. So:

0. **Now (no new motors).** Workspace's manual build with the observation layer
   added: joint camera on the collar, dot/corner readout, the stylus on the
   collar driving a screen readout. This is D on the table.
1. **Z first.** One NEMA 17 on the crank shaft of the belt-linked screws. The
   crank stays on the back shaft as the knob (knob-first). LOAD and WELD become
   buttons: 70 mm at 2 mm/rev and 300 rpm takes ~7 s. WELD height comes per tube
   from the work (next section). This makes the tube-change payoff automatic and
   removes the largest error.
2. **X second.** Radial runout follow during the weld from the stylus; corner
   scans and wall/cap-offset experiments in dry runs.
3. **Y third.** Plan-angle experiments by a tangent slide (0.93°/mm, loose
   tolerance: 0.1 mm = 0.09°); motorised retract for top loading.
4. **Roll, then tilt last.** The head replaces the saddles. Each rotation keeps
   the dot mechanically, so a sweep changes one welding variable.

θ exists throughout. It needs the dry-run mode discussed in the observation
layer: software turns the table only with the laser disabled, and the pedal
stays the deadman while armed.

### Work-referenced height: two ways, one motor

The shelf references the tube's far end, and the corner is what matters. Two
routes put the corner at a known height, and both end in the same Z motor.

- **E-h1, measured (my view).** On the first dry lap of each tube, the joint
  camera triangulates the red dot on the cap face at a known X, a few mm inboard
  of the corner, at several table angles. Z moves to the target height, and the
  lap's face-runout curve is kept for Z-follow. There is no extra hardware.
  Accuracy is the camera's (~0.05 mm estimated from the observation layer's
  numbers, not measured).
- **E-h2, referenced (work-as-datum's W1).** A spring plunger on an arm from the
  collar's free +Y edge carries a hardened ball. The ball drops into a
  countersink on a seat bar that sits on hex nipples in the plate's two tapped
  ports. Z drives up until the plunger reads zero. Here the plunger's dial
  becomes the $23.99 RS232 digital indicator workspace-as-structure sourced, so
  Z closes its loop on the plate centre. The ball-in-cone also centres the plate
  on the plunger axis, which removes radial runout at the plate centre
  mechanically.
  - Costs: nipples and a seat bar every closure.
  - Camera placement: the camera must look from +Y toward +X so it does not
    sight across the arm.
- **Both at once.** The plunger sets the height and the camera checks it. A
  disagreement is itself a measurement (plate seat depth, face tilt).
  Work-as-datum's W1b (a sprung shelf against a rigid centre) is less needed
  once Z has a motor; it stays their branch.

### The stylus follower in the table

- **Where it goes.** The D-s stylus (my exchange §3) reaches down the annular
  gap between the tube OD and the opening. That gap is 11.5 mm radially at
  Ø150 and 16.5 mm at Ø160. The stylus touches the OD at the dot's own angle
  (+X), ~15 mm below the table top (9 mm below the joint), with 0.5–1 N.
- **What carries it.** Its slide and scale sit on the collar top between the
  opening and the +X rail (x ≈ +60…+120 from the dot).
- **What it reads.** The wall's radial position where the bead is going, live
  during the weld: runout, ovality, bulk thermal growth. It drives X (motor
  step 2).
- **What it must do.** Lift out of the gap before LOAD, because the rim would
  otherwise catch it as the tube drops. A small servo flip-up or a manual latch;
  the station can refuse LOAD until the stylus reports up.
- **With E-h2.** It reads the residual radial motion: plate-edge versus port
  eccentricity, ovality, heat.

### What the cameras see from the table plane

The table top is 6.35 mm above the joint, so nothing at table height sees into
the recess. Everything that watches the joint must look down into it.

- **Across the bore from the front (−X, the operator's side).** The near rim is
  124 mm from the dot and 6.35 mm up, so any slope above 2.9° clears it. A PTZ
  camera high over the operator, 0.6–1.2 m away and 250–400 mm up (14–23°),
  sees the **+X wall nearly face-on**. It sees the wall share of the red line
  and the bead's wall toe, but the cap only at a grazing ~20° (foreshortened
  ~3×). At 20× zoom: roughly 10–24 µm/px (estimate, focal length assumed).
- **From the +Y end, displaced inboard** (x ≈ −150…−250). Looking straight
  along the tangent is blocked: the tangent line runs inside the wall for
  |y| < 14 mm. Inboard, the line of sight enters the bore and sees the dot, the
  arriving wire tip and the nozzle, with the whole gun beyond.
- **The joint camera** stays where the observation layer puts it (+Y, inboard,
  ~85 mm up, ~120 mm away), now on a post from the collar. It is the metrology
  camera: fixed, calibrated, ~69° from the beam for triangulation.
- **Fiducial tags printed on the collar** around the opening register every PTZ
  frame to the collar, because PTZ pointing is not metrology-grade. Tags on the
  shell give the gun's pose relative to the collar during welds, when the red
  dot is invisible.
- **Protection.** The table plane is also where spatter lands and hands
  work. Camera windows are sacrificial: a spare D18 protective lens in a
  printed hood.

### Loads, cables, what is fixed to what

- **Position loop:** gun → shell → head → X carriage → beam → Y rails → collar →
  posts → shelf → rotator → tube. The wood is outside the loop
  (workspace-as-structure's collar).
- **Gravity.** It loads the X carriage 0.3–1.6 N·m and the roll drive one way.
  The Z screws carry the shelf down, one way.
- **Umbilical.** It leaves the grip butt heading −Y, ~133 mm above the table,
  and is clamped on the carriage (after X/Y, before the head). It then runs
  along the bench to a ≥350 mm-radius saddle at the −Y end and down to the cart
  (workspace's route). Its pull goes into the carriage, not the head. It
  changes with Y by the free span's change; the camera measures its effect
  with a push test.
- **Trigger.** Pressed through the shell by a Bowden lever; the pressing force
  stays inside the shell.
- **Work lead and purge hose.** They ride the shelf. Hang the work lead from
  above so the shelf's Z screws see a constant load.

### How it is used

- **Setup of a recipe (once).**
  - Place the pose saddle, or command roll and tilt.
  - Set Y to the plan angle.
  - X scans find the corner.
  - The camera and stylus confirm across a dry lap.
  - The recipe is saved as numbers (and as a saddle name while the head is
    manual).
- **Each tube.**
  - LOAD button: the stylus lifts, the shelf drops 70 mm. Slide the tube out
    and the next in.
  - WELD button: the shelf rises to the per-tube height (E-h1 or E-h2), the
    stylus drops, and a dry lap maps runout.
- **Weld.** The pedal is the deadman and the trigger goes through the Bowden
  lever. X follows the stylus and Z follows the face-runout map. Release the
  trigger, then the pedal.
- **Stuck wire.** Axes hold; snip from above at countertop height (easy
  reach). The wire yields at 3–5 N, long before any drive.
- **Inverted second closure.** Same rim height and the same buttons. With
  E-h2 the seat bar goes on the new top plate.
- **Hours of AI dry runs.** Nobody present. The station cycles tubes only when
  a person swaps them; otherwise it repeats laps, sweeps and holds on one tube.
  Overnight holds measure drift of the collar, posts and shelf.
- **Second person.** Two buttons, a pedal, a Bowden pedal, and a screen that
  says when the dot is in tolerance.

## What I tried to break

- **A heavy shelf on belt-linked screws.** The shelf carries rotator + tube
  (~8–10 kg with the vessel). Four screws on one belt keep their relative
  heights only as well as the belt's tooth engagement. A skipped tooth tilts
  the shelf, and on a 185 mm half-span 1 mm of skip is ~5 mrad. The camera sees
  a per-tube height that disagrees across table angles (a tilt); the repair is
  to re-level by slipping the belt one tooth.
  - Alternative: four motors, one per screw, with Klipper's multi-Z levelling
    (printer-standard). This adds tilt control of the work (small work-angle
    trims, as A-tilt-1) at the price of three more drivers.
- **Posts and shelf compliance under follow loads.** Workspace estimates
  ~2,900 N/mm sideways for four 1 in aluminium posts. X-follow happens on the
  gun side in E, so the shelf only moves in Z, slowly. It is not the concern.
- **The stylus is in the gap the tube drops through.** Interlocked (above). A
  stylus that fails to lift bends and is replaced; the tube is not harmed at
  0.5–1 N.
- **Cables at countertop height.** The umbilical runs along the bench at
  ~133 mm, where hands and tools are. A printed trough along the bench keeps
  it in one shape. Y moves change the trough's free length: a slack loop at the
  bench end.
- **PTZ views are shallow for the cap.** The front camera sees the wall well
  and the cap poorly; the +Y-end camera sees the dot and wire; the joint camera
  measures. No single camera sees everything, which is why there are three.
- **Laser boundary.** The mouth is at countertop height with people nearby.
  Workspace-as-structure's lidded mouth (a lid on the collar with gas and
  camera ports) is the natural companion. This is not developed here.
- **Open face of the box vs. cameras and operator.** The front PTZ looks across
  the operator's workspace. Mount it high on a ceiling or overhead boom, not on
  the bench.

## Contribution

- Derek's table example and his automated-setup vision describe one machine.
  Its five motors are the five that matter; the vertical axis is his Y rails.
- Putting Z on the work side and X/Y at countertop height cuts the gravity
  moment on the gun-side stages 3–5× compared with my B. It also shortens the
  lever from the nearest stage to the dot from 536 mm to ~210 mm. That is the
  repair for B's main objection.
- Per-tube height is measured by the camera (or referenced by work-as-datum's
  plunger) and moved to by the first motor. That makes the table's "tube change
  without touching the gun" true across tubes of different lengths.
- The stylus gives a weld-time radial reference through the 11–16 mm gap that
  the table opening leaves anyway.

## Biggest open problems

1. **The head at countertop height.** An arc about the hole axis plus a
   grip-axis yoke, carried on a low carriage, must clear:
   - the table top (elevation ≥ ~9° at R 200);
   - the umbilical exiting at 133 mm;
   - the wire path;
   - the joint camera's sightline.

   None of that can be closed without the gun scan.
2. **Per-tube height by camera** assumes the triangulated cap-face height is
   good to ~0.05 mm on real brushed 316L. That is the first thing the E0 stage
   should measure.
3. **Cutting the bench** (or standing the collar on its own legs), and whether
   the VEVOR top's crossbars allow an opening ~650 mm from the −Y end near the
   front edge (workspace-as-structure's question to Derek).

## Wave 5 — workspace-as-structure's critique, and the branches it produces

Source: `exchange/workspace-as-structure--on--machine-that-learns-w4.md`, and
their `explorers/workspace-as-structure/ideas/split-mount-station.md` (sketch
`explorers/workspace-as-structure/sketches/split-mount-station.svg`, numbers
`split_mount.py`). The E described above stays readable as the gantry version.

### 1. A stuck wire goes into tension and drags a belt-driven Y silently → branch E-y (theirs), adopted

**Their argument, and I agree with it:**
- In the rig's procedure the wire sits on the arriving side and comes from the
  gun's side (−Y), so the surface at the dot moves +Y, away from the gun.
- Once the ~10 mm stick-out bends over, the stuck wire is pulled out along its
  axis, in tension.
- The wire (0.030 in ER316L, ~250–320 N ultimate, their estimate) outlasts the
  rotator's ~140 N pull. The drive gives first: a GT2 belt on a NEMA 17 holds
  ~60 N.
- So Y back-drives and loses steps without any report. Every millimetre is
  ~0.9° of plan angle, which the next lap reads as "the recipe drifted".

My line in E, "the wire yields at 3–5 N, long before any drive", described the
bending phase only. It was wrong for the tension phase.

**Branch E-y.**
- The Y carriage couples to its belt through a ball-and-spring (or magnet)
  detent that lets go at 20–30 N.
- A microswitch on the detent is wired as a Klipper endstop. Breakaway halts
  the recipe and asks for the snip; re-homing on the same switch restores the
  count.
- It also caps the pull that reaches the wire bracket and the roll yoke.

**What I add:**
- **Any Y drive needs it.** A ball screw back-drives too. A self-locking Tr8×2
  Y would instead put the rotator's full ~140 N into the wire bracket and head.
- **It combines with idea F's pullback case.** At trigger release the feeder's
  pullback pushes the bracket toward the bead while the tube drags the wire
  +Y. The detent limits the Y part of both.
- **The same switch could stop the table.** That would contradict the rotator's
  rule that nothing takes the table from the operator mid-bead, so it is
  recorded as Derek's decision, not proposed as a change.

**Still uncertain:** the detent's repeatability on re-seat (the camera checks it
on the next dry lap), and the feeder's pullback force.

### 2. The umbilical's first hard clamp must be far from the butt → branch E-c (theirs), adopted

**What was wrong.** E clamped the umbilical on the X carriage, before the head.
A roll of ±15° would then twist the ~100–150 mm between clamp and butt by
~±13°, about 90–130° per metre. The twist factor, ~0.87× the roll, is
carry-and-locate's estimate for a 30° cable exit.

**E-c.**
- A loose ring or saddle near the butt, which the cable can turn inside.
- The first hard clamp at the fixed saddle at the bench end, or at the cart
  mast when the cart is docked (workspace-as-structure's branch), at least
  700 mm away. The twist then spreads to ≤ 20° per metre.

**What changes:** the cable's axial pull now reaches the gun through the free
span instead of stopping at the carriage. It stays constant only if that span's
shape repeats, hence the dock. The camera's push test measures what it does to
the dot.

**Still uncertain:** the real cable exit direction (the scan), and the fiber's
tolerable twist per metre.

### 3. Stylus heat (their estimate) — no change, one refinement

- Their Rosenthal estimate puts the stylus tip 50–100 K above ambient. It reads
  the local thermal bulge (~0.05 mm), which the corner shares, so following it
  is right.
- The tube's seam and wall-thickness variation repeat every revolution. The
  weld-time signal is therefore **live reading − dry-lap map at the same
  azimuth**, which is how E's X-follow should use it.
- The ceramic tip and stainless stem are fine at that temperature; the scale
  stays 60 mm or more away.

### 4. The +Y side is everyone's → an azimuth and height budget on the collar

The gun owns about −10° to −120° of azimuth. Everything else wants the free
half:
- the joint camera (~85 mm up);
- work-as-datum's W1 arm (40–60 mm up);
- E-s's rollers (below 13 mm);
- the +Y-end PTZ;
- the lid's notch.

E adopts their one-page budget on the collar drawing: low parts first, the
camera looking over them.

### 5. Branch E-s — split mount (workspace-as-structure's, built to run E's automation)

**What it is.**
- A paddle rides the plate: a hub pin on work-as-datum's port seat, and two
  rollers on the plate face on a line through the dot, tilted 30° off the radius
  toward +Y.
- A foot P3 stands on the collar under the grip. Lifting P3 with a lead screw
  under the collar turns the gun about the roller line, so the dot stays put by
  construction (0.87 mm along the seam and 0.08 mm in height for 10°).
- The head shrinks to the roll yoke with the open C-ring. X and Y become
  micro-slides between the paddle and a carrier that also holds the rollers.

**Answering E's open problem 1.** It removes E's biggest open problem, the tilt
arc at countertop height, by making the remote centre out of where two contacts
sit on the work.

**What it changes in E:**
- Per-tube height and Z-follow are no longer needed to keep the dot on the
  corner, because the gun rides the plate.
- Radial runout at the plate centre is followed by the pin, so the stylus
  becomes optional (still useful for ovality and heat).
- Angles are read by an inclinometer on the shell, and the camera verifies.
- E's motor order, observation layer, dry-run rule, fiducials and LOAD/WELD
  carry over unchanged. Z still goes first, now only for LOAD/WELD and to bring
  the plate into range.

**What I would keep an eye on, from my view:**
- **Range for experiments.** Plan angle goes from tens of degrees (E's gantry
  Y) to about −4.6° … +2.3° around a nominal built into the carrier. Tilt is a
  30°-mixed axis. Two rotations still span a 2-D set of beam directions, and
  the inclinometer and camera measure the attitude actually reached, so the
  learning software can command work and travel angles as combinations.
  Recipe-level plan-angle changes become pose-block swaps.
- **Motors and cables ride the work.** Three small motors (X, Y, roll) sit on
  the paddle and carrier. Their cables join the umbilical's residual on P3 and
  the fence. That is fine when the cable shape repeats (dock), and worth a push
  test.
- **The joint camera looks over the rollers.** Budget above.
- **The wire path.** The gun's X/Y micro-slides move the bracket a few mm
  relative to a fixed feeder. Idea F's wire experiments prefer that span to be
  long and gentle.
- **Where precision comes from.** In E the camera and stylus close loops around
  the dot with motors. In E-s passive contacts on the plate hold the dot, and
  the camera only verifies. For hours-long AI runs E-s spends its motors on the

**Where I stand.** Two versions of the same station, kept side by side:
- **E (gantry)** has range: large plan-angle and tilt sweeps, and no parts on
  the work.
- **E-s (split mount)** has exactness: the dot is held by the work, and the
  head problem is gone.

The first recipes are likely found with range and then run with exactness.
Which to build first depends on measurements neither version has: gun mass and
CoM, spatter on the plate face under the rollers, and whether nipples may sit in
the ports during the weld.
