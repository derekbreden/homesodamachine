# who-moves-what — notebook

The view: every relative motion between gun and corner, and every phase (setup,
dry run, weld, lift-off, loading, inversion, stuck wire), is assigned to some
body. That body is the gun, its shell, a support, a fixture, the tube and
rotator, or a setup step. The allocation, and *when* each motion happens,
decides most of what the machine has to be.

Files: `ideas/*.md` (one per arrangement), `sketches/*.svg` (generated by
`sketches.py` from the pose.js port), `geometry.py` (exact port of
`web/public/js/weld-position/pose.js` plus a capsule gun traced from the manual
drawing), `allocation_calcs.py` (the numbers quoted below). Sourcing:
`../../sourcing/who-moves-what.md`.

## Cross-cutting findings (true for every arrangement)

1. **The tube's symmetry makes a slide into a yaw.** Rotation about the tube
   axis changes nothing, because the seam is a circle on that axis. Translating
   the gun (or the work) along the tangent therefore *is* a vertical-axis rotation
   relative to the local tangent, at **1.08 mm per degree**, with a second-order
   radial correction of r(1 − cos φ): 2.1 mm at 15° (calc 1; sketch S1).
   Consequences:
   - any X/Y carrier (Derek's gantry, a cross-slide) already owns the yaw. No
     yaw bearing is needed, and Derek's "the gun is not tangent" becomes a
     Y-position setting;
   - an unintended 1 mm Y error is a 0.93° yaw error. Y is not free just
     because the tube spins, and it should be set by dial, not by chasing the
     dot.
2. **Gravity-neutral versus gravity-changing motions.** X, Y (= yaw), Z and
   spin leave the weld downhand, so they can be given to the work. Grip roll and
   hole tilt change the gun's attitude; giving them to the work tilts the puddle.
   Tilting the existing rotator is possible up to roughly 20–30° before its
   gravity-seated turntable would lift onto the spool catch (rough CG estimate,
   calc in `geometry.py`). That would be a process change, not a positioning
   trick.
3. **Precision is continuous only in X and Z.** The runout passes under the
   station during the weld. The angles need precision at setup only, unless a
   compliant support lets them drift. Y needs ~1 mm for 1°. This decides where
   stiffness must live.
4. **Separate weight from location.** It appeared independently in every
   idea: stub (needs a balancer), rim rider (needs the gun nearly weightless to
   float), seat (needs cable force routed through the carrier), still gun
   (weight on the stand, but constant). A carrier that is soft but carries the
   load, paired with a locator that is stiff but lightly loaded, is the recurring
   repair.
5. **The spin belongs to the tube.** A gun orbiting a still tube would twist
   the fiber ~380° per weld (a lap plus 20° overlap), and the manual says
   "twisting is strictly forbidden". This is an allocation check, not a design
   preference.
6. **The tube limits orientation only at low hole dials.** At the opening pose
   (dials 45 / 30 / −15) the gun body, nozzle excluded, clears the tube by
   16.7 mm, and by 13–19.5 mm across yaw ±45°. The barrel meets the lip at low
   hole dials with large roll: dial 0 at roll ≥ 60, dial 5 at roll ≥ 75, dial 10 at
   roll 90 (proxy; `allocation_calcs.py` section 9). From dial 35 up, it never
   comes within 12 mm. That was the range first reported here, computed at dials
   35–95 before the dial correction.
7. **Rings round the umbilical must open.** The fiber ends in the QBH connector
   inside the grip, so a closed ring cannot be threaded onto the cable without
   unplugging the QBH at the gun (my inference; unplugging exposes the fiber
   face). Derek's "openable loop" is right for a physical reason. A closed hollow
   rotary stage or a closed thin-section bearing round the cable does not fit.
8. **Rolling about the grip axis moves the cable, though not its exit point.**
   The fiber leaves the butt ~35° off the grip axis (the grip rakes 30°,
   drawing-scaled). A 45° roll swings the exit direction 25° and twists the fiber
   ~37° at the exit (calc 5). The first metre of umbilical needs a free loop and
   ideally a swivel anchor. At the opening pose the fiber leaves the grip base
   nearly horizontally along −Y (direction 0.16, −0.99, 0.06 at yaw −15), about
   372 mm above the bench. Every support on the −Y side has to leave that line
   open.
9. **A fixed gun needs the work to drop before the tube can leave.** The wire
   tip sits in the corner, 6.35 mm below the rim, so a ≥ 10 mm drop and an exit
   toward +X within ±20° are required (sequence-of-use `calc/drawer_path.py`;
   wave 2). The nozzle tip alone would sit +5.0 mm above the rim at the proxy's
   16 mm standoff, +0.8 mm at 10 mm and −2.8 mm at 5 mm, at the opening pose
   (dial 30; the beam is 45° above horizontal). At dial 65 it was +8.8, +3.1 and
   −1.6 mm.
10. **A possible electronic fine axis inside the gun.** The HMI's red-light
    adjustment (manual fig. 21: "adjust the red light to the centre of the nozzle
    by pressing the left and right buttons") implies the scan mirror's zero can
    be offset along one axis. If it moves the process beam too, the gun owns a
    1-D electronic dot offset with no mechanism. Unverified; see questions.
11. **The grip-axis endpoint lies on the hole-tilt arc.** At room yaw 0, for
    any roll and any hole tilt, the QBH stays in the tangent plane at 279.2 mm
    from the dot, as many degrees above the −Y horizontal as the hole dial reads.
    At dial 30 that is 242 mm out along −Y and 372 mm above the bench. One
    carriage on an arc centred on the hole axis, carrying a bearing that points
    at the dot, realizes both gun rotations. See `protractor-and-stub.md`; at the
    true pose its arc moves ~60 mm to −X to clear the fiber.

## Derek's examples read as allocations (what this view adds)

- **Table opening + rotator below + gantry above.** Z goes to the shelf (the
  work), X/Y to the gantry, roll and tilt to a fixed shell. This view adds that
  the gantry's Y already *is* the yaw (finding 1), so the arrangement covers all
  the relative freedoms with only two fixed angles left in the shell. Branch 2c
  gives those two a physical axis through the dot. Branch 1c swaps X/Y to the
  shelf, leaving the gun still. The original is kept as he described it.
- **Monitor arm.** Weight and gross motion go to the gas spring and joints;
  location is claimed by nothing. Paired with a seat, "too flexible" stops
  mattering (`carrier-and-seat.md`, D1).
- **Suspension.** Weight goes to the wire (or balancers: constant force), one
  axis to bungees, the rest free. The two loops lie almost on the grip axis
  already (7.5 mm off at the nozzle tip, 17 mm at 20 mm back). Make the loop
  contacts journals concentric with that axis and the suspension *is* a
  grip-roll hinge (`carrier-and-seat.md`, D2). Location still needs a partner:
  a seat, the rim rider, or Derek's hand, now weightless.

### Combination: `ideas/gravity-in-the-gun-plane.md` (`gravity_plane_calcs.py`, `sketches/s7-gravity-in-the-gun-plane.svg`)

- **Gravity at τ\* ≈ 32.5°:** the torque about the grip axis falls from 0.77 N·m
  (upright, 1.5 kg) to 0.13 N·m, and crosses zero near 38°. The vertical axis has
  none. The hole axis carries all of it (1.3 N·m).
- **So:** the dot goes in work-as-datum's dot-centred sprung cup on the plate
  hanger. Hole is one vertical wire from Derek's base loop (one-knob's W_hole,
  gravity-tensioned). Yaw is a pin in a vertical slot. Roll is Derek's rings,
  with a friction lock. Ballast at the base loop sets the wire tension (16.6 N
  with 1 kg) without loading the cup (~8 N).
- **Each support is a single knob** by one-knob's plane rule. The pivot is the
  cup (at the dot), so there is no couch and no per-tube step.
- **Docking** is a straight drop along the escape direction, and lift-off
  releases everything with no unhooking.
- **The tilt knob measures the real gun's centre-of-mass plane:** step τ until
  the unlocked, hanging gun stops turning.
- **Biggest open problems:** the tilted weld process; the loose tube under ~8 N
  at a tilt; the rotator's tipping near 38°; the real mass and centre of mass.
