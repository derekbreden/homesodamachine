# Brief

Derek's request for this study, verbatim (2026-09-28):

> I'd like to build machines and robots to build JST connectors. I have no specific ideas. I need the breadth of possibilities for what we can construct to perform this task.
>
> Please keep in mind that our volume is incredibly low, and so we have about a week of print time per unit anyways, and so if this machine works ***VERY*** slowly that is perfectly okay, one single little crimp on one 3P or 4P or 5P cable at a time.
>
> There are a number of problems to solve (spreading the cables apart, stripping a bit of the tip of the cable, placing the metal bit on the end of the cable, placing that metal bit and cable in something that crimps and crimping it, and the finally inserting that completed crimp into the plastic housing). And within that problem set, it is this:
>
> > placing the metal bit on the end of the cable, placing that metal bit and cable in something that crimps and crimping it
>
> That I desire most to automate, but really getting the whole procedure automated would be ideal.
>
> I think your first response is likely to be "this costs tens of thousands if not hundreds of thousands to do right in the way other manufacturers do this" ... and I think my hope is that you can get past thinking along those lines, and start thinking along the lines of the great many number of things that are possible to do simply and cheaply and easily when slow speed is our defining characteristic.

## What this study is

An exploration of what can be constructed to make the XH cable ends this
machine's harnesses need: splay, strip, place the contact, crimp, insert. Nine
explorers each see the whole procedure a different way, with the most weight on
the step Derek most wants automated (place the contact on the conductor, hold
both in something that crimps, crimp). They explore, exchange critiques across
views, revise and combine, and a coordinator assembles an entry point.

Slowness is the defining condition, not a concession. Every idea is developed
for a machine that can take minutes per crimp, run unattended while the
printers run, and build one conductor at a time. Industrial wire-processing
practice is a source of mechanisms and physics, not a price anchor.

Nothing is ranked or selected. Nothing is built, bought or operated.
