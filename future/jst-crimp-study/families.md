# Families of arrangements

Every arrangement in the study, grouped by how the contact and the conductor come
together, the rows of the [map](README.md#a-map). Within a family, arrangements
sit beside the originals they branch from and the combinations that grew from
them. The order reflects those relationships, not merit. Each entry links its idea
file, which carries the development. The last section, [Connections](#connections),
covers the mechanisms and combinations that cross families.

The entry point is [README.md](README.md), and [ideas-index.md](ideas-index.md)
lists every arrangement in one line.

## The conductor comes to a fixed die

The die is bolted down. The ribbon end rides on something that moves (a
carriage, a printer stage, a pallet, a cassette, a spool feed) and brings one
conductor at a time to a contact already waiting in the die, or already pinned
to the conductor by a lighter station. Most arrangements here automate placing
the contact on the conductor and the crimp; several also take splitting,
stripping or insertion. 52 arrangements, from all nine explorers.

**Shared across the family.**
- "Fixed" is the lower die: a hand tool's lower jaw, an applicator's anvil, a
  knife-set anvil block or a fin. The carriage is steered to it by touch-off or
  by the camera, not by its own step count.
- Waiting neighbours are kept out of the die by a fork that stands the working
  conductor out, by folding the others back, by keys or a lifter bar, by a crown
  the fresh contacts fall below, by two planes, or by lifting only the working
  conductor.
- The conductor is placed by its own insulation edge: the camera sees the torn
  edge (strip scatter ±0.2 mm against a 0.5–1.0 mm window), or an insulated
  neck blade or a grounded tip plate stops it.
- The far end names the conductor before force, and a ~20 N proof pull follows
  the crimp, reacted at a web clamp or the reel.
- Most arrangements receive the end already split and stripped. Crossings and
  pairs mostly go back to the person.

### The hand tool held still: an SN-2549 or WC-110 in a cradle

Sketches: [a2's plan](explorers/hand-tool-as-press/sketches/a2-ribbon-to-fixed-tool.svg),
[the jaw law](explorers/hand-tool-as-press/sketches/jaw-law-and-c-frame.svg),
[f1's cradle](explorers/force-and-form/sketches/f1-motorised-ratchet.svg).

The tool's own linkage, ratchet and dies form the crimp, and the force closes
inside the tool head. A printed cradle and a pusher close the handles through a
load cell: a NEMA 17 on a Tr8×2 screw, a 12 V actuator, a worm winch, or a foot
treadle. The tool has no locator, so each arrangement adds one. *Open across
them:* whether the SN-2549's XH nest makes a sound crimp on this ribbon, whether
its jaws bottom, its handle force, jaw opening and jaw-half depth, and wear over
~3,200 crimps.

- **[HT a2 — The ribbon comes to a fixed tool](explorers/hand-tool-as-press/ideas/a2-ribbon-to-fixed-tool.md)** · S. A three-axis head (short
  rails or a bed-slinger printer) sets a genuine SXH stub by its pilot hole in
  [HT a1](explorers/hand-tool-as-press/ideas/a1-squeezer-cradle.md)'s open nest, and a servo blade shears the tab before any wire
  arrives. A fork stands conductor *k* out of the row, a backlit tip picture
  corrects its line, and it feeds until the insulated neck blade reads green;
  ~90–100 s a crimp [estimate]. *Open:* the jaw-half depth; ~4.5–5 mm of open
  jaw to carry the contact in; a 25–35 mm split.
- **[HT a2b — Gravity: the tool lies flat](explorers/hand-tool-as-press/ideas/a2b-gravity-tool-flat.md)** · L. For loose kit contacts. The
  SN-2549 lies with its nest axis vertical; a hand-filled revolver or a stick of
  pre-formed contacts drops one down a chute sized to the lot's measured end
  view (each contact is photographed on a light pad before loading) onto a stop
  pocket, and the ribbon hangs from an X–Z carriage into it. *Open:* kit-contact
  uniformity; a 0.043 g contact tumbling in the chute; room for the chute tongue
  in the open jaw.
- **[HT a2c — One baseplate, three stations](explorers/hand-tool-as-press/ideas/a2c-one-baseplate-strip-crimp-insert.md)** · S. Batches: the Klein 11063W in
  a second squeezer strips every conductor, then each is crimped, then inserted
  by a stencil-steel blade with a 7.5–10 mm hump storing the seating travel. The
  head routes any conductor to any cavity, so it makes J4's and J7's crossings.
  *Open:* whether the Klein strips this silicone cleanly at 2.4 mm; the hump in
  set copper; the push face is weak above ~15 N.
- **[HT a2d — Strip all, crimp all, square the row, push the housing on](explorers/hand-tool-as-press/ideas/a2d-batch-then-gang-push.md)** · S.
  Crimps are laid into a 2.5 mm squaring comb in pin-map order, crossings last
  and over the others, and a NEMA 17 slide drives the housing onto the whole row
  ([IH i3](explorers/into-the-housing/ideas/i3-converging-shuttles-gang-push.md)'s press). *Open:* a summed push force hides one unlatched contact;
  ~2 mm of slack behind the housing; jam recovery on nine contacts.
- **[TS a2c — The strip gives the hand tool the locator it lacks](explorers/terminal-supply/ideas/a2c-strip-locator-for-hand-tool.md)** · ◇ S. A
  printed clip on the jaw screw, with a fence for the carrier edge and a sprung
  pin in the pilot hole, places the strip's lead contact the way the WC-110's
  flap does. One click holds it, and the tab is snipped before the wire. By hand
  on day one; later a strip stepper, a NEMA 17 on the handle and a carriage.
  *Open:* whether one click holds against the 48–158 N tab cut; jaw thickness
  against contact plus tab.
- **[TS x1 — The post feeds the hand tool](explorers/terminal-supply/ideas/x1-post-feeds-the-hand-tool.md)** · ◇ L. A 0.64 mm header post spears
  a kit contact from a lance-grooved plate, is measured, and slides it barrels
  first into the cradled SN-2549's nest to a jaw fiducial. Its motorless form,
  the "post pen", is a header pin in a printed pen against a fence on the jaw

  contact's grip on the pin; box-to-barrel spread between clone brands (up to
  ±0.25 mm against a ±0.1 mm window).
- **[BM b2 — The ratchet hand crimper in a frame, fed from the strip](explorers/borrowed-machines/ideas/b2-hand-crimper-in-a-frame.md)** · S. A

  SXH strip, the tab shears on a steel land, a post in the box carries the
  contact into the jaws, and a feeler flap drops into the neck before the
  conductor comes in through a funnel. *Open:* whether the jaws bottom; a neck

- **[FF f1 — The hand tool is the press](explorers/force-and-form/ideas/f1-motorised-ratchet-crimper.md)** · ◇ L/S. A second SN-2549 lies flat,

- **[FF f1b — JST WC-110 in the same cradle](explorers/force-and-form/ideas/f1b-wc110-in-the-cradle.md)** · ◇ L/S. JST's tool (~$536 at

- **[HT a6c — Flags by machine, crimped by foot](explorers/hand-tool-as-press/ideas/a6c-flags-by-machine-foot-crimp.md)** · L/S. [SL v8](explorers/machine-that-sees-and-learns/ideas/v8-tack-look-crimp.md)'s tack
  station with [HT a6](explorers/hand-tool-as-press/ideas/a6-foot-closed-jig-bench.md)'s foot bench: a printer stage lays each conductor into a
  contact, a servo former closes the insulation wings loosely, and a camera looks

- **[CQ c6b — By hand this week](explorers/change-the-question/ideas/c6b-by-hand-this-week.md)** · ◇ P. No motor, no microcontroller. A
  toggle-clamp steel pre-former turns a unit's 53 kit contacts into loom-order

  [estimate]: consistency, not minutes. *Open:* whether the SN-2549 opens

### A bought side-feed applicator in a slow press

Sketches: [b1b's crank in the shop press](explorers/borrowed-machines/sketches/b1b-crank-press.svg),
[f2's crank press](explorers/force-and-form/sketches/f2-crank-applicator-press.svg),
[a1's pallet tour](explorers/ribbon-as-pallet/sketches/a1-pallet-tour.svg),
[v9's tack at the anvil](explorers/machine-that-sees-and-learns/sketches/w3-v9-tack-at-the-anvil.svg).

An OTP-style "XH2.54" side-feed mini-applicator (~$150–167 plus $80–91 shipping
on eBay, $200–250 on Alibaba [source]; JST's MKS-L is the genuine one) feeds the strip, locates the
contact, crimps both barrels, shears the tab downward and sets height on its own
dials [mfr]. It needs a 30 or 40 mm stroke to a 135.78 mm shut height, so the
frame must open 166–176 mm: the 3 t VEVOR AP-3 or PR-3 ($255.90, $262.14
[Prime]; thin listings) or the idle 12-ton shop press; the 1 t presses do not
open far enough. A crank at bottom dead centre, a screw to a hard stop, a stop
collar or the shop press's bottle jack drives it, with a disc stack or spring
link as the force cap. *Open across them:* which contact the OTP die is cut for
and what it does to this ribbon; the applicator's interface and feed timing,
unpublished until one is on the bench; weeks of lead time, since no applicator,
knife set or XH reel is on Prime; sight lines past the plates; guarding.

### Knee and eccentric micro-presses, with made or harvested dies

Sketches: [f3's knee press](explorers/force-and-form/sketches/f3-knee-micropress.svg),
[v1's watched nest](explorers/machine-that-sees-and-learns/sketches/v1-watched-nest.svg),
[a4c's two stations](explorers/hand-tool-as-press/sketches/a4c-t-then-c.svg).

A palm-sized steel die set on two ground pins, closed by a knee pushed at
60–160 N (a NEMA 17, a servo or a hand lever) or an eccentric. A wedge under the
anvil is the height dial; a load cell and a 0.001 mm indicator log force against
true die gap, and a ~10 N re-touch reads height. The dies come from [FF f7](explorers/force-and-form/ideas/f7-where-the-steel-comes-from.md)'s
four sources. Several tack first: a light station closes the insulation wings
loosely, and a camera looks into the open conductor barrel before the heavy
stroke, so a failed look costs a contact, not a ribbon end. *Open across them:*
a JST-correct profile without the licence-gated drawing; cutting hardened SN
pieces without cracking; the tack's grip; whether tack-then-crimp equals one
pass; ejecting a box from a keyed nest thousands of times.

## Two pallets dock: every contact placed at once

likes. 12 arrangements.

## The housing locates the contact

The contact goes into a housing cavity while it is still bare and rigid, and the
cavity holds it square for the crimp. What remains of insertion is a short
straight push of a box already in its hole, and no crimped contact on a floppy
wire has to find a cavity. 11 arrangements
([sketch](explorers/into-the-housing/sketches/i2-crimp-in-the-cavity.svg)).

**Shared across the family.**
- An XHP cavity is a molded locator at 2.5 mm pitch, for a few cents. The
  contact goes box-first 1.4–2.4 mm into the rear entry, or onto a 0.64 mm post
  through the front opening.
- The nest is located only along the wire and floats ±0.2 mm across it; the
  steel anvil is master.
- Narrow stepped dies at the mouth: a 3.1 mm conductor step with ~0.8 mm walls
  (2.9 and 0.7 in [FF f8](explorers/force-and-form/ideas/f8-narrow-press-at-the-housing-mouth.md)) landing on anvil shoulders. Only wire EDM makes
  them, and ±0.05 mm quick-turn EDM is coarse for 0.8 mm walls.
- The insulation mouth, ≤ ~2.3–2.6 mm, against clone wings of 2.46–3.25 mm
  ([sketch](explorers/into-the-housing/sketches/insulation-mouth.svg)): push a
  seated neighbour's jacket up to 0.33 mm aside (untested), pre-form a keyhole,
  or use kit wings that measure narrow. Beside crimped neighbours nothing gives
  way.
- A contact crimped short of its seat and pushed alone needs its conductor to
  store that length (~5 mm from the mouth, ~14 mm from a post tip); crimping
  every contact at one position and seating all with one push stores nothing.
- IH i2, FF f8 and TS a5 crimp and seat one cavity at a time in cavity order;
  the batch forms (IH i2b, TS x3, TS a4c) take the odd cavities first, then the
  evens between crimped neighbours. The ~20 N proof pull comes before the push. A light under the mating face shows each
  empty cavity as a bright square that a seated box darkens.

*Open across the family:* the front-wall thickness and the rear opening's
clearance, both unmeasured.

## The person presents, the machine takes

The person's hands still aim and carry. The machine, or a jig standing in for
one, takes what hands do unevenly: the stops, the crimp force, the order of
conductors and cavities, and a check on every crimp. Most single-head and jig
forms keep attended minutes near today's; [BM b2b](explorers/borrowed-machines/ideas/b2b-pedal-less-hand-station.md) takes ~10 off (~37
against 46) by taking every contact touch and the stripping from the person,
and [PM p4b](explorers/procedure-is-the-machine/ideas/p4b-two-heads-one-person.md) brings them to 28–39; what the rest change is that every placement lands against a stop and every crimp is logged,
pulled or gauged. 12 arrangements, several with no motor. Motorless first steps
in other families sit beside these: [TS a2c](explorers/terminal-supply/ideas/a2c-strip-locator-for-hand-tool.md), [TS x1](explorers/terminal-supply/ideas/x1-post-feeds-the-hand-tool.md), [IH i2d](explorers/into-the-housing/ideas/i2d-locator-the-lance-never-touches.md),
[CQ c6b](explorers/change-the-question/ideas/c6b-by-hand-this-week.md), [RP a1b](explorers/ribbon-as-pallet/ideas/a1b-hand-shuttle.md), [RP a2d](explorers/ribbon-as-pallet/ideas/a2d-by-hand.md).

## A general-purpose arm or gantry hand

One mover visits fixed stations. What separates these is where the precision
lives: in the hand, corrected by a camera and a force reading, or in kinematic
docks and the stations, with the arm trusted only to arrive inside a funnel.
Kurabo's harness robot, which operates an unmodified crimp press, is the
industrial pattern ([prior art](context/prior-art.md)). The self-closing heads
that light gantries carry are under "A die head comes to a still conductor". 5
arrangements.

- **[IH i4 — A slow gantry hand with eyes and a force wrist](explorers/into-the-housing/ideas/i4-gantry-hand-with-eyes.md)** · L/S. A
  3018-class frame carries two steel-tipped fingers, a wrist flexure that reads
  push and pull and locks or goes loose, and a camera, among a tray, a crimp
  station that captures the contact first ([FF f3](explorers/force-and-form/ideas/f3-knee-micropress.md), or the SN-2549 with
  [IH i2d](explorers/into-the-housing/ideas/i2d-locator-the-lance-never-touches.md)'s stub), a ribbon clamp with a loft, and a housing nest. Per cavity it
  seats a contact, threads the conductor, crimps with the wrist loose, pulls with
  it locked, and inserts by lean-and-slide (18/20 against 3/20 straight in
  Sogang's inserter [source]); crossings and pairs are software. *Open:* grip
  design at 2.5 mm; silicone springback; the time to make vision dependable.
- **[TS a6 — The post is the gripper](explorers/terminal-supply/ideas/a6-post-is-the-gripper.md)** · L/S. A near-pointed 0.64 mm header pin
  in a narrow holder spears each contact's box from the front, measures it on a
  backlight, sets it on an open die on a ±0.2 mm float, stays in through the crimp
  and retracts like a pen; ~115 s a crimp [calc]. Reaching a housing needs another
  hold ([TS a4b](explorers/terminal-supply/ideas/a4b-through-cavity-post.md), [TS a4c](explorers/terminal-supply/ideas/a4c-post-bed-one-push.md), [TS x2](explorers/terminal-supply/ideas/x2-crown-then-sort.md)). *Open:* the contact's grip on the pin;
  pin, float, backstop and force reading in a few cubic centimetres; pin wear.
- **[SL v1b — A cheap printer's three axes become the stage](explorers/machine-that-sees-and-learns/ideas/v1b-printer-as-stage.md)** · L/S. An
  Ender-3-class printer ($186–219 [Prime]) without its hot end gives the axes for
  v1, v8, HT a4c or RP a1, in four layouts. Pictures close the loop, so the
  printer only has to repeat over a few tenths from one side
  ([sketch](explorers/machine-that-sees-and-learns/sketches/w3-v1b-axes.svg)).
  *Open:* which layout suits the chosen press; frame stiffness on its back; a

- **[BM b5 — A desktop arm as the operator of the other stations](explorers/borrowed-machines/ideas/b5-desktop-arm-as-operator.md)** · L/S. A ring
  of one-step stations with kinematic docks round a desktop arm. At the cheap tier
  ($185–460, SO-101 class) it only carries cassettes, approaching every dock the

- **[SL v2 — An arm taught by Derek's hands](explorers/machine-that-sees-and-learns/ideas/v2-arm-taught-by-hand.md)**. An SO-101 follower arm with its
