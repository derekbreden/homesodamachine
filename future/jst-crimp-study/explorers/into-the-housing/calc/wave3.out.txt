
==============================================================================
A. The insulation step's mouth at 2.5 mm pitch, in every host that crimps in the row
==============================================================================
The stepped crimper's insulation step must swallow the insulation wings at first touch.
Outer half-width needed = s/2 + m (capture margin) + l (wall land at the mouth), and that
outer face passes down beside the neighbour. Available half-width depends on what lies
beside the working axis at the insulation barrel's Y [ctq w3 section 1, reproduced]:
   i2/K1, i1b, k6 (uncrimped side): a seated or waiting neighbour's 1.7 mm jacket: 2.5 - 0.85 = 1.65 mm
   i2b pass 2, k6 (crimped side): a crimped insulation barrel ~2.0 wide:        2.5 - 1.00 = 1.50 mm
   i2b pass 1: empty cavities both sides:                                          >= 2.5 mm

   beside a jacket (1.65):
     pre-formed keyhole (c6), low     s 1.96  tight: need 1.13, margin +0.52 | generous: need 1.33, margin +0.32
     pre-formed keyhole (c6), high    s 2.14  tight: need 1.22, margin +0.43 | generous: need 1.42, margin +0.23
     clone open, drawing min          s 2.46  tight: need 1.38, margin +0.27 | generous: need 1.58, margin +0.07
     clone open, typical              s 2.80  tight: need 1.55, margin +0.10 | generous: need 1.75, margin -0.10
     clone open, drawing max          s 3.00  tight: need 1.65, margin -0.00 | generous: need 1.85, margin -0.20
     clone open, max + tol            s 3.25  tight: need 1.78, margin -0.13 | generous: need 1.98, margin -0.33

   beside a crimped barrel (1.50):
     pre-formed keyhole (c6), low     s 1.96  tight: need 1.13, margin +0.37 | generous: need 1.33, margin +0.17
     pre-formed keyhole (c6), high    s 2.14  tight: need 1.22, margin +0.28 | generous: need 1.42, margin +0.08
     clone open, drawing min          s 2.46  tight: need 1.38, margin +0.12 | generous: need 1.58, margin -0.08
     clone open, typical              s 2.80  tight: need 1.55, margin -0.05 | generous: need 1.75, margin -0.25
     clone open, drawing max          s 3.00  tight: need 1.65, margin -0.15 | generous: need 1.85, margin -0.35
     clone open, max + tol            s 3.25  tight: need 1.78, margin -0.28 | generous: need 1.98, margin -0.48

Push-aside (i2, i1b, k6's uncrimped side only): the step's outer face, with a lead-in
chamfer on its neighbour side, moves the neighbour's jacket sideways by the shortfall.
The neighbour's wire is held at its crimped insulation barrel (inside the cavity, 0.2-1.8 mm
inside the rear face, section H) and at its saddle 10-20 mm back; the push lands at contact
n's insulation barrel, 2.3-5.3 mm behind the rear face [estimate from box 1.4-2 mm deep and
contact length 5.8-6.73 mm, source S19-S22]. Fixed-fixed beam, point load:
   load 2.5 mm from the barrel, span 12.0: push 0.10 mm ->  0.56 N; root bend radius   16.5 mm (sets a little)
   load 2.5 mm from the barrel, span 12.0: push 0.33 mm ->  1.85 N; root bend radius    5.0 mm (sets a little)
   load 2.5 mm from the barrel, span 20.0: push 0.10 mm ->  0.42 N; root bend radius   18.2 mm (sets a little)
   load 2.5 mm from the barrel, span 20.0: push 0.33 mm ->  1.37 N; root bend radius    5.5 mm (sets a little)
   load 4.5 mm from the barrel, span 12.0: push 0.10 mm ->  0.20 N; root bend radius   42.2 mm (sets a little)
   load 4.5 mm from the barrel, span 12.0: push 0.33 mm ->  0.65 N; root bend radius   12.8 mm (sets a little)
   load 4.5 mm from the barrel, span 20.0: push 0.10 mm ->  0.10 N; root bend radius   52.3 mm (sets a little)
   load 4.5 mm from the barrel, span 20.0: push 0.33 mm ->  0.34 N; root bend radius   15.9 mm (sets a little)
   load 7.0 mm from the barrel, span 12.0: push 0.10 mm ->  0.18 N; root bend radius   68.1 mm (elastic)
   load 7.0 mm from the barrel, span 12.0: push 0.33 mm ->  0.58 N; root bend radius   20.6 mm (sets a little)
   load 7.0 mm from the barrel, span 20.0: push 0.10 mm ->  0.05 N; root bend radius  106.2 mm (elastic)
   load 7.0 mm from the barrel, span 20.0: push 0.33 mm ->  0.15 N; root bend radius   32.2 mm (sets a little)
   Local indentation instead of bending: ~1 N on a 1.5 x 0.5 mm patch is ~1.3 MPa, strain
   0.25-0.5 at E 2.5-5.5 MPa: 0.12-0.25 mm of the push can be taken by the jacket itself.
   Line load on the jacket <~1 N over ~1.5 mm = <~0.7 N/mm, against a tear strength of 15-25 N/mm
   [source Primasil, via ribbon-as-pallet]: a polished face with a lead-in does not cut it.
   Friction drag on the neighbour, <~1 N toward -Z, is below the 5 N latch test.
-> Beside a jacket, pushing it aside 0.10-0.33 mm costs 0.05-1.9 N (less where the jacket
   indents) and, when the push is large and close to the housing, a slight set in the
   neighbour's wire 2-7 mm behind the rear face, bent away from the working cavity. With that allowance the
   step swallows every clone spread (need <= 1.98 mm, available 1.65 + 0.33). Beside a crimped
   barrel (i2b pass 2, k6's crimped side) nothing yields: only pre-formed contacts, or kit
   wings narrower than ~2.5 mm (tight) to ~2.2 mm (generous), fit.

==============================================================================
B. Holding the camera-set tip until the crimper arrives (i2)
==============================================================================
The hump presser sets the tip's Y by pressing the saddle hump. If it lifts before the crimp,
the hump's elastic share recovers and pulls the tip back. The most that recovery can push or
pull along the wire is of the order of the hump's elastic buckling load:
   hump chord 10.0 mm, EI 14.5: pinned 1.43 N, clamped 5.72 N
   hump chord 15.0 mm, EI 14.5: pinned 0.64 N, clamped 2.54 N
   hump chord 20.0 mm, EI 14.5: pinned 0.36 N, clamped 1.43 N
   A keyhole bore grips the jacket axially at 0.2-4 N [change-the-question c6, w2 section 4].
-> If the hump presser stays down through the stroke (it works 5-15 mm behind the dies, outside
   their footprint), nothing recovers and the tip's Y is geometry: web clamp, pressed hump, tip.
   The presser foot at the barrels must leave; it held Z, not Y. The conductor's lift at the
   barrels is 1-44 mN [ctq w3 section 2], so a sprung hold-down pad in the insulation step's
   channel (a few newtons, leading the walls by ~1 mm) keeps it down through first touch.

==============================================================================
C. A box-nose stop that does not fight conductor-barrel growth (i2d, i2d'')
==============================================================================
Growth of the conductor barrel's front toward the box: 0.03-0.11 mm; the flow can push with
80-520 N; the transition yields at ~90-310 N [ctq w3 section 3, estimates].
Preloaded stub: a spring holds the stub carrier -Y against the adjusting screw with preload P.
The person's push (1-2 N [estimate]) never lifts it off; growth does, at force P.
   P 10.0 N: nose bearing on the PA6 face   5.9 MPa (PA6 yield ~50-90 [estimate]); transition load 10.0 N against 90-310 N yield
   P 20.0 N: nose bearing on the PA6 face  11.8 MPa (PA6 yield ~50-90 [estimate]); transition load 20.0 N against 90-310 N yield
   P 30.0 N: nose bearing on the PA6 face  17.6 MPa (PA6 yield ~50-90 [estimate]); transition load 30.0 N against 90-310 N yield
Unpreloaded but compliant stub (no hard stop), stiffness k in Y:
   k   100 N/mm: the 1-2 N push moves the reference 0.010-0.020 mm; growth 0.03-0.11 mm raises the force to  3.0-11.0 N
   k   250 N/mm: the 1-2 N push moves the reference 0.004-0.008 mm; growth 0.03-0.11 mm raises the force to  7.5-27.5 N
-> A 10-30 N preload against the adjusting screw keeps the reference exact under the person's
   push and gives way to growth at 6-18 MPa on the PA6 face, a third or less of its yield.
   A steel pocket (i2d'') on a preloaded slide behaves the same. A rigid steel nose stop does not.

==============================================================================
D. Roll of a box in a stub: clone boxes, and a floor shim that takes up height
==============================================================================
Small-angle roll of a W x H box in a channel C wide and D tall is the smaller of
(C - W) / H and (D - H) / W.
   JST envelope  1.95 x 2.40 in C 2.00: width-limited roll +/- 1.2 deg | with height clearance 0.05 (shim): +/- 1.2 deg | with height clearance 0.10 (shim): +/- 1.2 deg
   JST envelope  1.95 x 2.40 in C 2.10: width-limited roll +/- 3.6 deg | with height clearance 0.05 (shim): +/- 1.5 deg | with height clearance 0.10 (shim): +/- 2.9 deg
   clone large   1.90 x 2.35 in C 2.00: width-limited roll +/- 2.4 deg | with height clearance 0.05 (shim): +/- 1.5 deg | with height clearance 0.10 (shim): +/- 2.4 deg
   clone large   1.90 x 2.35 in C 2.10: width-limited roll +/- 4.9 deg | with height clearance 0.05 (shim): +/- 1.5 deg | with height clearance 0.10 (shim): +/- 3.0 deg
   clone small   1.85 x 2.20 in C 2.00: width-limited roll +/- 3.9 deg | with height clearance 0.05 (shim): +/- 1.5 deg | with height clearance 0.10 (shim): +/- 3.1 deg
   clone small   1.85 x 2.20 in C 2.10: width-limited roll +/- 6.5 deg | with height clearance 0.05 (shim): +/- 1.5 deg | with height clearance 0.10 (shim): +/- 3.1 deg
-> Clone boxes roll up to +/-6.5 deg in a loose cavity, above the 5 deg low end of the 5-11 deg
   window [digest]. A stencil-foil shim on the stub's floor (0.05-0.10 mm of height clearance
   left) limits roll to +/-1.5-3.1 deg whatever the width clearance, and it touches only the box.

E. Contact length spread in a gang push (i6, i6b, i3)
==============================================================================
Clone drawings: 5.8 / 6.2 / 6.73 / 5.9 mm, each +/-0.25 [source S19-S22]; within a lot unknown.
A rigid backing blade sets every rear on one line; the longest contact bottoms first.
Two repairs:
 (1) Tines on constant-force springs at 1.25 x single insertion: each rides back after it bottoms.
     XHP-4: preload 12.2 N each, all bottomed:   49.0 N on the nest cell
     XHP-4: preload 18.8 N each, all bottomed:   75.0 N on the nest cell
     XHP-7: preload 12.2 N each, all bottomed:   85.8 N on the nest cell
     XHP-7: preload 18.8 N each, all bottomed:  131.2 N on the nest cell
     XHP-9: preload 12.2 N each, all bottomed:  110.2 N on the nest cell
     XHP-9: preload 18.8 N each, all bottomed:  168.8 N on the nest cell
     Ride-back = spread + overtravel; the conductor stores it as a bow over its split:
       ride-back 0.10 mm over a 20.0 mm split: bow height ~0.9 mm
       ride-back 0.10 mm over a 30.0 mm split: bow height ~1.1 mm
       ride-back 0.25 mm over a 20.0 mm split: bow height ~1.4 mm
       ride-back 0.25 mm over a 30.0 mm split: bow height ~1.7 mm
       ride-back 0.50 mm over a 20.0 mm split: bow height ~1.9 mm
       ride-back 0.50 mm over a 30.0 mm split: bow height ~2.4 mm
 (2) Push to the first wall, then one finishing tine visits each cavity and pushes that contact
     to its own wall with a force ceiling, logging fold, snap and wall per contact. The extra
     travel is the spread (<= ~0.5 mm), drawn from the conductor's bow; the target comb drops
     away first so its slot grip (1.4-20 N, section G) is not added to the push.
-> (2) needs one tine and the stage that already exists; (1) needs a spring per tine at 2.5 mm
   pitch. Flat constant-force coils are wider than 2.5 mm [assumption], so they stagger in
   3-4 rows or act through push-wires.

I. k8 (c1c + i6): pallet A swinging into the working plane over sorted row-B conductors
==============================================================================
Sorted conductors run from the root (Z = 0) down to the target row (Z = -h) over the span; at a
fraction f of the span they are f*h below the working plane. A carrier body reaches d_c below
the plane; a wire's radius is 0.85 mm. Clear when f*h > d_c + 0.85 + 0.3.
   drop h 4.0 mm, carrier depth 1.0: pallet must sit at f > 0.54 of the span
   drop h 4.0 mm, carrier depth 2.0: pallet must sit at f > 0.79 of the span
   drop h 6.0 mm, carrier depth 1.0: pallet must sit at f > 0.36 of the span
   drop h 6.0 mm, carrier depth 2.0: pallet must sit at f > 0.53 of the span
   drop h 8.0 mm, carrier depth 1.0: pallet must sit at f > 0.27 of the span
   drop h 8.0 mm, carrier depth 2.0: pallet must sit at f > 0.39 of the span
-> A pallet parked up and back reaches the working plane from above and never sweeps the
   sorted conductors; once there, shallow carriers near the tips clear them for h >= ~6 mm.
   Parked down and back (c1c as drawn), its arc sweeps the space in front of and below the
   root, where every sorted conductor lies.

J. k7: what the pre-formed contact does at 2.5 mm, and the snap's load path
==============================================================================
Pre-formed insulation barrel: keyhole outside 1.96-2.14 mm, bore 1.55-1.60, throat 1.3-1.5
[change-the-question c6, w2 section 2]. Height of the keyhole's top above the barrel floor's
underside ~ floor 0.2 + bore 1.6 + wing 0.2 = ~2.0 mm [estimate], against 2.75-3.20 open
[source S19-S22] and 1.50-1.60 for the conductor wings.
   keyhole 1.96: need 1.13; beside a jacket margin +0.52, beside a crimped barrel +0.37
   keyhole 1.96: need 1.33; beside a jacket margin +0.32, beside a crimped barrel +0.17
   keyhole 2.14: need 1.22; beside a jacket margin +0.43, beside a crimped barrel +0.28
   keyhole 2.14: need 1.42; beside a jacket margin +0.23, beside a crimped barrel +0.08
   Loading an even cavity between crimped odds (gap = 2.5 - s/2 - 1.0):
     spread 1.96: gap +0.52 mm
     spread 2.14: gap +0.43 mm
     spread 2.46: gap +0.27 mm
     spread 2.80: gap +0.10 mm
     spread 3.00: gap +0.00 mm
     spread 3.25: gap -0.12 mm
Snap: 0.5-20 N down on the jacket over the insulation barrel [c6 w2 section 4]. The anvil
must be under the insulation barrel too. Its front edge sits behind the lance tip (2.24-2.64 mm
behind the contact's front [source S22]); the insulation barrel is ~4.3-5.7 mm behind the front
[estimate], so the snap lands 1.7-3.5 mm behind the anvil's front edge, and the lance carries none.
Bore grip 0.2-4 N against the hump's elastic push (section B): with the hump presser held down
the bore need only resist the crimper's first touch, which the steel flare and walls take.
