
============================================================================
1. How far behind the crimp the wire may be pushed without buckling
============================================================================
strand-sum EI (60 x 0.08 mm copper)       =   14.115 N*mm^2
  + silicone E= 1.0 MPa, OD 1.7: EI total =  14.511 N*mm^2
  + silicone E= 5.0 MPa, OD 1.7: EI total =  16.099 N*mm^2
  + silicone E=10.0 MPa, OD 1.7: EI total =  18.082 N*mm^2

Longest unsupported wire (mm) that carries the push without buckling, P = pi^2 EI/(K L)^2
K = 2.0 (gripped behind, contact free to swing), 1.0 (both ends guided), 0.5 (both ends clamped square)
  F (N) |  K=2 soft K=2 stiff |  K=1 soft K=1 stiff | K=0.5 soft K=0.5 stiff
    3.0 |      3.45      3.86 |      6.91      7.71 |      13.82       15.43
    8.0 |      2.12      2.36 |      4.23      4.72 |       8.46        9.45
   14.7 |      1.56      1.74 |      3.12      3.48 |       6.24        6.97
   25.0 |      1.20      1.34 |      2.39      2.67 |       4.79        5.34
-> a gripper pushing through the insulation must hold the wire within ~1-3 mm of the
   insulation barrel (K=2, the contact nose free before it finds the cavity); a guide that
   keeps the contact square (K=0.5-1) stretches that to ~3-8 mm. Pushing from farther back
   needs a tube or channel around the wire.

2. Room for a tool at 2.5 mm pitch, between neighbours
============================================================================
wire OD 1.6: free width between the two neighbour wires = 3.40 mm (tool half-width must be < 1.70)
wire OD 1.7: free width between the two neighbour wires = 3.30 mm (tool half-width must be < 1.65)
wire OD 1.8: free width between the two neighbour wires = 3.20 mm (tool half-width must be < 1.60)
neighbour contacts crimped at 1.80 mm wide: free width between their barrels = 3.20 mm
neighbour contacts crimped at 1.90 mm wide: free width between their barrels = 3.10 mm
neighbour contacts crimped at 2.05 mm wide: free width between their barrels = 2.95 mm
  punch walls 0.3 mm on a 1.80 barrel -> punch 2.40 wide; clearance to a 1.7 mm neighbour wire each side +0.45 mm
  punch walls 0.3 mm on a 1.90 barrel -> punch 2.50 wide; clearance to a 1.7 mm neighbour wire each side +0.40 mm
  punch walls 0.3 mm on a 2.05 barrel -> punch 2.65 wide; clearance to a 1.7 mm neighbour wire each side +0.32 mm
  punch walls 0.4 mm on a 1.80 barrel -> punch 2.60 wide; clearance to a 1.7 mm neighbour wire each side +0.35 mm
  punch walls 0.4 mm on a 1.90 barrel -> punch 2.70 wide; clearance to a 1.7 mm neighbour wire each side +0.30 mm
  punch walls 0.4 mm on a 2.05 barrel -> punch 2.85 wide; clearance to a 1.7 mm neighbour wire each side +0.23 mm
  punch walls 0.5 mm on a 1.80 barrel -> punch 2.80 wide; clearance to a 1.7 mm neighbour wire each side +0.25 mm
  punch walls 0.5 mm on a 1.90 barrel -> punch 2.90 wide; clearance to a 1.7 mm neighbour wire each side +0.20 mm
  punch walls 0.5 mm on a 2.05 barrel -> punch 3.05 wide; clearance to a 1.7 mm neighbour wire each side +0.12 mm
-> a narrow blade punch (<= ~3.0 mm wide where it passes the wire plane) fits between
   neighbours with 0.1-0.3 mm to spare; anything wider (a hand-tool jaw) needs the
   neighbours moved out of the plane, or the conductor moved out of the row.
  pusher blade for cavity 2.00 wide: slot 1.35 (squeezes the 1.7 wire), tines 0.30 mm, bearing on a 1.90 barrel 0.27 mm per side
  pusher blade for cavity 2.00 wide: slot 1.45 (squeezes the 1.7 wire), tines 0.25 mm, bearing on a 1.90 barrel 0.22 mm per side
  pusher blade for cavity 2.00 wide: slot 1.55 (squeezes the 1.7 wire), tines 0.20 mm, bearing on a 1.90 barrel 0.17 mm per side
  pusher blade for cavity 2.10 wide: slot 1.35 (squeezes the 1.7 wire), tines 0.35 mm, bearing on a 1.90 barrel 0.27 mm per side
  pusher blade for cavity 2.10 wide: slot 1.45 (squeezes the 1.7 wire), tines 0.30 mm, bearing on a 1.90 barrel 0.22 mm per side
  pusher blade for cavity 2.10 wide: slot 1.55 (squeezes the 1.7 wire), tines 0.25 mm, bearing on a 1.90 barrel 0.17 mm per side

Round guide tube around the wire: the box's diagonal is 3.09 mm, so a round tube must have
ID >= 3.09 mm; with a 0.225 mm wall (K&S metric brass) OD >= 3.54 mm, wider than the
3.30 mm free between neighbour wires. K&S 3.0 x 0.225 (ID 2.55) does not pass the box.
-> a guide around the contact at 2.5 mm pitch is a U-channel or a rectangular slot, not a round tube.

3. Pin order: which looms can be laid straight across from ribbon to housing
============================================================================
J4 SENSORS (4P+3P -> XHP-7): minimum crossings = 3, ribbons left->right ('4P', '3P')
     4P: pins [1, 3, 4, 5]
     3P: pins [2, 6, 7]
J7 REEDS B (5P+3P -> XHP-7, one 3P conductor trimmed): minimum crossings = 2, ribbons left->right ('5P', '3P')
     5P: pins [1, 2, 3, 4, 7]
     3P: pins [5, 6]
J2 MANIFOLD B (3P+3P -> XHP-6, cavity 3 empty): minimum crossings = 0, ribbons left->right ('3P a', '3P b')
     3P a: pins [1, 2, 3]
     3P b: pins [4, 5, 6]
J1 (5P+4P), J3, J5, J6, J9, J11, J13: single ribbon or split not stated -> assumed straight
-> J4 needs one conductor (the 3P's GND, to pin 2) to cross three 4P conductors;
   J7 needs the 5P's GND (to pin 7) to cross CLO and CHI. A gang arrangement that
   keeps ribbon order into the housing cannot make J4 or J7 as the repo assigns them.

4. Where the contact sits in the housing, and the crimp-in-the-cavity window
============================================================================
contact 6.10 long, front wall 0.4: seated rear of insulation barrel is +1.25 mm inside the rear face
contact 6.10 long, front wall 0.8: seated rear of insulation barrel is +0.85 mm inside the rear face
contact 6.50 long, front wall 0.4: seated rear of insulation barrel is +0.85 mm inside the rear face
contact 6.50 long, front wall 0.8: seated rear of insulation barrel is +0.45 mm inside the rear face
contact 6.73 long, front wall 0.4: seated rear of insulation barrel is +0.62 mm inside the rear face
contact 6.73 long, front wall 0.8: seated rear of insulation barrel is +0.22 mm inside the rear face
-> the last 0.4-1.2 mm of the push happens with the insulation barrel inside the
   cavity (unless the seated contact stands proud): the pusher must fit the cavity,
   or the push ends on something already inside (the box shoulder is gone by then).

Crimp in the cavity: box held in the cavity entry by depth d; conductor-barrel front
must be >= c clear of the rear face for the die's front wall.
  transition 0.2, die wall clearance 0.2, entry chamfer 0.2: box engaged 2.00 mm, of which straight wall 1.80 mm
  transition 0.2, die wall clearance 0.2, entry chamfer 0.4: box engaged 2.00 mm, of which straight wall 1.60 mm
  transition 0.2, die wall clearance 0.4, entry chamfer 0.2: box engaged 1.80 mm, of which straight wall 1.60 mm
  transition 0.2, die wall clearance 0.4, entry chamfer 0.4: box engaged 1.80 mm, of which straight wall 1.40 mm
  transition 0.5, die wall clearance 0.2, entry chamfer 0.2: box engaged 2.30 mm, of which straight wall 2.10 mm
  transition 0.5, die wall clearance 0.2, entry chamfer 0.4: box engaged 2.30 mm, of which straight wall 1.90 mm
  transition 0.5, die wall clearance 0.4, entry chamfer 0.2: box engaged 2.10 mm, of which straight wall 1.90 mm
  transition 0.5, die wall clearance 0.4, entry chamfer 0.4: box engaged 2.10 mm, of which straight wall 1.70 mm
  lance tip 2.20 behind front -> lance first touches the entry at depth ~2.20 mm
  lance tip 2.44 behind front -> lance first touches the entry at depth ~2.44 mm
  lance tip 2.60 behind front -> lance first touches the entry at depth ~2.60 mm
-> the box can sit 1.4-2.3 mm deep with the conductor barrel still outside; the lance
   meets the entry at ~2.2-2.6 mm. The window exists only if the transition is long enough,
   which one kit housing and contact under a caliper settles.

5. One contact at a time, or all of a ribbon at once
============================================================================
4 contacts pushed together:    12 to   100 N total (4x 3-25 N)
5 contacts pushed together:    15 to   125 N total (5x 3-25 N)
7 contacts pushed together:    21 to   175 N total (7x 3-25 N)
9 contacts pushed together:    27 to   225 N total (9x 3-25 N)
Staggered gang (spring-backed wire clamps): each clamp's preload must exceed the
largest single insertion force so it stays put until its contact bottoms.
  preload 20 N, rate 2 N/mm, 1 mm stagger: first contact's clamp rides back up to 8 mm, ending at 36 N (J1, 9 contacts)
  preload 20 N, rate 5 N/mm, 1 mm stagger: first contact's clamp rides back up to 8 mm, ending at 60 N (J1, 9 contacts)
  preload 30 N, rate 2 N/mm, 1 mm stagger: first contact's clamp rides back up to 8 mm, ending at 46 N (J1, 9 contacts)
  preload 30 N, rate 5 N/mm, 1 mm stagger: first contact's clamp rides back up to 8 mm, ending at 70 N (J1, 9 contacts)
-> the stagger turns one push into N separate force events in a known order;
   each clamp's own switch reports that its contact bottomed.

6. Sensing the latch
============================================================================
  push 0.05 mm/s, HX711 at 10 SPS:    200 samples per mm of travel
  push 0.05 mm/s, HX711 at 80 SPS:   1600 samples per mm of travel
  push 0.20 mm/s, HX711 at 10 SPS:     50 samples per mm of travel
  push 0.20 mm/s, HX711 at 80 SPS:    400 samples per mm of travel
  push 1.00 mm/s, HX711 at 10 SPS:     10 samples per mm of travel
  push 1.00 mm/s, HX711 at 80 SPS:     80 samples per mm of travel
A lance snap is a force drop over roughly 0.1-0.3 mm of travel [estimate]; at <= 0.2 mm/s
and 80 SPS that is 40-120 samples: a slow push resolves it with a $4 bar cell.

Force-limited pusher without a load cell: a spring and a displacement switch.
  trip at 15 N after 1.0 mm spring travel -> spring rate 15.0 N/mm
  trip at 15 N after 2.0 mm spring travel -> spring rate 7.5 N/mm
  trip at 25 N after 1.0 mm spring travel -> spring rate 25.0 N/mm
  trip at 25 N after 2.0 mm spring travel -> spring rate 12.5 N/mm

Pull-back test window:
  lance retention min (analog) 14.7 N; crimp pull-out min 39.2 N
  pull back at 3 N =  20% of retention min,   8% of crimp pull-out min
  pull back at 5 N =  34% of retention min,  13% of crimp pull-out min
  pull back at 8 N =  54% of retention min,  20% of crimp pull-out min
-> 3-8 N pulls an unlatched contact out and leaves a latched one and its crimp unharmed;
   pass = < ~0.2 mm of travel at the test force (Boeing US 11,374,374 logic).

8. Time per unit (53 crimps), rough, for scale
============================================================================
  i1 walk the housing past the head (feed 20 s, lay 10, crimp 30, insert 20, test 10): ~  80 min per unit
  i2 crimp in the cavity (load 20 s, lay 10, crimp 30, push 15, test 10): ~  75 min per unit
  i3 shuttles + gang push (per contact 60 s, plus 60 s per ribbon for converge/push): ~  67 min per unit
  i4 gantry hand (pick 30 s, look 10, insert 30, pull test 10) on top of any crimper: ~  71 min per unit
  i5 person inserts on the sensing nest (attended, ~8 s per contact): ~   7 min per unit
-> every arrangement fits inside an afternoon; none of these times matters against a
   week of printing. What matters is how often the person is called back.

9. Feed-length rule: one-at-a-time insertion stores the insertion travel as slack
============================================================================
  crimp in the cavity (box 2.0 mm in the entry): conductor must feed  5.2 mm after its crimp
  crimp with box nose 1 mm behind the rear face: conductor must feed  8.2 mm after its crimp
  crimp at a head 5 mm behind the rear face: conductor must feed 12.2 mm after its crimp
  gang push of a row (housing moves): conductor must feed  0.0 mm after its crimp

Stored as a hump between ribbon clamp and tip (parabolic arch, chord c, sag h):
  h = sqrt(3 c f / 8)
  chord 10.0 mm -> f=5: h= 4.3  f=8: h= 5.5  f=10: h= 6.1
  chord 15.0 mm -> f=5: h= 5.3  f=8: h= 6.7  f=10: h= 7.5
  chord 20.0 mm -> f=5: h= 6.1  f=8: h= 7.7  f=10: h= 8.7
  chord 30.0 mm -> f=5: h= 7.5  f=8: h= 9.5  f=10: h=10.6

Stored as a straight lift of the tip up to a head above the row (conductor straight
from the ribbon clamp to the tip, free length L, tip pulled back f):
  L 20.0 mm -> f=8: lift 16.0  f=10: lift 17.3  f=13: lift 18.7
  L 25.0 mm -> f=8: lift 18.3  f=10: lift 20.0  f=13: lift 21.9
  L 30.0 mm -> f=8: lift 20.4  f=10: lift 22.4  f=13: lift 24.7
  L 40.0 mm -> f=8: lift 24.0  f=10: lift 26.5  f=13: lift 29.5
-> a head above the row sits ~15-25 mm up; a hump of 5-9 mm stores the feed in place.
   Either way the split conductors need ~10-30 mm of free length behind the housing
   during the build, even though the finished fan needs only ~6-9 mm.
