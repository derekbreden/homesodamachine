A. How far a contact travels from where it is crimped to where it seats
==============================================================================
Seated box front = housing height 7.75 [mfr S2] minus front wall 0.8-1.0 [assumption, as TS
calc 4] inside the rear face. Staged/loaded box front positions are TS's own numbers.
   seated box front: 6.75-6.95 mm inside the rear face

 a5 (bare contact staged d mm into the rear mouth, crimped there, then pushed home):
   staged 1.0 mm -> push after the crimp 5.75-5.95 mm   (TS a5 text says '~4.5 mm')
   staged 1.5 mm -> push after the crimp 5.25-5.45 mm   (TS a5 text says '~4.5 mm')
   staged 2.0 mm -> push after the crimp 4.75-4.95 mm   (TS a5 text says '~4.5 mm')
   (4.5 mm corresponds to a seated contact whose rear is flush with the rear face; TS calc 4
    puts the seated rear 0.25-0.85 mm inside it, which gives the numbers above)

 a4b (post stands P mm out of the rear face, box engaged e mm on its tip, crimped there):
   post 9.0 proud, box on 1.5: box front 7.5 mm outside -> travel 14.25-14.45 mm
   post 9.0 proud, box on 1.8: box front 7.2 mm outside -> travel 13.95-14.15 mm

 i6b (this explorer; posts 8.5 proud, crimped box threaded 1-2 mm on, housing slides onto all):
   box on 1.0: box front 7.5 outside -> housing travel 14.25-14.45 mm (i6b's housing travel)
   box on 2.0: box front 6.5 outside -> housing travel 13.25-13.45 mm (i6b's housing travel)

==============================================================================
B. Where that travel is stored when one contact is crimped and seated at a time
==============================================================================
With one web clamp for all conductors, square-cut ends, and every contact seated taut, a
contact crimped T short of its seat needs its conductor T longer than the straight line
at the moment of the crimp. The length lives in a bow between root and crimp
(sag h = sqrt(3 L T / 8), shallow-arch approximation) - or, if the housing moves onto
the contact instead, in the already-seated neighbours. A gang push stores none: every
conductor gets its T at once from one move of the web or the housing.
   a5 as written (4.5)          T  4.50 mm -> bow sag  L 20:  5.8,  L 25:  6.5,  L 30:  7.1,  L 40:  8.2 mm
   a5 from TS calc 4 geometry   T  5.35 mm -> bow sag  L 20:  6.3,  L 25:  7.1,  L 30:  7.8,  L 40:  9.0 mm
   a4b, post 9 proud            T 14.20 mm -> bow sag  L 20: 10.3,  L 25: 11.5,  L 30: 12.6,  L 40: 14.6 mm
Tightest bend in that hump if both ends are held straight (clamped-clamped cosine shape,
excess length pi^2 h^2 / 4L, peak curvature 2 pi^2 h / L^2), against the strands' yield
radius ~67 mm [digest]:
   a5   T  5.35, L 25: hump  7.4 mm, tightest radius  4.3 mm -> sets (kinks)
   a5   T  5.35, L 30: hump  8.1 mm, tightest radius  5.7 mm -> sets (kinks)
   a4b  T 14.20, L 25: hump 12.0 mm, tightest radius  2.6 mm -> sets (kinks)
   a4b  T 14.20, L 30: hump 13.1 mm, tightest radius  3.5 mm -> sets (kinks)
   plastic moment of the 60-strand bundle ~0.36 N mm; drawing a 7-13 mm hump straight needs ~0.03-0.05 N of tension
-> each conductor stands in a 6-8 mm hump (a5) or an 11-15 mm hump (a4b) while it is
   crimped, between the web and the die, beside conductors lying flat. The hump bends the
   strands past yield, so it holds its shape rather than springing against neighbours, and
   the push draws it straight with a few hundredths of a newton.

D. Lance and anvil: the window for a flat anvil's front edge (IH calc wave2 G)
==============================================================================
Anvil front edge must sit >= lance tip + 0.1 behind the contact front and <= the conductor
barrel's front (box 2.0 + transition t). Margin = (2.0 + t) - (tip + 0.1).
Lance tip 2.24-2.64 mm [xh S19-S22]; transition t 0.4-0.6 [TS calc wave2 section 5 reading].
   t 0.4:  tip 2.24: +0.06,  tip 2.44: -0.14,  tip 2.64: -0.34 mm
   t 0.5:  tip 2.24: +0.16,  tip 2.44: -0.04,  tip 2.64: -0.24 mm
   t 0.6:  tip 2.24: +0.26,  tip 2.44: +0.06,  tip 2.64: -0.14 mm
   TS a5's own staging numbers (tip 0.94 outside the face at 1.5 staged -> tip 2.44;
   conductor barrel 1.1 behind the face -> front at 2.6): margin +0.06 mm
   TS a5's sentence 'tip ~0.1 mm ahead of the conductor barrel's start' is margin 0 by construction.
-> across the clone-drawing ranges a flat anvil fits in about half the cases; the rest need
   a lance slot in the anvil (conductor barrel carried on the slot's shoulders).

==============================================================================
G. Floating the housing instead of setting the anvil by wedge (TS a5)
==============================================================================
Seated conductor EI ~15.7 N mm^2 [TS calc]; as a fixed-guided beam between web and housing k = 12EI/L^3.
   L 15 mm, 4 seated conductors:  0.223 N/mm -> 0.2 mm of float costs  0.045 N
   L 15 mm, 8 seated conductors:  0.447 N/mm -> 0.2 mm of float costs  0.089 N
   L 20 mm, 4 seated conductors:  0.094 N/mm -> 0.2 mm of float costs  0.019 N
   L 20 mm, 8 seated conductors:  0.188 N/mm -> 0.2 mm of float costs  0.038 N
   L 30 mm, 4 seated conductors:  0.028 N/mm -> 0.2 mm of float costs  0.006 N
   L 30 mm, 8 seated conductors:  0.056 N/mm -> 0.2 mm of float costs  0.011 N
-> the seated wires do not resist a floating nest; a flexure nest of 1-2 N/mm costs 0.2-0.4 N
   at 0.2 mm, against tens of newtons of punch lead-in at first touch. The anvil can be the
   master and the housing can follow, with no per-cavity measurement.
