Reading it:
- A machine that only strips, places and crimps while the person waits (p4) saves no
  minutes unless its cycle is shorter than a hand crimp; it buys consistency and a
  log, and the person does the insertion in the gaps.
- Minutes fall when the person stops being needed per conductor: loading an end's
  worth at once (cassette, spool) and inserting at the machine.
- The crimp is the most skill-dependent step but not the largest share of the
  person's time: peeling, laying in and inserting are.
- 'longest alone' for p3 is the 4P spool run: five units' worth of 4P ends, 140 crimps.
- Calibration: these task estimates give 46 min for the XH work alone by hand, while the
  ledger books 45 min for all twelve harnesses. The absolute minutes are probably high;
  compare the rows with each other, not with the ledger.
