1. Lift needed for an upright crimp from a flat row, and the set each lift leaves in k
============================================================================================
Only k is lifted; the neighbours stay in the row. Heights from the row axis.
(a) Fin anvil from below (p1d, p5c): only the fin passes through the row plane, in k's own
    empty slot. The crimper is <= 3.1-4 mm wide [force-and-form f8], so it never overlaps a
    neighbour laterally; the lift has to clear the posted contacts' lances over their own
    unlifted conductors and the crimped neighbours' tops (+0.95..+1.25):
    h >= 2.8-3.1 mm [force-and-form calc exchange_procedure_w3 §1a]; h = 3.5 mm used here.
(b) SN-2549 closing normal to the row (tool on its side over a flat row, or tip-down over a
    row stood on edge): the jaw half facing the row must pass outside the row plane.
    h = a + 2.7 mm, a = nest floor to the back face of that jaw half, 6-12 mm [assumption,
    hand-tool-as-press a3, calc w3 §2] -> h = 8.7-14.7 mm.
(c) SN-2549 hung tip-down, closing along the row: h = nest-to-tip + 1.85 = 3.9-7.9 mm, but
    the jaw closing direction is the contact's floor normal, so every crimp is rolled 90 deg
    (hand-tool-as-press a3; into-the-housing calc ex §8). Not used.

============================================================================================
2. The fin anvil (p1d, p5c; p1 B-drop and p5 use it free-standing)
============================================================================================
k lifted 3.5 mm: barrel floor underside at +2.94 mm; fin at rest -1.85 mm;
fin travel to the floor 4.79 mm [calc]. The lift finger and the fin meet the
contact at the same height, so the finger sets it and the fin only has to arrive under it.

Width: the anvil sits inside the crimper's channel. JST's analog SXA-01T-P0.6 at 22 AWG
crimps 1.50 wide x 0.80 high [mfr S14], xh-facts estimates ~1.5 for SXH-001T-P0.6. So the
fin is stepped: ~1.45 mm under the conductor barrel, 1.8-1.9 mm under the insulation
barrel [force-and-form calc exchange_procedure_w3 §2].

Clearance, fin side to the neighbour's surface, neighbours at 2.5 mm centres:
  conductor step 1.45 vs uncrimped bare strands                       1.42 mm a side
  conductor step 1.45 vs uncrimped jacket (strip in pose)             0.92 mm a side
  conductor step 1.45 vs crimped conductor barrel (1.5 wide)          1.02 mm a side
  insulation step 1.88 vs uncrimped jacket                            0.71 mm a side
  insulation step 1.88 vs crimped insulation barrel (1.8-2.0 wide)    0.56 mm a side

Where the tip comb can stand. The contact spans, from k's tip (Y=0, +Y toward the box):
  box front ~+2.7, box rear ~+0.7; conductor barrel to ~-1.5..-2.4;
  insulation barrel rear at -3.5 to -4.2 mm.
  Tip-comb pins (0.64 mm) sit between conductors: the clear half-gap between k's two pins is
  0.93 mm, against the insulation step's half-width 0.94 mm, so the fin's rear end
  must stop ahead of the pins: the tip comb stands >= ~4.7 mm behind the tip line
  (5-7 mm), not 4 mm.

Column (fixed base, free top, K = 2; E 205 GPa):
  p1d/p5c fin, narrow only through the row band      1.45 x 4.0 x  5.0 mm: Euler  20.6 kN, stress at 3 kN  517 MPa
  p1/p5 B-drop fin at the JST width, 9 mm tall       1.45 x 4.0 x  9.0 mm: Euler   6.3 kN, stress at 3 kN  517 MPa
  p1/p5 B-drop fin at the JST width, 10 mm tall      1.45 x 4.0 x 10.0 mm: Euler   5.1 kN, stress at 3 kN  517 MPa
  (the crimper's legs guide the top at compaction, so the real load is higher)

Laminated 1095 blue-tempered shim [Prime: spring steel shim 0.005-0.032 in]:
  conductor step: 0.032 + 0.025 in = 1.448 mm
  insulation step: 0.032 + 0.032 + 0.01 in = 1.880 mm
  Spring temper (~Rc 45-50 [assumption]) against 400-900 MPa mean die pressure [xh-facts §4] is
  the open question; ground O1 or gauge plate stood on edge is the harder route.

============================================================================================
3. A knee near straight: the bottom is geometry
============================================================================================
Two links of length L; the joint sits x off the straight line. Height short of straight
= 2(L - sqrt(L^2 - x^2)) ~ x^2 / L:
  L 15 mm: x 0.1 mm ->   0.7 um, x 0.2 mm ->   2.7 um, x 0.5 mm ->  16.7 um, x 1.0 mm ->  66.7 um
  L 20 mm: x 0.1 mm ->   0.5 um, x 0.2 mm ->   2.0 um, x 0.5 mm ->  12.5 um, x 1.0 mm ->  50.0 um
  L 30 mm: x 0.1 mm ->   0.3 um, x 0.2 mm ->   1.3 um, x 0.5 mm ->   8.3 um, x 1.0 mm ->  33.3 um
-> A cam or screw that puts the joint within 0.2-0.5 mm of straight sets the bottom to a few
   microns. What moves the crimp height is the loop's stretch under force scatter:
   loop  40 kN/mm, +/-300-600 N of scatter -> +/-7.5-15.0 um
   loop 100 kN/mm, +/-300-600 N of scatter -> +/-3.0-6.0 um
   loop 200 kN/mm, +/-300-600 N of scatter -> +/-1.5-3.0 um

============================================================================================
4. Stops under an eccentric (p5): what a margin costs, and a compliant loop
============================================================================================
The eccentric must be able to push past the stops by a margin m (frame creep, bearing play,
shim error: 0.02-0.10 mm [assumption]). Once the stops touch, the loop is squeezed by the
rest of the travel, so the frame carries F + k m at bottom dead centre (k = loop stiffness
outside the stops). A doubled contact (a rigid 0.2 mm) meets F + k (m + 0.2).
crimp force 2.43 kN ('high' case)
 k kN/mm    m 0.02    m 0.05    m 0.10   doubled, m 0.10
      40     3.23      4.43      6.43           14.43 kN
      20     2.83      3.43      4.43            8.43 kN
      10     2.63      2.93      3.43            5.43 kN
       5     2.53      2.68      2.93            3.93 kN
       2     2.47      2.53      2.63            3.03 kN
-> A stiff loop pays 1.3-2.6x the crimp every stroke for the margin and puts 14 kN into a
   doubled contact; a loop of 5-10 kN/mm pays 1.05-1.4x and caps the doubled contact at
   3.9-5.4 kN with no preloaded stack. Height stays on the stops, in a short steel loop at the
   dies (~670 kN/mm [hand-tool-as-press calc H §4]). Peak shaft torque with the frame's
   wind-up counted is 1.0-2.3 N*m for loops of 2-40 kN/mm [force-and-form calc
   exchange_procedure_w3 §3].

A rod spring that sets k when the frame is stiffer than wanted [assumption: DIN 2093 series A
table values, not fetched]: one A25 disc (25 x 12.2 x 1.5 mm, h0 0.55) carries ~2.9 kN at 75 %
of its travel (~0.41 mm): ~7 kN/mm, nearly linear; two stacked in series ~3.5 kN/mm.
  frame 20 kN/mm + rod spring 3.5 kN/mm -> loop  3.0 kN/mm, surplus at m 0.10 0.30 kN
  frame 20 kN/mm + rod spring 7.0 kN/mm -> loop  5.2 kN/mm, surplus at m 0.10 0.52 kN
  frame 40 kN/mm + rod spring 3.5 kN/mm -> loop  3.2 kN/mm, surplus at m 0.10 0.32 kN
  frame 40 kN/mm + rod spring 7.0 kN/mm -> loop  6.0 kN/mm, surplus at m 0.10 0.60 kN

Load cell placement: a 500 kg button cell deflects ~0.05-0.1 mm at full scale (~50-100 kN/mm
[estimate]). Inside the stop loop (between fin and fin holder) it moves height by:
  50 kN/mm: +/-6-12 um for +/-300-600 N of scatter
  100 kN/mm: +/-3-6 um for +/-300-600 N of scatter
  Under the whole lower die (fin holder and stops together) it is outside the stop loop and
  sees the stop contact as a sharp rise in stiffness. Foil gauges on a steel member add no
  compliance at all [Prime: foil strain gauges BF350].

============================================================================================
5. p7: pushing the slug through pads, and what the pads' squeeze costs
============================================================================================
The blades' front faces bear only on the cut caps' end faces: 0.28-0.65 mm2 per conductor,
which crush at 2-5x silicone's 8-11 MPa before the flanks tear [force-and-form calc
exchange_procedure_w3 §6]. A pad pair on the slug's top and bottom, travelling with the
blades, carries the push by friction. The same squeeze presses the jacket onto the strands,
so the slug's own friction on the strands rises too:
  net drive per conductor = 2 N (mu_pad - mu_js) + F_cap,  need >= T_tear + S0
  N = pad force per side per conductor; mu_pad pad on silicone; mu_js silicone on tinned
  strands; F_cap what the cap faces carry before they crush (8-11 MPa x 0.28-0.65 mm2);
  T_tear 3-15 N per conductor [digest; wave2 §7]; S0 the unsqueezed slug's friction on its
  strands, 0.5-3 N [assumption].
  smooth TPU pad: mu_pad 0.8, mu_js 0.5
    T  3 N/cond: pad force   6.3 N per side per conductor,    32 N per side for a 5P; pad pressure on ~2.6-3.3 mm2 of crown  1.9- 2.4 MPa
    T 15 N/cond: pad force  26.3 N per side per conductor,   132 N per side for a 5P; pad pressure on ~2.6-3.3 mm2 of crown  8.0-10.1 MPa
  grippy TPU pad: mu_pad 1.2, mu_js 0.3
    T  3 N/cond: pad force   2.1 N per side per conductor,    11 N per side for a 5P; pad pressure on ~2.6-3.3 mm2 of crown  0.6- 0.8 MPa
    T 15 N/cond: pad force   8.8 N per side per conductor,    44 N per side for a 5P; pad pressure on ~2.6-3.3 mm2 of crown  2.7- 3.4 MPa
  fine-toothed pad (teeth bite the jacket) [assumption]: mu_pad 2.0, mu_js 0.4
    T  3 N/cond: pad force   1.2 N per side per conductor,     6 N per side for a 5P; pad pressure on ~2.6-3.3 mm2 of crown  0.4- 0.5 MPa
    T 15 N/cond: pad force   4.9 N per side per conductor,    25 N per side for a 5P; pad pressure on ~2.6-3.3 mm2 of crown  1.5- 1.9 MPa
-> Smooth pads need 30-130 N per side on a 5P, and their squeeze presses the jacket onto the
   strands hard enough to cancel most of what they deliver. Grippy TPU needs 11-44 N per side;
   pads that bite (fine teeth, or a knurled steel face) 6-25 N. The teeth mark only the slug,
   which is thrown away.

Slice while pressing: each blade drawn 2-4 mm along its own edge as it closes (an inclined
slot in the carrier). A sharp edge pressed straight in indents silicone ~0.03-0.25 mm before it
cuts [force-and-form estimate], comparable to the 0.2-0.3 mm ligament, so a pressed kerf can
stop short of the steel stop; a sliced one reaches it.
Lower blade slot: the lower blade rides the carriage +Y 3-4 mm, so either the slot is 3.3-4.3 mm
long or the floor ahead of the strip line is part of the carriage (a split floor) and the
lower blade rises through the joint.
