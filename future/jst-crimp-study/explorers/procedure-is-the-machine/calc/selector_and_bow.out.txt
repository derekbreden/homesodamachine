
==============================================================================
1. Selecting one conductor: out-of-plane separation and tip retraction
==============================================================================
Neighbours at 2.5 mm pitch leave 3.30 mm between them [study: into-the-housing].
A crimp jaw wider than that must see its neighbours moved out of the plane by at least
the jaw's depth below (or above) the conductor axis plus OD/2 plus clearance.
  jaw reaches 2 mm past the conductor axis -> separation h >= 3.9 mm
  jaw reaches 4 mm past the conductor axis -> separation h >= 5.8 mm
  jaw reaches 6 mm past the conductor axis -> separation h >= 7.8 mm
  jaw reaches 8 mm past the conductor axis -> separation h >= 9.8 mm

Lifting the active conductor by h at a point s behind its tip region, pivoting at the
web split, pulls its tip back by s - sqrt(s^2 - h^2); the change with s is what a
ragged hand-peeled split point (+/-3 mm) does to the tip:
  s mm  h mm   angle  retract    d/ds  tip var +/-3mm
    15     6   21.8d    1.25  -0.091           0.27
    15     8   28.1d    2.31  -0.182           0.55
    15    10   33.7d    3.82  -0.342           1.02
    20     6   16.7d    0.92  -0.048           0.14
    20     8   21.8d    1.67  -0.091           0.27
    20    10   26.6d    2.68  -0.155           0.46
    25     6   13.5d    0.73  -0.030           0.09
    25     8   17.7d    1.31  -0.056           0.17
    25    10   21.8d    2.09  -0.091           0.27
    30     6   11.3d    0.61  -0.021           0.06

-> retraction is a constant offset if every conductor is lifted the same way; the
   split-point scatter adds 0.1-0.6 mm, which is the same order as the axial window
   (insulation edge between the barrels, brush visible, +/-0.3 mm [estimate]).
   Trimming each tip in the lifted pose, at the crimp station, removes it.

==============================================================================
2. Fan to a working pitch, trim, then converge to 2.5 mm: built-in length error
==============================================================================
Ribbon conductors sit at 1.7 mm; the fan runs over length Lf from the web split to a
comb where the conductors are parallel at working pitch w. Tips trimmed on a straight
line in that pose are equal in x, not in length. Converged to 2.5 mm, the outermost
conductor carries the excess below as slack (bow amplitude ~ 0.64*sqrt(excess*L)).

-> crimping in the row at 2.5 mm needs neighbours moved out of plane (section 1);

==============================================================================
3. One-at-a-time insertion with the ribbon clamped: bow per conductor
==============================================================================
The contact must start 7.4 mm behind its seated position (contact ~6.4 mm
long, plus 1 mm clearance): the conductor between its web split and the gripper
has to shorten its reach by that much, by bowing.
 free length mm  tent height  sine amplitude
             15         6.5             6.7
             20         7.8             7.7
             30         9.9             9.5
             40        11.6            11.0
-> each conductor rises ~8-11 mm out of the row while it is pushed home; the
   bow straightens as the contact seats. Gang insertion (the housing slides onto
   every contact at once) needs no bow: the order 'crimp all, then insert' removes it.

4. Tip wander of an unsupported stick-out under a side load (cantilever)
==============================================================================
 stick-out mm    F=0.02 N    F=0.05 N     F=0.2 N
            5   0.05-0.06   0.12-0.15   0.46-0.59
            8   0.19-0.24   0.47-0.61   1.89-2.42
           10   0.37-0.47   0.92-1.18   3.68-4.73
           15   1.24-1.60   3.11-3.99 12.43-15.96
0.02 N is a light brush; 0.2 N a firm nudge [estimate]. Gravity on 10 mm of conductor
  sags 0.004 mm at 10 mm stick-out (self weight, 5.0 g/m)
  sags 0.022 mm at 15 mm stick-out (self weight, 5.0 g/m)
-> a tip is where the trim put it only until something touches it; each station
   that needs the tip to +/-0.1-0.3 mm should capture it (fork, funnel, jaw) at the
   moment of use, 2-5 mm from the tip.
