1. The stroke that follows a tack: which crimper touches first
==============================================================================
conductor wings meet their crimper with 0.62-0.87 mm of stroke left [SL wave2 s5]
  final 1.8-2.1 (SL wave2 s5 input):
    untacked: insulation wings meet their crimper with +0.65 to +1.40 mm left
    after a 2.3-2.5 mm tack: insulation crimper meets the tacked barrel with +0.20 to +0.70 mm left
  final 2.03-2.46 (SL w3 s9, W 1.80-1.95, no silicone flow):
    untacked: insulation wings meet their crimper with +0.29 to +1.17 mm left
    after a 2.3-2.5 mm tack: insulation crimper meets the tacked barrel with -0.16 to +0.47 mm left
  -> after a tack the conductor wings are met first in most combinations; with the SL w3 s9
     heights the tack can be at or below the final height (negative rows), i.e. not a tack
     but the crimp. A tack height has to be set relative to the final height at the same
     width (v3's tack arm already does this), not as an absolute 2.3-2.5 mm.  [calc]

6. The crank as the height knob and as its own re-touch gauge
==============================================================================
  10:1 planetary, 3200 microsteps/rev: ram travel per microstep at 0.02 mm: 0.16 um; 0.05 mm: 0.26 um; 0.1 mm: 0.36 um; 0.2 mm: 0.51 um
  30:1 worm, 3200 microsteps/rev: ram travel per microstep at 0.02 mm: 0.05 um; 0.05 mm: 0.09 um; 0.1 mm: 0.12 um; 0.2 mm: 0.17 um
  AS5600 12-bit on the crankshaft: height per count at 0.01 mm: 0.9 um; 0.03 mm: 1.6 um; 0.05 mm: 2.0 um
  14-bit magnetic encoder on the crankshaft: height per count at 0.01 mm: 0.2 um; 0.03 mm: 0.4 um; 0.05 mm: 0.5 um
  holding 1000 N at 0.1 mm above BDC: 2.18 N*m at the crank (0.22 N*m at the motor through 10:1)
  holding 3000 N at 0.1 mm above BDC: 6.54 N*m at the crank (0.65 N*m at the motor through 10:1)
  -> stopped short of BDC, the crank sets height in fractions of a micron, and a 10 N re-touch
     read by the shaft encoder measures the unloaded crimp to ~1 um of geometry once r, L and
     BDC are calibrated with one gauge block. Bearing play is taken up in the same direction
     at 10 N as at 3 kN. With both crimpers on one ram the re-touch reads whichever barrel
     springs back higher, the same ambiguity an indicator across the dies has.  [calc]
