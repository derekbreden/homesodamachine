## Things the view turned up that I did not expect

- **Neighbour conductors.** The problem common to every "wire to a fixed tool"
  arrangement is the neighbours, not the contact. A bought applicator solves
  "place the contact" completely, and still crushes any conductor lying beside
  the target at 1.7 or 2.5 mm spacing. The fold-back park with a fixed-axis
  fork came out of that.
- **Crank torque.** A crank press needs almost no torque for the crimp itself:
  3.8 N·m for a 3 kN peak through a 15 mm crank. The applicator's own springs
  may need more at mid-stroke.
- **HX711 force curve.** Slowness makes the cheapest load-cell converter
  useful: ~19 samples in the compaction zone at a 10 s stroke, ~2 at press
  speed. A slow machine can also stop before bottom when the force curve is
  wrong.
- **Wiper motors.** A car wiper motor's park switch is a single-revolution
  clutch, which is exactly what a crimp press needs.
- **Silicone.** Its weak tear, the blade stripper's enemy, is what makes laser
  scoring plus a slug comb work.
- **Shop press.** The idle 12-ton shop press is already an O-frame, and its jack
  is already a slow manual ram for trying an applicator.

### Where I disagreed with the critique, and why

- **The compliant fork (its R2 for b1).** The 60 strands bend one by one;
  their plastic moment is 0.36–0.51 N·mm, so 24–85 mN at the contact, 6–15 mm
  from the hold, sets the conductor. Lay-in loads at the fork are 8–330 mN,
  and 250–2,000 mN wherever a foot presses the insulation. There is no
  breakaway force between the two. The repair that works on the fast press is
  its R3 made mechanical: post-feed, with the conductor-holding parts on the
  ram. The critique's physics of the feed conflict itself stands.

### Looked at in wave 2 and not developed

- **A tightened wire noose as a self-limiting stripper.** It cuts silicone
  evenly from all sides, but once it reaches the bundle a 2–5 N loop puts
  0.4–1.1 N on each 0.08 mm strand it crosses: several thousand MPa of Hertz
  contact against copper's 70–100 MPa. Not self-limiting; it needs a stop like
  any blade. Revive: none unless a much thicker, softer loop shows a useful
  cut-versus-dent window in a test.
- **The harp (egg slicer) as its own idea.** Taut music wire at each seam, the
  end pushed through. It works in compression (a 5P buckles at ~6–56 N over
  15–5 mm free) and its tear runs 0.6–2 mm ahead, so it needs guide combs and a
  clamp at the root: ribbon-as-pallet a7's arrangement with wires for tines.
  Kept as a paragraph in b6. Revive: if printed or stainless tines at 1.7 mm
  prove too thick for the neck.
- **Pneumatic snatch as b1's main repair.** 22–48 ms against a 50–90 ms window
  is tight, and it only helps once the holding parts ride the ram anyway.
  Kept as a second route in b1.
- **Kingsing KS-T903-class strip-and-crimp machine** as a replacement for b1's
  press: it strips with blades on a wire placed by hand, 50 kg, 220 V; the
  blade geometry is unstated. b7's rule applies to it: worth trying only if its
  strip head is rotary with centring, or die-hole.
- **Cryogenic stripping, solvent swelling, abrasive stripping.** Silicone stays
  rubbery far below freezer temperatures; solvents swell it and would reach the
  strands; abrasives are for enamel. None transfer.
- **Aglet, zipper-stop and eyelet machines** (other trades that crimp small
  metal parts onto soft ends from a feed). They feed round or symmetric parts
  from bowls; an XH contact is neither. No transfer beyond what applicators
  already do.

### Where I disagreed, and why

- **Crank angles in terminal-supply's §6.** Their 226° and 274–293° come from
  the slider-crank formula with the obliquity term subtracted, which is a
  crank below a ram it pulls. b1b's crank is above the ram, where the term adds;
  a vector check gives 4.0 mm above bottom at 40° from bottom (220° from top).
  Their point stands with my angles: a back-out from a late gate re-crosses the
  feed finger's retract band. It is recorded in b1c.
- **K1's spreading comb "slides from the clamp face toward the tips".** It
  cannot start there (inside the clamp), and dropped in place its wide end lands
  on conductors still at 1.7 mm. In b8b it enters from the tips after the strip,
  threading the ~1.0 mm gaps between stripped bundles.
- **b1's ±0.4 mm window.** The critique is right that it is a clone number. The
  V on the ram is what centres the conductor at first die touch; with genuine
  wings the V's one-time setting is the whole margin.
- **Where their combinations went.** K1 became b8b. K2 became b1b's and b1c's
  gate 0 and reject turn. K3 went into b2 and b2b (the post head). K4 went into
  b1b's jack test. K5 became b5's precise tier. K6 is a variant inside b8b.
