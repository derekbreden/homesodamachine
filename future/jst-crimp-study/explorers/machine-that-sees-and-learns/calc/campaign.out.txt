1. A crimp-height sweep for SXH-001T-P0.6 (or the kit clone) on the 22 AWG ribbon
============================================================================
  conductor crimp-height set points: 0.70 .. 1.00 mm, step 0.02 -> 16 levels
  5 crimps per level -> 80 crimps, each pulled to failure
  + insulation-crimp settings, 6 levels x 3: 18
  + insertion depth / insulation-edge position, 5 levels x 3: 15
  + proof-then-destroy pairs at the chosen setting: 20
  + genuine SXH vs kit contacts at the chosen setting, 10 + 10: 20
  total ~153 test crimps
  per test: crimp 150 s + pull 24 s + photos 15 s = 3.1 min
  campaign: 8.0 h unattended (one or two nights)
  wire: 153 x 40 mm = 6.1 m of conductor = 1.2 m of 5P ribbon
  contacts: 153; at LCSC reel-cut prices (~$0.01 each [xh-facts s6]) about $1.53
  Person: strip the coupons (or let v6 strip them), load pallets, ~1 min per 5 coupons.

3. What crimp-force monitoring can see on 60 x 0.08 mm strands
============================================================================
  one strand = 1.7% of the copper; a typical monitor's band is ~+/-4% [prior-art s6]
    1 strands missing -> 1.7% less copper (inside a 4% band, before scatter)
    2 strands missing -> 3.3% less copper (inside a 4% band, before scatter)
    3 strands missing -> 5.0% less copper (outside a 4% band, before scatter)
    4 strands missing -> 6.7% less copper (outside a 4% band, before scatter)
    6 strands missing -> 10.0% less copper (outside a 4% band, before scatter)
  So the force curve catches: no wire, no contact, a doubled or misfed contact, insulation
  under the conductor barrel, the wrong gauge, and drift in the peak over hundreds of
  crimps. It does not catch a few cut strands; the camera at the stripped tip does, or
  nothing does. [calc on prior-art numbers]

4. A spring the camera reads, as the force gauge for pulls  [calc]
============================================================================
  spring-steel leaf 12x1.0 mm, 20 mm long: k =  75.0 N/mm;  25 N ->  0.33 mm, root stress  250 MPa
  spring-steel leaf 12x1.0 mm, 20 mm long: k =  75.0 N/mm; 100 N ->  1.33 mm, root stress 1000 MPa
  spring-steel leaf 12x1.5 mm, 20 mm long: k = 253.1 N/mm;  25 N ->  0.10 mm, root stress  111 MPa
  spring-steel leaf 12x1.5 mm, 20 mm long: k = 253.1 N/mm; 100 N ->  0.40 mm, root stress  444 MPa
  spring-steel leaf 12x1.0 mm, 30 mm long: k =  22.2 N/mm;  25 N ->  1.12 mm, root stress  375 MPa
  spring-steel leaf 12x1.0 mm, 30 mm long: k =  22.2 N/mm; 100 N ->  4.50 mm, root stress 1500 MPa
  spring-steel leaf 10x0.8 mm, 20 mm long: k =  32.0 N/mm;  25 N ->  0.78 mm, root stress  469 MPa
  spring-steel leaf 10x0.8 mm, 20 mm long: k =  32.0 N/mm; 100 N ->  3.12 mm, root stress 1875 MPa
  read by ELP at 100 mm (45 px/mm) at 0.2 px: 4.4 um -> 0.33 N resolution
  read by 12 mm M12 lens at 100 mm (122 px/mm) at 0.2 px: 1.6 um -> 0.12 N resolution
  Spring steel yields at ~1,000-1,500 MPa [assumption, hardened 1075/1095 shim]. The
  12x1x20 leaf suits ~25 N proof pulls (250 MPa); pulls to failure near 100 N want the
  12x1.5x20 leaf (~440 MPa, 0.4 mm at 100 N). A $10 load cell and HX711 does the same job
  with a wire instead of a picture; the leaf's merit is that it lives in the same frame as
  the crimp, so one image holds both the force and whether the conductor slipped.
