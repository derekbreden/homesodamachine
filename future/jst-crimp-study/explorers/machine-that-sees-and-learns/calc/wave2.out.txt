1. v5 / v1: the contact's box as its own roll gauge in the side silhouette
==============================================================================
Box silhouette growth with roll (the box is square-sided; use its front 1 mm, ahead of the lance):
  box 1.85 x 2.20: 0.5 deg +  16 um     1 deg +  32 um     2 deg +  63 um     3 deg +  94 um
  box 1.95 x 2.40: 0.5 deg +  17 um     1 deg +  34 um     2 deg +  67 um     3 deg +  99 um
Roll recovered from the box, then the crimp height corrected by W_c sin r:
  A ELP 45 px/mm: box edge-pair sigma 1.6 um -> roll sigma 0.05 deg -> crimp-height error from correction 1.4-1.6 um
  A ELP 45 px/mm: box edge-pair sigma 9.4 um -> roll sigma 0.29 deg -> crimp-height error from correction 8.1-9.6 um
  D 122 px/mm: box edge-pair sigma 0.6 um -> roll sigma 0.02 deg -> crimp-height error from correction 0.5-0.6 um
  D 122 px/mm: box edge-pair sigma 3.5 um -> roll sigma 0.11 deg -> crimp-height error from correction 3.0-3.6 um
  Near zero roll the box's growth is first-order in |r| only once r exceeds the
  measurement noise; below ~0.3 deg the box says 'flat' and the crimp error is
  under ~0.01 mm anyway (row 0.5 deg above).
Reference for the box's true height:
  the lot's minimum box silhouette height, from a +/-4 deg roll sweep of five contacts at lot
  start (the sweep ribbon-as-pallet names as Repair B, done once per lot instead of per crimp).
  In line (v1) the nest picture at step 3 is a check on it, good only to the box slot's clearance:
  box slot 0.03 mm wider than the box: roll in the nest up to 0.7 deg -> box reads up to +25 um at step 3
  box slot 0.10 mm wider than the box: roll in the nest up to 2.5 deg -> box reads up to +80 um at step 3
  Box-to-box spread inside a lot (unmeasured) adds straight to the roll estimate: +/-10 um of
  spread is +/-0.3 deg, +/-0.01 mm of crimp-height error. Five contacts under the camera measure it.  [calc]
Residual after correction, rounded-bottom crimp (width term about half the rectangle's):
  roll 1 deg, crimp 0.73x1.6: rectangle term 28 um, rounded 14 um; correcting with the rectangle term over-corrects a rounded crimp by up to 14 um
  roll 1 deg, crimp 0.9x1.9: rectangle term 33 um, rounded 17 um; correcting with the rectangle term over-corrects a rounded crimp by up to 17 um
  roll 2 deg, crimp 0.73x1.6: rectangle term 56 um, rounded 28 um; correcting with the rectangle term over-corrects a rounded crimp by up to 28 um
  roll 2 deg, crimp 0.9x1.9: rectangle term 66 um, rounded 33 um; correcting with the rectangle term over-corrects a rounded crimp by up to 33 um
  roll 3 deg, crimp 0.73x1.6: rectangle term 84 um, rounded 42 um; correcting with the rectangle term over-corrects a rounded crimp by up to 42 um
  roll 3 deg, crimp 0.9x1.9: rectangle term 99 um, rounded 50 um; correcting with the rectangle term over-corrects a rounded crimp by up to 50 um
  -> correct by the mean of the two bounds and flag any crimp rolled > 2 deg for a re-seat;
     then the residual stays within ~+/-0.01-0.015 mm.  [calc, crimp cross-section shape assumed]

5. v8: the watched tack
==============================================================================
Insulation barrel closing force per contact: 33-132 N [xh-facts calc C1 s4].
  25 kg.cm servo, 20 mm horn, lever 1:1 ->   123 N at the former
  25 kg.cm servo, 20 mm horn, lever 3:1 ->   368 N at the former
  25 kg.cm servo, 25 mm horn, lever 1:1 ->    98 N at the former
  25 kg.cm servo, 25 mm horn, lever 3:1 ->   294 N at the former
  35 kg.cm servo, 20 mm horn, lever 1:1 ->   172 N at the former
  35 kg.cm servo, 20 mm horn, lever 3:1 ->   515 N at the former
  35 kg.cm servo, 25 mm horn, lever 1:1 ->   137 N at the former
  35 kg.cm servo, 25 mm horn, lever 3:1 ->   412 N at the former
  NEMA 17 on a Tr8x2 screw: ~280 N [hand-tool-as-press calc s2]. Either closes a tack;
  a hard stop sets the tack height, so the drive only has to exceed the forming force.
  anvil stress under the insulation barrel at 33 N: 14 MPa (PETG yields ~50; steel insert under the barrel)
  anvil stress under the insulation barrel at 132 N: 57 MPa (PETG yields ~50; steel insert under the barrel)
Order of wing contact in an ordinary one-stroke crimp (both crimpers on one ram):
  insulation wings meet their crimper with ~0.65-1.40 mm of stroke left
  conductor wings meet theirs with          ~0.62-0.87 mm left
  -> on these clone dimensions the insulation barrel starts closing first in a one-stroke
     crimp and is largely formed before conductor compaction (the last 0.1-0.2 mm).
     A tack, then the full stroke, keeps that order with a pause in it. Two-squeeze hand
     tools (conductor first) are the other order.  [calc on clone dims; crimper mouth
     flare, which shifts both first touches, is not modelled]
The grip window of a loose tack  [force-and-form calc wave2 s5, estimates]:
  insulation barrel squeezing the jacket 10 %: strands slip in the jacket at 0.4-3.1 N
  squeezing 30 %: 1.1-9.4 N (the jacket never slips in the barrel before the strands do)
  grip 0.4 N -> roll torque it resists ~0.34 N.mm at the jacket's radius
  grip 1.0 N -> roll torque it resists ~0.85 N.mm at the jacket's radius
  grip 3.0 N -> roll torque it resists ~2.55 N.mm at the jacket's radius
  the parted conductor's own torsion is 0.33-0.58 N.mm/rad [ribbon-as-pallet calc P s5]:
  a 0.4 N tack holds roll unless the conductor is twisted more than ~0.6-1 rad by handling.
What handling asks of the tack  [estimate]:
  contact weight 0.043 g -> 0.0004 N; lowering box-first into a vertical keyed slot with
  chamfers: <0.05-0.2 N of friction and <0.05 N.mm of squaring torque if it arrives within
  a few degrees. A tack at 5-15 % squeeze (0.2-1.5 N) carries it.
Backing a failed tack out, contact slid forward off the tip:
  release force 0.4 N, jacket free 1.5 mm between key grip and tack: stretches 0.06-0.13 mm while it slides (elastic, returns)
  release force 0.4 N, jacket free 3.0 mm between key grip and tack: stretches 0.12-0.26 mm while it slides (elastic, returns)
  release force 1.0 N, jacket free 1.5 mm between key grip and tack: stretches 0.15-0.32 mm while it slides (elastic, returns)
  release force 1.0 N, jacket free 3.0 mm between key grip and tack: stretches 0.29-0.64 mm while it slides (elastic, returns)
  release force 1.5 N, jacket free 1.5 mm between key grip and tack: stretches 0.22-0.48 mm while it slides (elastic, returns)
  release force 1.5 N, jacket free 3.0 mm between key grip and tack: stretches 0.44-0.97 mm while it slides (elastic, returns)
  -> a loose tack comes off with the jacket edge dragged forward a few tenths of a mm and
     returning; a tight one drags the jacket along the strands. The look after the back-out
     decides between 'new contact, same strip' and 're-strip or cut back'.  [estimate]
Time at the tack station, one conductor  [estimate]:
  contact into the nest (plate pick or strip index)                5  25 s
  look: contact seated, lance, wings                               1   3 s
  hover look, predict X/Y, key down, side look                     8  20 s
  tack: former down to its stop at ~1 mm/s, force trace            3   6 s
  look: top-down, 4-8 lighting states, focus 2 slices              6  15 s
  carry to the crimp nest (20-40 mm), seat, one side look          6  12 s
  tack side of one conductor: 29-81 s; the heavy crimp follows at its own station

  above wire break; one joint per coupon with the bench Hakko.  [ribbon-as-pallet calc P s4]
  bare copper wrapped 2 turns on a steel pin, mu 0.2: clamp after the wrap carries 8.10 N of a 100 N pull
  bare copper wrapped 3 turns on a steel pin, mu 0.2: clamp after the wrap carries 2.31 N of a 100 N pull
  bare copper wrapped 2 turns on a steel pin, mu 0.3: clamp after the wrap carries 2.31 N of a 100 N pull
  bare copper wrapped 3 turns on a steel pin, mu 0.3: clamp after the wrap carries 0.35 N of a 100 N pull
  bare copper wrapped 2 turns on a steel pin, mu 0.5: clamp after the wrap carries 0.19 N of a 100 N pull
  bare copper wrapped 3 turns on a steel pin, mu 0.5: clamp after the wrap carries 0.01 N of a 100 N pull
  0.08 mm strand bent over a 3 mm pin: surface strain 2.7% (annealed copper elongates 20-30 %)
  0.08 mm strand bent over a 5 mm pin: surface strain 1.6% (annealed copper elongates 20-30 %)
  -> stripping 25-30 mm of the coupon's far end and wrapping the bare bundle two turns on
     a 3-5 mm pin before a screw clamp holds a pull to failure with no jacket in the path;
     wire break may start at the pin entry at some fraction below straight break [estimate:
     80-95 %], still above the 39-54 N region the campaign resolves.
  preparation for 150 coupons: soldered lugs ~2.5-3.8 h; wraps ~1.2-2.0 h [estimate 60-90 s and 30-50 s each]
