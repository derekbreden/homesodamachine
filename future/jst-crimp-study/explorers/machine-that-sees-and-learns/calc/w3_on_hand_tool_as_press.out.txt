1. a1b: proof pull with the dies re-closed on the crimped barrels
==============================================================================
held die force = grip rise x gain; extra grip on the strands ~ mu x held die force
(htp calc/exchange_procedure.out.txt s3: 50 N held -> 8-25 N, 200 N -> 30-100 N)
  grip rise 2.0 N x gain 15 =    30 N at the dies -> extra grip    4 /    9 /   15 N at mu 0.15 / 0.3 / 0.5   vs a 20 N proof
  grip rise 2.0 N x gain 30 =    60 N at the dies -> extra grip    9 /   18 /   30 N at mu 0.15 / 0.3 / 0.5   vs a 20 N proof
  grip rise 2.0 N x gain 40 =    80 N at the dies -> extra grip   12 /   24 /   40 N at mu 0.15 / 0.3 / 0.5   vs a 20 N proof
  grip rise 5.0 N x gain 15 =    75 N at the dies -> extra grip   11 /   22 /   38 N at mu 0.15 / 0.3 / 0.5   vs a 20 N proof
  grip rise 5.0 N x gain 30 =   150 N at the dies -> extra grip   22 /   45 /   75 N at mu 0.15 / 0.3 / 0.5   vs a 20 N proof
  grip rise 5.0 N x gain 40 =   200 N at the dies -> extra grip   30 /   60 /  100 N at mu 0.15 / 0.3 / 0.5   vs a 20 N proof
For the re-closure to add no more than 2 N (10 % of a 20 N proof):
  mu 0.15: held die force <= 13.3 N -> at the grip <= 0.33-0.89 N (gain 40-15)
  mu 0.30: held die force <=  6.7 N -> at the grip <= 0.17-0.44 N (gain 40-15)
  mu 0.50: held die force <=  4.0 N -> at the grip <= 0.10-0.27 N (gain 40-15)
What the grip can resolve [estimate]: HX711 on a 50 kg S-cell ~0.05-0.2 N of noise;
the empty-tool curve (return spring, pawl, pins) repeats to perhaps +/-0.5-1 N at the grip.
  gain 15: +/-0.5-1 N at the grip = +/-  8- 15 N of unknown die force
  gain 40: +/-0.5-1 N at the grip = +/- 20- 40 N of unknown die force
-> a grip-side 'few newtons' is 30-200 N at the dies; a crimp with little grip of its own
   passes a 20 N pull by die friction. The re-closure cannot be set light enough from the
   grip to keep the test honest.

Position hold instead (dies stopped short of the barrels, pull reacts on the jaw face):
  transition 0.20, lance tip 2.24: tip 0.04 mm behind the face, so it hangs in a relief in the anvil; a -Y pull carries it along the relief, and what meets steel first depends on the relief's rear end
  transition 0.20, lance tip 2.64: tip 0.44 mm behind the face, so it hangs in a relief in the anvil; a -Y pull carries it along the relief, and what meets steel first depends on the relief's rear end
  transition 0.35, lance tip 2.24: tip 0.11 mm in front of the face, box shoulder 0.35 mm away -> lance tip meets the face first
  transition 0.35, lance tip 2.64: tip 0.29 mm behind the face, so it hangs in a relief in the anvil; a -Y pull carries it along the relief, and what meets steel first depends on the relief's rear end
  transition 0.50, lance tip 2.24: tip 0.26 mm in front of the face, box shoulder 0.50 mm away -> lance tip meets the face first
  transition 0.50, lance tip 2.64: tip 0.14 mm behind the face, so it hangs in a relief in the anvil; a -Y pull carries it along the relief, and what meets steel first depends on the relief's rear end
-> with the dies open to a position, the lance, not the box, meets steel first wherever the
   tip hangs in front of the anvil face, and at best ties with an unknown relief otherwise.
   The pull belongs on a backed plate on the box's rear walls with the lance relieved.

2. a1b: where the force wall lives when the stroke goes through a hand tool
==============================================================================
grip speed 2.0 mm/s [htp calc s3]; die speed = grip speed / gain
  gain  3: die moves   667 um/s; HX711 at 80 SPS ->  8.33 um of die per sample
  gain  6: die moves   333 um/s; HX711 at 80 SPS ->  4.17 um of die per sample
  gain 15: die moves   133 um/s; HX711 at 80 SPS ->  1.67 um of die per sample
  gain 30: die moves    67 um/s; HX711 at 80 SPS ->  0.83 um of die per sample
  gain 40: die moves    50 um/s; HX711 at 80 SPS ->  0.62 um of die per sample
-> near closure (gain 15-40) the die already crawls at 0.05-0.13 mm/s, which is v7's
   crawl; a hand tool gives the slow approach to compaction for free.

Overshoot past a force wall, a1b (no ratchet, dies meet face to face):
grip-side stiffness after die contact k_g = 1/(1/k_h + G^2/k_d)
k_h handles + pins + cradle 20-100 N/mm [estimate]; k_d die loop 5-30 kN/mm [estimate]
  MCU, one HX711 sample         12.5 ms ->     25 um of grip ->     3-   21 N extra at the dies
  Mac, typical USB round trip   30.0 ms ->     60 um of grip ->     6-   51 N extra at the dies
  Mac, bad moment              500.0 ms ->   1000 um of grip ->   108-  857 N extra at the dies
-> a 0.5 s stall on the Mac costs roughly as much as it does in a steel press at crawl
   (v7: 375-750 N): the handle crawls the die but the pusher does not crawl. a1b's
   wall is set and enforced on the station MCU, and the Mac commands whole squeezes.
