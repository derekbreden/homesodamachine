1. Seeing a strip-fed contact in silhouette, across the strip, before the stroke
====================================================================================
  gap to the upstream neighbour at the conductor barrel: 5.20-5.42 mm (pitch 7.1 minus open width 1.68-1.9)
  gap to the upstream neighbour at the insulation barrel: 4.10-4.64 mm (pitch 7.1 minus open width 2.46-3.0)

  A grazing view from upstream, tilted down by theta, passes over the upstream neighbour.
  The silhouette's top edge is the ray that grazes OUR FAR wing tip; that ray meets the
  neighbour's nearer wing one full pitch later, so it clears it (excess height dh within one
  strip, 0.02 mm to spare) when
      tan(theta) >= (dh + 0.02) / pitch
  Our near wing tip sits w*tan(theta) below that edge, so a 0.08 mm strand lying on the near
  tip sticks up by only  0.08 - w*tan(theta)
    neighbour 0.00 mm taller -> theta >= 0.16 deg; strand bump   75 um =  3.4 px @45   6.4 px @86   9.1 px @122
    neighbour 0.05 mm taller -> theta >= 0.56 deg; strand bump   61 um =  2.8 px @45   5.3 px @86   7.5 px @122
    neighbour 0.10 mm taller -> theta >= 0.97 deg; strand bump   48 um =  2.2 px @45   4.1 px @86   5.8 px @122
    neighbour 0.15 mm taller -> theta >= 1.37 deg; strand bump   35 um =  1.6 px @45   3.0 px @86   4.2 px @122
    neighbour 0.25 mm taller -> theta >= 2.18 deg; strand bump    8 um =  0.3 px @45   0.7 px @86   0.9 px @122
  -> with neighbours within ~0.15 mm of our wing height the grazing view works, and a strand on
     the near wing tip shows as a 2-7 px bump. Much above that the neighbour forces a tilt that
     hides a strand (a bent-up neighbour wing does exactly that).
     A white vane slid into the 4.1-5.4 mm gap from the box side, lit from above, puts the
     backlight between the two contacts and removes the squeeze.
  Downstream the carrier is empty, so the camera can also sit downstream at theta ~0 if the
  backlight is that vane (the light cannot come from beyond the upstream neighbour).

2. The ribbon's other conductors against fresh contacts waiting on the strip
====================================================================================
  collision if |j*f - m*7.1| < 2.35 mm (open insulation wings 3.0, conductor 1.7)
  3P, all 2 others on the upstream side: collision-free fan pitch (1.7-15 mm): 1.7-2.3
  4P, all 3 others on the upstream side: collision-free fan pitch (1.7-15 mm): none
  5P, all 4 others on the upstream side: collision-free fan pitch (1.7-15 mm): none
    fan pitch 1.7: upstream conductors j = [3, 4] sit on a fresh contact
    fan pitch 2.5: upstream conductors j = [2, 3] sit on a fresh contact
    fan pitch 3.5: upstream conductors j = [2, 4] sit on a fresh contact
    fan pitch 5.5: upstream conductors j = [1, 3, 4] sit on a fresh contact
  -> a flat fan cannot keep 4P/5P conductors off the fresh contacts; only the conductors on
     the empty-carrier (downstream) side are free. The rest must be lifted out of plane, or
     crimping must run so that every waiting conductor is downstream (then each crimped one
     is upstream and must stay lifted). Either way it is a per-conductor lift, and a look at
     'no other tip below the fresh wing tips' belongs in the gate.

3. Axial shortfall that a fan puts into each conductor (why one taught Y is not enough)
====================================================================================
  5P into XH 2.5 mm pitch, outer               d =  1.6 mm -> shortfall  10 deg: 0.14  20 deg: 0.28  30 deg: 0.43 mm
  J1 5P+4P into XHP-9, outermost               d =  3.2 mm -> shortfall  10 deg: 0.28  20 deg: 0.56  30 deg: 0.86 mm
  5P fanned to 5.5 mm (v1 fan-comb), outer     d =  7.6 mm -> shortfall  10 deg: 0.66  20 deg: 1.34  30 deg: 2.04 mm
  5P to 7.1 mm strip pitch (a2b comb), outer   d = 10.8 mm -> shortfall  10 deg: 0.94  20 deg: 1.90  30 deg: 2.89 mm
  Compare: insulation edge must land in the window, ~0.5-1.0 mm long (+/-0.25 to +/-0.5 mm)
  [vision_budget s4]; strip length itself is 2.4 (JST) or 1.6-2.1 (clone spec) and silicone
  tears. If the ribbon end is cut square before it is fanned, the outer conductors' insulation
  edges stand 0.3-1.9 mm behind the centre one. A per-slot taught Y absorbs the repeatable
  part; the insulation edge as seen, per conductor, absorbs the rest. Cutting and stripping
  after fanning, against one datum line on the pallet, removes most of it at the source.

4. a2b: a proof pull reacted through the carrier tag
====================================================================================
  neck 0.6 mm, Mp 3.0 N*mm -> tab yields at a pull of  e=0.35:  8.6 N  e=0.55:  5.5 N  e=0.85:  3.5 N  e=1.20:  2.5 N
  neck 0.8 mm, Mp 4.0 N*mm -> tab yields at a pull of  e=0.35: 11.4 N  e=0.55:  7.3 N  e=0.85:  4.7 N  e=1.20:  3.3 N
  neck 1.0 mm, Mp 5.0 N*mm -> tab yields at a pull of  e=0.35: 14.3 N  e=0.55:  9.1 N  e=0.85:  5.9 N  e=1.20:  4.2 N
  e: 0.35 mm is the conductor axis after a ~0.88 mm crimp less half the stock; 0.85 the
  insulated wire's axis; 1.2 adds the 30 deg lift a2b uses to clear the pad.
  -> any proof pull above ~2-16 N bends the tab; a 20 N pull always does. The tab carries
     66-110 N only if the load lies in its own plane, and a pull on the wire never does.

  The tag hole as the datum for the box tip (5.0-6.5 mm ahead of the hole):
    1 deg of bend-up/down or neck set -> box tip off by 0.09-0.11 mm (cavity entry window ~+/-0.2 mm [vision_budget/arm_and_feeder s1])
    2 deg of bend-up/down or neck set -> box tip off by 0.17-0.23 mm (cavity entry window ~+/-0.2 mm [vision_budget/arm_and_feeder s1])
    3 deg of bend-up/down or neck set -> box tip off by 0.26-0.34 mm (cavity entry window ~+/-0.2 mm [vision_budget/arm_and_feeder s1])
    5 deg of bend-up/down or neck set -> box tip off by 0.44-0.57 mm (cavity entry window ~+/-0.2 mm [vision_budget/arm_and_feeder s1])

5. a2c: bending the strip +/-90 deg about the tab twists the carrier
====================================================================================
  90 deg twist spread over   7.1 mm of carrier: shear strain 0.0442, stress   1814 MPa (shear yield ~289)
  90 deg twist spread over  14.2 mm of carrier: shear strain 0.0221, stress    907 MPa (shear yield ~289)
  90 deg twist spread over  30.0 mm of carrier: shear strain 0.0105, stress    429 MPa (shear yield ~289)
  90 deg twist spread over  50.0 mm of carrier: shear strain 0.0063, stress    258 MPa (shear yield ~289)
  90 deg twist spread over 100.0 mm of carrier: shear strain 0.0031, stress    129 MPa (shear yield ~289)
  -> the next contact is one pitch (7.1 mm) upstream: a +/-90 deg swing of the strip yields
     the carrier there and rolls that contact. Only a carrier already cut free can swing.

6. a2: dropping the carrier 0.3-0.5 mm between two pinned neighbours
====================================================================================
  S-bend span 4.6 mm each side; first yield at 0.16 mm of drop, whatever the carrier's
  width (the slots only decide where the hinge forms)
    a 0.3 mm drop leaves ~0.14 mm of set, a kink of ~1.7 deg at the next pilot hole
    a 0.4 mm drop leaves ~0.24 mm of set, a kink of ~3.0 deg at the next pilot hole
    a 0.5 mm drop leaves ~0.34 mm of set, a kink of ~4.2 deg at the next pilot hole
  -> the next contact can arrive rolled by ~1.5-4 deg. Inside the 5-11 deg roll window
     [digest], but it eats half of it; the waiting-contact picture measures roll directly.
     Dropping only the downstream (scrap) side keeps the upstream carrier flat.

7. What a vacuum nozzle can push
====================================================================================
  50 kPa, nozzle bore 0.5 mm: holds   9.8 mN normal, ~ 2.9 mN sideways (mu 0.3)
  50 kPa, nozzle bore 0.8 mm: holds  25.1 mN normal, ~ 7.5 mN sideways (mu 0.3)
  50 kPa, nozzle bore 1.0 mm: holds  39.3 mN normal, ~11.8 mN sideways (mu 0.3)
  80 kPa, nozzle bore 0.5 mm: holds  15.7 mN normal, ~ 4.7 mN sideways (mu 0.3)
  80 kPa, nozzle bore 0.8 mm: holds  40.2 mN normal, ~12.1 mN sideways (mu 0.3)
  80 kPa, nozzle bore 1.0 mm: holds  62.8 mN normal, ~18.8 mN sideways (mu 0.3)
  contact weight 0.42 mN [xh-facts s1, 0.043 g]; post grip 200-2000 mN [terminal-supply calc s7]
  -> a nozzle can place a contact into an open nest; it cannot push one onto a post or into a
     cavity against any friction. A post must come to a contact that is backed by a stop.

10. a4: what continuity through the post can tell
====================================================================================
  22 AWG ribbon conductor: 58 mohm per metre
      0.1 m ->     5.8 mohm
      0.6 m ->    34.9 mohm
     15.2 m ->   883.0 mohm
  a crimp itself: ~0.2-1 mohm; post-to-box contact on a 0.2-2 N grip: ~5-30 mohm and variable
  [estimates]. -> the path reads 'joined' or 'open'; it cannot grade the crimp. It also needs
  the far end bared (a pogo pin on the cut face, or the spool's slip ring).

12. a2b: how far the box tip moves on the tab neck under a small side load
====================================================================================
  neck 0.8 x 0.2 x 1.0 mm: rotation stiffness ~59 N*mm/rad; first yield at 2.7 N*mm
    side load 0.05 N at the box (5.0-6.5 mm out): tip moves 0.021-0.036 mm
    side load 0.10 N at the box (5.0-6.5 mm out): tip moves 0.043-0.072 mm
    side load 0.20 N at the box (5.0-6.5 mm out): tip moves 0.085-0.144 mm
    side load 0.50 N at the box (5.0-6.5 mm out): tip moves 0.213-0.360 mm
  -> a conductor's drape (~0.05-0.1 N) moves the box tip 0.02-0.07 mm; a firmer tug moves it
     by the whole cavity window. An offset measured at inspection holds only if the load is the
     same at insertion; a look at the box tip just before the mouth does not depend on that.
