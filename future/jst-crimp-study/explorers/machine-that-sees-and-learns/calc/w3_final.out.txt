1. The tack, defined against the final insulation crimp at the same contact
==============================================================================
Lateral squeeze of the jacket by a closed barrel of outside width W (inside W - 2t):
  former width = final width + 0.0 mm: squeeze  3.1-22.2 % -> grip ~0.12-6.9 N  [estimate]
  former width = final width + 0.1 mm: squeeze  0.0-16.7 % -> grip ~0.00-5.2 N  [estimate]
  former width = final width + 0.2 mm: squeeze  0.0-11.1 % -> grip ~0.00-3.4 N  [estimate]
  wanted: 0.2-1.5 N (carries a 0.043 g contact through handling, slides off for a back-out)
  -> at the final crimper's own width the lateral squeeze alone is ~3-22 %, which the
     thin-layer model puts at up to ~7 N: a tack there can exceed the release target.
     A former 0.1-0.2 mm wider than the final crimper brings the squeeze to 0-17 % or 0-11 %;
     the grip then comes more from the wing tips on the jacket's top, set by height. The
     spread is the grip model's (x8) and the jacket's +/-0.1 mm; the bench finds the setting.
     The final crimper's mouth must then take a barrel 0.1-0.2 mm wider than its own width
     [assumption: a flared B-crimper entry does; the jack test or f3b's sections show it].

Tack height H_t = H_f + dH, dH = 0.2-0.5 mm above the final insulation height at that width:
  final force-and-form 2.0-2.3 [ff w2 s5]: tack 2.20-2.80 mm
  final ellipse upper bound 2.03-2.46 [sl w3 s9]: tack 2.23-2.96 mm
  final KONNRA PVC-wire figure 1.8-2.1 [sl w2 s5 input]: tack 2.00-2.60 mm
  (an absolute 2.3-2.5 mm tack lies -0.16 to +0.47 mm from the 2.03-2.46 final heights, so
   at the low end it is the crimp, not a tack [bm W s1]; the relative definition cannot be)

First touches, remaining ram travel (both crimpers on one ram):
  untacked, final force-and-form 2.0-2.3 [ff w2 s5]: insulation wings met at +0.45 to +1.20 mm; conductor wings at 0.62-0.87 mm
  untacked, final ellipse upper bound 2.03-2.46 [sl w3 s9]: insulation wings met at +0.29 to +1.17 mm; conductor wings at 0.62-0.87 mm
  untacked, final KONNRA PVC-wire figure 1.8-2.1 [sl w2 s5 input]: insulation wings met at +0.65 to +1.40 mm; conductor wings at 0.62-0.87 mm
  after a tack at H_f + 0.2-0.5: the insulation crimper meets the tacked barrel with 0.20-0.50 mm
  left, always after the conductor wings (0.62-0.87 mm).
  the conductor wings curl for 0.12-0.67 mm of stroke before the insulation crimper retouches;
  compaction (0.1-0.2 mm) starts with the retouch or after it.
  -> in both strokes the jacket is held before compaction. The untacked order (insulation
     first) holds only where the final insulation height is low; on this silicone it can be
     either. After a tack, the conductor wings are always met first.  [calc, clone dims,
     crimper mouth flare not modelled]

2. The tack drive and where its load cell sits
==============================================================================
  35 kg.cm servo, 20 mm horn, lever 1:1 ->   172 N at the former
  35 kg.cm servo, 20 mm horn, lever 2:1 ->   343 N at the former
  35 kg.cm servo, 20 mm horn, lever 3:1 ->   515 N at the former
  35 kg.cm servo, 25 mm horn, lever 1:1 ->   137 N at the former
  35 kg.cm servo, 25 mm horn, lever 2:1 ->   275 N at the former
  35 kg.cm servo, 25 mm horn, lever 3:1 ->   412 N at the former
  forming force wanted 33-132 N [sl w2 s5, from xh-facts C1 s4]
  5 kg bar (Prime pair, $9.99): rated   49 N, ~  74 N safe overload [assumption: 150 % typical]
  10 kg bar: rated   98 N, ~ 147 N safe overload [assumption: 150 % typical]
  20 kg bar: rated  196 N, ~ 294 N safe overload [assumption: 150 % typical]
  -> bench T (v8): the cell under the nest insert reads only the forming force, because the
     loose stop carries the servo's excess; 20 kg covers 132 N.
  -> T at the applicator (v9): the anvil is the applicator's own block, so the cell sits in
     the T-arm's link and reads servo force. The forming curve is the trace up to the knee
     where the former lands on its stop; after the knee it reads the servo's excess. With a
     1:1 lever the excess tops out at 137-172 N, inside a 20 kg cell's overload.

  stop 0.20 mm above bottom, conductor wings first met at 0.87: curled 0.67 mm of ~0.67 -> opening left ~  0 %
  stop 0.35 mm above bottom, conductor wings first met at 0.62: curled 0.27 mm of ~0.42 -> opening left ~ 36 %
  stop 0.35 mm above bottom, conductor wings first met at 0.87: curled 0.52 mm of ~0.67 -> opening left ~ 22 %
  stop 0.50 mm above bottom, conductor wings first met at 0.62: curled 0.12 mm of ~0.42 -> opening left ~ 71 %
  stop 0.50 mm above bottom, conductor wings first met at 0.87: curled 0.37 mm of ~0.67 -> opening left ~ 45 %

     opening taken as shrinking in proportion to curl travel]. The separate former is what
     keeps the barrel open for the straight-down look.
