# a10b The tacked row into the hand tool: dock and tack every contact with two levers, then crimp each in the SN-2549

## Picture it

A branch of [a10](a10-dock-tack-then-nest.md) with no motors. The docking, tack
and tab shear are the same, worked by levers. The heavy station is the bench's
iCrimp SN-2549, in the person's hand. What changes for the person is the part
Derek most wants gone: nobody places a contact on a conductor, closes a ratchet
one click on a loose contact, or feeds a wire into it. Each contact arrives in
the tool already on its conductor, at its depth and roll. Sketch: panel C of
[`../sketches/a10-dock-tack-then-nest.svg`](../sketches/a10-dock-tack-then-nest.svg)
(schematic).

**Where things start.**
- **Contacts:** a strip segment (Digi-Key's 100-piece SXH strip, $4.71), or the
  CQRobot kit contacts already on the bench in [a2c](a2c-loose-contact-cassette.md)'s
  keyed nest bar at 5 mm, each pocket's rear shoulder behind the box.
- **The ribbon end,** in a printed ribbon pallet, prepared on the bench blocks:
  hand zip comb ([a7](a7-zip-station.md)), fan block closed by a lever, flush cut
  and knob-and-lever ring strip at the fan face ([a8](a8-rolling-ring-scorer.md));
  or stripped webbed first with procedure-is-the-machine's
  [p7](../../procedure-is-the-machine/ideas/p7-strip-before-split.md) lever and
  fanned in an equal-path block ([a5](a5-part-fan-strip-in-the-pallet.md),
  order B).
- **The dock jig:** a steel plate with slot pins (or the nest bar), a hardened
  rail under the insulation barrels only with its rear edge as the shear edge, and
  three hardened seats for the ribbon pallet's balls.
- **Two levers:** a notched tack comb and a carrier-drop shear, each on a POWERTEC
  305CM push-pull toggle clamp driving to a hard stop (two for $18.25 [Prime]).

**What moves.**
1. **Lay** the segment on the slot pins, or check the nest bar's pockets.
2. **Dock:** set the ribbon pallet on its seats; the magnets click it home and
   every conductor goes into its contact, pressed in with a finger comb if needed.
3. **Look and test:** photograph the row; touch a meter from each conductor's far
   end (or read LEDs through a far-end block) to the carrier.
4. **Tack:** push the first toggle. The comb closes every insulation barrel
   loosely on its jacket, 165–660 N for a 5P [w3 §3], to its stop.
5. **Shear:** with the comb still down as the pad, push the second toggle; the
   carrier drops past the rail edge and every tab is cut (250–800 N for a 5P
   [calc X §5]). The kit-contact version has no tabs; this step is skipped.
6. **Lift** the ribbon pallet off: every contact hangs from its own conductor.
7. **Crimp each in the SN-2549.** The pallet sits on a stand at the bench edge
   with its conductors overhanging. The person brings the tool to one contact,
   seats the contact in the XH nest (the conductor already sits at its depth and
   roll in the contact), squeezes to the ratchet's release, and moves on. The
   neighbours stand 7.1 mm (or 5 mm) away on 21–30 mm of free conductor; those on
   the jaw's pivot side are bent aside with a finger, and the copper keeps the
   bend until the closing block straightens it.
8. **Pull** each with a luggage scale on a hook, then **insert** with the hand
   insertion jig ([a6](a6-housing-as-last-comb.md)).

**What locates what, and the reference for fixed.**
- Up to the tack, "fixed" is the slot pins (or the nest bar's pockets): the dock
  places every conductor in its contact, and the tack freezes its depth, its roll
  and where the insulation edge sits in the window.
- In the tool, the contact's position against the dies is the person's eye, as
  today, or a printed stop clipped to the tool's side that the box meets
  (terminal-supply's
  [a2c](../../terminal-supply/ideas/a2c-strip-locator-for-hand-tool.md) strip clip
  is the same kind of part). The tack does not locate the contact in the die; it
  removes the conductor-into-contact step from the hand.

**What drives the crimp and carries its force.** The person's grip through the
SN-2549's ratchet and jaws. Crimp height is whatever the tool's dies give when
the ratchet releases.

**How it knows.** Continuity and a photograph at docking, before any force; the
person's look at each crimp; the pull; a caliper on a sample crimp per session.

**What the person does.** Everything except placing contacts: lay the segment,
dock, two levers, every squeeze, every pull, insertion, far ends. Per ribbon end
about a minute for the jig [w3 §3, estimate], then each crimp without the
captive-click and wire-feeding steps of today's procedure [estimate: 10–20 s a
crimp].

## Steps it covers and what it hands back

- **Fixture:** placement of every contact on its conductor by one docking, and
  the tack that holds it there; contact supply and orientation from the strip (or
  the keyed nest bar).
- **Person:** preparation at the bench blocks, the levers, every crimp, pull and
  insertion, far ends.

## Why it is a first build

- It needs no applicator, no motor and no custom die: printed parts, a laser-cut
  or hand-filed comb, two toggle clamps, balls, magnets, and either a $4.71 strip
  or the kit contacts on hand.
- It tests the three things every docking idea in this directory rests on:
  whether conductors go into open contacts by docking, whether a tack holds on
  silicone, and whether a tack-first crimp is sound on this ribbon.
- It grows into [a10](a10-dock-tack-then-nest.md) (a heavy station takes the
  tool's place), [a9](a9-reel-end-docks.md) (whose stage 0 crimps this way), or
  [a2e](a2e-docked-strip-through-a-feedless-applicator.md).

## A test before the jig

1. Lay three contacts of a strip segment under three split, stripped conductors.
2. Close each insulation barrel loosely with smooth-jaw pliers (TEKTON PMN23001,
   $17.00 [Prime]), and cut the tabs.
3. Pull and twist each contact on its conductor against the bench scale; then
   crimp each in the SN-2549 and pull again.

## Major unresolved problems

- **The tack's grip on silicone** (0.4–9 N by force-and-form's estimate) and
  whether the SN-2549's insulation die re-forms a pre-closed barrel cleanly.
- **Whether the neighbours clear the SN-2549's jaw head** on its tip side at
  7.1 mm or 5 mm, or all must be bent aside.
- **The SN-2549's crimp on this ribbon:** whether its jaws bottom face to face
  (the dies set height) or the tool's stiffness does. Five crimps under a caliper
  settle it.
- **The tack comb's notch profile** for a loose tack that stays out of the window.
