1. Ribbon pitch tolerance vs comb capture
============================================================================
capture limit for a comb slot with a lead-in: half a ribbon pitch = 0.85 mm before a conductor can fall into the wrong slot
3 conductors: far conductor error from EDGE datum worst 0.25 / RSS 0.09 mm;  from CENTRE datum worst 0.12 / RSS 0.06 mm
4 conductors: far conductor error from EDGE datum worst 0.35 / RSS 0.10 mm;  from CENTRE datum worst 0.18 / RSS 0.07 mm
5 conductors: far conductor error from EDGE datum worst 0.45 / RSS 0.12 mm;  from CENTRE datum worst 0.23 / RSS 0.08 mm
7 conductors: far conductor error from EDGE datum worst 0.65 / RSS 0.14 mm;  from CENTRE datum worst 0.33 / RSS 0.10 mm
8 conductors: far conductor error from EDGE datum worst 0.75 / RSS 0.16 mm;  from CENTRE datum worst 0.38 / RSS 0.11 mm
9 conductors: far conductor error from EDGE datum worst 0.85 / RSS 0.17 mm;  from CENTRE datum worst 0.43 / RSS 0.12 mm
-> even the worst-case 9-conductor stack from one edge (0.85 mm) sits at the capture
   limit; from a centred datum, or with a channel slightly narrower than nominal
   (the AMP US 4,230,008 trick), every conductor is inside a comb slot's lead-in.
   The ribbon's own pitch is good enough to be the reference at the clamp; the comb
   takes over at the tip.

3. How wide the crimp pitch must be: tool nose vs neighbours
============================================================================
Neighbour half-widths: bare conductor 0.85; crimped contact (ins. barrel) 1.03;
open contact (ins. barrel) 1.23-1.50
(a) Open contacts side by side at 2.5 mm pitch: gap between open insulation wings -0.50 to +0.04 mm -> they collide or nearly so:
    contacts cannot be pre-loaded in a row at housing pitch; one at a time only.
(b) tool nose  3.0 mm wide, neighbours in plane: crimp pitch >= 2.8 mm
(b) tool nose  4.0 mm wide, neighbours in plane: crimp pitch >= 3.3 mm
(b) tool nose  6.0 mm wide, neighbours in plane: crimp pitch >= 4.3 mm
(b) tool nose 10.0 mm wide, neighbours in plane: crimp pitch >= 6.3 mm
(c) neighbours lifted 4-5 mm above the active contact: they only have to clear the
    part of the tooling that reaches below their level at the bottom of the stroke -
    the forming noses of punch and hold-down, typically 3-4 mm wide [estimate] ->
    crimp pitch >= 2.9-3.3 mm. 5.0 mm is a round choice with margin.

4. The conductor as a beam: sag, lay-in force, and why nobody pushes on a bare wire
============================================================================
insulation I = 0.397 mm^4 -> EI 0.79-2.38 N mm^2; strands (free to slip) 14.115 N mm^2
total EI ~14.91-16.50 N mm^2: the copper strands carry most of the elastic stiffness
annealed strands take a permanent set below a bend radius of ~67 mm; plastic moment of the bundle ~0.36 N mm
-> the conductor keeps whatever shape the comb gives it (a fan stays fanned), and
   handling leaves set in it; the silicone jacket springs back only part of the way
weight 0.048 N/m (4.8 g/m)
  cantilever  5 mm from a comb: gravity sag 0.000-0.000 mm
  cantilever 10 mm from a comb: gravity sag 0.004-0.004 mm
  cantilever 15 mm from a comb: gravity sag 0.018-0.020 mm
  cantilever 20 mm from a comb: gravity sag 0.058-0.064 mm
  -> within ~10 mm of a comb the conductor points where the comb points; residual set
     from the spool, not gravity, is the error source [assumption: <0.3 mm at 10 mm].
  pushing a 10 mm free length down 4 mm into a waiting contact: 197.9 mN
  pushing a 15 mm free length down 5 mm into a waiting contact: 73.3 mN
  (elastic figures; the strands yield once the root moment passes 0.36 N mm, i.e. a
   tip force of ~36 mN on a 10 mm length, so the real force is lower still)
  -> a lay-in finger needs a fraction of a newton.
Euler buckling of the free conductor under an axial push (pinned-pinned):
  L =  1 mm: 147.14-162.80 N
  L =  2 mm: 36.78-40.70 N
  L =  5 mm: 5.89-6.51 N
  L = 10 mm: 1.47-1.63 N
-> insertion wants up to 9.8 N per contact [source, clone spec]. Elastic Euler already
   limits a free length to ~3-4 mm, and yielding strands lower that further. The push
   must come from a clamp gripping the insulation within ~2 mm of the insulation barrel,
   or through a closed channel. Clamp grip needed:
   mu 0.5: normal force >= 20 N per conductor (two jaws: 10 N each face)
   mu 1.0: normal force >= 10 N per conductor (two jaws: 5 N each face)

============================================================================
5. Parting the web: tear and cut forces
============================================================================
web 0.2 mm thick: tear force to run a split 2-5 N per web
web 0.4 mm thick: tear force to run a split 4-10 N per web
web 0.6 mm thick: tear force to run a split 6-15 N per web
-> a few newtons to ~15 N per web. A wedge comb opening all webs of a 5P at once
   (4 webs) pulls ~10-60 N on the clamp. A hand or a hobby servo does this.
   A sharp blade drawn along a valley cuts at well under the tear force [assumption].

============================================================================
6. Pulling the strip slug
============================================================================
friction of a 2.4 mm slug if the ring is fully cut: ~0.22 N
uncut ligament 0.05 mm left by a depth-limited blade: tear 1.0-2.5 N per conductor
uncut ligament 0.15 mm left by a depth-limited blade: tear 3.0-7.5 N per conductor
uncut ligament 0.30 mm left by a depth-limited blade: tear 6.0-15.0 N per conductor
-> leaving 0.1-0.2 mm of silicone uncut costs only a few newtons per conductor to tear,
   so the blade can stop well short of the 0.08 mm strands.
   Whole-end strip of a 9-conductor pair: ~9 x (3-15) = 30-135 N through the clamp.

============================================================================
7. Gang strokes: all crimps of a ribbon at once
============================================================================
3 contacts in one stroke: 2.4-7.8 kN (VEVOR 12 t shop press ~118 kN; margin 15x at the high end)
4 contacts in one stroke: 3.2-10.4 kN (VEVOR 12 t shop press ~118 kN; margin 11x at the high end)
5 contacts in one stroke: 4.0-12.9 kN (VEVOR 12 t shop press ~118 kN; margin 9x at the high end)
9 contacts in one stroke: 7.1-23.3 kN (VEVOR 12 t shop press ~118 kN; margin 5x at the high end)
-> force is not the obstacle; die making and height control are. A die set that
   bottoms on hardened stop blocks makes crimp height a property of the die set,
   not of the press.
  stop blocks 100 mm^2 total at 20 kN overdrive: 200 MPa (hardened steel ok < ~1000)
  stop blocks 200 mm^2 total at 20 kN overdrive: 100 MPa (hardened steel ok < ~1000)
  stop blocks 400 mm^2 total at 20 kN overdrive: 50 MPa (hardened steel ok < ~1000)

============================================================================

5 contacts straight in together: up to 49 N through the clamp [source max per contact]; tilted 'lean and slide' (Sogang) keeps ~1-2 in the lead-in at any moment: ~10-20 N
7 contacts straight in together: up to 69 N through the clamp [source max per contact]; tilted 'lean and slide' (Sogang) keeps ~1-2 in the lead-in at any moment: ~10-20 N
9 contacts straight in together: up to 88 N through the clamp [source max per contact]; tilted 'lean and slide' (Sogang) keeps ~1-2 in the lead-in at any moment: ~10-20 N
Sogang #26 ribbon into 2.5 mm housings [source arXiv 2608.06996]: straight parallel approach
3/20 success (pitch and yaw errors); lean-and-slide 18/20; with weaving and a guide clamp,
insertion 49/50.

============================================================================
9. Electrical access through the far end (pogo on the cut face)
============================================================================
conductor resistance 57 mOhm/m; a 100-600 mm loom 6-34 mOhm
crimp resistance ~1-2 mOhm [mfr: 10 mOhm initial max for the XH contact interface]
-> two-wire through the loom sees continuity, opens and shorts, and blade-to-copper
   touches; it cannot grade a crimp. Four-wire at the crimp needs two probes on the
   contact and two on the conductor's far end.

============================================================================
10 housings, 55 conductors, 53 crimps, 45 webs to part
machine time ~156 min per unit (2.6 h); person's hands ~15 min (load/unload)
