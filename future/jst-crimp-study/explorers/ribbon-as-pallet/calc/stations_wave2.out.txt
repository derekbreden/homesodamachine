
==============================================================================
1. The web line: how much silicone lies between two bundles, and what a blade leaves
==============================================================================
pitch = OD = 1.7 mm, so neighbouring jackets meet at the valley plane (tangent circles,
fused over some neck thickness t_n that BNTECHGO does not state).
silicone along the mid-plane between two bundle surfaces: 0.98 mm (0.49 mm each side of the valley plane)

A slitting blade of thickness t_b centred delta off the valley plane leaves, on the
near conductor's flank at the equator, wall = 0.49 - (|delta| + t_b/2); it reaches the
outermost strand when that wall falls below the loose-strand allowance (0.04 mm).
  double-edge razor, 0.10 mm:
    offset 0.00 mm -> near flank wall 0.44 mm  
    offset 0.05 mm -> near flank wall 0.39 mm  
    offset 0.10 mm -> near flank wall 0.34 mm  
    offset 0.15 mm -> near flank wall 0.29 mm  
    offset 0.20 mm -> near flank wall 0.24 mm  jacket thin
    offset 0.25 mm -> near flank wall 0.19 mm  jacket thin
    offset 0.30 mm -> near flank wall 0.14 mm  jacket thin
    offset at which the blade reaches strands: 0.40 mm; keeps >=0.25 mm wall up to 0.19 mm
  single-edge razor, 0.23 mm:
    offset 0.00 mm -> near flank wall 0.38 mm  
    offset 0.05 mm -> near flank wall 0.32 mm  
    offset 0.10 mm -> near flank wall 0.27 mm  
    offset 0.15 mm -> near flank wall 0.22 mm  jacket thin
    offset 0.20 mm -> near flank wall 0.17 mm  jacket thin
    offset 0.25 mm -> near flank wall 0.12 mm  jacket thin
    offset 0.30 mm -> near flank wall 0.08 mm  jacket thin
    offset at which the blade reaches strands: 0.34 mm; keeps >=0.25 mm wall up to 0.12 mm

Lateral error budget for a FIXED gang slitter on a 5P (centred datum):
  ribbon pitch stack worst 0.23 / RSS 0.08; blade-stack spacers +/-0.04; channel +/-0.05
  -> worst 0.32 mm, RSS 0.09 mm
Lateral error for a FLOATING plough (each blade on a flexure, V-nose riding its own
valley) [estimate]: valley-finding +/-0.05, nose-to-blade +/-0.02 -> +/-0.07 mm,
independent of the ribbon's pitch stack.
-> a 0.10 mm blade floating on its valley keeps >=0.25 mm of flank wall; a fixed 0.23 mm
   gang on a 5P can reach within 0.1 mm of the strands at worst case.

==============================================================================
2. Tearing the web: force, which path the tear takes, the wedge tines
==============================================================================
Peel of one web (trouser-type tear): F ~ T x t_n, T = tear strength
[source: Primasil, wire grades 15-25 N/mm; general grades ~10 N/mm]
  neck t_n 0.15 mm:  1.5- 3.8 N per web; 4 webs of a 5P at once   6.0- 15.0 N
  neck t_n 0.25 mm:  2.5- 6.2 N per web; 4 webs of a 5P at once  10.0- 25.0 N
  neck t_n 0.35 mm:  3.5- 8.8 N per web; 4 webs of a 5P at once  14.0- 35.0 N
  neck t_n 0.50 mm:  5.0-12.5 N per web; 4 webs of a 5P at once  20.0- 50.0 N
  neck t_n 0.70 mm:  7.0-17.5 N per web; 4 webs of a 5P at once  28.0- 70.0 N

Which path the tear takes [estimate, energy argument]: a tear running along the neck
cuts t_n of silicone per mm of advance. A tear that leaves the neck and runs along a
jacket must cut the jacket wall (~0.49 mm) instead, and a tear that reaches the
strands then runs along the silicone-copper interface, which has almost no bond.
The neck is the cheaper path while t_n is well under the wall:
  t_n 0.15 mm: t_n / wall = 0.31  -> zips (neck much cheaper)
  t_n 0.25 mm: t_n / wall = 0.51  -> zips (neck much cheaper)
  t_n 0.35 mm: t_n / wall = 0.71  -> marginal: the tear may wander
  t_n 0.49 mm: t_n / wall = 1.00  -> wanders into a jacket: cut, don't tear
  t_n 0.70 mm: t_n / wall = 1.43  -> wanders into a jacket: cut, don't tear
-> one cross-section photograph of the ribbon (fresh blade cut, ELP camera) that shows
   t_n decides between tearing (a7) and cutting (a7b).

tine 0.2 x 1.5 mm, 10 mm long: buckles at 4.9 N unsupported; 40-69 N when the silicone on both sides supports it
tine 0.3 x 1.5 mm, 10 mm long: buckles at 16.7 N unsupported; 73-127 N when the silicone on both sides supports it
tine 0.2 x 1.5 mm, 5 mm long: buckles at 19.7 N unsupported; 40-69 N when the silicone on both sides supports it
-> a 0.2 mm tine pushed into a web tear (2-15 N) needs the conductors' support,
   which it has while it is inside the split; the free length ahead of the ribbon
   must stay short (<5 mm) or the tine is thicker there.

Tines that part and fan to housing pitch: tine noses at 1.7 mm pitch, roots at 2.5 mm,
so tine j (counted from the centre valley) diverges j x 0.8 mm over its length:
  tine length 15 mm, tine 0.5 pitches out: 0.4 mm over 15 mm = 1.5 deg
  tine length 15 mm, tine 1.5 pitches out: 1.2 mm over 15 mm = 4.6 deg
  tine length 15 mm, tine 2.5 pitches out: 2.0 mm over 15 mm = 7.6 deg
  tine length 20 mm, tine 0.5 pitches out: 0.4 mm over 20 mm = 1.1 deg
  tine length 20 mm, tine 1.5 pitches out: 1.2 mm over 20 mm = 3.4 deg
  tine length 20 mm, tine 2.5 pitches out: 2.0 mm over 20 mm = 5.7 deg
-> for single ribbons (<=5 conductors) the tines splay <=8 deg; the comb that parts is
   also a housing-pitch fan.

==============================================================================
3. The fan shortens the outer conductors: flush cut and strip AFTER fanning
==============================================================================
A conductor anchored at the split root follows its groove; the groove's path is
longer than its axial span, so the tip recedes by (path - axial). Centred datum,
R 5 mm, 30 deg, each ribbon fanned alone:
 pitch | 3P outer recedes | 4P outer recedes | 5P outer recedes
   2.5 |           0.11 mm |           0.20 mm |           0.31 mm
   4.0 |           0.49 mm |           0.80 mm |           1.11 mm
   5.0 |           0.76 mm |           1.20 mm |           1.65 mm
   7.1 |           1.32 mm |           2.05 mm |           2.77 mm
-> at 5 mm crimp pitch a 5P's outer tips sit ~1.6 mm behind the centre tip; at 7.1 mm
   strip pitch ~2.7 mm. A flush cut before the fan with the strip after it gives unequal
   stripped lengths; cutting and stripping both before the fan gives equal stubs whose
   insulation edges are staggered by the same amount. Order: part, fan, then flush-cut
   at the fan block face and strip, all referenced to the fan block.

Closing from crimp pitch back to 2.5 mm reverses it: the outer contacts advance
relative to the inner ones by the difference in recession:
  3P from 5.0 mm to 2.5 mm: outer contact leads by ~0.65 mm (taken up as bow if the insertion clamp sets the fronts)
  4P from 5.0 mm to 2.5 mm: outer contact leads by ~1.00 mm (taken up as bow if the insertion clamp sets the fronts)
  5P from 5.0 mm to 2.5 mm: outer contact leads by ~1.34 mm (taken up as bow if the insertion clamp sets the fronts)
  3P from 7.1 mm to 2.5 mm: outer contact leads by ~1.22 mm (taken up as bow if the insertion clamp sets the fronts)
  4P from 7.1 mm to 2.5 mm: outer contact leads by ~1.85 mm (taken up as bow if the insertion clamp sets the fronts)
  5P from 7.1 mm to 2.5 mm: outer contact leads by ~2.47 mm (taken up as bow if the insertion clamp sets the fronts)

==============================================================================
4. Ring score: blade radius budget, score depth, ligament, tear-off force
==============================================================================
Blade tip radius r_s must clear the outermost strand with margin:
  r_s >= r_bundle + loose strand + eccentricity + centring error + setting error + margin
  (centring error: 0.02 for two blades closing together, 0.05 for the axis height of a
   conductor rolled on a flat bed with OD 1.7 +/-0.1, 0.125 for one blade in a 1.85 bore)
  twin blades, self-centring, e 0.05  : r_s 0.55 mm, score depth 0.30 of 0.49 wall (61 %), ligament 0.19 mm, tear-off 2.2-6.0 N
  twin blades, self-centring, e 0.10  : r_s 0.60 mm, score depth 0.25 of 0.49 wall (51 %), ligament 0.24 mm, tear-off 2.9-8.0 N
  rolled under fixed blades, e 0.05   : r_s 0.58 mm, score depth 0.27 of 0.49 wall (55 %), ligament 0.22 mm, tear-off 2.6-7.1 N
  rolled under fixed blades, e 0.10   : r_s 0.63 mm, score depth 0.22 of 0.49 wall (45 %), ligament 0.27 mm, tear-off 3.4-9.2 N
  one blade in a 1.85 bore, e 0.05    : r_s 0.66 mm, score depth 0.19 of 0.49 wall (40 %), ligament 0.30 mm, tear-off 3.8-10.3 N
  one blade in a 1.85 bore, e 0.10    : r_s 0.71 mm, score depth 0.14 of 0.49 wall (30 %), ligament 0.35 mm, tear-off 4.6-12.7 N
  (tear-off = ligament ring area x 8-11 MPa, lower bound halved for the notch)
-> two opposed blades that close together centre the conductor and roughly double the
   usable score depth over a single blade in a bore. Tear-off stays at 3-15 N per
   conductor, a few tens of N for a whole 5P pulled one after another.

  cutting energy 0.1 N/mm, depth 0.2 mm: 0.04 N at the blades, 0.02 N*mm at the spindle
  cutting energy 0.1 N/mm, depth 0.3 mm: 0.06 N at the blades, 0.04 N*mm at the spindle
  cutting energy 1.0 N/mm, depth 0.2 mm: 0.40 N at the blades, 0.24 N*mm at the spindle
  cutting energy 1.0 N/mm, depth 0.3 mm: 0.60 N at the blades, 0.36 N*mm at the spindle
-> an N20 gearmotor (tens of N*mm) turns it with room; the conductor, held 10-20 mm
   back, twists a few degrees at most.

Twist after a partial pull (RotaryStrip's 'controlled twisting'):
  0.25 turn over the 2.4 mm stub: lay length 9.6 mm; outer strand angle 13 deg
  0.5 turn over the 2.4 mm stub: lay length 4.8 mm; outer strand angle 25 deg

5. Whole-end crown score while webbed (flat blades top and bottom)
==============================================================================
  each blade 0.20 mm deep: cuts +/-40 deg of each crown; 200 deg of 360 left for the flanks to tear; strand margin 0.15-0.25 mm (e 0.10-0)
  each blade 0.25 mm deep: cuts +/-45 deg of each crown; 180 deg of 360 left for the flanks to tear; strand margin 0.10-0.20 mm (e 0.10-0)
  each blade 0.30 mm deep: cuts +/-50 deg of each crown; 161 deg of 360 left for the flanks to tear; strand margin 0.05-0.15 mm (e 0.10-0)
  each blade 0.35 mm deep: cuts +/-54 deg of each crown; 144 deg of 360 left for the flanks to tear; strand margin 0.00-0.10 mm (e 0.10-0)
-> flat blades need no pitch match, but leave ~45 % of each circumference (the flanks
   and the web) to tear. The flank tear quality is the unknown this branch rests on.

==============================================================================
6. Touch-off: the flush-cut copper face as a switch
==============================================================================
  free conductor 5 mm: buckles (fixed-free) at 1.47 N
  free conductor 9 mm: buckles (fixed-free) at 0.45 N
  free conductor 15 mm: buckles (fixed-free) at 0.16 N
  (a floor: inside the scorer's bore the tip is guided laterally, so it cannot take
   the free buckling shape; the bore raises the limit several-fold)
  touch force needed to read continuity through tinned strand ends on steel:
  ~0.05-0.2 N [assumption]; the stage stops at the first reading.
  stage resolution: Tr8x2 lead screw, 200 steps x 16 microsteps: 0.62 um per microstep; repeatability ~0.01 mm [estimate]
-> each conductor's tip is located to ~0.01-0.05 mm (cut-face squareness dominates),
   so the score lands 2.4 mm (or the clone's 1.6-2.1) behind every tip individually.

   up, so no separate ramp is needed.

   silicone into the window between the barrels.
