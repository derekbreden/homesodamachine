1. Half-rows and the feed-length rule (f5b, f2c, f4's 'where it leads')
==============================================================================
Insertion stroke s from a crimped contact fully behind the rear face to latched:
  s = pocket gap + contact length + seated inset of the contact's rear
  contact length 5.8-6.73 (clone drawings), 6.1 / 6.5 (JST catalogs) [xh-facts s1];
  seated inset 0.2-1.25 [into-the-housing estimate]; pocket gap 0-1 mm [estimate]
  -> s = 6.0-9.0 mm (JST lengths: 6.3-8.2); c1 uses ~7 mm

Slack a straight-laid half-row can give by straightening its spread 3.4 -> 5.0 mm
(outer conductor of a centred row moves m = (n-1)/2 x 1.6 mm):
  row | m mm | straight diagonal L=12 / 16 / 20 | a1 fan rule R5/30deg
   2  |  0.8 | 0.027 / 0.020 / 0.016 mm           | 0.108 mm
   3  |  1.6 | 0.106 / 0.080 / 0.064 mm           | 0.306 mm
   4  |  2.4 | 0.238 / 0.179 / 0.143 mm           | 0.520 mm
   5  |  3.2 | 0.419 / 0.317 / 0.254 mm           | 0.734 mm
  -> at most ~0.4 mm, against s = 6-9 mm.

Geometry of two equal-length half-rows from one clamped web:
  final state: every contact latched at the same distance D from the web.
  row B must start behind the rear face, i.e. at D - s, on a conductor of length D:
  it needs s of stored feed whatever row A did. Row A needs s of stored feed
  too, unless the web follows its push, and if the web follows, row B's contacts
  are carried to D with it, which is inside the housing.
Hump that stores s (parabolic arch, h = sqrt(3 c s / 8)) [into-the-housing s9]:
  chord 10.0 mm: s 6 -> h 4.7 mm; s 7 -> h 5.1 mm; s 8 -> h 5.5 mm
  chord 15.0 mm: s 6 -> h 5.8 mm; s 7 -> h 6.3 mm; s 8 -> h 6.7 mm
  chord 20.0 mm: s 6 -> h 6.7 mm; s 7 -> h 7.2 mm; s 8 -> h 7.7 mm

Both half-rows spread about the ribbon's centre by 5.0/3.4 interleave at 2.5 mm:
  4 conductors: odd ['-3.75', '1.25'], even ['-1.25', '3.75']; spacing [2.5]
  5 conductors: odd ['-5.00', '0.00', '5.00'], even ['-2.50', '2.50']; spacing [2.5]
  9 conductors: odd ['-10.00', '-5.00', '0.00', '5.00', '10.00'], even ['-7.50', '-2.50', '2.50', '7.50']; spacing [2.5]
-> a 1.7 -> 2.5 mm fan of the whole ribbon IS the two half-rows at 5.0 mm, already
   interleaved at housing pitch. Crimped contacts fit side by side at 2.5 mm
   (insulation crimp 1.8-2.05 wide leaves 0.45-0.7 mm) [a6], so one merged row and one
   housing move (i3) insert both half-rows with zero stored feed.

2. f5b: stripped before the spread, and a V-tooth comb against a root-captured fan
==============================================================================
c1/f5b strip flat while webbed, then spread 3.4 -> 5.0. The spread pulls outer tips
back relative to inner ones by the slack above, so insulation edges stagger:
  row of 2: insulation-edge stagger 0.02-0.03 mm (diagonal, L 20-12); 0.11 mm (R5/30 fan)
  row of 3: insulation-edge stagger 0.06-0.11 mm (diagonal, L 20-12); 0.31 mm (R5/30 fan)
  row of 4: insulation-edge stagger 0.14-0.24 mm (diagonal, L 20-12); 0.52 mm (R5/30 fan)
  row of 5: insulation-edge stagger 0.25-0.42 mm (diagonal, L 20-12); 0.73 mm (R5/30 fan)
  axial window for the conductor ~+/-0.2-0.3 mm [f&f placement_budget]
-> rows of 2-3 are inside the window; J1's 5-row and the 4-rows spend 0.14-0.8 mm of
   it before any lay error. Strip after the spread, at the comb face (a8 rolls a
   whole half-row at any pitch), and the stagger is zero by construction.

Comb capture. A straight-descending V-tooth comb captures a conductor that moves
less than half a slot (2.5 mm) minus lay error; margins +1.5/+0.7/-0.1/-0.9 mm for
rows of 2/3/4/5 [ctq calc off s4].
A grooved fan block pressed down from the root captures each conductor where it
already lies (groove pitch = in-plane pitch 3.4 mm at the root) and the capture
propagates toward the tip, so the move itself never limits capture. What limits it
is the ribbon's own pitch stack at the root, from a centred datum [pg s1]:
  3-row plane out of a 5-conductor end: worst stack 0.23 mm against a 1.70 mm half-pitch in the plane -> margin 1.47 mm
  5-row plane out of a 9-conductor end: worst stack 0.43 mm against a 1.70 mm half-pitch in the plane -> margin 1.27 mm
-> rows of four and five lay without a tilted or rolling comb.

3. K1: dock on strip, tack, cut, then press one at a time
==============================================================================
Parted length to fan each ribbon alone to strip pitch ~7 mm [pg s2]: 3P 21, 4P 25, 5P 30 mm

Tack stroke on the strip (insulation barrels closed loosely, conductor barrels open):
  per contact 33-132 N [ctq c1b: 66-660 N for 2-5 contacts]
  3P: 99-396 N   (tab shear after it, same comb as pad: 150-480 N [calc X s5])
  4P: 132-528 N   (tab shear after it, same comb as pad: 200-640 N [calc X s5])
  5P: 165-660 N   (tab shear after it, same comb as pad: 250-800 N [calc X s5])
  tack-comb steel between notch mouths at pitch 6.8: 3.55 mm (mouth 3.25 mm)
  tack-comb steel between notch mouths at pitch 7.1: 3.85 mm (mouth 3.25 mm)
  tack-comb steel between notch mouths at pitch 8.0: 4.75 mm (mouth 3.25 mm)
  tack-comb steel between notch mouths at pitch 9.5: 6.25 mm (mouth 3.25 mm)
  (c1b at 3.4 mm needs two passes at 6.8 mm for the same reason; at strip pitch one
   pass does.) A loose tack's profile tolerance is a tenth or two, so a laser-cut
   plate (+/-0.127 mm, SendCutSend [f&f f7]) serves; no EDM.

Seating the box against the nest's front stop after a vertical entry: the stage
pushes the contact +Y through the straight overhang between fan-block face and barrel.
  overhang 9 mm: pinned-pinned Euler 1.8 N (elastic, EI from [pg s4])
  overhang 12 mm: pinned-pinned Euler 1.0 N (elastic, EI from [pg s4])
  A box resting in a slot ~0.3 mm longer than itself slides at tenths of a newton
  [estimate]: under the overhang's buckling load and under the tack's 0.4-9 N.
  A vertical entry loads the tack across the jacket, where the wrapped wings hold it;
  c1b's box-first push into a slot (0.1-0.5 N [c1b estimate]) loads it along the jacket,
  where a loose tack may hold as little as 0.1-0.4 N [c1b; f&f wave2 s5].

Heavy station: tacked neighbours stand one strip pitch away. Neighbour half-width
~1.1 mm (loosely closed insulation barrel 2.0-2.2 wide; open conductor wings
1.68-1.90 [xh-facts s1]); 0.3 mm air [estimate]:
  pitch 6.8: jaws, nest block and crimper holder <= 10.8 mm wide where neighbours pass
  pitch 7.1: jaws, nest block and crimper holder <= 11.4 mm wide where neighbours pass
  pitch 8.0: jaws, nest block and crimper holder <= 13.2 mm wide where neighbours pass
  pitch 9.5: jaws, nest block and crimper holder <= 16.2 mm wide where neighbours pass
  (f4 needed <= ~6 mm at 5 mm pitch; f3's crimper is 3.5-4.4 mm [f&f gang s1])

Handing a tacked contact to a keyed nest: what the tack must resist
  free length 20 mm, contact moved 0.3 mm sideways by a chamfer: 2 mN (elastic 1.7-1.9, plastic cap 18)
  free length 20 mm, contact moved 0.5 mm sideways by a chamfer: 3 mN (elastic 2.8-3.1, plastic cap 18)
  free length 30 mm, contact moved 0.3 mm sideways by a chamfer: 1 mN (elastic 0.5-0.6, plastic cap 12)
  free length 30 mm, contact moved 0.5 mm sideways by a chamfer: 1 mN (elastic 0.8-0.9, plastic cap 12)
  torsion of the free conductor: strands free to slip 10.9 + jacket 0.79 = 11.7 N mm^2 [estimate]
  square a contact rolled  5 deg on 10 mm of free conductor: 0.102 N mm
  square a contact rolled 10 deg on 10 mm of free conductor: 0.203 N mm
  square a contact rolled  5 deg on 20 mm of free conductor: 0.051 N mm
  square a contact rolled 10 deg on 20 mm of free conductor: 0.102 N mm
  square a contact rolled  5 deg on 30 mm of free conductor: 0.034 N mm
  square a contact rolled 10 deg on 30 mm of free conductor: 0.068 N mm
  tack grip: strands slip in the jacket at 0.4-9 N; torque 0.4-2.5 N mm [f&f wave2 s5; f9]
-> the conductor bends and twists long before a tack slips: the nest locates the
   contact, and 10 mm or more of free conductor makes any stage error harmless.

Contacts and time per unit
  14 ribbon ends, 53 crimps; N+2 per end with trimmed positions snipped out: 81 contacts per unit
    at $0.0235 (SXH reel): $1.90 per unit
    at $0.0471 (SXH 100-piece strip): $3.82 per unit
    at $0.0079 (CJT clone reel): $0.64 per unit
  heavy station at 1 min per crimp: 53 min per unit, unattended
  heavy station at 2 min per crimp: 106 min per unit, unattended
  person per ribbon end ~55 s (strip 20, dock 10, levers 15, move 10) [estimate]: 13 min per unit, plus pallet loading and insertion

6. f6: which way bend-and-look can bend at the press
==============================================================================
  pin diameter 1 mm (radius 0.5): jacket surface strain +/-0.63
  pin diameter 2 mm (radius 1.0): jacket surface strain +/-0.46
  pin diameter 3 mm (radius 1.5): jacket surface strain +/-0.36
  (f&f wave2 s7 gives 0.46 / 0.30 / 0.22 for '1 / 2 / 3 mm pins': those are radii;
   f6's 2 mm hardened dowel is a diameter, so its surface strain is 0.46, not 0.30)
  The wing tips of a B/F insulation crimp sit on TOP of the crimp [f&f wave2 s5], so a
  DOWN bend puts them on the outside (a cut gapes) and an UP bend on the inside (it closes).
Room each direction needs right behind the insulation barrel, 2 mm pin:
  60 deg: the bent wire sweeps >= 1.8 mm to that side of its own axis
  90 deg: the bent wire sweeps >= 2.7 mm to that side of its own axis
  neighbour at 3.4 mm leaves a 1.7 mm gap between jackets
  neighbour at 5.0 mm leaves a 3.3 mm gap between jackets
  neighbour at 7.1 mm leaves a 5.4 mm gap between jackets
  below: f3's strip track and carrier lie at floor level right behind the dies
-> at f3/f6's press, with a fan of neighbours and the track behind, only UP is
   free, and up puts the tip zone on the inside of the bend.

A row bent down at once over an edge by swinging the ribbon pallet about it (K7):
  fan-block face 9 mm behind the edge, 60 deg: face moves 7.8 down, 4.5 toward the edge (a hinge on the edge line)
  fan-block face 9 mm behind the edge, 90 deg: face moves 9.0 down, 9.0 toward the edge (a hinge on the edge line)
  fan-block face 15 mm behind the edge, 60 deg: face moves 13.0 down, 7.5 toward the edge (a hinge on the edge line)
  fan-block face 15 mm behind the edge, 90 deg: face moves 15.0 down, 15.0 toward the edge (a hinge on the edge line)
