1. c6's round keyhole against a closed insulation crimp
==============================================================================
Tips lie on the bore circle with gap g at the top. Height of the highest metal above
the contact's underside = floor t + r + y_tip + t*sin(phi), phi = angle of the tip's radius
above horizontal. Throat opening angle = 2*asin(g/D) seen from the bore centre.
Closed insulation crimp height: 1.80 mm at 22 AWG on the KONNRA clone spec [digest];
the SN-2549's own insulation height is unmeasured [assumption: 1.8-2.1 mm].

  bore D (mandrel+springback) | throat g | tip-top metal | open arc | jacket top (on bore floor) | tip-top below 1.80 by
        1.57                 |   1.3    |   1.54 mm     |   112 deg |       1.90 mm            |   +0.26 mm
        1.57                 |   1.4    |   1.43 mm     |   126 deg |       1.90 mm            |   +0.37 mm
        1.57                 |   1.5    |   1.28 mm     |   146 deg |       1.90 mm            |   +0.52 mm
        1.60                 |   1.3    |   1.58 mm     |   109 deg |       1.90 mm            |   +0.22 mm
        1.60                 |   1.4    |   1.48 mm     |   122 deg |       1.90 mm            |   +0.32 mm
        1.60                 |   1.5    |   1.35 mm     |   139 deg |       1.90 mm            |   +0.45 mm
        1.65                 |   1.3    |   1.66 mm     |   104 deg |       1.90 mm            |   +0.14 mm
        1.65                 |   1.4    |   1.57 mm     |   116 deg |       1.90 mm            |   +0.23 mm
        1.65                 |   1.5    |   1.45 mm     |   131 deg |       1.90 mm            |   +0.35 mm
-> the keyhole's metal ends 0.14-0.52 mm below the clone spec's closed insulation height,
   with 104-146 deg of the jacket's top uncovered. A die that closes to 1.80 mm meets the
   jacket first and reaches the wing tips only where its profile dips below ~1.3-1.6 mm
   at x = +/-0.65-0.75 mm. A B-profile dips at x = 0 (the cusp), where there is only jacket.
   The final stroke then presses the jacket through the throat and squeezes the sides;
   it does not turn the tips over the jacket [assumption: B-profile insulation section].

  How a B-profile die forms an insulation barrel (kinematics, all single-stroke tools):
  the open wing tips meet the die's flared walls, are pushed inside the die width, run up
  the arches toward the central cusp, meet there and are driven down into the jacket.
  The wing roots stay near vertical until the tips reach the cusp. No point of that
  stroke is a round bore with an open 1.3-1.5 mm throat. Stopping the stroke early gives a
  narrowed U with tips curling inward high up; stopping it at the pin gives tips closed
  over the pin (no throat).

2. The keyhole's axial grip with springback and the jacket's OD tolerance
==============================================================================
change-the-question's grip model [w2 section 4], unchanged, evaluated at the bore the
mandrel actually leaves (mandrel + 0.02-0.05 springback, as c6's own go/no-go text says),
and at the ribbon's 1.7 +/-0.1 mm per conductor [source S29].

  mandrel | bore after springback | jacket OD 1.60 | jacket OD 1.70 | jacket OD 1.80
   1.55  |        1.57           |   0.05- 1.0 N  |   0.20- 3.8 N  |   0.32- 6.0 N
   1.55  |        1.60           |   0.00- 0.0 N  |   0.16- 3.0 N  |   0.28- 5.3 N
   1.60  |        1.62           |   0.00- 0.0 N  |   0.13- 2.4 N  |   0.26- 4.9 N
   1.60  |        1.65           |   0.00- 0.0 N  |   0.08- 1.5 N  |   0.22- 4.1 N
  as c6 quotes it (bore = mandrel, OD 1.70): 0.16-4.3 N ('0.2-4 N')
  with springback, OD 1.70: 0.08-3.8 N
  a keyed slot asks 0.1-0.5 N, a post 0.2-1.6 N [ts otq section 7, via w2 section 4]
-> springback halves the low end; a conductor at the low edge of 1.7 +/-0.1 mm is not
   gripped at all by a 1.60 mandrel's bore. The bore has to be chosen against the ribbon's
   measured OD, not its nominal pitch.

==============================================================================
3. The crimp tool as its own pre-former: is there a stroke window?
==============================================================================
Jaw gap above bottom dead centre at which each die first touches its open wing tips.
Edge model: the die's flared side walls come down to within e of the anvil at BDC
(they form the barrel's sides), and the open tips lie outside the die width, so they
meet the flare when its lower edge reaches the tip height: gap = H_open - e.
Apex model (pessimistic): the tips are first touched near the arch, gap = H_open - H_closed.
  edge model, e = 0.0: insulation first touch at 2.75-3.20 mm gap, conductor at 1.50-1.60; window 1.15-1.70 mm (nominal drawings)
  edge model, e = 0.2: insulation first touch at 2.55-3.00 mm gap, conductor at 1.30-1.40; window 1.15-1.70 mm (nominal drawings)
  edge model, drawing tolerances against each other: window down to 0.65 mm
  apex model: insulation 0.75-1.40, conductor 0.62-0.87; window -0.12 to +0.78 mm
  in grip travel at the tool's open end (gain 3-6 [ht w2 section 5, assumption]):
    die window 0.65 mm ->  2.0- 3.9 mm of grip travel
    die window 1.15 mm ->  3.4- 6.9 mm of grip travel
    die window 1.70 mm ->  5.1-10.2 mm of grip travel
-> on the edge model the insulation wings are pushed inside the die width over 0.65-1.7 mm
   of stroke before the conductor die touches anything: several mm of grip, likely one or
   more ratchet clicks [estimate; click pitch unmeasured]. On the apex model the window can
   vanish. One slow close on an empty contact, looked at end-on, decides which.
  what the tool-made pre-form looks like at the end of the window [estimate]:
    outer width ~ die width 1.8-2.0 (+0.02-0.05 springback), tips curling inward high in the
    arches, height ~2.3-3.0 mm, conductor barrel untouched.

4. A flag pushed box-first through the open SN-2549: lance, grip and opening
==============================================================================
  lance fold force 1.0-5.0 N [ith ex section 3] against the flag's axial grip 0.08-3.8 N [section 2]
  -> if the flag slides on the anvil, the lance is dragged across the jaw's thickness and
     the contact is pushed back along the jacket whenever fold force exceeds grip: most
     of the range. The tip-to-contact position the snap block set is then lost unseen.
  flag carried level at height h above the anvil: entry needs h >= 0.8-1.1 mm (lance + 0.2); exit needs lift 1.0-1.7 mm [ith ex section 2]
    h 1.1: round keyhole (box governs)  -> jaw opening at the nest 3.5-3.7 mm
    h 1.1: tool-made U 2.3-3.0          -> jaw opening at the nest 3.6-4.3 mm
    h 1.4: round keyhole (box governs)  -> jaw opening at the nest 3.8-4.0 mm
    h 1.4: tool-made U 2.3-3.0          -> jaw opening at the nest 3.9-4.6 mm
    h 1.7: round keyhole (box governs)  -> jaw opening at the nest 4.1-4.3 mm
    h 1.7: tool-made U 2.3-3.0          -> jaw opening at the nest 4.2-4.9 mm
  for comparison: an open contact needs 3.35-4.10 mm [ith ex section 2]; a flag dragged on
  the anvil needs only the box, 2.4-2.6 mm, but drags its lance.

5. One SN nest as c1c's punch: how narrow, how tall
==============================================================================
  tongue width allowed at 3.4 mm pitch, 0.1 mm clearance each side, beside:
    c6 round keyhole 1.96-2.14 [w2 s2]         4.46-4.64 mm
    tool-made U 1.82-2.05 [s3 est.]            4.55-4.78 mm
    crimped insulation 1.90-2.00 [est.]        4.60-4.70 mm
    open conductor barrel 1.43-2.15 [S19-S22]  4.45-5.17 mm
  tongue must stay that narrow up to the tallest neighbour inside the punch's footprint + 0.3:
    c6 keyhole with jacket (jacket top)    -> narrow to 2.1-2.3 mm above the anvil
    tool-made U tips                       -> narrow to 2.6-3.3 mm above the anvil
    open conductor wings                   -> narrow to 1.6-2.1 mm above the anvil
  walls either side of a 1.5 mm conductor arch in a 4.46 mm tongue: 1.48 mm
  walls either side of a 1.6 mm conductor arch in a 4.46 mm tongue: 1.43 mm
  anvil blade through a 2.0 mm pocket with 0.05 mm clearance: <= 1.90 mm wide
  a 3 x 3 mm HSS blank (c1's anvil) must lose >= 1.1 mm of width
  hand-tool-as-press a4's 'one-nest die narrowed to 6-8 mm' is 1.5-3.5 mm too wide here.
