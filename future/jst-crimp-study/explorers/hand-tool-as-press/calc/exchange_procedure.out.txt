2. What lies behind the strip line on a side-feed contact (p1/p5 fork at 3 mm)
==============================================================================
clone dimensions [xh-facts §1]: window 0.5-0.8, insulation barrel 0.8-1.5, tab 0.7-1.15
  window 0.50, ins. barrel 0.80, tab 0.70: insulation barrel 0.25-1.05 mm behind the strip line; tab 1.05-1.75; carrier edge (shear line) at 1.75
  window 0.65, ins. barrel 1.15, tab 0.90: insulation barrel 0.33-1.47 mm behind the strip line; tab 1.47-2.38; carrier edge (shear line) at 2.38
  window 0.80, ins. barrel 1.50, tab 1.15: insulation barrel 0.40-1.90 mm behind the strip line; tab 1.90-3.05; carrier edge (shear line) at 3.05
-> a fork 3 mm behind the strip line sits over the tab or the carrier edge, the line
   where the applicator's floating shear and its punch act [study: exchange
   borrowed-machines--on--ribbon-as-pallet, MKS-L manual]. p5 keeps the fork closed
   from 30 to 330 deg, through the punch stroke at 170-240 deg.
Free length from fork to strand tip, and the tip wander under a light 0.02 N brush
(EI 14.1-18.1 N*mm^2 [study: procedure-is-the-machine calc/selector_and_bow §4]):
  fork 3 mm behind the strip line -> 5.4 mm free -> wander 0.06-0.07 mm
  fork 5 mm behind the strip line -> 7.4 mm free -> wander 0.15-0.19 mm
  fork 6 mm behind the strip line -> 8.4 mm free -> wander 0.22-0.28 mm
  lateral window: insulation into the narrowest clone insulation barrel +/-0.33 mm

3. p4 proof pull while the punch holds the crimp at bottom dead centre
==============================================================================
If the dies still clamp the barrels, the pull meets die friction before it meets the
crimp's own grip. Friction on the barrels = mu x residual die force.
  die force held N   mu 0.15    mu 0.3    mu 0.5   vs proof 15-20 N
                50         8        15        25
               200        30        60       100
               800       120       240       400
              2000       300       600      1000
-> above ~100 N of held die force, a crimp with no grip of its own passes a 20 N pull.
   Holding at the hard stop leaves most of the 0.8-2.6 kN in the loop. The pull has to
   go through the box (hook or blade in the neck, dies open), as p1 and p5 do.

4. p5 with a die stop plus a preloaded disc-spring stack in the rod
==============================================================================
Without a stop (p5 as written): crimp height moves by force scatter / loop stiffness.
  loop  5 kN/mm: +/-300 N -> +/-  60 um; +/-600 N -> +/- 120 um
  loop 10 kN/mm: +/-300 N -> +/-  30 um; +/-600 N -> +/-  60 um
  loop 15 kN/mm: +/-300 N -> +/-  20 um; +/-600 N -> +/-  40 um
  loop 30 kN/mm: +/-300 N -> +/-  10 um; +/-600 N -> +/-  20 um
With a stop: steel stop blocks beside the punch (10 x 10 mm, 30 mm long loop through
  the punch holder and anvil holder) are ~667 kN/mm; +/-600 N -> +/-0.9 um.
  The frame then only has to deliver the dies to the stop, through the crimp force.
  Die contact must happen early enough in the crank that the frame's deflection at
  crimp force (plus 0.1 mm margin into the stack) is used up before BDC:
  e = 2.5 mm, crimp force at die closure 2.6 kN, NEMA17+30:1 = 3.1 N*m, +50:1 = 4.5 N*m
 frame kN/mm  overtravel mm  crank deg  torque N*m
           2           1.40         64        5.84
           5           0.62         41        4.28
          10           0.36         31        3.36
          20           0.23         25        2.72
          40           0.17         21        2.32
-> with a stop, crimp height no longer depends on loop stiffness. The frame still has
   to be ~10 kN/mm or stiffer for a NEMA 17 through a 50:1 worm to finish the stroke,
   because a soft frame moves the crimp force away from bottom dead centre where the
   eccentric has little advantage. The stack caps an obstruction at preload + travel.

5. Combination p5 x a1: the camshaft squeezes a hand tool's handle
==============================================================================
Handle need <= 220 N, rising only over the last ~8 mm of grip travel (die compaction
magnified 15-40x); approach ~45 mm at a light return-spring load (~20 N) [estimate].
                        lobe  lift mm   deg  dr/dtheta mm/rad  force N  torque N*m
                    approach       45   120              21.5       20        0.43
                     squeeze        8    40              11.5      220        2.52
  squeeze, spring link 275 N        8    40              11.5      275        3.15
            squeeze, gentler        8    60               7.6      220        1.68
  cam radius: base 30 mm + 53 mm lift -> ~83 mm, a ~170 mm plate cam, or a 4:1 lever
  (cam lift 13 mm at 4x the force, same torque).
  follower 16 mm dia x 8 mm on PET-CF (E ~5 GPa), 275 N: Hertz line contact ~83 MPa
  follower 16 mm dia x 16 mm on PET-CF (E ~5 GPa), 275 N: Hertz line contact ~58 MPa
-> a NEMA 17 through p5's 30:1 worm (3.1 N*m) turns a squeeze lobe spread over ~40 deg;
   a PET-CF lobe is near its compressive limit under a narrow roller, so the squeeze
   lobe wants a wide roller or a steel insert. The spring link caps die force at
   ~1.25x the handle need if the tool jams (procedure-is-the-machine exchange calc §4).

6. Axial chain: insulation edge in the window, with the contact's own reference added
==============================================================================
p1 chain without lift scatter (k is never lifted in p1): RSS +/-0.21 mm
  + contact located by pilot hole on a pin (strip)              +/-0.075 -> RSS +/-0.23
  + contact located by box rear shoulder on a blade             +/-0.080 -> RSS +/-0.23
  + contact located by box front on a stop (loose kit contact)  +/-0.250 -> RSS +/-0.33
with the bare length measured by the ELP camera (0.02 mm/px) and the cassette or the
tool moved in Y to put the insulation edge mid-window (strip term -> +/-0.03):
  contact by pilot hole on a pin (strip)              -> RSS +/-0.11
  contact by box rear shoulder on a blade             -> RSS +/-0.11
  contact by box front on a stop (loose kit contact)  -> RSS +/-0.26
  usable window about +/-0.3 mm [study: procedure-is-the-machine calc/transfer_capture §3]
-> a loose kit contact referenced by its box front uses most of the window by itself;
   the box's rear shoulder (a blade in the neck) or the pilot hole keeps it small.
   Measuring the bare length and correcting in Y removes the dominant strip term.

7. p4 with a hand-tool head: cycle and the person's wait
==============================================================================
  clamp, strip pull                    6 s
  feed to touch-off                    4 s
  tool approach 45 mm at 15 mm/s       3 s
  squeeze last 8 mm at 2 mm/s          4 s
  open                                 3 s
  proof pull via blade                 3 s
  release, camera                      3 s
  machine cycle ~26 s [estimate]
  person per conductor: present 5 s + insert 8 s = 13 s [p4's estimates]
  1 head(s): conductor every 26 s, person waits 13 s per conductor; 53 conductors -> 23 min
  2 head(s): conductor every 13 s, person waits 0 s per conductor; 53 conductors -> 11 min
-> one squeezer at ~26 s keeps the person waiting ~13 s a conductor; two squeezers
   ($18-21 tools, one pusher each) alternating bring the person's own pace back.
