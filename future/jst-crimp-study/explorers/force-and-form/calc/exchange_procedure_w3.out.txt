Knee or crank at a geometric bottom, no stop (for comparison): the frame carries the crimp
force only; the price is that height moves by force scatter / loop stiffness:
  local loop    15 kN/mm: +/-300-600 N of scatter -> +/-20.0-40.0 um of crimp height
  local loop    40 kN/mm: +/-300-600 N of scatter -> +/-7.5-15.0 um of crimp height
  local loop   100 kN/mm: +/-300-600 N of scatter -> +/-3.0-6.0 um of crimp height
  local loop   200 kN/mm: +/-300-600 N of scatter -> +/-1.5-3.0 um of crimp height
-> With stops, height does not depend on the loop, and neither does the torque much: the peak
   is 1.0-2.3 N*m for frames of 2-40 kN/mm, inside a NEMA 17 through 30:1 (3.1 N*m). What the
   loop stiffness does set is the surplus: the margin m that guarantees reaching the stops
   costs k x m on every stroke. A stiff frame (>= 20 kN/mm) with m 0.05-0.10 mm reaches the
   stack's preload every stroke (3.5-4.5 kN, 1.5-2.1x the crimp); a soft loop (5-10 kN/mm)
   stays at 1.05-1.4x and caps a doubled contact at 3.9-5.4 kN with no stack at all, where a
   40 kN/mm frame without the stack would put 14 kN into it.
   With stops, the compliant rod is the design, not the defect. A knee or crank bottom in a
   small steel C (100-200 kN/mm) is the other route: +/-1.5-6 um, no surplus, no stops.
