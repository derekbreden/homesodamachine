
======================================================================================
1. f6 bend-and-look: pin diameter, strain, gape, bend direction, room below
======================================================================================
Outer-surface strain of the jacket bent over a pin of diameter d: eps = r_w / (d/2 + r_w),
r_w = 0.85 mm. A cut of depth a gapes ~2.5 x eps x a [estimate, edge-crack opening].
  pin diameter 1 mm (radius 0.5): strain 0.63; a 0.20 mm cut gapes 0.31 mm, a through-cut (0.49) 0.77 mm = 11-28 px at 36 px/mm [px/mm unconfirmed]
  pin diameter 2 mm (radius 1.0): strain 0.46; a 0.20 mm cut gapes 0.23 mm, a through-cut (0.49) 0.56 mm = 8-20 px at 36 px/mm [px/mm unconfirmed]
  pin diameter 3 mm (radius 1.5): strain 0.36; a 0.20 mm cut gapes 0.18 mm, a through-cut (0.49) 0.44 mm = 7-16 px at 36 px/mm [px/mm unconfirmed]
  (calc/wave2.py §7 labels its pins by radius: its '1 / 2 / 3 mm pin' rows are radius 1 / 2 / 3,
   i.e. diameters 2 / 4 / 6 mm. f6's 2 mm hardened dowel is a diameter: strain 0.46.)
Which way: the wing tips of a B/F insulation crimp sit on TOP and press into the jacket's
top [calc wave2 §5]. A cut there opens only on the outside of the bend, so the wire's tail
must bend DOWN (away from the tips). Bending UP closes the cut while the camera looks.
  60 deg down over a 2 mm pin just behind the barrel: the wire sweeps >= 1.7 mm below its axis within the first ~3 mm
  90 deg down over a 2 mm pin just behind the barrel: the wire sweeps >= 2.7 mm below its axis within the first ~3 mm
-> room below the barrel: ~3 mm, clear of any strip track, carrier or anvil. f3's strip
   track lies there, so either a section of track drops after the tab cut, or the bend
   happens at a separate bend nest (open below) the carriage visits after release.

2. f9b: shearing the tabs of tacked contacts on the rail, under the tack comb
======================================================================================
Tab ~0.8-1.0 wide x 0.20 thick [xh-facts §1; calc X §5]. Phosphor bronze yield 450-600 MPa
[assumption, C5191 spring temper range]. Shear strength ~0.6-0.8 x UTS 550-650 MPa.
  tab 0.8 wide, sy 450: plastic moment 3.6 N mm; comb reacts it over a 0.8 mm barrel with  4.5 N
  tab 0.8 wide, sy 450: plastic moment 3.6 N mm; comb reacts it over a 1.5 mm barrel with  2.4 N
  tab 0.8 wide, sy 600: plastic moment 4.8 N mm; comb reacts it over a 0.8 mm barrel with  6.0 N
  tab 0.8 wide, sy 600: plastic moment 4.8 N mm; comb reacts it over a 1.5 mm barrel with  3.2 N
  tab 1.0 wide, sy 450: plastic moment 4.5 N mm; comb reacts it over a 0.8 mm barrel with  5.6 N
  tab 1.0 wide, sy 450: plastic moment 4.5 N mm; comb reacts it over a 1.5 mm barrel with  3.0 N
  tab 1.0 wide, sy 600: plastic moment 6.0 N mm; comb reacts it over a 0.8 mm barrel with  7.5 N
  tab 1.0 wide, sy 600: plastic moment 6.0 N mm; comb reacts it over a 1.5 mm barrel with  4.0 N
  tab 0.8 wide, tau 330 MPa: shear    53 N per tab
  tab 0.8 wide, tau 520 MPa: shear    83 N per tab
  tab 1.0 wide, tau 330 MPa: shear    66 N per tab
  tab 1.0 wide, tau 520 MPa: shear   104 N per tab
-> The carrier going down behind the rail edge can only put the tab's plastic moment into
   the contact, 2.4-6 N mm; the comb, standing at its stop on the loosely closed wings, holds
   it with 2-8 N. The 50-160 N of shear goes rail edge -> tab -> shear punch, all steel; the
   tack itself carries none of it. What is left: whether the comb's stop sits exactly on the
   wings it just formed (it does if it never lifts between tack and shear).

3. The neck blade for the proof pull only; the jacket grip a pull needs
======================================================================================
Lance tip 2.44+/-0.20 mm behind the front; box 2.0 mm: tip 0.24-0.64 mm behind the box's rear, under the transition [calc on_ith §1].
  brush 0.1: blade dropped AFTER the crimp (brush known, no scatter gap): t >= 0.50 mm; blade in place while threading: t >= 0.70 mm [calc FP §5]
  brush 0.3: blade dropped AFTER the crimp (brush known, no scatter gap): t >= 0.70 mm; blade in place while threading: t >= 0.90 mm [calc FP §5]
  clone-drawing length budget: t = 0.30-2.28 mm [calc on_ith §1d]
  blade bearing on the box's rear face above floor + 1.0 mm (0.82 mm2): 20.0 N -> 24 MPa (bronze yield ~450-600)
  blade bearing on the box's rear face above floor + 1.0 mm (0.82 mm2): 39.2 N -> 48 MPa (bronze yield ~450-600)
Jacket grip for a pull that must reach the copper: strands slip inside a squeezed jacket at
0.5-2.1 N per mm of grip at 10 % squeeze, 1.4-6.3 N/mm at 30 % [calc FP §4].
  10 %: a 20 N pull needs  9.5-40.0 mm of jaw
  30 %: a 20 N pull needs  3.2-14.3 mm of jaw
-> A 3-5 mm throat jaw reaches 20 N only at ~30 % squeeze and the stiffer silicone.
   The ribbon pallet's web clamp (all conductors, 10-20 mm long) is the surer reaction:
   pull by moving the pallet, the neck blade holding the box.

======================================================================================
4. f1: the fork holds neighbours clear of the SN-2549's jaw plate
======================================================================================
Neighbour tips must stand h above conductor i's tip (barrels 3-4 mm into the nest plus
clearance: h 4-6 mm [estimate]). All conductors have the same length L from the web.
A neighbour bent aside by angle a at the fork line shortens its reach by L(1 - cos a).
  split 20 mm: 37-46 deg
  split 30 mm: 30-37 deg
  split 40 mm: 26-32 deg
  split 50 mm: 23-28 deg
Elastic alternative: bend the whole neighbour in one arc of radius >= 67 mm (copper's
set radius [ribbon-as-pallet pg §4]); it shortens by only L^3/(24 R^2):
  split 20 mm: 0.07 mm, against 4-6 mm needed
  split 30 mm: 0.25 mm, against 4-6 mm needed
  split 40 mm: 0.59 mm, against 4-6 mm needed
-> The neighbours take a set at the fork line in any version of f1. What can be chosen is
   its direction: every non-working conductor bent the same way (one side, out of the
   ribbon plane), so the row carries one uniform set for the insertion comb to take out.

7. Target crimp height against the machine's own channel width
======================================================================================
Compaction scales with W x H. References: JST SXA analog 1.50 x 0.80 = 1.20 mm2 [mfr S14];
context estimate 1.50 x 0.88 = 1.32; KONNRA clone 1.75 x 0.73 = 1.28 [ribbon-as-pallet w3 §7].
  channel 1.45: target H 0.83-0.91 mm
  channel 1.50: target H 0.80-0.88 mm
  channel 1.54: target H 0.78-0.86 mm
  channel 1.58: target H 0.76-0.84 mm
  channel 1.63: target H 0.74-0.81 mm
  channel 1.67: target H 0.72-0.79 mm
  channel 1.75: target H 0.69-0.75 mm
  a +/-0.05 mm channel error on 1.5 mm changes compaction by +/-3.3 % at a fixed height;
  at H 0.80 the height that restores W x H moves by -/+0.027 mm
-> The copper correction from the JST reference lead (change-the-question c2) must be done
   at the machine's measured channel width. A channel cut to +/-0.05 mm and measured with
   pin gauges is usable when height is set per die (wedge, stop or chased hits). What
   no tolerance here covers is the roof's form: arches and cusp.
