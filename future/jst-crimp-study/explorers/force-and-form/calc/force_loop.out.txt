2. Crimp-to-crimp height scatter under position control
============================================================================
Peak force varies crimp to crimp (strand count, contact hardness, stripping):
take +/-10 % and +/-25 % of a 1.6 kN central peak [assumption]. Height scatter
= dF / (k_loop + k_crimp), k_crimp = crimp's own slope near BDC (stroke_model
gives 12-70 kN/mm). Crimp height tolerance to hold: +/-0.05 mm [mfr analog].
printed C-frame, 30x40 section, 40 mm throat        : +/- 0.005- 0.011 mm at 10 %, +/- 0.012- 0.027 mm at 25 %  (loop-only bound  0.142)
printed C-frame, 60x50 section, 40 mm throat        : +/- 0.004- 0.007 mm at 10 %, +/- 0.010- 0.017 mm at 25 %  (loop-only bound  0.036)
steel C-frame, 2 x 12.7 mm plate, 60 mm deep        : +/- 0.000- 0.000 mm at 10 %, +/- 0.001- 0.001 mm at 25 %  (loop-only bound  0.001)
cast-iron arbor press body (assumed section)        : +/- 0.001- 0.001 mm at 10 %, +/- 0.003- 0.003 mm at 25 %  (loop-only bound  0.003)
the two die blades themselves, 8 mm free each       : +/- 0.003- 0.004 mm at 10 %, +/- 0.007- 0.010 mm at 25 %  (loop-only bound  0.013)
ratchet hand tool, jaws + pivots (guess, not measured): +/- 0.003- 0.005 mm at 10 %, +/- 0.008- 0.013 mm at 25 %  (loop-only bound  0.020)
  same, pessimistic guess                           : +/- 0.004- 0.009 mm at 10 %, +/- 0.011- 0.021 mm at 25 %  (loop-only bound  0.060)
   (steel plate figure is bending only; bolted joints and pins add compliance, so
   read it as tens to hundreds of kN/mm [estimate])
-> scatter: the crimp's own steep curve near BDC carries most of the force change,
   so even a printed loop scatters inside +/-0.05 mm IF its offset stays put.
-> offset: a printed loop sits 0.2-0.85 mm open at peak force, and that offset
   drifts with creep, temperature and humidity (PETG ~60-80 um/m/K, and a few %
   creep of 0.85 mm is 0.02-0.04 mm) [estimate]. Steel loops stretch microns.
   A printed loop is usable only with a hard stop in a steel local loop, or with
   a per-crimp crimp-height measurement that re-sets the next stroke.

============================================================================
3. Hard stop inside the local loop: what the outer frame then has to do
============================================================================
printed C-frame, 30x40 section, 40 mm throat        : extra drive travel to reach the stop at 3.1 kN =  1.10 mm
printed C-frame, 60x50 section, 40 mm throat        : extra drive travel to reach the stop at 3.1 kN =  0.28 mm
steel C-frame, 2 x 12.7 mm plate, 60 mm deep        : extra drive travel to reach the stop at 3.1 kN =  0.01 mm
cast-iron arbor press body (assumed section)        : extra drive travel to reach the stop at 3.1 kN =  0.03 mm
-> with a stop block beside the dies, a sloppy or springy outer frame only costs
   drive travel. The crimp height is then set by the stop-to-anvil stack, which
   is a few mm of steel (k in the hundreds of kN/mm).
   e.g. a 10 x 10 mm steel stop, 10 mm tall: 0.50 um/kN

============================================================================
4. Printed parts under the crimp force: stress, for the record
============================================================================
printed anvil seat 10 x 10 mm                       :     24.0 MPa (printed polymer compressive ~50-100 MPa [estimate])
printed seat 20 x 20 mm                             :      6.0 MPa (printed polymer compressive ~50-100 MPa [estimate])
wing tip on printed punch: 0.2 x 1.3 mm edge        :   9230.8 MPa (printed polymer compressive ~50-100 MPa [estimate])
insulation crimp 130 N over 1.9 x 1.2 mm            :   57.0 MPa mean (printed PET-CF compressive ~60-100 MPa [estimate]); tip line loads far higher
-> printed faces can carry a spread-out seat under a steel die block, never a die
   face; the insulation crimp is the only forming load within reach of polymer on
   average, and its wing-tip contact still is not.
