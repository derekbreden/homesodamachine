============================================================================
1. Re-touch crimp height
============================================================================
re-touch at    5 N: die blades compress  0.167 um
re-touch at   10 N: die blades compress  0.333 um
re-touch at   20 N: die blades compress  0.667 um
gauge options (gap measured across the dies, not through the frame):
  0.001 mm digital indicator with data port                         :   1.00 um resolution
  iGaging/Shahe capacitive scale (TouchDRO reads them on an ESP32)  :  10.00 um resolution
  stepper position through a 2 mm screw, 1/16 microstep             :   0.62 um resolution
  a capacitive 0.01 mm scale resolves a fifth of the +/-0.05 mm band: coarse.
  a 0.001 mm indicator resolves it 50 times over. Motor position counts the
  drive, not the dies, and includes every stretch in between.

============================================================================
2. Gap under load versus crimp height
============================================================================
central   1680 N: die blades  56.0 um, crimp block recovery ~ 8.4 um, lobe-shape springback 10-30 um [estimate]
high      2432 N: die blades  81.1 um, crimp block recovery ~12.2 um, lobe-shape springback 10-30 um [estimate]
-> the gap at peak force reads 20-60 um low; the re-touch at 5-20 N reads the
   crimp as the dies see it, within ~1 um of blade compression.
   The punch arches touch the tops of the crimp lobes and the anvil touches the
   floor: the same two surfaces a point-and-blade crimp micrometer measures
   between, provided the crimp has not moved in the nest [assumption].

============================================================================
3. Which faults move the curve, and by how much
============================================================================
one strand of 60 missing                                          : -0.0050 mm^2 -> compaction onset -   4.0 um
six strands missing (10 %)                                        : -0.0302 mm^2 -> compaction onset -  24.2 um
no conductor at all                                               : -0.3278 mm^2 -> compaction onset - 263.3 um
0.5 mm of 0.49 mm wall silicone under the conductor barrel (strip short): +0.6423 mm^2 -> compaction onset + 515.9 um

Force at BDC, position-controlled, when compaction onset moves by dh:
  central : onset     4 um later -> peak  1589 N vs  1680 N ( -5.4 %)  [upper bound: model is steep]
  central : onset    30 um later -> peak  1081 N vs  1680 N (-35.6 %)  [upper bound: model is steep]
  central : onset   270 um later -> peak   255 N vs  1680 N (-84.8 %)  [upper bound: model is steep]
  high    : onset     4 um later -> peak  2314 N vs  2432 N ( -4.8 %)  [upper bound: model is steep]
  high    : onset    30 um later -> peak  1660 N vs  2432 N (-31.7 %)  [upper bound: model is steep]
  high    : onset   270 um later -> peak   409 N vs  2432 N (-83.2 %)  [upper bound: model is steep]
  steep   : onset     4 um later -> peak  2131 N vs  2432 N (-12.4 %)  [upper bound: model is steep]
  steep   : onset    30 um later -> peak   912 N vs  2432 N (-62.5 %)  [upper bound: model is steep]
  steep   : onset   270 um later -> peak   382 N vs  2432 N (-84.3 %)  [upper bound: model is steep]
Industrial crimp force monitoring: one strand of 7 is ~6.7 % of force, one of 19
~2.5 % and not reliably seen [source: Assembly Magazine, in prior-art]. That is
~0.47 x the copper fraction; for 1 of 60 strands it gives ~0.8 %.
-> one missing strand is below any force signal here. Missing conductor, missing
   contact, insulation in the barrel, a contact seated high or rolled: all move
   the curve by tens of percent or tenths of a mm, and a slow press sees them.

============================================================================
4. Stop at force versus stop at position
============================================================================
low      : curve shifts by 10 % of force -> stop-at-force height moves   6.5 um (slope 12.0 kN/mm)
low      : curve shifts by 25 % of force -> stop-at-force height moves  16.4 um (slope 12.0 kN/mm)
central  : curve shifts by 10 % of force -> stop-at-force height moves   7.6 um (slope 22.2 kN/mm)
central  : curve shifts by 25 % of force -> stop-at-force height moves  18.9 um (slope 22.2 kN/mm)
high     : curve shifts by 10 % of force -> stop-at-force height moves   8.5 um (slope 28.5 kN/mm)
high     : curve shifts by 25 % of force -> stop-at-force height moves  21.3 um (slope 28.5 kN/mm)
high+cut : curve shifts by 10 % of force -> stop-at-force height moves   8.5 um (slope 28.5 kN/mm)
high+cut : curve shifts by 25 % of force -> stop-at-force height moves  21.3 um (slope 28.5 kN/mm)
steep    : curve shifts by 10 % of force -> stop-at-force height moves   3.5 um (slope 68.8 kN/mm)
steep    : curve shifts by 25 % of force -> stop-at-force height moves   8.8 um (slope 68.8 kN/mm)
-> if the real curve is as steep as the family, a force-limited drive (air
   cylinder, current-limited motor) also lands inside +/-50 um. If the real
   slope near BDC is 5 kN/mm, a 25 % shift moves it 80 um. The slope is the
   first thing the logged curve tells you; the re-touch height is the check.
