1. The lance and the anvil (i2, and every die in the study)
==============================================================================
The lance hangs 0.6-0.9 mm below the box floor, its tip 2.44 +/-0.20 mm behind the
contact front [source S22], i.e. 0.24-0.64 mm behind the box's rear end, in the
transition between box and conductor barrel. The barrel floor and box floor are one
plane (the contact is formed from one flat strip) [assumption].

1a. i2's own sketch (25 px/mm): rear face x=560, cavity floor y=300,
    anvil rect x=466..554, top y=300; lance drawn from (575,300) to (549,317).
    anvil front edge   0.24 mm behind the rear face, top at the floor
    lance tip          0.44 mm behind the rear face, 0.68 mm below the floor
    lance root         0.60 mm inside the rear face
    -> the drawn lance tip lies 0.20 mm inside the anvil in Y and 0.68 mm below its top: the two occupy the same steel.

1b. Where the lance tip sits relative to the rear face, box engaged d mm
    (lance free, i.e. not yet folded by the entry):
     d   | tip behind rear face for tip = 2.24 / 2.44 / 2.64 | lance over an anvil whose front is 0.2-0.3 mm back?
     1.4 |   0.84   1.04   1.24                             | yes, yes, yes
     1.8 |   0.44   0.64   0.84                             | yes, yes, yes
     2.0 |   0.24   0.44   0.64                             | yes, yes, yes
     2.2 |   0.04   0.24   0.44                             | edge, yes, yes
     2.4 |  -0.16   0.04   0.24                             | inside housing, edge, yes

1c. What a flat anvil under a free lance does: the contact rests on its lance tip.
    lance 0.60 proud -> barrels start 0.60 mm above the anvil; the punch meets them 0.60 mm early and the stroke flattens the lance (the retention feature) and bends the transition
    lance 0.67 proud -> barrels start 0.67 mm above the anvil; the punch meets them 0.67 mm early and the stroke flattens the lance (the retention feature) and bends the transition
    lance 0.90 proud -> barrels start 0.90 mm above the anvil; the punch meets them 0.90 mm early and the stroke flattens the lance (the retention feature) and bends the transition
    A force monitor sees this as 'contact seated high': the curve rises ~0.6-0.9 mm early,
    far outside any band [calc: metrology §3 logic]. It is detected, not prevented.

1d. Repair: a lance relief. The anvil's front end stops behind the lance tip, or is
    slotted under the lance, as applicator strip tracks are [assumption: applicator practice].
    The conductor barrel floor must still be supported, so the barrel's front must lie
    behind the lance tip + margin. Transition t = conductor-barrel front minus box rear.
    t needed (lance tip + 0.1 mm margin - box length):
      lance tip 2.24: t >= 0.34 mm
      lance tip 2.44: t >= 0.54 mm
      lance tip 2.64: t >= 0.74 mm
    t from the length budget (overall - box - conductor barrel - window - insulation barrel):
      t ranges 0.30 .. 2.28 mm over the drawn lengths and estimated barrels;
      i2 assumed 0.2-0.5. The lance-relief condition and i2's barrel-clear condition both
      turn on t, and one kit contact photographed side-on under the ELP camera gives it.

1e. The i2 window with the lance included (all behind-rear-face distances, mm):
    conditions: box engaged d >= 1.4 (walls hold it) [i2 calc §4];
    conductor-barrel front (2.0 + t - d) >= 0.2 die-wall clearance [i2];
    anvil front >= lance tip (L - d) + 0.1 and <= barrel front (lance free), OR
    lance root inside the entry so the entry folds the lance (then no anvil conflict).
    t = 0.2: box depth d with barrel clear AND a supported barrel behind a lance relief: none
    t = 0.4: box depth d with barrel clear AND a supported barrel behind a lance relief: none
    t = 0.6: box depth d with barrel clear AND a supported barrel behind a lance relief: none
    t = 0.8: box depth d with barrel clear AND a supported barrel behind a lance relief: 1.4-2.4
    t = 1.0: box depth d with barrel clear AND a supported barrel behind a lance relief: 1.4-2.4
    -> with t below ~0.7 no depth works while the lance hangs free under the barrel front;
       the idea then needs the lance folded by the entry (lance root inside) or a
       notched anvil that leaves the barrel's front edge unsupported.

3. Narrow crimper: what governs is wall bending, not the column (i1b, i2, i2b)
==============================================================================
Free half-width beside the working axis, set by the neighbour wire (1.7 mm round,
centre 2.5 mm away). Neighbour wire centre height above the floor h_c ~1.05 mm
(0.2 stock + 0.85 radius) [estimate]. Z measured up from the barrel floor.
  Z = 0.00: tool may be up to 5.00 mm wide (below/above the neighbour wire)
  Z = 0.20: tool may be up to 5.00 mm wide (below/above the neighbour wire)
  Z = 0.40: tool may be up to 3.90 mm wide 
  Z = 0.60: tool may be up to 3.56 mm wide 
  Z = 0.85: tool may be up to 3.35 mm wide 
  Z = 1.05: tool may be up to 3.30 mm wide 
  Z = 1.30: tool may be up to 3.38 mm wide 
  Z = 1.60: tool may be up to 3.70 mm wide 
  Z = 1.90: tool may be up to 5.00 mm wide 
-> the narrowest place is at the neighbour's equator (Z ~1.05), just ABOVE a conductor
   crimp whose top is at ~0.8-0.9. Below Z ~0.6 there is 3.9 mm or more.

Wall bending at the root of the conductor crimper's channel (root at the roof, Z ~0.85).
Lateral pressure on each wall = k x mean die pressure; k 0.3-1.0 [assumption: confined
compaction pushes sideways at a fraction of the vertical pressure].
  F  1680 N (p  800 MPa), k 0.3: wall t: stress MPa  0.30:  3920  0.40:  2205  0.55:  1166  0.70:   720  0.85:   488
  F  1680 N (p  800 MPa), k 0.5: wall t: stress MPa  0.30:  6533  0.40:  3675  0.55:  1944  0.70:  1200  0.85:   814
  F  1680 N (p  800 MPa), k 1.0: wall t: stress MPa  0.30: 13067  0.40:  7350  0.55:  3888  0.70:  2400  0.85:  1628
  F  2430 N (p 1157 MPa), k 0.3: wall t: stress MPa  0.30:  5670  0.40:  3189  0.55:  1687  0.70:  1041  0.85:   706
  F  2430 N (p 1157 MPa), k 0.5: wall t: stress MPa  0.30:  9450  0.40:  5316  0.55:  2812  0.70:  1736  0.85:  1177
  F  2430 N (p 1157 MPa), k 1.0: wall t: stress MPa  0.30: 18900  0.40: 10631  0.55:  5623  0.70:  3471  0.85:  2354
  hardened D2/SKD11 transverse-rupture strength ~2500-3500 MPa; keep working stress
  well under ~1200-1500 MPa for thousands of cycles [estimate].
-> 0.3-0.4 mm walls on the CONDUCTOR crimper crack; 0.7-0.85 mm walls hold at k <= 0.5.
   A 1.5 mm channel + 2 x 0.8 walls = 3.1 mm: fits the 3.35 mm free at Z 0.85 with
   ~0.1 mm a side, and narrows to <= 3.2 above, where the neighbour's equator is.
Insulation crimper: 30-130 N [xh-facts §4]; at 130 N over 1.9 x 1.2 mm, k = 1:
  wall 0.3:   2737 MPa
  wall 0.4:   1539 MPa
-> the insulation crimper can be thin-walled; the conductor crimper cannot.
Column check of the narrow neck, 2.9 x 1.4 mm, 4 mm tall, K = 2, at 2.43 kN:
  compressive 599 MPa, Euler load 21 kN: not the limit.

Dies that bottom on each other inside the narrow envelope: the crimper walls land on
shoulders of the anvil block below the floor (Z < 0, where the neighbour wires are not).
  surplus force   300 N on 2 wall ends of 0.8 x 1.4 mm:    134 MPa
  surplus force  1000 N on 2 wall ends of 0.8 x 1.4 mm:    446 MPa
  surplus force  3000 N on 2 wall ends of 0.8 x 1.4 mm:   1339 MPa
-> workable only with the surplus capped near 1 kN: a spring-limited or current-limited
   drive, or a knee whose straight position arrives with the dies just touching.

==============================================================================
4. A gripper that holds the wire rigidly through the crimp (i1, i4)
==============================================================================
The anvil sets the barrel floor; a rigid grip 2-3 mm behind sets the wire. A Z (or X)
mismatch delta between them is forced into an S-bend over the gap L.
S-bend radius R ~ L^2 / (4 delta) [geometry]; copper strands set below ~67 mm.
  L 2 mm, mismatch 0.05 mm: R   20.0 mm -> sets (permanent kink)
  L 2 mm, mismatch 0.10 mm: R   10.0 mm -> sets (permanent kink)
  L 2 mm, mismatch 0.20 mm: R    5.0 mm -> sets (permanent kink)
  L 2 mm, mismatch 0.40 mm: R    2.5 mm -> sets (permanent kink)
  L 3 mm, mismatch 0.05 mm: R   45.0 mm -> sets (permanent kink)
  L 3 mm, mismatch 0.10 mm: R   22.5 mm -> sets (permanent kink)
  L 3 mm, mismatch 0.20 mm: R   11.2 mm -> sets (permanent kink)
  L 3 mm, mismatch 0.40 mm: R    5.6 mm -> sets (permanent kink)
-> any mismatch above ~0.02 mm leaves a permanent bend at the barrel, JST's
   'bend up/down' fault and the handover's 'straight' requirement.

5. Touching a wire stop with the stripped tip (i1, i4)
==============================================================================
  one 0.08 mm strand, 1.6 mm free past the insulation, K = 2: buckles at 0.227 N
  one 0.08 mm strand, 2.1 mm free past the insulation, K = 2: buckles at 0.132 N
  one 0.08 mm strand, 2.4 mm free past the insulation, K = 2: buckles at 0.101 N
-> a ragged cut touches with a few strands first; the stop must be felt at ~0.1-0.3 N
   or the first strands fold back. A 5 kg bar cell + HX711 resolves ~0.01-0.02 N at
   10 SPS [estimate], so it is possible, but drag along the barrel floor is the same size.
   The camera (insulation edge between the barrels) is the stronger axial reference.

6. i3b's staggered push loads the first crimps in compression
==============================================================================
Force path: housing front wall -> box -> contact -> crimps -> wire -> clamp 2-4 mm back.
   20 N:   51 % of JST's 39.2 N pull-out minimum; clamp-to-crimp wire buckles (K=0.5) beyond 5.3-6.0 mm
   30 N:   77 % of JST's 39.2 N pull-out minimum; clamp-to-crimp wire buckles (K=0.5) beyond 4.4-4.9 mm
   36 N:   92 % of JST's 39.2 N pull-out minimum; clamp-to-crimp wire buckles (K=0.5) beyond 4.0-4.5 mm
   46 N:  117 % of JST's 39.2 N pull-out minimum; clamp-to-crimp wire buckles (K=0.5) beyond 3.5-3.9 mm
   60 N:  153 % of JST's 39.2 N pull-out minimum; clamp-to-crimp wire buckles (K=0.5) beyond 3.1-3.5 mm
   70 N:  179 % of JST's 39.2 N pull-out minimum; clamp-to-crimp wire buckles (K=0.5) beyond 2.9-3.2 mm
-> above ~20 N the crimp is asked to hold more than half its minimum pull-out, pushed
   the way that drives the brush toward the box; at 60-70 N a 4 mm clamp distance
   also buckles. Constant-force springs (or weights) on the clamps hold the load at the
   preload however far the clamp rides back.
  measured single insertion  5 N -> preload   6.2 N ( 16 % of pull-out min)
  measured single insertion  8 N -> preload  10.0 N ( 26 % of pull-out min)
  measured single insertion 12 N -> preload  15.0 N ( 38 % of pull-out min)
  measured single insertion 15 N -> preload  18.8 N ( 48 % of pull-out min)
  measured single insertion 20 N -> preload  25.0 N ( 64 % of pull-out min)
  measured single insertion 25 N -> preload  31.2 N ( 80 % of pull-out min)
-> staggering is comfortable if one contact inserts at <= ~12-15 N; Derek's scale
   measurement under a housing decides it.

7. The force ladder: why a crimp proof-pull must come before insertion (all ideas)
==============================================================================
     5.0 N  latch test pull-back (i2, i3, i4)
    14.7 N  lance retention minimum, analog
    19.6 N  crimp proof pull, half of JST pull-out
    39.2 N  JST crimp pull-out minimum, 22 AWG
    85.0 N  22 AWG conductor break
-> a proof load big enough to mean something about the crimp (~20 N) exceeds what a
   latched contact holds (~15 N analog). After insertion only the latch can be tested;
   the crimp can be proof-loaded only between the crimp and the push.

8. How far a lance can bend and spring back (i2c)
==============================================================================
Cantilever tip deflection at first yield: delta = 2 sy L^2 / (3 E t) [beam theory].
  lance 1.0 mm long, 0.2 mm thick: elastic to 0.015-0.020 mm of tip travel
  lance 1.5 mm long, 0.2 mm thick: elastic to 0.034-0.044 mm of tip travel
  lance 2.0 mm long, 0.2 mm thick: elastic to 0.061-0.079 mm of tip travel
  lance 3.0 mm long, 0.2 mm thick: elastic to 0.136-0.177 mm of tip travel
  against 0.6-0.9 mm of stand-proud [source S19-S22].
-> folding the lance into a cavity is partly plastic by design [assumption: the lance
   is not much longer than the drawings suggest]. Every extra fold (stub latch, lift,
   re-latch in the product housing) is another plastic event of unknown cost to
   retention. A stub that never engages the lance removes those events.

9. Insertability is an insulation-die dimension (handover item 3)
==============================================================================
Closed insulation barrel on 1.7 mm silicone, modelled as an ellipse of inner width
w_i = channel - 2 x 0.2 holding the wire's section; e = share of silicone pushed out
along the wire. Cavity envelope 1.95 wide x 2.4 tall [mfr S1, contact end view].
  channel 1.80 wide       : e 0.0: 2.46 tall (over); e 0.1: 2.26 tall; e 0.2: 2.05 tall
  channel 1.85 wide       : e 0.0: 2.39 tall; e 0.1: 2.19 tall; e 0.2: 1.99 tall
  channel 1.90 wide       : e 0.0: 2.33 tall; e 0.1: 2.13 tall; e 0.2: 1.94 tall
  channel 1.95 wide       : e 0.0: 2.26 tall; e 0.1: 2.08 tall; e 0.2: 1.89 tall
  channel 2.00 wide (over): e 0.0: 2.21 tall; e 0.1: 2.03 tall; e 0.2: 1.84 tall
-> [estimate, crude model] 1.7 mm silicone nearly fills the cavity section; a narrower
   insulation crimp grows taller. Today's SN-2549 crimps evidently enter the housing,
   so their insulation width and height (caliper) are the target any machine die copies.

12. The saddle hump turns Z error into Y error (i2, i1b)
==============================================================================
Stored feed f = 8 h^2 / (3 c) for a shallow arch of chord c, sag h [into-the-housing §9].
df/dh = 16 h / (3 c): how far the tip moves in Y per mm the hump sits low or high.
  chord 10.0, feed 5.2: sag 4.42 mm, gain 2.36 -> 0.1 mm of sag error = 0.24 mm at the tip
  chord 10.0, feed 8.2: sag 5.55 mm, gain 2.96 -> 0.1 mm of sag error = 0.30 mm at the tip
  chord 15.0, feed 5.2: sag 5.41 mm, gain 1.92 -> 0.1 mm of sag error = 0.19 mm at the tip
  chord 15.0, feed 8.2: sag 6.79 mm, gain 2.41 -> 0.1 mm of sag error = 0.24 mm at the tip
  chord 20.0, feed 5.2: sag 6.24 mm, gain 1.67 -> 0.1 mm of sag error = 0.17 mm at the tip
  chord 20.0, feed 8.2: sag 7.84 mm, gain 2.09 -> 0.1 mm of sag error = 0.21 mm at the tip
-> the tip's Y is ~1.5-3x as sensitive to how the conductor sits on its saddle; the
   +/-0.2-0.3 mm axial window is gone at 0.1 mm of seating error. The same gain is a
   lever: a Z presser on the hump moves the tip in Y by twice its own travel, which
   gives a camera-measured axial correction without a Y axis on the conductor.
