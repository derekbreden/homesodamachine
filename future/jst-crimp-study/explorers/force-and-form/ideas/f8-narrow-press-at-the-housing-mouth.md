## Picture it

- **The press.** f4's fist-sized steel C stands fixed on the bench with its
  throat facing a housing carriage. Its knee drives a **narrow stepped
  crimper**: a conductor step 2.9 mm wide (a 1.5 mm channel with 0.7 mm walls)
  and an insulation step 2.5–2.7 mm wide. Below, an **anvil block** rises on
  its own small knee or wedge. Its top is level with the housing's cavity floor;
  its front end is cut back behind the contact's lance; and it has two
  **shoulders** below the floor on which the crimper's walls land at the bottom
  of the stroke. A button load cell sits under the anvil block, and a 0.001 mm
  indicator reads across crimper and anvil.
- **The housing.** An XHP sits in a nest on an X carriage, rear face toward the
  press, 0.2–0.3 mm in front of the anvil. The nest is located only along the
  wire (Y) against a steel stop plate, and floats in X and Z on light springs:
  during the crimp the steel die is the master and the housing follows it.
  A printed comb 3–5 mm behind the rear face, outside the crimper's reach in Y,
  centres the seated neighbours' wires.
- **The contact.** A strip runs over a crowned block beside the nest, so the
  lead contact stands up level with the cavity floor and its neighbours on the
  strip drop 3.6–4 mm, clear of the housing (crown radius 6–12.5 mm, the carrier
  taking a one-way curl) [calc: on_into_the_housing §10]. A pusher slides the
  lead contact box-first into cavity n's rear entry, 1.4–2.4 mm deep. The box's
  walls in the cavity hold its roll and height; the carrier, still attached,
  holds it along the wire.
- **The conductor.** The ribbon's web is clamped on the same carriage; each
  conductor lies over a printed saddle whose hump stores the ~5.2 mm it will
  need to follow the contact home (into-the-housing's feed-length rule). A
  presser lays conductor n into the open barrels. The camera sees the
  insulation edge in the window; the presser's height on the hump corrects the
  tip's axial position, at 1.7–3 times its own travel [calc:
  on_into_the_housing §12].
- **Crimp.** The anvil rises to the floor. The knee straightens until the
  crimper's walls land on the anvil block's shoulders: that landing is the
  crimp height. The steel between the barrels' die and the housing's PA6 face
  is 0.2–0.3 mm of air. Force against true die gap is logged, then re-touched
  at ~10 N for height.
- **Cut, drop, push.** A drop-shear under the carrier parts the tab. The anvil
  drops 1.5 mm, so the lance, 0.6–0.9 mm below the floor, clears it by 0.6 mm
  or more [calc: on_into_the_housing §11]. A slotted stencil-steel blade (or
  into-the-housing's silicone grip within ~2 mm of the barrels) pushes the
  contact home while the load cell in its drive logs lance fold, snap and
  bottoming, then a 5 N pull-back.
- **Step.** The carriage moves 2.5 mm to the next cavity, or 5.0 mm past J2's
  empty cavity 3.
- **What locates what.** Fixed along the wire (Y) is the nest's steel stop
  plate; across it (X, Z) the steel die is the master and the floating housing
  follows. The cavity's walls hold the box's roll and height; the carrier holds
  it along the wire until the drop-shear; the saddle and presser place the
  conductor, corrected by the camera.
- **What carries the force.** The knee inside the fixed C, into the crimper,
  the crimp, the anvil block's shoulders and the load cell. The housing and
  its nest carry none of it: the die stops 0.2–0.3 mm short of the PA6 face.
- **How it knows.** The camera frame before the stroke; the force curve and the
  re-touch height; the push's trace (lance fold, snap, bottoming) and the 5 N
  pull-back; then a real XH wafer. No proof pull before insertion (below).
- **The person** splits and strips the ribbon, lays its conductors over the
  saddles in cavity order (making J4's and J7's crossings there), threads the
  strip, and loads and unloads housings.

## What each side brings

- **into-the-housing:** a molded, $0.05 locator for every contact; no
  transfer between crimp and insertion; the feed-length rule and saddles;
  latch sensing on the push.
- **force-and-form:** a geometric bottom in a short steel loop; per-crimp
  height by re-touch; the rule for how thin a crimper's walls may be; the lance
  relief; the drop-shear under the carrier.

## The steel, in 3.3 mm of room

- **What the neighbours leave.** A seated neighbour's 1.7 mm wire, 2.5 mm
  away, leaves 3.30 mm of free width at its equator (about 1.05 mm above the
  floor), 3.35 mm at the conductor crimp's roof, 3.56 mm or more below 0.6 mm,
  and 5 mm below 0.2 mm [calc: on_into_the_housing §3]. The seated wire also
  has 0.07–0.25 mm of play in the cavity's rear opening (an assumed 1.95–2.10 mm
  opening, unmeasured) [RP w3 §8].
- **What the walls need.** Confined compaction pushes the conductor crimper's
  walls apart at k × 800–1,160 MPa. Walls of 0.3–0.4 mm reach 2,200–9,450 MPa
  and crack; 0.7–0.85 mm walls reach 490–1,740 MPa at k = 0.3–0.5, inside
  hardened D2 [calc: on_into_the_housing §3].
- **So:** 0.8 mm walls (a 3.1 mm step) leave 0.05–0.15 mm a side at the
  equator and, less the wire's play, −0.10 to −0.02 mm: they rub each seated
  neighbour's jacket. 0.7 mm walls (a 2.9 mm step) leave 0.15–0.25 mm a side and
  0.00 to +0.08 mm net, at 720–1,740 MPa of root stress for k = 0.3–0.5 before
  the shoulders brace the walls' lower ends [calc final §5]. The insulation
  crimper, at 30–130 N, can have 0.4 mm walls.
- **Bottoming braces the walls.** Landing the walls on shoulders below the
  floor, where there is 5 mm of room, sets crimp height and pins each wall's
  lower end at the moment of peak side load, cutting its root moment by roughly
  2–4× [estimate]. The shoulders take any surplus force: ~1 kN on two 0.7 ×
  1.4 mm wall ends is ~510 MPa, 3 kN is ~1,530 MPa [calc: on_into_the_housing
  §3, scaled from 0.8 mm walls]. So the knee arrives at straight with the walls just touching, or the
  drive is spring-limited near 1 kN above the crimp.
- **Where it comes from.** Only wire EDM makes this profile
  ([`f7`](f7-where-the-steel-comes-from.md)); quick-turn at ±0.05 mm is
  coarse for 0.7 mm walls around a 1.5 mm channel, unless each crimper is measured and the anvil block ground to it.

## What was tried against it, and the repairs

1. **The lance sits where the anvil's front edge would be.** The lance tip is
   0.24–0.64 mm behind the box's rear end, under the transition. A flat anvil
   level with the floor would carry the contact on its lance and the stroke
   would flatten it.
   - *Repair A:* the anvil's front stops behind the lance tip. The conductor
     barrel still needs steel under it, so the transition t (box rear to
     conductor barrel front) must be at least 0.34–0.74 mm; with t ≥ ~0.8 mm
     there is a window for box depths of 1.4–2.4 mm, and below ~0.7 mm there is
     none [calc: on_into_the_housing §1d–e].
   - *Repair B:* push the box deeper until the entry folds the lance. The
     folded lance springs the box against the cavity roof, which also grips it.
     It costs box depth and brings the conductor barrel closer to the PA6 face.
   - *Left:* t, the lance root, and the entry chamfer. One kit contact
     photographed side-on, and one pushed into a kit housing to depth and
     photographed, settle them.
2. **Two locators for one contact.** The PA6 cavity holds the box; the steel
   die centres the barrels. The offset between them is ±0.14 mm RSS,
   ±0.22 mm worst case, and whatever the box's play does not absorb goes into
   an S-bend of the transition [calc: on_into_the_housing §2].
   - *Repair A (in the picture):* steel is the master in X and Z during the
     crimp. The nest floats; a shallow radius on the anvil top centres the
     contact under the presser's load, and the box drags the housing onto the
     die axis. The seated neighbours' wires accept 0.1–0.2 mm over 10–30 mm of
     free length. With the crimper's walls leaving at most 0.08 mm net beside a
     seated wire, this is not optional: the ±0.14 mm RSS offset has nowhere
     else to go.
   - *Repair B:* the camera measures each cavity's X on the rear face at load,
     and the carriage steps to it with the nest rigid.
3. **Crimp height through a screw.** A screw-driven punch that "stops at a set
   bottom" follows step loss and frame stretch. The knee in a short steel loop,
   bottoming on shoulders, replaces it.
4. **Crimping 0.2–0.3 mm from a PA6 face.** The die never touches the housing:
   the anvil's front is behind the lance and the crimper's front face stops at
   the conductor barrel's front. What is uncertain is whether the curling wings
   or the bellmouth push the transition toward the face.
5. **No per-crimp proof pull.** Before the push, the contact is held along the
   wire only by its carrier; after the push, the latch holds 14.7 N (a Molex
   analog) to at least 19.6 N (the KONNRA XH clone spec), so a ~20 N proof load
   sits at or past what the latch holds [calc final §9]. The box's rear face is
   at the housing's rear face, so no blade reaches it either.
   A neck blade between the rear face and the conductor barrel needs t ≥ ~0.8 mm
   plus 0.3 mm. Until t is known, f8 proves crimps by re-touch height, the
   force curve, and sample pulls to failure (machine-that-sees-and-learns v3).

## Contribution

- **The contact is located by the part it will live in**, and the crimp needs
  no transfer before insertion.
- **Narrow dies that hold**, because their walls are 0.7 mm and land on
  shoulders below the floor, where the neighbours are not.
- **A combination that stands or falls on one photograph**: t and the lance
  root.

## Major unresolved problems

- **t and the lance**, as above: the decisive unknown.
- **EDM of a 2.9 mm stepped crimper** with 0.7 mm walls and a
  shoulder-bottoming anvil block, and its price.
- **The cavity's real rear opening**, which sets the seated wires' play.
- **X registration** of a floating nest against a shaped anvil.
- **The strip over a crown**: feeding on an arc, and whether the lead contact
  can be pushed box-first into a cavity while its carrier is attached.
- **Proof loading** the crimp before the push.
- **Two-ribbon housings** (J1, J4, J7): the second ribbon's conductors pass
  the first ribbon's seated wires on their saddles.

## Which conclusions rest on assumptions

- **Wall stresses** rest on k = 0.3–1.0, the share of the vertical die pressure
  that confined compaction puts on the walls.
- **Neighbour wire height** (centre ~1.05 mm above the floor) is an estimate.
- **Box depths and t** come from clone drawings and a length budget, not from a
  measured contact.
