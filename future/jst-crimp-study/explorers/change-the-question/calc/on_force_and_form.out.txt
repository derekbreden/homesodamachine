
==============================================================================
1. Threading a 1.7 mm silicone conductor through a captured insulation barrel (f1, f3, f4)
==============================================================================
At capture the insulation wings sit inside the insulation crimper's channel, whose width
is ~1.8-2.0 mm [mfr analog SXA-01T, f&f f3]. Inner clear width = channel - 2 x stock.
Silicone must squeeze through it. Friction estimate: per-side normal force =
E_si x (radial strain) x (barrel length x ~1.0 mm contact height); push = mu x 2 x N.
E_si 2-5 MPa [estimate, Shore ~40-60A silicone]; mu 0.5-1.0 [estimate, silicone on tin];
insulation barrel length 0.8-1.5 mm [estimate, xh-facts §1].
  channel 1.8 -> clear 1.40 mm; OD 1.6: interference 0.20 mm, wall strain  20%, push ~ 0.3- 3.1 N
  channel 1.8 -> clear 1.40 mm; OD 1.7: interference 0.30 mm, wall strain  31%, push ~ 0.5- 4.6 N
  channel 1.8 -> clear 1.40 mm; OD 1.8: interference 0.40 mm, wall strain  41%, push ~ 0.7- 6.1 N
  channel 1.9 -> clear 1.50 mm; OD 1.6: interference 0.10 mm, wall strain  10%, push ~ 0.2- 1.5 N
  channel 1.9 -> clear 1.50 mm; OD 1.7: interference 0.20 mm, wall strain  20%, push ~ 0.3- 3.1 N
  channel 1.9 -> clear 1.50 mm; OD 1.8: interference 0.30 mm, wall strain  31%, push ~ 0.5- 4.6 N
  channel 2.0 -> clear 1.60 mm; OD 1.6: interference 0.00 mm, wall strain   0%, push ~ 0.0- 0.0 N
  channel 2.0 -> clear 1.60 mm; OD 1.7: interference 0.10 mm, wall strain  10%, push ~ 0.2- 1.5 N
  channel 2.0 -> clear 1.60 mm; OD 1.8: interference 0.20 mm, wall strain  20%, push ~ 0.3- 3.1 N
Buckling of the conductor under that push (strand-sum EI 14.1 N*mm^2 + silicone), pinned-pinned:
  2 mm 37-41 N, 5 mm 5.9-6.5 N, 10 mm 1.5-1.6 N [source: sibling calc ribbon-as-pallet
  pallet_geometry §4 and into-the-housing insertion_geometry §1]
-> worst case (1.8 mm conductor into a 1.4 mm clearance) is ~6 N, the buckling load of a
   5 mm free length. A guide within ~3 mm of the barrel mouth carries it with ~2x margin.
   The repo's hand procedure already threads into a one-click-captured SN-2549 contact
   [repo cable-assemblies.md], so some capture height exists where this works by hand.
   The number to take from the bench is that capture gap (feeler or pin gauge at click 1).

2. f3: where the lead contact's pilot hole sits relative to the threading path
==============================================================================
  tab 0.70 mm, carrier 3.0 mm: pilot-hole centre 2.20 mm behind the insulation barrel, on the contact centreline; hole spans +/-0.75 mm across it
  tab 1.15 mm, carrier 3.0 mm: pilot-hole centre 2.65 mm behind the insulation barrel, on the contact centreline; hole spans +/-0.75 mm across it
  the insulated conductor (1.7 mm OD) slides on the carrier's top face, spanning +/-0.85 mm
-> any pin standing above the carrier top in the lead contact's own hole is in the conductor's
   path. Riding over a 0.3-0.5 mm pin tilts the conductor up by:
     pin 0.3 mm proud at 2.20 mm behind the barrel:  7.8 deg
     pin 0.3 mm proud at 2.65 mm behind the barrel:  6.5 deg
     pin 0.5 mm proud at 2.20 mm behind the barrel: 12.8 deg
     pin 0.5 mm proud at 2.65 mm behind the barrel: 10.7 deg

3. f4: can the head leave a crimped contact, and can it pick one off the strip?
==============================================================================
  crimped contact end view 1.95 x 2.4 mm, diagonal 3.09 mm; funnel exit 2.0 mm
  -> a closed funnel round the conductor cannot pass back over the crimped contact, and a
     downward move would take the funnel through the wire.
  crimp height 0.73: opened crimper roof 5.73 mm above the floor; the crimper is above the
     contact, so a downward head move closes on it after 3.33 mm and never frees it.
     An axial retreat (dies slide off past the box) needs an opening of only 1.97 mm.
  crimp height 0.88: opened crimper roof 5.88 mm above the floor; the crimper is above the
     contact, so a downward head move closes on it after 3.48 mm and never frees it.
     An axial retreat (dies slide off past the box) needs an opening of only 1.82 mm.
  At the pick, the carrier (3.0 mm wide, 0.7-1.15 mm tab) lies directly behind the insulation
  barrel, where the head's rear funnel has to be. A funnel fixed to the head collides with it.

4. f5 x c1: a half-row cassette at 5.0 mm (twice the housing pitch)
==============================================================================
Half-rows (c1): odd cavities in one pass, even in the other. Each row's conductors lie at
3.4 mm in their own plane (2 x ribbon pitch) and are spread to 5.0 mm (2 x housing pitch).
A straight-descending V-tooth comb puts a conductor in the right slot if it moves < 2.5 mm
(half the slot pitch); +/-0.2 mm lay uncertainty [estimate] is taken off the margin.
  row of 2 cavities: outer conductor moves 0.8 mm, comb margin +1.5 mm; gang force kN: low  1.6  central  3.4  high  4.9; 1 t arbor press: covers
  row of 3 cavities: outer conductor moves 1.6 mm, comb margin +0.7 mm; gang force kN: low  2.3  central  5.0  high  7.3; 1 t arbor press: covers
  row of 4 cavities: outer conductor moves 2.4 mm, comb margin -0.1 mm; gang force kN: low  3.1  central  6.7  high  9.7; 1 t arbor press: central only
  row of 5 cavities: outer conductor moves 3.2 mm, comb margin -0.9 mm; gang force kN: low  3.9  central  8.4  high 12.2; 1 t arbor press: central only
  crimper outer width 3.5 mm [f&f calc: gang §1] at 5.0 mm pitch: 1.5 mm of steel between stations
  crimper outer width 4.4 mm [f&f calc: gang §1] at 5.0 mm pitch: 0.6 mm of steel between stations
  per unit: 20 row strokes, 53 crimps; in rows of <=3 cavities 36 (68%), rows of 4 12 (J1 B, J4 A, J7 A), row of 5 5 (J1 A)
  -> a straight comb lays every T4, J2 and J6 row and the 3-rows of J4 and J7; the 4-rows
     have ~0 margin and J1's 5-row fails, so those want a tilted or rolling comb.
  Compare f5 at strip pitch: a 5P fans 30-38 mm, outer conductors move 12-15 mm [f&f calc: gang §1].

6. Reference crimp (c2): correcting a genuine JST lead's crimp height to the ribbon's copper
==============================================================================
  ribbon conductor: 60 x 0.08 mm = 0.302 mm^2 [source S29]
  consistency: wings 0.82 + copper 0.30 mm^2 in a 1.5 mm wide B at fill 0.80 -> H = 0.93 mm (the C1 estimate is 0.88)
  consistency: wings 0.82 + copper 0.30 mm^2 in a 1.5 mm wide B at fill 0.90 -> H = 0.83 mm (the C1 estimate is 0.88)
  reference lead 22 AWG nominal           0.324 mm^2: same profile and compaction crimps the ribbon 0.017-0.019 mm lower
  reference lead 17/0.16 (JIS-style 22)   0.342 mm^2: same profile and compaction crimps the ribbon 0.030-0.034 mm lower
  reference lead 7/30 AWG (7 x 0.254)     0.355 mm^2: same profile and compaction crimps the ribbon 0.039-0.044 mm lower
  reference lead 19/34 AWG (19 x 0.160)   0.382 mm^2: same profile and compaction crimps the ribbon 0.060-0.067 mm lower
-> H_ribbon ~= H_ref - (A_ref - 0.302) / (1.5 x 0.8-0.9). Count the reference lead's strands
   under the ELP camera and caliper one strand to get A_ref. The correction is 0.02-0.07 mm,
   the same size as JST's +/-0.05 mm tolerance, so it cannot be ignored.

7. How often each arrangement calls the person back, per unit
==============================================================================
  f1 as written (contact dropped per crimp)             67 calls   (every 1-2 min through the run [f&f f3 cycle estimate])
  f1 + keyed stick magazine loaded at leisure           15 calls   (53 contacts still handled, in one sitting)
  f1 + strip dispenser and shear over the flap          14 calls   (reel every ~150 units)
  f1 fed tacked ribbon ends from a c1b tack station     34 calls   (pallets loaded per half-row at the tack station)
  f2, f3, f4 as written (strip fed)                     14 calls   (plus a reel)
  f5 as written, shop press pumped by hand              28 calls   (load, then pump)
  f5 x c1 half-row cassette, loose contacts             30 calls   (housing and ribbon, then pockets per row)
-> loose-contact arrangements call the person once per row or per crimp; strip-fed ones once
   per ribbon end. The contact's form sets the call count more than any mechanism does.

  insulation crimp 2.00 wide x 1.80 tall, wire 1.6: side rim beyond a 1.65 mm slot +0.175 mm per side; crimp top vs wire top +0.00 mm
  insulation crimp 2.00 wide x 1.80 tall, wire 1.7: side rim beyond a 1.75 mm slot +0.125 mm per side; crimp top vs wire top -0.10 mm
  insulation crimp 2.00 wide x 1.80 tall, wire 1.8: side rim beyond a 1.85 mm slot +0.075 mm per side; crimp top vs wire top -0.20 mm
  floor's rear edge below the wire: 0.20 mm (and the tab stub, if cut)
  box rear face (2.2 tall, 1.9 wide) exposed above a 0.73 mm conductor crimp: 1.47 mm; above the 1.80 mm insulation crimp: 0.40 mm
  box rear face (2.4 tall, 1.9 wide) exposed above a 0.73 mm conductor crimp: 1.67 mm; above the 1.80 mm insulation crimp: 0.60 mm

   1.8 mm wire in a 1.8 mm crimp, plus 0.2 mm of floor edge. A fork there bears mostly on

   offers 1.3-1.7 x 1.9 mm of steel-to-bronze
   bearing for a proof pull; its top 0.4-0.6 mm clears the insulation crimp for a pusher that
