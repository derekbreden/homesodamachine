2. Locating a contact by its carrier strip
============================================================================
Pilot hole on the contact centreline, one per contact [source S19; mfr-an Wurth].
  hole 1.50 (+/-0.02 stamped [estimate]) with pin 1.45: float +0.015 to +0.035 mm radial
  hole 1.50 (+/-0.02 stamped [estimate]) with pin 1.48: float +0.000 to +0.020 mm radial
  a tapered pin driven home takes the float out entirely: it centres, then the hold-down clamps
hole-to-contact-centreline within one reel: +/-0.02-0.05 mm [estimate, progressive-die stamping]
between reels / brands: tab length 0.8+/-0.2 (HDGC), 1.00+/-0.15 (DLL) [source] ->
  axial barrel position relative to the carrier edge can differ ~0.2-0.4 mm between lots:
  re-teach the axial offset once per reel (camera finds the barrel's rear edge vs the hole)
What the die needs [xh-facts; MKS-L manual 6-10]:
  bellmouth ~1-2 x stock = 0.20-0.40 mm -> axial placement window ~+/-0.1 mm
  lateral: the punch lead-in centres an open barrel offset by ~0.1 mm; 'twisting' and 'rolling'
  come from a contact not square on the anvil [MKS-L 7-8, 7-9] -> hold the carrier flat
Indexing error does not accumulate if the station locates on holes each cycle:
  sprocket counting   1 pitches: +/-0.02 mm (rss of 0.02/pitch) ; pin at station: +/-0.03
  sprocket counting  10 pitches: +/-0.06 mm (rss of 0.02/pitch) ; pin at station: +/-0.03
  sprocket counting  53 pitches: +/-0.15 mm (rss of 0.02/pitch) ; pin at station: +/-0.03

6. Hanging rail: what a slot can tell apart
============================================================================
A contact hangs box-down from its insulation wings; everything else is below the rail
tolerances: box +/-0.10, open barrels +/-0.25 [source]
 slot 2.00: box W MAYBE (1.75-2.00 vs 2.00)    box H never passes                 cond barrel MAYBE (1.55-2.15 vs 2.00)    ins wings W never passes
 slot 2.05: box W always passes                box H never passes                 cond barrel MAYBE (1.55-2.15 vs 2.05)    ins wings W never passes
 slot 2.10: box W always passes                box H MAYBE (2.10-2.45 vs 2.10)    cond barrel MAYBE (1.55-2.15 vs 2.10)    ins wings W never passes
 slot 2.30: box W always passes                box H MAYBE (2.10-2.45 vs 2.30)    cond barrel always passes                ins wings W never passes
 slot 2.40: box W always passes                box H MAYBE (2.10-2.45 vs 2.40)    cond barrel always passes                ins wings W never passes
-> a single slot of ~2.4 lets the box and conductor barrel through and hangs on the wings,
   but lets the box hang either way round (box H also < 2.4): 4 roll states.
-> a stepped rail, 2.4 at the barrels and 2.05 at the box, accepts the box only W-across:
   2 roll states left (U open fore or aft along the rail); a contact that arrives box H-across
   rides ~2 mm high on the step and a height wiper knocks it back into the hopper.
   The last 2 states are told apart by camera (U opening seen from above) or by the lance side.

7. Holding a contact by mating it onto a 0.64 mm square post
============================================================================
  total normal force 1 N, friction 0.2: axial hold 0.2 N (474 x the contact's weight)
  total normal force 2 N, friction 0.3: axial hold 0.6 N (1,422 x the contact's weight)
  total normal force 4 N, friction 0.4: axial hold 1.6 N (3,793 x the contact's weight)
  loads it must resist: weight 0.0004 N; conductor sliding into an open U ~0.01-0.1 N [estimate];
  during the crimp the die clamps the barrels and the post only locates the box
  release: pull the crimped wire back, 0.2-2 N; a 22 AWG conductor breaks at ~85-100 N [xh-facts]
Post stiffness, square 0.64 mm, cantilever k = 3EI/L^3:
  brass L  3 mm:   155.34 N/mm
  brass L  6 mm:    19.42 N/mm
  brass L 10 mm:     4.19 N/mm
  brass L 17 mm:     0.85 N/mm
  steel L  3 mm:   310.69 N/mm
  steel L  6 mm:    38.84 N/mm
  steel L 10 mm:     8.39 N/mm
  steel L 17 mm:     1.71 N/mm
  -> a 3 mm post is stiff (locates); a 17 mm post through a housing is soft (the die and the
     cavity steer it; it only needs to carry the contact, not to fight the die)

8. Crimping at the housing's rear face next to a seated neighbour
============================================================================
 staged 1.0 mm deep: lance tip +1.44 mm outside the face; conductor barrel 1.6-3.0 mm behind the face; insulation barrel 3.5-5.0
 staged 1.5 mm deep: lance tip +0.94 mm outside the face; conductor barrel 1.1-2.5 mm behind the face; insulation barrel 3.0-4.5
 staged 2.0 mm deep: lance tip +0.44 mm outside the face; conductor barrel 0.6-2.0 mm behind the face; insulation barrel 2.5-4.0
Neighbour's wire (1.7 OD) leaves the face at 2.5 mm pitch; the punch straddles the barrel.
  conductor punch 2.7 wide: neighbour must move 0.00 mm sideways where the punch is
      at 0.5 mm behind the face -> neighbour bent  0.0 deg from the face
      at 1.0 mm behind the face -> neighbour bent  0.0 deg from the face
      at 2.0 mm behind the face -> neighbour bent  0.0 deg from the face
      at 3.0 mm behind the face -> neighbour bent  0.0 deg from the face
  conductor punch 3.5 wide: neighbour must move 0.30 mm sideways where the punch is
      at 0.5 mm behind the face -> neighbour bent 31.0 deg from the face
      at 1.0 mm behind the face -> neighbour bent 16.7 deg from the face
      at 2.0 mm behind the face -> neighbour bent  8.5 deg from the face
      at 3.0 mm behind the face -> neighbour bent  5.7 deg from the face
  insulation punch 3.5 wide: neighbour must move 0.30 mm sideways where the punch is
      at 0.5 mm behind the face -> neighbour bent 31.0 deg from the face
      at 1.0 mm behind the face -> neighbour bent 16.7 deg from the face
      at 2.0 mm behind the face -> neighbour bent  8.5 deg from the face
      at 3.0 mm behind the face -> neighbour bent  5.7 deg from the face
  insulation punch 4.0 wide: neighbour must move 0.55 mm sideways where the punch is
      at 0.5 mm behind the face -> neighbour bent 47.7 deg from the face
      at 1.0 mm behind the face -> neighbour bent 28.8 deg from the face
      at 2.0 mm behind the face -> neighbour bent 15.4 deg from the face
      at 3.0 mm behind the face -> neighbour bent 10.4 deg from the face
Why not thin die walls instead: a punch wall 0.5 mm thick x 1.5 mm long carrying a side load
  side load 100 N at 1.5 mm: bending stress ~2,400 MPa (tool steel ~2,000-2,500 yield)
  side load 300 N at 1.5 mm: bending stress ~7,200 MPa (tool steel ~2,000-2,500 yield)
-> bending the seated neighbour ~20-30 deg at the face clears the dies from ~1 mm back;
   staging 1.5 mm deep puts the conductor barrel 1.1-2.5 mm back, the insulation 3.0-4.5.

9. Separating a crimped contact from its carrier
============================================================================
shear the tab: 48-158 N at the edge [xh-facts calc C1]
  flush cutter with jaw leverage 4: 12-40 N at the handle
  flush cutter with jaw leverage 6: 8-26 N at the handle
  servo 0.9 N*m on a 20 mm arm: 45 N
  servo 1.1 N*m on a 25 mm arm: 44 N
  bend the tab around R 0.2 mm: strain amplitude 0.33; eps_f' 0.3: ~0.5 full cycles to break [estimate]
  bend the tab around R 0.2 mm: strain amplitude 0.33; eps_f' 0.6: ~1.3 full cycles to break [estimate]
  bend the tab around R 0.4 mm: strain amplitude 0.20; eps_f' 0.3: ~1.0 full cycles to break [estimate]
  bend the tab around R 0.4 mm: strain amplitude 0.20; eps_f' 0.6: ~3.1 full cycles to break [estimate]
  -> a sharp +/-90 deg bend breaks the tab in 1-5 cycles; the break lands at the neck, not flush
