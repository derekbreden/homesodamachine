
==============================================================================
1. Skip-pitch strip: where a ribbon's other conductors may lie in the strip's plane
==============================================================================
Fresh contacts stand upstream of the station every s mm (s = 7.1 normal; 14.2 with every
other contact removed; 21.3 with two of three removed). A waiting conductor (OD 1.7) lying
in the plane collides if it lands within 2.35 mm of a fresh contact's centre
[critique calc 2]; a done conductor carrying its crimped contact within 2.50 mm.
Worst case: all N-1 other conductors on the upstream side, at fan pitch p, at -p ... -(N-1)p.

  fresh contacts every 7.1 mm
    3P (N-1=2 upstream): clear for fan pitch 1.70-2.30 mm (1.70-8.00 scanned)
    4P (N-1=3 upstream): clear for fan pitch none mm (1.70-8.00 scanned)
    5P (N-1=4 upstream): clear for fan pitch none mm (1.70-8.00 scanned)

  fresh contacts every 14.2 mm
    3P (N-1=2 upstream): clear for fan pitch 1.70-5.85 mm (1.70-8.00 scanned)
    4P (N-1=3 upstream): clear for fan pitch 1.70-3.90, 5.60-5.85 mm (1.70-8.00 scanned)
    5P (N-1=4 upstream): clear for fan pitch 1.70-2.90, 5.60-5.85 mm (1.70-8.00 scanned)

  fresh contacts every 21.3 mm
    3P (N-1=2 upstream): clear for fan pitch 1.70-8.00 mm (1.70-8.00 scanned)
    4P (N-1=3 upstream): clear for fan pitch 1.70-6.25, 7.95-8.00 mm (1.70-8.00 scanned)
    5P (N-1=4 upstream): clear for fan pitch 1.70-4.70, 6.00-6.25, 7.95-8.00 mm (1.70-8.00 scanned)

  -> with every contact on the strip (7.1 mm) no 4P or 5P fan is clear [agrees with critique];
     with every other contact removed (14.2 mm) any fan up to ~2.9 mm is clear for 5P, ~3.9 for 4P.
     The punch holder still needs the nearest neighbour outside its footprint (section 2 note).

2. Crowned anvil: fresh contacts fall below the plane the conductors lie in
==============================================================================
The strip runs over a crown (a cylinder whose axis is along the contacts). The station
contact sits on a flat land at the crest; a contact at arc distance s lies rotated by s/R
and its open wing tips (height h above its floor) sit at (R+h)cos(s/R) - R relative to the crest.
Carrier bending strain = t/(2R); yield strain = Sy/E = 0.41-0.59 %.
  smallest elastic crown radius: 16.9-24.4 mm

    R mm  strain %  elastic? | wing tip vs crest (mm), h=3.2:  s=7.1   s=14.2   s=21.3
       8      1.25        no |                                    -0.93   below   below
      10      1.00        no |                                    +0.01   -8.02   below
      12      0.83        no |                                    +0.62   -6.26   below
      15      0.67        no |                                    +1.20   -4.36  -12.27
      20      0.50      edge |                                    +1.75   -2.41   -8.76
      25      0.40       yes |                                    +2.07   -1.23   -6.43
      30      0.33       yes |                                    +2.27   -0.45   -4.82
      40      0.25       yes |                                    +2.52   +0.51   -2.78

  -> an elastic crown (R >= ~25 mm) drops the 14.2 mm neighbour's wing tips 1.2 mm below the
     crest plane: every other conductor can lie flat in the station plane at any fan pitch.
     With every contact on the strip, the 7.1 mm neighbour clears only at R <= ~8 mm, which
     bends the carrier plastically at every contact that passes.
  R 25: the crown surface at x = 2.5 mm lies 0.12 mm below the crest plane
  R 25: the crown surface at x = 3.0 mm lies 0.18 mm below the crest plane
  R 25: the crown surface at x = 4.0 mm lies 0.32 mm below the crest plane
  R 25: the crown surface at x = 5.0 mm lies 0.50 mm below the crest plane
  R 25: the crown surface at x = 7.1 mm lies 1.00 mm below the crest plane
  -> pins in the removed contacts' holes at +/-7.1 mm sit ~1.0 mm below the plane: a flush or
     recessed pin tip never touches a conductor lying over it.

3. The gap a skip-pitch strip opens beside the station
==============================================================================
  neighbour at  7.1 mm: clear gap between open wings  4.1 mm
     neighbour taller by 0.00 mm -> grazing view needs >= 0.16 deg; near wing tip hidden by   5 um
     neighbour taller by 0.10 mm -> grazing view needs >= 0.97 deg; near wing tip hidden by  32 um
     neighbour taller by 0.15 mm -> grazing view needs >= 1.37 deg; near wing tip hidden by  45 um
     neighbour taller by 0.25 mm -> grazing view needs >= 2.18 deg; near wing tip hidden by  72 um
  neighbour at 14.2 mm: clear gap between open wings 11.2 mm
     neighbour taller by 0.00 mm -> grazing view needs >= 0.08 deg; near wing tip hidden by   3 um
     neighbour taller by 0.10 mm -> grazing view needs >= 0.48 deg; near wing tip hidden by  16 um
     neighbour taller by 0.15 mm -> grazing view needs >= 0.69 deg; near wing tip hidden by  23 um
     neighbour taller by 0.25 mm -> grazing view needs >= 1.09 deg; near wing tip hidden by  36 um
  -> at 14.2 mm the grazing view needs half the tilt, and an 11 mm gap takes a backlight tile
     (a 3-5 mm wide diffuser with one white LED) dropped in from above between look and stroke.
  -> on a crown (section 2) the neighbour lies below the station plane, so the view along the
     strip at wing height is unobstructed and needs no tilt at all.

4. The set a lifted conductor keeps (the lifter repair)
==============================================================================
A conductor clamped at the web and lifted delta at distance a (a bar or comb) bends most at the
clamp. Strands slide on one another, so each 0.08 mm strand bends about its own axis.
  strand yield curvature 0.0150 /mm (radius 67 mm) [calc, agrees with digest ~67 mm]
  EI strands 14.1 N mm^2, silicone ~1.6 N mm^2

   a mm  lift mm  push N  max curv /mm  x yield  set kept mm
     10      3.5    0.06        0.1672     11.2         2.18
     15      3.5    0.03        0.0775      5.2         1.23
     20      3.5    0.02        0.0330      2.2         0.31
     25      3.5    0.01        0.0169      1.1         0.00
     30      3.5    0.01        0.0117      0.8         0.00
  -> lifted 3.5 mm at 10-15 mm from the web, a conductor keeps ~1.3-2.2 mm of lift when
     released; at 25-30 mm it keeps only a few tenths. The set is harmless during crimping
     and is undone by whatever combs the conductors to 2.5 mm for insertion.

5. The post as a gripper (a6)
==============================================================================
  weight 0.42 mN; post grip 0.2-2.0 N [estimate]: 474-4741 x weight
  vacuum nozzle 1.0 mm at 80 kPa: 63 mN normal [critique calc 7]: a nozzle
  places into an open pocket; it cannot push a box onto a post. A post pushed into a box
  whose rear rests on a pocket wall needs only that wall to react 0.2-2 N.

  Capture of a chamfered post tip by the box mouth [source S19-S21, clone box]:
    box inside ~1.45 x 1.80 mm; post 0.64 mm square
    lateral capture +/-0.41 mm, vertical +/-0.58 mm before the
    spring leaves' own lead-in centre the post; the pocket holds the box to ~+/-0.1 mm.

  Post stiffness, square 0.64 mm, k = 3EI/L^3:
    brass free length 2 mm:    524 N/mm
    brass free length 3 mm:    155 N/mm
    brass free length 4 mm:     66 N/mm
    steel free length 2 mm:   1049 N/mm
    steel free length 3 mm:    311 N/mm
    steel free length 4 mm:    131 N/mm
  -> a 2-4 mm post is rigid against every placement load; it must float in its holder so
     the punch's lead-in can centre the barrels (as a4). Float +/-0.2 mm on springs of
     1-2 N/mm resists with 0.2-0.4 N, against punch lead-in forces of tens of N.

  Axial chain, box front (the post holder face) to the conductor barrel's rear edge:
    clone drawings: box 2.0 + transition 0.4-0.6 + conductor barrel 1.25-1.5 -> 3.65-4.1 mm
    within one lot +/-0.05 mm [estimate, progressive die]; between brands up to +/-0.25 [source]
    bellmouth window +/-0.1 mm [xh-facts]: dead reckoning holds within a lot, not across lots.
    backlit silhouette on the post at 45 px/mm: 0.1 mm = 4.5 px; an edge fit to 0.2 px = 4.4 um
    backlit silhouette on the post at 86 px/mm: 0.1 mm = 8.6 px; an edge fit to 0.2 px = 2.3 um
  -> the camera measures each contact on the post; the post carriage moves by the error.
     Carriage resolution (NEMA 17 on Tr8x2, 1/16 step) 0.6 um; repeatability of a printer-class
     axis +/-0.02-0.05 mm, inside the window, and the look is repeated after the move.

6. Cutting the tab before the wire exists (strip on the post)
==============================================================================
  With the post in the leading contact's box and pins in the carrier, nothing lies over the tab:
  a flush punch from above, or a drop-shear, cuts it. Shear force 48-158 N [xh-facts C1].
    metal-gear servo 45 N at a 20-25 mm arm x 3:1 lever = 135 N
    metal-gear servo 45 N at a 20-25 mm arm x 4:1 lever = 180 N
  Stub target ~1 stock thickness (0.2-0.3 mm) [xh-facts S5, S23]. The shear line is set from
  the contact's own rear edge as seen (per contact), not from the carrier, so tab variation
  between brands (0.8 +/- 0.2 vs 1.00 +/- 0.15) drops out.

9. Where the drop-shear's kink goes after the next index (a2 Break 3 repair)
==============================================================================
  Upstream carrier clamped flat to an edge at x_c (between the upstream contact's tab and the
  station tab); everything downstream of x_c drops 0.3-0.5 mm; the carrier yields at x_c
  [critique calc 6: yields at 0.16 mm of drop, 0.14-0.34 mm set, 1.7-4.2 deg].
    index  7.1 mm, clamp edge at -2.0 mm: the kink moves to +5.1 mm, downstream of the new station tab (0): scrap side
    index  7.1 mm, clamp edge at -3.0 mm: the kink moves to +4.1 mm, downstream of the new station tab (0): scrap side
    index 14.2 mm, clamp edge at -2.0 mm: the kink moves to +12.2 mm, downstream of the new station tab (0): scrap side
    index 14.2 mm, clamp edge at -3.0 mm: the kink moves to +11.2 mm, downstream of the new station tab (0): scrap side
  -> the next contact's own tab and the carrier upstream of it are never bent.
