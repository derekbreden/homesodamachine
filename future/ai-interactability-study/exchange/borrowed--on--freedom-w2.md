How I read freedom. Every idea in `freedom/` says which motion is free, which force pushes it and who is master of each axis. My question is the one my framing asks of any of them: **which mass-produced product already does this piece, what is taken from it, what changes when it is taken, and how fast is it in hand?** Most of freedom's parts have such a product: a monitor arm is a shipped balanced arm, a plumb-bob gun is a camera stabiliser, six lines from above are a cable-driven robot, a fluid damper is a video head. ## 1. freedom-02, the balanced arm (deep): the arm forgives about 90 grams, and everything on the tip is payload

**The difficulty, in the drawn variant.** The scene's arm is a single boom with a spring set for a design payload and joint friction of 0.4 N·m over a 0.45 m reach **[freedom]**. Its default is 1.5 kg on a 2.0 kg setting, where the net torque is 2.2 N·m against 0.4 N·m of friction and the arm rises to its stop; the repair is 0.5 kg of ballast. Three numbers sit under that.

1. **The forgiveness is friction divided by (g × reach).** The arm stays put while |spring − payload| g L cos θ ≤ friction, so the mass mismatch it holds is 0.4 / (9.81 × 0.45 × cos 5°) = **±0.091 kg** at the drawn friction and reach: 6 % of 1.5 kg. At a reach of 0.8 m it is ±0.051 kg. With freedom's brake (×20, 8 N·m) it is ±1.8 kg, and the brake widens the window without stiffening the tip **[freedom, entry 5]**; at 2 N·m it is ±0.45 kg. This is the number the window plot is drawn from, but the scene never states it in kilograms, and in kilograms it is a measurement Derek can act on: a kitchen scale and 25 g fishing weights.
2. **The payload is everything on the tip.** freedom-02 counts gun, shell and 25 % of a 0.35 kg fibre share. The idea also puts an XZ vernier stage on the tip (two mini linear rails with motors, sourcing/freedom.md) and a camera on the shell. Take 0.6 kg for the two rails, 0.15 kg for the camera (both illustrative; neither listing states a weight): the payload is 2.3 kg, not 1.5, and the ballast the repair adds is already there. The floor of the monitor arms (2 kg **[freedom]**) is met by the parts; what is not met is the ±0.09 kg.
3. **What changes the payload while the arm is used.** The fibre's share: a quarter of 0.35 kg is 0.088 kg, all of the tolerance. The vernier's own motion: moving the 1.2 kg gun 10 mm along the arm changes the shoulder torque by 1.2 × 9.81 × 0.010 = 0.118 N·m, **29 % of the 0.4 N·m friction band**, before the cable has done anything. And heavier payload narrows a gas spring's window: with the drawn error slope of 0.15 per radian the matched window is −19° to 32° at 1.5 kg and **−10° to 21° at 2.3 kg**, because the error torque scales with the payload (`calc/w2-arm.cjs`, checked against freedom's `calc/20-balanced-arm.js`, same windows).

**The assumption behind it.** freedom's: the payload is the gun and its shell, and the arm's job is to be "roughly balanced". Mine, from the products: a monitor arm is designed for a payload that never changes (a monitor), set once with a hex key; the friction that lets it stay put is also the tolerance on that setting. Here the payload changes with the cable and the vernier, and the setting is being asked to be right to a tenth of a kilogram.

**Repair and branch: `borrowed-14-arm-mass-window`** (`branchOf` `freedom-02-balanced-arm`; Derek's balanced-arm example, treated through the bought range). The scene draws the arm with its spring, stacks the payload from named parts (gun, vernier, camera, fibre share, ballast) and reads the mismatch tolerance in grams at the set angle. Three springs:

- the **gas spring** of freedom-02, error slope as a slider;
- a **spring-and-parallelogram** (an Anglepoise lamp or a microphone boom) with a *plain* coil spring: torque = k a b cos θ (1 − l₀/l), so a free length l₀ leaves an error that varies with angle: at 40 mm it holds −20° to 29° when matched to 1.5 kg;
- the same with a **zero-length spring** (a close-wound spring with initial tension, or a spring routed over a pulley): torque = k a b cos θ, exactly the payload's cos θ, so for the matched payload the arm is balanced at every angle and the window is the whole −20° to 50°. A payload 0.1 kg too heavy moves the gas window to 7°–50°, the plain coil to −20°–2° and the zero-length spring to 25°–50°: the tolerance is the same in all three (friction), what moves is *where along the travel* it holds.

The strip in the scene is the range of bought arms, from Prime listings observed today: a microphone boom rated 0.25 to 1.2 kg (RØDE PSA1+, $112, 2,701 ratings, "1K+ bought") **[listing]**, a scissor-spring boom rated 1.5 kg (InnoGear, $19.99, **24,099 ratings, "2K+ bought"**) **[listing]**, a gas-spring mic arm rated 3 kg (Elgato Wave Mic Arm Pro, $179.99) **[listing]**, the 2 to 9 kg monitor arms of `sourcing/freedom.md` (HUANUO, 16,507 ratings) and the 0.5 to 1.5 kg tool balancer (Tigon, $39.00). Everything from 0.25 to 9 kg is on Prime with two-day delivery at $20 to $180. The floor is a selection, not a wall, once the total is known.

**What it leaves standing.**
- The rating says nothing about the balance-error slope, the friction, the reach or the tip stiffness. A bought arm is a candidate for a payload bracket; the scene's slider values are what to measure on it (push the tip with the Newton meter, freedom-14's question, and read the give).
- Whether a monitor arm's spring can be tuned to a payload near its lower rating is unchecked (freedom's entry 2). A mic boom's spring is adjusted by a screw within the range on the box.
- The scene, like freedom-02's, is one joint in a vertical plane. A monitor arm has two swivels with no gravity torque, whose pre-sliding decides the yaw of the shell, and yaw about the vertical moves the dot radially by the lever times the angle (freedom-04). freedom-02's vernier closes on *position*; the orientation the swivels hold is not observed by it (freedom-11's beam-turn point again).

**A question for freedom.** Your window plot uses one friction number for the whole arm. In a real arm the shoulder joint and the head each have their own, and the mismatch only matters at the shoulder. When you say "brake ×20 for the weld", is the intended state sequence *release, place with the balance forgiving ±0.09 kg, brake, weld*, and what mismatch do you expect between the placing state and the welding state (the fibre's clip point moves the moment the operator lets go)?

## 2. freedom-03, six taut lines (developed): the lines are taut inside a cone of pulls, and the shipped layout's cone is small

**The difficulty, in the shipped layout.** freedom-03's picture is "gravity keeps them taut". In the scene, set the umbilical pull to 0: the panel reads *all taut* and the six tensions are 3.6 / 3.8 / 2.8 / **0.1 / 0.2** / 4.8 N. With gravity alone two lines carry a tenth of a newton. Freedom's 2 N along the exit axis lifts them to 2.4 and 1.8 N; the layout is taut *because the fibre pulls*.

The reason is arithmetic, not layout. Six lines for six freedoms fix the tensions uniquely: t = −A⁻¹ w, and the layout is taut only when the load lies inside the cone of wrenches the six lines can supply. So I asked the layout, for every direction of the fibre's pull at the grip base, how many newtons before the first line goes slack (rigid lines, the reference pose, the same 1.2 kg, COM and lugs; `calc/w2-taut-set.cjs`, six thousand directions):

- **worst direction: 0.13 N** (line 4). Median over the sphere 0.68 N.
- **58 % of directions slacken a line below 1 N, 82 % below 4 N, 96 % below 8.7 N** (freedom-15's top of the recoil range at 240 mm).
- along the exit axis blended toward straight down (the scene's droop) it holds to **5.5 N** (droop 0), 6.4 (0.5) and 6.8 (1). That is the number freedom-03's entry 2 reports, and it is a property of one direction.

`calc/22-cable-layout.cjs` searched for positive tensions under three particular pulls (2 N at droop 0.5, 4 N at droop 0 and at droop 1). It optimised for three vectors and got a layout that is taut for three vectors. **The sphere is the harsh reading, and I owe the fair one** (`calc/w2-cone-check.cjs`): over a cone of directions around the scene's own pull (droop 0.5) the shipped layout's minimum slack pull is **4.2 N within ±15°**, 1.6 N within ±30° and 0.39 N within ±45°. It is a good layout for a fibre whose direction is known to ±15° and a fragile one for a fibre whose direction is not, which is exactly the fact nobody has.

**The assumption behind it.** freedom's: "gravity keeps it taut" and the umbilical pulls along its exit. Mine: for a hung platform gravity is the *only* preload, so every pull direction that has a component against gravity's cone removes tension from some line, and the fibre's direction is a routing choice nobody has made yet (borrowed-11, travel-08).

**Repair and branch: `borrowed-16-taut-cone`** (`branchOf` `freedom-03-cable-platform`). Three layouts, one clearance rule (the lines stay 32 mm off the barrel and housing, the same lugs and frame height):

| layout | worst-direction slack pull | directions below 4 N | line tensions with no pull |
|---|---|---|---|
| freedom-03 as shipped | 0.13 N | 82 % | 0.3 to 4.8 N |
| searched for the largest worst-direction slack pull, gravity only | **2.6 N** | 55 % | 2.3 to 3.8 N |
| searched with a constant 10 N downward pull at the grip base | **4.75 N** | 0 % | 2.8 to 8.9 N |

The searches are random over sixteen million tries (`w2-robust-layout.cjs`), verified on six thousand directions (`w2-verify-layouts.cjs`). They are search results, not optima, and the cone depends on the shell's centre of mass. Two things the table shows. **A factor of twenty in the worst case is a layout choice, not a stiffer line**, and it is paid for at the direction the shipped layout was tuned to: within ±15° of the drawn exit the searched gravity-only layout holds 3.0 N against the shipped 4.2 N, and it wins at ±30° (2.7 N against 1.6 N) and beyond. The search buys robustness to an unknown direction, not margin at a known one. And **a preload only helps a layout made for it**: 10 N down at the grip base takes the *shipped* layout to two slack lines (−0.9 and −1.3 N), because that layout is tuned to gravity's particular wrench.

The bought preload is the spring balancer of `sourcing/freedom.md` (Tigon TW-1R, 0.5 to 1.5 kg, i.e. 5 to 15 N of near-constant force, $39.00, two-day delivery **[listing]**): a constant-force line under the bench adds no stiffness in this model and no dependence on stretch. Real cable robots do it differently: Hangprinter-class machines pull the effector from lines on both sides so that no gravity is needed **[search]**; six lines from above are the minimum, and a hung platform's taut set is a cone.

**What it leaves standing.**
- The balancer's line has to reach the grip base past the tube and the bench, and its friction and stroke are unchecked. A friction of a few percent of 10 N is 0.3 N: a disturbance of the size the layout is trying to defend against.
- 4.75 N does not cover 8.7 N. With the preload 76 % of directions still slacken a line below 8.7 N, so a route that keeps the pull under about 4 N and inside the cone is still a requirement (freedom-15's ledger says how to measure it).
- Rigid lines at the reference pose only; freedom-03's stretch, creep and frame-spread entries still apply.

**A question for freedom.** Which is the design variable: the layout for the route, or the route for the layout? The map is what makes the fibre's direction measurable against a design (one number and one direction from the Newton meter). If the route is chosen first (a gallows, a festoon), do you want the layout searched for the *cone of that route* rather than for the whole sphere?

## 3. freedom-08, gravity as the tilt actuator (sketch): a stabiliser sled is the plumb bob with two more knobs, and it rings

**The difficulty, in the drawn variant.** freedom-08's numbers are the spring (0.42 N·m/rad at the housing top, [freedom]), the cable torque (0.29 N·m for 2 N at 145 mm) and the lean (about 39°). What the scene does not show is time. The pendulum's period at K = 0.42 with the gun's inertia about the pivot (0.0106 kg·m², illustrative) is **0.99 s and the bearing-only damping ratio 0.03**: a step of 1 N of pull at bead start leans the gun about 20° (69 mm at the dot, small-angle) and the *first swing goes to twice that*, 137 mm. Three more numbers, all from `calc/w2-sled.cjs`:

1. To hold **0.15 mm** at the dot for a 1 N step with gravity as the only spring, the cable's line of action must pass within **0.31 mm** of the pivot (K 0.42 N·m/rad, pivot 202 mm from the dot).
2. The trim mass (100 g, ±30 mm) supplies 0.029 N·m: it cancels **0.2 N** of change in pull at 145 mm, a fifth of the 1 N step. It is the slow, fine part.
3. Nothing damps the pendulum but the bearing: a step of pull rings it for the whole bead.

**The assumption behind it.** freedom's: gravity's stiffness is whatever the pivot choice gives (K = M g ℓ with ℓ the pivot-to-COM distance) and the trim mass is the actuator. Mine, from the product: a camera stabiliser sled (Steadicam-style, FLYCAM, Glidecam) has *two independent knobs*, the balance (a fine-tune stage that moves the COM under the gimbal, which is freedom's trim) and the **bottom weight** on a post, which sets how stiff gravity is *without moving the equilibrium*. Operators tune the stiffness by the "drop time", the time to fall from horizontal to plumb, aiming for 2 to 3 s (a slow, nearly neutral pendulum) **[search]**; here a stiff gravity spring is wanted, so the scene's drop time is 0.27 s.

**Repair and branch: `borrowed-15-sled-keel`** (`origin` combination: `branchOf` `freedom-08-gravity-tilt`, `combines` `freedom-02-balanced-arm`, `freedom-14-hand-plus-guidance`). A side view of the gun on a gimbal with a keel post, a rotary damper and the cable's line of action; a replay rings the pendulum with a step of pull and the trace is the dot in millimetres against the tolerance band. What each part does, with the scene's defaults:

- **The keel.** 0.6 kg on a 200 mm post takes K from 0.42 to **1.60 N·m/rad**, cuts the lean per newton from 69 to 18 mm, adds 0.6 kg to the payload (which fills the gap to the 2 kg floor of freedom-02's monitor arms: 1.2 + 0.6 = 1.8 kg) and makes the trim **3.8 times finer** (1 mm of 100 g is 0.035° instead of 0.133°) and its total range 3.8 times smaller (±1.05°). A keel is a gear reduction on the trim as much as a spring.
- **The damper.** The critical damping for those masses is 0.47 N·m·s/rad; below it the scene rings, above it the trace settles. A video fluid head is a rotary viscous damper with stepped drag, a 5 kg rating and tilt +90/−50° for $57 to $99 on Prime **[listing]**. **None of the listings states a damping value** (drag is in steps); a spring scale on the handle at a known speed measures it.
- **The anchor.** Slide the cable's lever to zero (the "cable anchored on the pivot" preset) and the lean per newton goes to zero: the fibre's force has no lever about the pivot. That is a routing statement, not a keel statement: the collar of the cable has to sit *on the gimbal axis* (a hollow ball through which the straight fibre passes). It is the same rule freedom-15 states for the exit axis, applied to the pivot instead of the dot; the difference is in section 4.

**Why "bought" matters here.** The FLYCAM HD-3000 (a 3-axis ball-bearing gimbal, micro-balance knobs, up to 3.5 kg, $178, 362 ratings, two-day) **[listing]** is a product the gun could be hung on today for a first test of period, drop time and trim. It is a sled for cameras and its post and weights are a kilogram-scale mass on a lever. Whether a printed shell and the gun can sit on it is unchecked.

**What it leaves standing.**
- One axis only. Roll behaves the same way with its own COM offset; the vertical-axis turn has no gravity spring at all (freedom's entry 3).
- The step is now a lean of tens of millimetres per newton *unless the anchor is on the pivot*. Even at the pivot the couple (section 4) remains.
- Stick at small angles in a real gimbal is not in the model: the bearing term is a small viscous number, freedom's friction-free bearing stays.
- Room: the keel post hangs below the pivot and the bench is 140 mm below the dot; the scene puts the limit at about 280 mm.

**A question for freedom.** You gave the pivot three candidate sites (housing top, collar, housing back). With a keel supplying K, the pivot can go wherever the fibre can be brought to. Would you place it *on the fibre's line* (so that the cable has no lever at all) and let the keel and trim do the rest, rather than the site that makes the working pose a plumb pose?

## 4. freedom-15, the pull ledger (sketch): the ledger has a column missing, the couple

**The difficulty, in the ledger as written.** freedom-15's entry 1 says a pull along the exit axis has no torque about the dot or any pivot on that line, so "route the fibre straight along the exit axis for a stretch". That holds for the *force*. A fibre clamped at the exit and leaving it already curved also applies a **moment** to the grip base, up to EI/R when it leaves at the curvature of its route (for the placeholder EI of 0.05 to 0.5 N·m² and R = 350 mm: **0.14 to 1.4 N·m**), and about EIΔα/ℓ times three or four for an end-angle mismatch Δα over a free length ℓ. Against gravity's 0.42 N·m/rad (freedom-08) and 0.29 N·m from 2 N at 145 mm, that is the same order as the force term, and it does not go away when the line of action is moved onto the pivot. In `borrowed-15` the "couple at the exit" slider makes the point: **0.05 N·m of couple alone, with the cable's force anchored at the pivot, moves the dot 6.3 mm** (0.2 N·m: 25 mm).

**The assumption behind it.** freedom's: the fibre is a force with a direction. Mine, from how cables are handled in products (a bend restrictor on a fibre connector, a service loop on a gimbal, a strain relief on a camera cable): a clamped cable is a force *and* a couple, and product designers deal with it by making the cable leave the clamp *straight* for a stretch and by putting a slack loop between the clamp and the moving body.

**Repair.** Two moves; the first is the sled scene's "cable anchored on the pivot" preset and its couple slider, the second is a change to the ledger.
- Anchor the cable's collar at the pivot and leave a *slack service loop* from the exit to the collar. The loop has a stiffness EI/ℓ_loop: for a 150 mm loop and EI 0.05 to 0.5 N·m² that is 0.33 to 3.3 N·m/rad, **the same order as the keel's gravity spring (1.6)**, acting in parallel. It is a spring whose rest shape can be chosen: pre-form the loop so that it is at rest at the working pose and it adds stiffness without a bias; form it wrong and it is a push.
- Add the fifth column to the ledger: *bending moment at the exit, and what changes it at bead start*. It changes when the route differs between the dry turn and the bead (freedom-13 entry 4 already names the 240/350 mm change of radius), so it is one more step nobody put a number on.

**What it leaves standing.** EI, the fibre's weight per metre and the wire conduit and gas hose beside it are unmeasured; the ledger's "how Derek can measure it" column has no measurement for the couple. One is already in freedom-08's questions: hang the gun from a thread through the candidate pivot with the fibre attached, and read the tilt, which gives the total torque of the cable (force × lever + couple) at that pose. Two hangs with the fibre laid differently separate them.

**A question for freedom.** Does the ledger want a fifth column for the couple, and is the thread-hang tilt (already in freedom-08's questions) the way to read it for the whole cable at once?

## 5. freedom-14, hand plus guidance (rough, thin region): the damper is a bought part with no number

**The difficulty, in the variant.** The scene's damper is 0.05 N·s/mm at the grip, with a corner frequency K/(2πc) of about 2 Hz against a 10 Hz tremor **[freedom]**, and the idea file asks "whether a printed damper, an air dashpot or an adjustable damper is worth having". The bought part is not a dashpot at the grip; it is a rotary damper: a video fluid head (V504, $56.99, 5 kg, tilt +90/−50°, 100 ratings; SIRUI VA-5, $99, 230 ratings; Manfrotto and Benro heads $230 to $265) **[listing]**. A rotary damper acts on translation at the dot through the lever: c_lin = c_rot / L². So 0.05 N·s/mm at a 202 mm lever is **2.0 N·m·s/rad**, at the nose collar's 70 mm it is 0.25, and at the sled's critical damping (0.47 N·m·s/rad) the dot-space damping is only **0.0115 N·s/mm**, a quarter of freedom's. And the listings give *drag steps*, not values; there is no number to put in the scene except the one Derek can measure with a spring scale on a fluid head's handle.

**The assumption behind it.** freedom's: the damper is a free parameter, and its size is set by the corner frequency. Mine: the product fixes the *place* where damping happens (a pivot) and hides its *size* (steps), so the design variable is the pivot's distance to the dot, not the damping.

**A branch, drawn: `borrowed-19-haptic-jog`** (`combines` `freedom-14-hand-plus-guidance` and `borrowed-09-hand-controller`). Two loops on one error and "the eye's bias becomes a push" (entries 2 and 3) both come from putting the force on the *gun*. The bought part that renders resistance to a hand is a knob: a brushless gimbal motor with a magnetic encoder and field-oriented control, whose torque is a function of angle and speed, so its detents, walls, spring and damper are a program. The open-source SmartKnob and the haptic examples of SimpleFOC do it **[search]**, and a hollow-shaft gimbal motor kit with an AS5600 encoder and a SimpleFOC driver is $34.88 on Prime (sourcing/borrowed.md, wave 1). The knob jogs the fine axis (mm per turn) and the gun is never touched: the hand supplies the motion, software supplies resistance, and the motor's torque limit is a physical cap the hand can always overpower. In the scene: a detent every 0.05 mm (18° at 1 mm/turn, countable blind), a wall 0.3 mm beyond the eye's seam, a soft pull toward the seam capped at 10 mN·m, a damper for tremor. Encoder resolution is 0.24 µm of stage per count at 1 mm/turn; a 10° tremor is 28 µm at that ratio and 6 µm at 0.2 mm/turn. Set the eye 0.2 mm off and the pull leans the hand toward the wrong place, as in your entry 3, but on the knob, where it can be turned off. What the scene says it cannot settle: the torque a bought gimbal motor can render and its cogging are on no listing read (hobby motors cog moderately to severely, which the detents must beat), and whether feel helps a hand at all needs a printed knob and an afternoon.

**A second branch, no scene: assist from the hand's own force.** The other product pattern is an e-bike torque sensor or a cobot's hand guiding: assist proportional to the force *on the handle*, never a push unless the hand pushes. Here it is a load cell in the grip and a stage that moves by an admittance (mm/s per newton, low-passed): it cancels stick-slip and a first-order low-pass with a 1 Hz corner attenuates 10 Hz tremor tenfold. It changes the arrangement (software now moves the stage), so it stays a branch. A 6-DoF force puck is on Prime (SpaceMouse Compact, $171, 1,044 ratings, "200+ bought") **[listing, sourcing/borrowed.md wave 1]**.

**What it leaves standing.** Nothing about a real hand: freedom's stiffness, drift and tremor stay illustrative. A load cell in the grip reads everything that pushes on the gun, the fibre's pull included, so an admittance stage would follow the fibre unless the cell sits between the hand and the gun only; the knob has no such problem because it is not on the gun.

**A question for freedom.** With rotary dampers the interesting design variable is the lever (how far from the dot the damping acts). Would you rather put freedom-14's help on the gun, at the same pivot as freedom-08's gravity spring so that one pivot supplies weight, stiffness and damping, or on the handle, where a wall, a detent and a pull cost a $35 kit and never push the gun?

The vernier is two axes (XZ, ±10 mm) and the arm's swivels hold the orientation by friction; a small hexapod does all six with the centre of rotation set in software. It weighs about six rails and motors, roughly 1.8 kg at 0.3 kg each (illustrative), so gun, legs and rings come to about 3.2 kg: inside the 2 to 9 kg monitor arms, above the Elgato's 3 kg, which is what a monitor arm's 2 kg floor wants. The arm carries the base ring; the hexapod trims all six freedoms about the dot. A ring platform 18 mm above the dot gives **0.097 / 0.096 / 0.017 mm per newton** (radial / tangent / vertical) for a push at the dot (six legs of 20 N/mm), against 0.10 / 0.01 (radial / vertical) for freedom-01b's seat and 0.45 / 1.0 for a rigid-based stage [freedom].
- **freedom-01b's seat + the hexapod ring**: the same lever logic (pivot near the dot) with the seat unilateral (the fibre pulls it out at 6.7 N, freedom's entry 2) and the ring bilateral at the price of a cage of legs and a gap for the gun's body (clock the gap to within about ±8° of the tail in the scene).
- **freedom-06 / 10 + a locking gas spring**: a lockable gas spring holds any height rigidly under load and releases by a pin; Bansbach sells a "B-locking" spring for $71.57 on Prime (28 in, 135 N, "rigidly lock in both directions") **[listing]**, and chair gas lifts ($22.99, 2.2K ratings, "800+ bought") are the same idea in the billions. It is freedom-06's lock on the one axis where the load acts, freedom-10's retract (unlock and the spring's excess force lifts) and freedom-02's balance in one part, with no motor. Stiffness of the lock and the release force are unchecked.

## Smaller remarks on the rest

- **freedom-01 (Derek's rings).** The openable rubber-coated loop has a bought form: a rubber-cushioned P-clamp (LOKMAN 2 in, 20 pack, $17.99, 6,185 ratings, "100+ bought" **[listing]**; smaller sizes for a 25 to 30 mm sleeve), whose friction limit is set by the screw. That turns round 1's unknown friction limit (1, 4, 8 N) into a number Derek can read: pull the sleeve through the clamp with the Newton meter. The "wire held in Z" that must be a force element (round 2) is a spring balancer's cable, and two balancers opposing in X and Y are a *zero-stiffness* horizontal support where the friction is the only stiffness; Derek's bungee is one of them with a restoring force. A bungee's stiffness is EA/L, so its length is the stiffness knob. Round 4's hinge (two loops define a line and gravity spins the gun about it) is the sled: option (c) in that round, "use the hinge deliberately", with the keel of borrowed-15 supplying the spring and defining the equilibrium at the working roll instead of the 0.73 kg counterweight that only nulls the torque.
- **freedom-01b (nose seat).** See the hexapod ring above. A snap-in ball socket (the kind on RC ball links) is bilateral where the cone seat is unilateral; its play and pull-out force are unchecked.
- **freedom-04 (the tangent is free).** In your plot of dot displacement per degree against the pivot's distance from the dot (4.87 mm/° at the grip base down to 0.28 at the nozzle), a software pivot sits at 0 mm/° for any tilt, and the cost moves to the leg stroke, lever × angle (5.5 mm for 10° with the ring 18 mm above the dot, 19 mm with it at 140 mm: `borrowed-13`). With it the tangent slide can carry a yaw at no radial cost, which is your scene's toggle done by the actuators instead of the geometry.
- **freedom-09 and freedom-10 (pose logger, nudge box).** The two sketches are sold as one product for *two of the rotations*: a Sky-Watcher AZ-GTi ($525, 117 ratings, two-day) **[listing]** has motors and output-shaft encoders that follow a hand slew ("Freedom Find dual encoders ... manual slewing without losing alignment"), a 5 kg rating, a Wi-Fi interface, and a public command set on UDP 11880 with open Python and INDI drivers **[search]**; a step of 0.625 arcsecond is 0.0008 mm at a 250 mm lever [derived]. It has to be balanced, the pivot is not the dot, and backlash and periodic error are not on the listing. `borrowed-17-positioner-shelf` puts it beside the others.
- **freedom-06 (lock and release) and freedom-10 (retract).** At the scene's defaults the locked three-joint chain gives 176 mm at the tip under the gun's own weight (7.1 mm/N horizontally, 11.1 vertically; 20 N·m/rad of pre-sliding per joint, illustrative); a chain is the wrong shape for a load that is almost all vertical. The SCARA reading of a monitor arm says why: two swivels carry no gravity torque, so the lock belongs on the one axis where the load acts, and that is a locking gas spring (see the combination above). freedom-10's retract reaches its stop at 166 mm/s and puts 16.4 mJ of its 24 mJ into it as an impact on a 1.2 kg gun; a hydraulic soft-close damper or a gas spring with end-of-stroke damping is the mass-produced way to spend the last few millimetres [general knowledge, not sourced]. 
- **freedom-05 (runout table) and freedom-13 (map and step).** The vernier is a 100 mm mini rail with a T6×1 screw (5 µm per full step, $54.80). Nothing to add from my side.
- **freedom-07 and freedom-12.** The shoe, the touch stylus and my tonearm (borrowed-07) are one contact at different preloads; a phono cartridge tracks at about 10 to 20 mN [general knowledge, unchecked], which is 10 to 20 times lighter than the 0.18 to 0.36 N trigger of freedom-12 and says what a light enough follower can be (not verified for a steel corner).

## Questions for Derek collected here

Each is also with the idea it belongs to.

1. **Weigh the gun and the parts.** A kitchen scale, to 25 g, for the gun in its shell, the vernier stage with its motors and the camera (borrowed-14 needs the total to about 0.1 kg).
2. **Drop time.** Hang the gun from a thread through the housing top, the collar and the housing back in turn; nudge it, count ten swings and time them. The period gives K/I for each candidate pivot with nothing bought (borrowed-15, freedom-08). With the fibre attached and laid the way it will lie, the tilt from plumb gives the cable's torque at that pose, force and couple together (freedom-15).
3. **A fluid head's drag.** If you have or borrow one: pull its handle at a steady walking pace with the Newton meter on the smallest drag step and the largest, and note force and distance per second (borrowed-15, freedom-14).
4. **The friction and give of an arm.** Push the tip of a monitor arm or a mic boom with the Newton meter at 0.5, 1 and 2 N and note how far it gives and whether it stays (borrowed-14, freedom-02).
5. **Which way does the fibre pull?** Direction as well as size: the Newton meter along and across the exit at the working pose, and a photo of the fibre's route (borrowed-16 maps the direction against a layout).
