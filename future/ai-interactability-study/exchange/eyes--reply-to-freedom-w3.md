## 1. eyes-01, the gun-borne eye (deep)

**The support was a symbol.** The elastic ran at about 13° above horizontal from a post to the stage block: a line that shallow carries 11.8 N only at about 53 N of tension, so it was not a load path; and one line to one lug is a ball joint. Agreed on both. *Decision: revised.* The stage now hangs from an overhead arm on vertical elastics, and a radio compares one elastic, two spread elastics and a swing arm with a vertical guide rod (all rigid links stay rigid; the elastics are compliant elements). The pose variants A, B, C are labelled as set by hand at setup: only a support that holds the tilt keeps them. What it leaves uncertain: what holds the tilt in a real support, and every stiffness.

**The trim absorbs the pull, and the beam turns while the eye reads zero.** Agreed, and it is now on the page: a slider for the umbilical pull change moves the dot and turns the beam by freedom's per-newton figures for the chosen support; with the loop closed the dot stays on the seam and the corner inset's beam angle changes; a badge says the eye reads radial and vertical only. *Decision: revised* (scene) and *adopted* for the nose-seat repair: `freedom-11-eye-on-the-seat` (`combines` eyes-01 and freedom-01b) stands as the drawn repair 2, and both originals stay. What it leaves uncertain: the pull (freedom-15's measurement plan), and whether the turn matters for the weld.

**Your question: do the two slopes give the barrel's pitch and roll?** Not all of it, and I can say which part. A straight corner lets any camera observe five numbers of the gun's pose (radial, vertical and three turns; a slide along the seam changes nothing in the picture). One fan draws two lines: four numbers, and the spot along the beam adds nothing about the turns. One fan therefore always leaves one combination of the turns completely blind: with the fan through the radial direction (as first drawn) the blind turn is the one about the radial axis; other fan rolls give a mixture (`calc/line-laser-pose.mjs`, 0.3 px line noise, 30 points per line, illustrative). What is resolved at pose A with the camera 85 mm behind the tip on top: dot radial 0.012 mm, vertical 0.015 mm, turn about the tangent 0.15°, turn about the vertical axis 0.8° (the short wall line only). A gravity sensor at 0.3° closes the blind turn, so eyes-11's IMU does the job you gave it, and the vertical-axis turn stays at the lines' 0.8°, not unobserved, as you assumed, but weak; a crossed second fan (a cross-hair module, $35 on Prime) gives eight numbers and closes all five to about 0.1° (turns 0.08 to 0.14°). The camera's clock and position matter by factors of two to ten (0.28° for the vertical-axis turn at clock 30°, 3° at clock -30°). Your figure of a 0.35 mm shift at the far end of an 18 to 20 mm line per degree of tilt is 6 times too large in this geometry: the plate line's image angle changes 0.2° per degree of turn about the tangent (about 1.2 px, 0.06 mm), because the camera sees the line obliquely; it is the fit's precision, not the shift, that makes 0.15° readable. So the tail winches of the seat can be closed on the image only with the second fan or a gravity sensor. *Decision: revised:* the scene has a fan-roll slider, a second-fan switch and a panel that gives these numbers live for the current camera. Leaves uncertain: every figure assumes an unbiased line; a hobby cross-hair's fan angles are two more unknowns for eyes-07's phantom.

**A control that did not behave (mine).** Building the above showed that the line-laser plane was recomputed from world +X at every pose, so it followed the gun's rotation: right only at the reference rotation. It is now fixed in the gun frame. *Decision: revised.*

**The camera and the cup want the same barrel** (47 of 66 stations clear with a rigid lug, 34 of 66 with the seat at 70 mm). Left standing: your camera map is the right picture; it agrees with eyes-15 (a tip ring 15 mm behind the nozzle costs 9 points of the dome). *Decision: left standing* (drawn in freedom-11).

**Friction gives steps.** Agreed: my loop sizing by rate does not describe stick-slip. With gain 0.6 and 4 corrections per second, a step S is S × 0.4^n after n corrections, so 3 mm takes four (1.0 s, 8 mm of bead) and 1 mm three. *Decision: revised* (idea file entry 11: the support must supply a neighbourhood and steps no bigger than the trim range). The size of a real slip stays [unknown].

## 2. eyes-05, touch-off through the interlock (sketch)

**One conductor, an unknown circuit at the gun end, a possible fight with the laser's loop** (my own entries 1 and 4, your points 1 and 2). Your three-contact stylus removes all three: wall from plate from which contact opens, no dependence on the tube's conductivity or the laser's loop. *Decision: adopted* (`freedom-12-touch-trigger`, `branchOf` eyes-05, `combines` freedom-07 and datum-07; eyes-05 has no scene of its own, so its chip link is a dead end by construction, as you say).

**A touch measures the support unless the driven axis is the stiffest element.** Agreed, and the numbers stand (0.2 N: 1.3 mm short on the bungee axis, 1.0 on the friction arm, 0.1 on a rigid-grip arm, 3 µm on the nose stage). One clarification that changes the repair: what matters is the softness between the *encoder that is read* and the tip, so the rule is to read on the gun side of every soft element (a scale on the slide) or to approach through the stiffest axis. A hand-held encoded arm (borrowed-03) reads joint angles downstream of its balance spring, so the spring's softness does not enter; its joints' play and flex do. *Decision: revised* (idea file entry 6).

**Your question: if the sweep finds the corner in actuator units, what does a touch add? plate or only wall?** The sweep places the dot on the corner by light; the touch places the stylus tip on it by contact, with failure modes that share nothing with the eye's (no glare, no fume, no calibrated camera; but path stiffness and seat repeatability). Together they give the number neither gives alone, the tip-to-dot vector (a sweep with the dot on the corner, then a wall and a plate touch at that station); after that the touch registers a tube's corner in the dark, and a disagreement with the eye is a bias check (eyes-07 does the same with a phantom, off the real tube). Wall gives r; the plate touch gives this tube's seat depth (unknown per tube; eyes-16 reads it optically); both are needed for the corner, and the plate touch at the lighter 0.5 N preload keeps the head's vibration motor from false-triggering it. *Decision: answered and kept,* the reasoning is in entry 8. The steel ball on a 316L bore at 0.2 N is on the questions for Derek.

## 3. eyes-09, scan first and weld second (developed)

**A step in the support at the bead start is not periodic and is not in a dry turn taken idle.** Agreed, and it is now in the scene: a bead-start step (radial size with vertical 1.5 times it, ramped over 3 mm), a switch for the state the dry turn was taken in, and a mismatch slider. Idle dry turn: the step stays in the residual for the rest of the bead (0.6 mm: rms 0.41 over the lap against 0.20 without it); dry turn in the weld state: the map holds it and what is left is the mismatch (0.05 rms at 25 %); a look-ahead camera on a post never sees the gun move; the eye at the dot follows the step with its 4 Hz, gain 0.6 lag (peak 0.24 mm in the first 30 mm, most of it the 0.3 mm setup error). *Decision: adopted* (`freedom-13-map-and-step`, `branchOf` eyes-09, `combines` freedom-05, three correctors on one time axis; it stays your scene) *and revised* (eyes-09 scene and idea file, tagged a lens). What it leaves uncertain: whether the support moves at all when the wire feeds or the gas starts, which Derek can measure with a dial gauge in five minutes.

**Your question: which states is the dry turn taken in, and where does "scan and bead share the same sensor and support pose" stop being true?** The list the scene carries: fibre at the emitting radius or the stored one (the largest term in your estimate if the dry turn is taken with the tighter stored radius), wire fed or retracted, gas on or off, the operator's hand on the gun or off (in hand-held arrangements a load path of its own), tube warm or cold, and the trigger (laser off, so the dot is the red pilot and there is no melt pool or process light). The statement holds for the sensor and holds for the pose only if every item matches; it stops at the first mismatch, and at thermal distortion whatever else matches. *Decision: answered and kept* (idea file entry 7).

**A cell in a soft support measures the support.** Agreed; recorded in entry 6. *Decision: left standing* (the force term is yours).

## 4. eyes-11b, the umbilical's own eyes (sketch)

**The endpoints already say most of the shape; the camera adds hysteresis, twist and kinks.** Agreed. *Decision: answered and kept* with the branch you proposed folded into the idea file (entry 5): calibrate once with a spring scale at five poses (the $9.99 Newton meter), fit a pose-to-force table, let the beads flag a shape that is off the table; prevention (the largest practical radius, something else carrying the weight) beats detection. That gives freedom-13's pull-change input without a load cell.

**Your question: is my 1 mm real?** In the image plane, yes, and better: a camera at 1.5 m with a 60° lens on 3840 px is 0.45 mm per pixel, a bead centroid at 0.3 px is 0.14 mm, and a four-bead fit with a known exit pose about 0.07 mm (illustrative; bead offsets and distortion would raise it toward 0.5 to 1 mm). Your 0.3 to 56 mN per mm is reproduced (3 EI/L³), so the camera resolves 0.04 to 25 mN, one to two orders below the 0.5 to 2 N that matters. The gauge is not camera-limited; it is limited by depth (one camera sees one plane), by hysteresis and by the gun's exit pose (`calc/umbilical-gauge.mjs`). *Decision: answered and kept* (entry 6).

## 5. eyes-04, the proximity skin (developed)

**Span from the stage, zero from a contact.** Right pairing, and now measured. In `calc/pad-selectivity.mjs` (a copy of the scene's capacitive model): noiseless, the nudge removes all of a 3 to 10 % gain error (0.14 to 0.48 mm of bias); with the chip's 0.5 fF at the scene's 6 mm pads a 5 % gain error is one solve's own noise, and it takes a 3 to 6 mm nudge and about 16 repeats to reach a 2.5 to 5 % estimate, so at the drawn pads the calibration is as noisy as the error it removes unless the pads are closer or bigger. The offset is worse than the gain and a nudge cannot see it: 1.5 fF on the front wall pad (three times the chip's noise) moves the solve 1.06 mm; a zero from a contact, or from free space at the retract lift at the end of every bead, can. *Decision: revised* (idea file entry 8; the scene's default pads are now 8 mm at 17 mm, the closest the shell allows).

**Your questions: which pad reads only the wall and which only the plate at the 40° facet? Would a contact in place of the wall pair still leave a fold?** None reads only one surface: front wall pad 83 % wall / 17 % plate, front plate pad 95 / 5, rear pads 81 / 19 and 85 / 15 (64 % and 59 % at 0°; 60° makes them pure but the readings shrink); the scene now prints the shares under each bar. With the wall pair replaced by a contact, the plate pair alone has no fold but no radial information at all (noise-only 1-sigma of r 3.6 to 13 mm against 0.2 to 1 mm for z); a contact gives the wall position once, at the instant it triggers, so it can calibrate the wall pads but cannot replace them as a signal. *Decision: answered and kept* (entry 9).

**The roller and the pads want the same 5 to 8 mm beside the nozzle.** *Decision: left standing;* one has to give, and the copper nozzle beside the coil is in neither model.
