# datum: the seam is the datum. Every arrangement held

Framing: the pose that matters is gun-to-corner, and the corner belongs to each tube and plate. Which features of the work can establish or verify position; can the gun be located relative to the work instead of the room; what does a reference on the work make unnecessary. Nothing here is ranked or scored.

Idea files are in `ideas/`, scenes in `../../scenes/`. The exchanges are `../../exchange/datum--on--trials-w2.md` (wave 2) and `../../exchange/datum--reply-to-travel-w3.md` (wave 3); sourcing in `../../sourcing/datum.md`; numbers in `calc/`. ## What the work found, in a few lines

- The seam is a circle: rotation of the gun about the tube axis does not change gun-to-corner pose. One of six degrees of freedom is free; tethers can be soft; "tube turns" and "gun orbits" are the same problem for pose and different for cables.
- No datum on the work touches the dot-versus-melt term. Only a witness pass does (datum-11). It is a floor under every arrangement.
- The two largest unknown spreads in the chain are tube length and rim-to-plate-face depth; measuring them across the tubes on hand would say how much of the chain is worth removing (datum-01).
- The fine axis a seam signature needs is tiny (0.03 mm/s, +/-0.37 mm); the sensor and the hot drift are the hard parts (datum-02).
- A ring that rides the work has to keep out of the nozzle and beam corridor (the turning lower ring cannot have a gap), balance a gun whose centre of mass is about 118 mm from the axis, and cannot know seat depth (datum-03).
- Orbiting the gun round a still tube moves the difficulty to the umbilical: with the proxy exit direction a laid-flat wrap needs a track of about a metre radius and about 8 m of cable against a 5 m fibre, and a fixed hook on the axis twists the fibre (datum-03b).
- A simple edge search through the wall fails; a template fit works if the template is calibrated (datum-06). A tag ring's accuracy is set by its distance to the corner (datum-05). One touch-off tip is about ten times better than another (datum-07).
- The map has four owners with different lifetimes (rig, seat, work, judge bias); harmonics 2 and 3 are seat-free invariants a judge can check without truth; a few touches bound the judge's bias, and the ownership route saves time only for a lift and return (datum-14).
- Once a probe finds the corner on each tube the dock's coordinates stop carrying the trial; what a dock can still do is notice change, and a coupon under the docked dot separates the gun and camera from the rest (datum-15).
- Derek's loops carry from the room and the arm's base can ride the rim: the crown's counterweight need moves to the cable's pull, and a stiff support anchored in the room fights the rim by stiffness times the face wobble (datum-16). Stopping a crown tipping needs about 0.23 kg, contact all round about 0.7, centring it on the axis about 1.5 (datum-03, illustrative masses).
- An arrangement built on a judge cannot be scored by it; the requirement (a hand) needs a see-through corner with no judge in the number; machines are scored by touches at rest (datum-17).
- In the table opening the tabletop can be read as a datum plane: the shelf becomes a servo on a null and the tube's length drops out; three wall touches find the tube in a loose hole (datum-18).
- The rim edge is not the corner: a camera over the bore sees the corner displaced by 6.35 x tan(line of sight to the axis), 0.5 to 2.8 mm; the rim circles and ports give the camera's pose in every frame (datum-19).
- The wire must lean outward by about L/2R to clear the curved wall; one millimetre of feed is about 0.6 mm of tip height; the difference of the dot's and the wire's probes of one corner is the dot-to-wire vector (datum-20).

- **A mate on the whole circle follows the tube's motion and none of its shape.** A ring on the outside, a skirt or a set of fingers follows the rigid offset and tilt; k equal pads pass out-of-round harmonics k-1 and k+1 at gain 1 into the ring's own centre, so three pads on an oval tube add a first harmonic as large as the ovality. A roller on the bore at the station reads the shape too, up to 2 sin(n s / 2R) of harmonic n (datum-21, datum-23, `calc/mate_harmonics.js`).
- **The wall's eccentricity is common to a room-fixed gun and to every ring on the outside**, so it cannot cancel the crown's radial win; the win is the wobble left after indicating minus what the pads pass, a few hundredths of a millimetre either way for a tube the rig would accept (datum-03, reply to travel).
- **The slip fit does not square the plate.** The plate's diagonal (123.607 mm) is shorter than the bore (123.698 mm): it can lie at any tilt without touching the wall on both sides. Seat depth and tilt are how the plate was held, not properties of the tube (datum-22, `calc/plate_seat.js`).
- **The surface that sets the plate can be the surface the gun rides.** A plug carries the plate by its two ports (cone collars in the countersinks, one pin in a slot; the thread untouched) and three feet set its depth; the plate's mate is exactly six constraints; eight tacks leave a constant plus a first harmonic about half the spread between tacks; the ring is per closure, so the flip is answered by construction (datum-22).
- **A clip pinches one place from both sides.** A roller and pad on one radial line squeeze the wall (contact half-width 7 micrometres at 8 N); gripping a 1.65 mm wall from opposite sides would ovalise it by about 0.15 mm at 8 N (datum-23, `calc/wall_clip.js`).
- The stylus of a touch-off is at the dot only if the red pilot sits on the nozzle axis: the tip-to-dot vector is a systematic term better touching does not reduce; a dot sweep across a coupon corner measures it (datum-07).
- The hold between the dry turn and the weld turn moves the gun by change of force times compliance (44 micrometres per newton for a 12 mm steel rod); the dry turn is taken in the weld's mechanical state (datum-02).

## Questions that need Derek's observation, with the idea they belong to

- datum-14: indicate a tube at the rim, turn it 100 degrees in its nest, indicate again, compare the traces against table angle (ten minutes with the indicator). datum-02: what accuracy does the dot need?
- datum-15: probe one coupon and one tube ten times each, after re-seating the gun in its shell and after moving the camera 0.3 mm.
- datum-16: gun mass and centre of mass; the umbilical's pull at the exit (spring scale); may a crown sit on the rim of a finished tube.
- datum-17: would you hold the gun for ten laps over a clear twin; can you see the dot through clear acrylic from where you stand.
- datum-18: tube length and rim-to-plate-face depth over a dozen tubes; what the table is made of and how flat near the hole; may the rim be touched.
- datum-19: a phone photograph of the bore from 300 mm and from 150 mm straight above (are the rim circles and ports sharp; is the inner rim edge chamfered).
- datum-22: how the plate is held at its recess today before it is tacked; an indicator on the plate face at three azimuths before and after the eight tacks; plate depth from the rim over ten plates.
- datum-21 and datum-23: five tubes, eight positions each, outside diameter and wall thickness; rim flatness on a plate with a feeler gauge; a longitudinal weld seam on the bore (fingernail); may a roller touch the bore of a finished tube.
- datum-07: which nozzle extension the dot is set at; can the nozzle come off and what is its thread.
- datum-20: with the clip on and the laser disabled, does anything change when the nozzle or wire touches the tube; where does the wire land against the dot (paper under the dot); how is its outward lean set.
