# eyes-01 Gun-borne eye

Scene: `scenes/eyes-01-gun-borne-eye/` (worked deeply). Origin: swarm (eyes framing). Maturity: deep.

**Picture it.** A small camera and a line laser sit on the printed shell that already wraps the gun, a few centimetres behind the nozzle, looking at the dot and the inside corner. The picture shows a thin line that runs across the plate and kinks up the wall at the corner. The shell hangs from a loose support through a two-axis trim stage the size of a matchbox; software reads the kink and nudges the trim until the corner is where the dot is.

## The proposal

Measure the seam **relative to the dot in the gun's own frame**, instead of measuring the gun in the world and the seam in the world and subtracting. A camera on the shell moves with the gun, so the dot is a fixed point in its image (a calibration constant) and only the corner moves. A line laser on the shell (green, so the camera can tell it from the red dot) draws the profile: plate, corner, wall. The corner is where the line kinks, which is far easier to find than the faint edge between two shiny surfaces.

Because the error is measured where it matters, the support may be loose. The heavy, imprecise part (whatever holds the gun and umbilical) only needs to put the gun in a working neighbourhood; a small stage does the fine work. The scene draws the trim stage as two short axes (radial, vertical), the support as a compliant elastic whose stretch is the "support drift" nobody commands.

## What carries loads, what establishes position, what is free or restrained

- **Loads.** The printed shell carries the gun to a lug on the housing (drawn); a short rod carries the lug to the trim stage carriage; the stage base hangs from a soft support (wave 3: three are drawn, each on vertical elastics from an overhead arm or a swing arm and vertical guide rod, because a support that is soft in translation still has to hold the beam's turn, see entry 7); the umbilical hangs to a hook (fixed; its weight and stiffness are unknown). Nothing here is measured. Where the gun's weight really balances, and whether the umbilical pulls on the shell, are **[unknown]**.
- **Position.** No world reference at all. The corner in the image is the reference. The rotator's degrees give position along the seam (existing controller).
- **Free / restrained / driven.** The gun is restrained by the support in five ways loosely and driven in two (radial, vertical trim). Tilt about the three rotation axes is left as set by hand; the sensor sees tilt in section only partly (the slope of the profile lines).

## What software could command, observe, and what stays manual

- **Command:** trim radial and vertical (proposed axes, +/-6 mm illustrative), line laser on/off, rotator speed (existing).
- **Observe:** corner minus dot in the gun frame (radial and vertical), whether the corner is in view, the wire tip and dot in the same image, the profile's slopes. It does **not** observe the gun's position in the world, the support drift itself, or its own bias.
- **Manual / unresolved:** placing the gun in the working neighbourhood; calibrating the dot pixel and the profile-to-corner mapping (needs a ground-truth view: eyes-07); cleaning the lens.

## What was tried to break it

1. **A camera on the housing, 150 mm or more behind the nozzle.** In the scene this reports "corner not visible: blocked by gun". Assumption behind the first sketch: that a camera on the far end of the shell has an easy view down the barrel. What breaks: looking almost along the beam, the camera sees the nozzle tip standing in front of the dot and the corner beside it; the view must be more than about 12 to 15 degrees off the beam axis (nozzle proxy radius 2 to 5 mm at 16 mm from the dot, illustrative). What the repair changes: the camera moves onto the barrel, 30 to 130 mm behind the tip at a 27 mm stalk (poses A and C in the scene). What it leaves uncertain: a camera 60 to 110 mm from the dot is close to the spatter; the nozzle shape is a proxy.
2. **Another gun pose.** In pose B the line laser is blocked at clock angles 0 and 60 degrees and the corner is lost at 60 and 120 for some positions (swept in the scene: see the visibility grid: A and C see both everywhere for z of 40 and 85 mm; B does not). Assumption: that one mounting serves every pose. What changes: the mounting has to be chosen per pose, or the pose family fixed. Uncertain: nobody has said which poses will be used.
3. **Closing the loop on a biased estimate.** With a calibration bias of 0.30 mm radial and -0.20 vertical, six corrections drive the estimate to about zero and leave the dot wrong by minus the bias (0.27 / -0.19 mm measured in the scene, `window.__loop()`). Assumption: that a sensor that says zero means zero. What changes: nothing in the loop; the fix is outside it: a direct view (eyes-07) measures the bias. Uncertain: whether the bias is constant across poses.
4. **Tracking the runout.** With the runout limits (0.25 / 0.30 mm TIR) exaggerated 20 times and a 4 Hz loop with gain 0.6, the scene's closed loop follows the wobble to 0.10 mm rms (peak 0.18) against 1.41 mm rms open (a 1.0 / -0.5 mm drift included). At the real amplitude the seam moves at most 0.02 mm/s, so the lag error would be about twenty times smaller, roughly 0.005 mm (derived from the rate, calc/runout-rates.mjs). Assumption: that the loop is limited by the runout speed. What it shows: it is not; bias and noise are.
5. **Stage travel.** Trim limited to +/-6 mm (illustrative): beyond it the stage stops and a LIMIT badge shows. A support that drifts more than that needs manual re-placement. Uncertain: how much a loose support really drifts.
6. **The dot is not in the image.** From a camera on the barrel the nozzle tip hides the dot. What this changes: the measurement uses the line's kink beside the nozzle; the dot pixel is a calibration constant. Uncertain: whether the corner stays visible with the wire in the way (at clock angle 120 degrees the wire crosses the view).

### Wave 3: what freedom's exchange (`exchange/freedom--on--eyes-w2.md`) found, and what was done

7. **The support was a symbol, and "loose" was loose in one motion only if something held the rest** *(from freedom's exchange, section 1)*. Conflict, in the drawn variant: one elastic ran from the post top at about 13° above horizontal to the stage block. A line that shallow carries the gun's weight (11.8 N for the illustrative 1.2 kg) only at about 53 N of tension, so it was not a load path; and one line to one lug is a ball joint, so nothing held the beam's turn: in freedom's statics the gun hand-set at pose A settles about 29° away from it with 2 N of umbilical pull, and each further newton moves the dot 1.2 to 1.8 mm radially and 3.3 to 5.7 mm vertically and turns the beam 1.3°, spending the ±6 mm trim in 1 to 1.8 N. Assumption behind it: mine, that "loose" was a property of the elastic; theirs, that the four motions the eye cannot see stay where they were put. What the change alters: the scene now hangs the stage from an overhead arm on vertical elastics and offers three ways of holding its tilt (one elastic, two spread elastics, a swing arm with a vertical guide rod) with a slider for the pull change; per newton (freedom's `calc/eye-support.js`, illustrative): 1.44 / 4.22 mm and 1.31° on one elastic, 0.15 / 2.16 mm and 0.84° on two spread elastics (1.8 N·m/rad), 0.45 / 1.03 mm and 0.02° when the base holds the tilt (100 N·m/rad); the nose seat of freedom-11 (0.1 / 0.01 mm) is the other repair and stays their scene. What it leaves uncertain: the pull itself (freedom-15 lists how to measure it), what holds the tilt in a real support, and the stiffnesses.
8. **The eye's two numbers are held while the beam turns** *(from freedom's exchange, section 1, point 3)*. Conflict: with the loop closed the trim pulls the dot back to the seam, so the corner inset reads zero, while the beam has turned 1.3° per newton about the support's pivot; the eye's r and z are held and the incidence angle and the wire approach are not, and no reading in the scene said so. Assumption behind it: mine, that a measurement in the gun frame is a complete measurement of what matters; theirs, that the four motions the eye cannot see are not the eye's concern. What the change alters: the scene now shows it (the corner inset's beam angle moves with the pull, a badge says that the eye reads radial and vertical only, the panel says what the lines can tell). What it leaves uncertain: whether the turn matters for the weld (nobody has said how many degrees of incidence or wire approach the process tolerates) and how far the turn can go before the corner leaves the camera's view.
9. **Which turns do the two lines resolve?** *(freedom's question, section 1)* Conflict: freedom asked whether the two slopes give the barrel's pitch and roll, so that the tail winches could close on the same image. Assumption behind it: theirs, that a line's slope carries the tilt (with a 0.35 mm, 7 px shift at the far end of the line per degree); mine, until I ran it, that the lines and the dot's spot would together give all the turns. Re-run in `calc/line-laser-pose.mjs` (linearised, 0.3 px line-fit noise with 30 points per line, illustrative). A straight corner lets any camera observe five numbers of the gun's pose (radial, vertical and three turns; a slide along the seam changes nothing in the picture). One fan draws two lines: four numbers, and the spot along the beam adds nothing about the turns. So **one turn is always blind**: with the fan through the radial direction (as first drawn) it is the turn about the radial axis; other fan rolls give a mixture. What the lines do resolve at pose A, camera 85 mm behind the tip on top: dot radial 0.012 mm, vertical 0.015 mm, turn about the tangent 0.15°, turn about the vertical axis 0.8° (the short wall line only). A gravity sensor (0.3°) closes the blind one; a crossed second fan (a cross-hair module, $35 on Prime) gives eight numbers and closes all five to about 0.1° (radial 0.002 mm, vertical 0.006 mm, turns 0.08 to 0.14°), the vertical-axis turn included; camera clock and position change the figures by factors of two to ten (0.28° for the vertical-axis turn at clock 30°, 3° at clock -30°). Freedom's shift figure is 6 times too large here: the plate line's image angle changes 0.2° per degree of turn about the tangent (about 1.2 px, 0.06 mm over an 18 mm line), because the camera sees the line obliquely; it is the fit's precision, not the shift, that makes 0.15° readable. What it changes: the tail winches of a nose seat can be closed on the picture only if a second fan or a gravity sensor is added (eyes-11's IMU is the cheap way), and the vertical-axis turn needs the second fan. What it leaves uncertain: every figure assumes the line is found without bias; a hobby cross-hair has uncalibrated fan angles (two more unknowns for eyes-07's phantom to fix); glare doubles in a corner.
10. **A control that did not behave** *(mine, found while building 7)*. Conflict: the line-laser plane was recomputed from world +X at every pose, so it followed the gun's rotation instead of being fixed in the gun; that is right only at the reference rotation and would have hidden exactly the tilt entries 8 and 9 are about. Assumption behind it: that the fan is a world-frame line. What the change alters: the plane is fixed in the gun frame (through the emitter and the dot, rolled about that axis by a slider), so a tilted gun draws a different profile. What it leaves uncertain: how well a printed emitter mount holds the plane.
11. **Friction gives steps, and the eye's figures were for drift** *(from freedom's exchange, section 1)*. Conflict: the loop was sized by rate (drift slower than about 0.1 mm/s), and a seat or a rail slips: a 3 mm step takes four corrections at gain 0.6 and 4 Hz to get under 0.1 mm (1.0 s, 8 mm of bead at 8 mm/s); 1 mm takes three. Assumption behind it: mine, that the support's disturbance is a drift; theirs, that it is steps. What the change alters: what the support must supply is a neighbourhood and steps no bigger than the trim range, not only slow drift. What it leaves uncertain: the size of a real slip.
12. **The camera and the support's cup want the same barrel** *(from freedom's exchange, section 1)*. Conflict: with the nose seat at 70 mm, 34 of 66 camera stations are clear, against 47 with a rigid lug; ahead of the collar every clock angle is clear, behind it the top and the +side are taken by the cone; the scene of freedom-11 draws it, and eyes-15 found the same for rings (a tip ring 15 mm behind the nozzle costs 9 points of the dome). Assumption behind it: mine, that the barrel is free for the eye. What the change alters: nothing here; left standing, drawn in `freedom-11-eye-on-the-seat`. What it leaves uncertain: whether the camera can stand 30 to 55 mm behind the tip (the spatter-worst place) or the cup ring can be opened on the camera side.

## Branches and combinations

- Same eye, used as a **probe**: sweep the trim axis across the seam and find the corner from the break of the spot's path (`eyes-06-dot-as-probe`); this replaces the line laser and needs no profile calibration.
- **Ground truth** for the eye's bias: `eyes-07-sectioned-tube`.
- **Coarse pose from tags** plus this eye for the last millimetre: `eyes-03-marker-cube`, `eyes-04-proximity-skin` (a second, light-free measurement of the same two numbers).
- **Feed-forward**: `eyes-09-scan-then-weld` uses the same eye in a dry turn to build a seam map.
- **Combination (freedom)**: `freedom-11-eye-on-the-seat` puts this eye and its trim on the nose seat of freedom-01b: what the trim has to absorb per newton, and the camera map with the cup and collar as occluders. Both originals stay.
- **Hand-held**: `eyes-17-lit-bar-brake-wall` uses this eye as the sensor of a feedback layer on a hand-held gun (an LED bar and a brake wall); `eyes-18-what-the-estimate-owes` is the lens on what such a layer needs from the estimate.

## Assumptions

- Gun proxy: 253 x 143 x 34 mm envelope **[manual]** p.17; barrel, housing, grip, wire guide, the 60 degree pitch and 16 mm clearance are illustrative (kit).
- Camera 1280 px across, field of view 15 to 70 degrees; mm-per-pixel = 2 d tan(fov/2)/1280 (readouts in the scene: about 0.05 mm per pixel at 105 mm, 32 degrees). **illustrative**
- Estimate = exact scene offset + chosen bias + pseudo-noise; the scene only derives *availability* from geometry. **illustrative**
- Trim stage +/-6 mm, gain 0.6, 4 corrections per second. **illustrative**
- Runout limits 0.25 / 0.30 mm TIR and 5 to 15 mm/s speeds **[repo]** weld-rotation-rig.md; the seam speed figures are **[derived]**.
- Umbilical minimum bend 350 mm emitting / 240 stored **[manual]** p.20; the badge is an illustrative check.
- Wave 3: per-newton drift and tilt of the three supports from freedom's statics (1.2 kg, centre of mass 178 mm behind the nozzle, elastic 0.5 N/mm; illustrative); the pose panel: linearised, straight corner, 0.3 px line noise, N = 30, gravity sensor 0.3° (illustrative; `calc/line-laser-pose.mjs`).
