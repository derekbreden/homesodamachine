# eyes-15 What the holder hides (branch of Derek's three examples, and of room-08)

Scene: `scenes/eyes-15-what-the-holder-hides/`. Origin: Derek's examples seen through the eyes framing (wave 2), plus room-08's operator. Maturity: developed. Reuses the dome of `eyes-02`.

**Picture it.** The dome of camera stations from eyes-02, but now something holds the gun: a hand and forearm, a monitor arm with a stage above the shell, a table with a bridge and an arm to the shell's lug, or rings, two bungees and a gripping arm. Each holder is drawn only as far as it can hide something. Violet dots on the dome are the places the holder blocks.

## The proposal

Derek's examples (the monitor arm, the table opening, the rings and bungees) and room-08's hand are each a *thing between the observers and the dot*. Once eyes-02 showed the wall hides 55 % of the upper hemisphere and the gun 3 %, the question is what a holder adds. The scene tests every holder against the same dome and the same fixed-eye presets (beside, far side low, above), with and without the holder, and lets the parts be moved: the side the operator's forearm leaves toward, the side the monitor arm's column stands, how far behind the nozzle the tip ring sits, whether a third ring is added.

## What carries loads, what establishes position, what is free or restrained

Not addressed: this is an analysis view. The holders' loads are in their own scenes (freedom-01, freedom-02, room-01).

## What software could command, observe, and what stays manual

Nothing is commanded. Observed (drawn geometry only): the share of the dome that sees the dot, with the holder and without; which fixed eyes survive. Manual: choosing which side the operator or the arm comes from; aiming the eyes.

## What was tried to break it

(Dome radius 300 mm; illustrative geometry; bare gun sees 42 % of the upper hemisphere.)

1. **Assumed:** the holder is what hides the dot. **Found:** the wall does; a holder adds 0 to 10 points.
2. **Rings (freedom-01).** Only the ring around the tip stands in the sight lines: 15 mm behind the nozzle costs 8.9 points of the dome and 14.5 points of seam views; 30 mm 6.4 and 9.3; 45 mm 4.3 and 5.2; 80 mm 2.6 and 1.4; 130 mm 1.2 and 0.9. A third ring near the middle costs about one point more. Repair: put the tip ring 100 mm or more back (freedom-01b's nose seat is 60 to 110 mm from the dot already).
3. **Hand (room-08).** Forearm leaving behind the gun (170 to 270 degrees): 0 to 1 point. Coming in from the +Y side (90 to 120 degrees): 6 to 10 points of the dome and 6 of the seam views (9 at 200 mm, 10 at 300 and 450 for 90 degrees; 60 degrees: 1.8). The fixed stations are safe: swept over the operator's side at 15 degree steps, the Beside and Far-side presets never lose an eye to the forearm, and the Above preset loses one eye only near 75 degrees. So the frame's eyes work whichever hand holds the gun; the band the forearm removes matters only for a camera placed in it.
4. **Monitor arm (freedom-02).** The link and stage sit above the dome: 0 points at 200 to 300 mm; 0.6 to 3 points at 450 mm depending on the side. It hides little; it decides where a camera can be mounted.
5. **Table opening (room-01).** 0.4 points. The slab is at the rim plane, so nothing seen from above is under it; the bridge at x = 100 hides only what the wall hides. The post and arm to the collar lug block one of two "Above" eyes (250 degrees, 50 degrees up). The table's contribution is a plane at rim height to stand cameras on.
6. **What changes once the sight lines are known:** the eyes go between about 100 and 190 degrees round the tube whatever holds the gun. The choices that move the answer are the hand's side and the tip ring's distance.
