Scene: `scenes/use-15-examples-as-days/index.html`. Origin: branch of `freedom-01-ring-bungee`, `freedom-02-balanced-arm` and `room-01-table-opening` (Derek's three examples), read as lanes; it also answers travel-07's request for state columns. ## Picture it

Three schematic elevations side by side (rings, bungees and an arm; a monitor arm with vernier and brake; the table opening with a shelf and a gantry), redrawn for each of eleven states of one closure: load, indicate, plate, bring, trim, tack, dry lap, weld, escape, snip, swap. Under each panel: who carries the gun, what the hand does, what the eye is for, what software commands, and a red diamond where the gun's pose changes owner. Below: bars for each arrangement (and today's hand-held sequence) of minutes with a hand at the tube, a hand holding the gun, a hand only firing or cutting, and hands free.

## The proposal

Read the examples as sequences, not positions. Four things show.

1. **Parking is a state and costs each example something different.** The plate is seated and the tube indicated with the recess open, so the gun is out of the way for three states and again for the swap: free in the table opening (the hand works under the table), a swing held by friction on the monitor arm, a lift of the whole hanging assembly in the suspension (the wires and bungees are in the hand's reach at the tube).
2. **The handovers are not where the drawings put the effort.** The table has almost none between machine parts; the monitor arm has three (hand to friction to brake, brake back to hand for the lift-away, hand parks it); the suspension has one that matters, the arm lowering the gun into the rings so the wires take the weight (`use-14`: letting the arm go there leaves the bungees holding).
3. **Hanging and opening the loops.** freedom-01 has the loops opened for each tube. In the sequence nothing needs that: the arm lifts the whole hanging assembly for the plate and the swap, so the loops stay closed on the shell; the umbilical loop, which cannot be threaded over the connectors, is opened once at fitting. Opening and closing them per tube would add two hand-on-gun states to every closure.
4. **All three take the hand off the gun; none takes it off the tube.** At illustrative durations the tube states (load, indicate, plate, swap) are 11 of about 15.6 minutes for every row; the gun states are the rest. The monitor arm keeps today's two big hand actions (bring the head, lift it away) and removes only the hold.

## What carries the loads, what establishes position, what is free or restrained

Drawn per example in each state; the caption's "holds" line is the answer in time. Suspension: arm (rigid grip) with rings shedding weight. Monitor arm: gas-spring arm, friction window, brake, vernier. Table: shelf and gantry, stiff and motorised.

## What software could command, observe, and what stays manual

Per state and per example in the captions (arm axes, vernier, brake, shelf, gantry, rotator; camera, arm force, encoder, end-stops). A hand fires and cuts in every example; software never fires.

## What was tried to break it

1. **Durations are placeholders** (except the 48.6 s lap). *Change:* sliders for load, indicate, plate, bring, trim, tack, swap. *Leaves:* Derek's real ones.
2. **ESCAPE follows the shared context, not guide 46.** *Change:* a toggle removes it.
3. **Cell wording is mine and one line long.** *Leaves:* branches inside an example are not shown (freedom-01 has rubber loops, a seat, a ball-ended grip).
