## Findings that cut across the exchange

Agent findings; numbers are illustrative unless an idea file tags a source.

- **What is in series with the encoder decides what a touch reports.** A touch at 0.2 N reads 1.3 mm short on a soft axis, 0.1 mm on a rigid-grip arm and micrometres on a stage. (freedom on eyes-05)
- **A map learned in a dry turn holds the periodic part, not a step.** The bead brings load changes: wire feed, gas, the fibre's own recoil (0.4 to 4 N at the 350 mm emitting radius against 0.9 to 8.7 N at 240 mm). The dry turn has to be taken in the weld's state. (freedom on eyes-09, freedom-13, travel-18, datum-14)
- **The judge's own bias is replayed as seam.** Any observer error that repeats with table angle looks like the seam. (datum on trials-04)
- **A camera sees a spot where the beam meets a surface, not the dot's 3-D position.** Sliding the gun along its beam leaves the spot on the corner unchanged. (eyes on room-06, trials-20)
- **A seat that hangs from the same post as the arm shares its compliance.** The load path from dock to corner has links the dock does not check. (room on use-02, datum on trials-02)
- **The second closure inverts the tube.** A crown or presetter that stays on its tube serves one closure. (use on travel-14b)
- **The first number in a yardstick becomes the requirement.** If the hand is scored by the camera judge, the hand's figure sets the bar. (datum on trials-11)
- **Soft carriers need a mass window.** A balanced arm forgives a limited error in payload (about ±0.09 kg at one drawn geometry). Bought balanced arms exist for every payload from 0.25 to 9 kg. (borrowed on freedom-02, borrowed-14)
- **Six lines from above are taut only inside a cone of pulls.** The fibre's pull decides which layouts stay taut. (borrowed on freedom-03, borrowed-16)
- **Sight lines from above are the constraint, and the holders rarely change them.** Bare gun: 42 % of the upper hemisphere sees the dot, the wall blocks 55 %, the gun 3 %; a monitor arm removes 0 to 3 points, a table opening 0.4. (eyes-15)
- **A tabletop flush to the rim plane is a surface plate.** The tube is then found by touch from the table, and the tube-length spread stops mattering to a room-referenced gun. (datum-18)
- **If the filler is already in the corner, or the ring carries the wire guide, the reason for the tangent attitude goes.** The gun can then stand in the radial plane over the bore, its centre of mass falls from 118 to 50 mm, and the weld can go either way round (travel-16); the cable still leaves radially outward.

## Derek's examples through the four framings that began without them

**datum** — Suspension: the explorers who began with Derek's loops (freedom-01, 01b, 01c, room-11) asked which element locates on each axis and found that the stiffest one does, so the loops carry and the arm locates; the arm's base was still assumed to be in the room. My framing asks what the arm's base takes its reference from. Put the base on the rim (a crown) and the tube's own runout (0.125 mm radial, 0.15 mm face) never reaches the gun, while the loops stay soft and in the room. That splits the weight path (room, soft, long wires) from the location path (work, stiff on radial and vertical, free on the tangent), which is what Derek's third ring was reaching for. Consequences the earlier scenes did not have: the split between the two wires moves the gun's weight moment to the loops, so the crown needs no counterweight for the weight (0.8 kg with no loops, none with 85 % relief and the split set for the weights); the cable's pull becomes the crown's main load (an auto-null on the two wire tensions can retune it); and a stiff vertical support anchored in the room and attached to a gun that rides the rim fights the rim by stiffness times face wobble (30 N for a 200 N/mm wire against 7.4 N of crown), so the supports must be forces. Table opening: room-01 reads the table as the rim plane; I read it as a datum a probe can compare the rim against, which turns the shelf from a precision axis into a servo on a null (tube length drops out; dot-height error over twelve tubes 913 micrometres by dead reckoning, 353 flush, 25 with a plate touch, illustrative spreads) and finds the tube in a loose 160 mm hole by three wall touches (two wall points 15 mm either side differ by 0.485 of the tangent offset, so 20 micrometres of noise gives the centre to about 60 micrometres), the job the fitted collar was meant to do. Both examples are read as questions of where the reference comes from, not of where the stiffness is. The monitor arm follows the suspension case: an arm-class support whose base stands on the crown is the same statics with a friction window in place of loops. What the explorers who began with the examples did not see: the reference can be checked against the work at run time by a probe, dropping a link from the chain instead of choosing it at design time.

**eyes** — Seen through observation, Derek's examples were drawn for their loads, and for the observers the constraint is the tube, not the holder. With eyes-02's dome (bare gun: 42% of the upper hemisphere sees the dot; the wall blocks 55%, the gun 3%), the holders add 0-10 points: monitor arm 0-3 (its link and stage stand above the dome, hiding little unless a camera is 450 mm out in line), table opening 0.4 (the slab is at the rim plane so nothing seen from above is under it; the bridge at x=100 hides only what the wall hides; the arm to the lug costs one 'Above' eye), rings 4-9 depending on how far behind the nozzle the tip ring sits (15 mm costs 9 points and 14.5 points of seam views, 45 mm 4, 130 mm 1: the one part of the suspension example that stands in the sight lines), hand 0-10 depending on the operator's side (6-10 from the +Y side, 0-1 behind the gun), though the Beside and Far-side presets never lose an eye to the forearm at any operator side. What each example changes for the observers is where a camera can be mounted, not what it sees: the table gives a flat plane at rim height for cameras on 20 mm posts at the far-side low station; the monitor arm is a carrier that follows the gun (a gun-borne eye); rings give nothing to mount on; a hand gives a moving occluder. In the automated-setup vision, PTZ cameras are moving windows, not sensors: 0.1 degree of pointing error is 0.86 mm at the far wall, 24 pixels at 4K and 9 degrees, 17 times a 0.05 mm dot correction, so an AI iterating for days needs every picture to carry its own reference (two features in one frame; the tube's diameter as the ruler) and its judge must not be the camera closing the loop, or the loop's bias is invisible to it. The vision's first measurement is not of the gun: measure the tube, gun-free, at loading (eyes-16), because plate seat depth is what every motor stage is waiting for. The explorers who began with the examples drew the monitor arm, the opening and the rings for their loads and constraints; the observers' sight lines, the camera's own pointing and scale error, and the tube-first measurement did not appear.

**borrowed** — I began without the examples. Through the borrowed framing they became questions about products that already exist, and the answer to most is a range with a price and a delivery date. (1) Monitor arm: a shipped balanced arm exists for every payload from 0.25 to 9 kg (mic booms, gas-spring mic arms, monitor arms, tool balancers, $20-$180, two-day), so the payload floor is a selection once the total is weighed; what nobody had was the tolerance in grams (friction/(g L cos th) = +-0.09 kg at freedom's numbers), that the vernier and camera are payload, that a zero-length spring (Anglepoise) balances at every angle, and that a monitor arm is a SCARA whose two swivels carry no gravity torque. (2) Suspension: the openable loop is a rubber-cushioned P-clamp whose friction the screw sets; the wire held in Z is a spring balancer's cable (a force element); two opposing balancers are a zero-stiffness horizontal support; a bungee's stiffness is EA/L; the two-loop hinge is a stabiliser-sled gimbal whose bottom weight (keel) sets the spring, which freedom-01/08 lacked. (3) Table opening: the countertop-height gantry is a CNC or printer gantry with G-code, homing and probing included (a router spindle is the gun's mass class), sit-stand frames and drawer slides state loads and never resolution or side play. (4) PTZ cameras are look-here actuators with presets and no stated protocol; the desktop arms on Prime carry 0.25-0.5 kg, only a maker's cobot states 3 kg and +-0.02 mm; a worm-geared telescope mount gives two encoded rotations with an open command set for $525; a hexapod gives six with the pivot as a coordinate, which removes the axis-through-the-dot problem the ring pivots fought. Across all of them, the best-selling parts state a step or a load and never a repeatability: it has to be measured.

**use** — I began without the examples. Read as sequences, they differ from the position-and-statics readings of freedom-01, freedom-02 and room-01 in six ways (drawn in use-15-examples-as-days, and for the suspension's release in use-14-handover-shift). (1) Parking is a state: the recess has to be open for indicating, seating the plate and the swap, so the gun is out of the way three times; it is free in the table opening (the hand works under the table), a swing held by a friction window on the monitor arm, and a lift of the whole hanging assembly in the suspension, with wires and bungees in the hand's reach. (2) The loops: freedom-01 has them opened per tube; nothing in the sequence needs that (the arm lifts the whole hanging assembly), the umbilical loop is opened once at fitting, and per-tube opening would add two hand-on-gun states per closure. (3) The handovers are not where the drawings put effort: the table has almost none between machine parts, the monitor arm has three (hand to friction to brake, brake back to hand for the lift-away, hand parks it), the suspension has one that matters, the arm lowering the gun so the wires take the weight, and letting the arm go there leaves the bungees holding: release shift 0.77 mm RMS, and 1 N of weld load moves a 0.15 N/mm bungee 6.7 mm, so rings carry and hold a neighbourhood but something stiffer must hold the weld. (4) The monitor arm keeps today's two big hand actions (bring the head to the recess, lift it away) and removes only the hold, which is the least disruptive property; its cost is that the neighbourhood is forgotten each time. (5) All three take the hand off the gun and none takes it off the tube: at illustrative durations 11 of about 15.6 minutes per closure are tube states (load, indicate, plate, swap) in every row, and the rows differ only in the hatched (holding), amber (firing/cutting) and teal (free) blocks. (6) A hand at the tube and a hand on the gun are different resources, and a hand that only fires is not a hand that holds.
