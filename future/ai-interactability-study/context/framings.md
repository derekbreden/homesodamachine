# The eight ways of seeing the whole problem

Each framing is a way to read the entire situation — gun, tube, plate, rotator, supports, cables, operation, software control and observation. None is a department.

## Begin with Derek's examples

**freedom** — *Freedom and force.* An arrangement is a set of motions that are free, restrained, driven or locked, and the forces that push along each: gravity, umbilical pull, trigger, wobble, actuators, a hand. Precision belongs to whichever motions are constrained at the moment it matters, not to how stiff or heavy the parts are. Questions: which motion is free, which force displaces it, what happens at each contact, when do the constraints change, who supplies precision at each moment.

**room** — *The room is the first stage.* Positioning begins in the space the gun and rotator already occupy: bench, table, opening, wall, ceiling, floor, shelf, frame, rails. The coarse stage may be furniture or building; the fine stage is what remains. Questions: what structure around the tube and gun can carry, guide or reference them, what is ordinary and available at volume, how does the coarse stage shrink what the fine stage has to do.

**travel** — *Split the travel.* The relative pose between gun, tube and room can be produced by moving the gun, the tube, a support or a setup adjustment; by motor, hand, spring or gravity; at setup or during the weld. A small angle at the grip is a long travel at the dot. Questions: which body moves and which stays, what range and resolution each stage needs, which stage's error shows up as which error at the dot.

**trials** — *The machine runs trials.* The station is an instrument for many repeated dry-run experiments conducted by software — an AI iterating for hours or days across tubes, judging by cameras and the laser dot. Questions: what software commands and what it sees; what resets between trials, above all the swap from one tube to the next; what makes one trial comparable to the next; what the AI learns about the rig; which partial versions exist (observe only, command only, one motorised axis).

## Begin without them

**datum** — *The seam is the datum.* The pose that matters is gun-to-corner, and the corner belongs to each tube and plate. Questions: which features of the work — rim, plate face, ports, wall, the joint itself — can establish or verify position; can the gun be located relative to the work instead of the room; what does a reference on the work make unnecessary.

**eyes** — *Seeing first.* Begin from what can be observed about the dot, the seam, the gun's pose, the tube and the wire, by cameras, markers, light, touch, electrical continuity, sound, inertial sensors. Questions: what can be seen from where; what the rim, the gun and the glare on stainless hide; which measurements are direct and which inferred; how the arrangement changes once it is known how the gun will be seen.

**borrowed** — *Borrowed from elsewhere.* Mass-produced products sold for other purposes already do parts of this job: cameras and photo supports, 3D printers, CNC and engravers, telescopes and microscopes, medical and lab stands, furniture, garage and automotive parts, hobby robotics, drones, appliances. Questions: which product or mechanism does a piece of the job, what is taken from it, what changes, how fast it is in hand.

**use** — *The day of use.* Someone or something sets up for a tube, establishes a working neighbourhood, dry-runs the dot, welds, retracts, swaps, repeats. The arrangement is a sequence of states with handovers. Questions: which states it must take, what changes between setup and weld, where a hand or an eye is essential or wasted, which partial arrangement already improves the day.
