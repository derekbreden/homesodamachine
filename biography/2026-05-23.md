## The welding

The carbonator vessel is the gating part of the integrated appliance — every other subsystem is downstream of whether the vessel can exist as a thing in the world. The vessel is custom-fabricated: a commodity 5" OD × 0.065" wall 316 welded SS round tube, two 1/4"-thick 316 SS plates laser-cut by SendCutSend, joined with an XLaserlab X1 Pro handheld laser welder Derek imported from China.

Derek had never welded before — the title of the first published video is exactly that, *"I've Never Welded."* Production notes for that first video session were committed April 13, but the video itself didn't ship until May 4, by which point Derek was on attempt four or five at learning to weld. Practice in between happened on 304L test vessels (the plan-B fallback material, cheaper to learn on than the 316L plan-A stock).

Through May, six videos shipped on the channel — four on welding, two on adjacent threads:

- **May 4 — *First Weld***. *"I've Never Welded — First Welds on the Xlaserlab X1 Pro."* Three and a half minutes of attempt four or five at learning, on the way to a stainless steel carbonation tank.
- **May 8 — *First Tap***. *"I've Never Tapped — First NPT Threads in 316L Stainless."* Six and a half minutes of cutting the first of forty NPT threads by hand into the end caps — adjacent to the welding work since direct-tapped 1/4" NPT into the end plates means no weld bungs.
- **May 9 — *Second Weld***. *"Stuck Less That Time — Laser Welding A 304L Pressure Vessel."* Seven and a half minutes naming the wire-stick problem from session one, fumbling a patch in session two, committing to a trim-the-wire-every-time method in session three.
- **May 10 — *Faucet Shell***. *"PET-CF Beat Me 8 Times."* Ninth print attempt at the Touch-Flo faucet shell in carbon-fiber PET, after eight that clogged the hotend or damaged the right Induction Heating Assembly.
- **May 18 — *One Out of Three***. Five minutes of welding stainless on the X1 Pro — three attempts at the same bead, two stuck, one clean break. The bushing delay on the trigger-release cycle cranked from the factory 400 ms to 5000 ms.
- **May 22 — *Don't Let Go***. The wire-stick problem solved by combining techniques pulled from two Chinese welding shorts — different welder brands across the two shorts, neither the X1 Pro, but the move repeated across both: keep the trigger held, let the safety clip's conductance break on the lift, and the welder treats it as a normal release with the laser stopping and the wire retracting, no stick. Three pull-aways in a row with no stick, captured on camera.

About five and a half weeks of welding work — from the April 13 video-session planning through the May 22 wire-stick technique landing. A technique learned not from a manual but by pattern-matching across short videos in a language Derek doesn't read, applied to a welder neither video's owner had ever used. Practice on 304L test vessels (the plan-B material, ER308L filler) before the ER316L filler arrived April 27 for the plan-A 316L work that comes next.

This is what cumulative weeks of effort look like. Not a course taken, not a manual followed, not a teacher consulted. Five and a half weeks of trying, observing, iterating, and publishing each attempt for the channel.

## Marketing as a turning point

The day after the founding doc was written, `marketing/target-market.md` appeared. It opened with *"The target market is people who hate the cans"* — Steve Martin from *The Jerk*, "He hates these cans!" — and proceeded to define everything else as qualifying filter. Five competitor docs landed the same day.

That folder didn't sit static. Through April and May it grew into the current Founder Edition / Standard Edition tier structure with the "rings of trust" internal plan — the first 10 units going to people Derek knows directly or one degree out, with the $7,500 Founder Edition as the public anchor for cold buyers but actual ring-1 sales happening well below it. The trust-gap analysis named the central problem: at 10 units, the brand is not a company, it's a person — Derek's face, his kitchen, his story.

That positioning wasn't separate from the engineering. It was its catalyst. Knowing the buyer hated the cans meant the product had to be *the can dispensed differently* — same product, colder and fizzier, on tap. Knowing the buyer was a homeowner with 2+ diet sodas/day at $200K+ income meant the appliance had to fit under-counter and run on plug-and-play 120 VAC. Knowing the trust-gap was closed by Derek-as-person meant the YouTube channel and the kitchen on camera weren't promotional extras — they were the product.

Writing the founding doc was the hinge. Articulating where the prototype had landed made what the project actually was the next legible question. The marketing folder is what answering that looked like, and the engineering work that followed was aimed at that buyer.

## The engineering

A great deal of effort, across many subsystems running in parallel:

- **The cold core.** Vertical 316L vessel + copper evaporator coil wrapped tight + 3D-printed inner shell + pour-in-place 2 lb closed-cell PU foam + flavor reservoirs nested between inner and outer shells, pre-chilling passively to 8-15°C + outer shell + outer skin.

- **The refrigeration.** April 16 pivot from the RIGID DV1910E compressor kit (~$600) to harvested-from-ice-maker (~$60 donor): compressor + condenser + capillary tube + factory drier all preserved from a donor unit, only the factory finger-plate evaporator discarded and replaced with the custom wound coil. R-600a refrigerant procedure committed (EPA Section 608 venting carve-out, no certification required). The full subsystem comparison is genuinely ambiguous — the RIGID path was abandoned before resolving whether it would need an additional evaporator coil on top.

- **The faucet.** Touch-Flo R2031 ordered April 23, delivered April 26, valve body measurement + reference-solid + custom shell zones 1-6 designed in PET-CF starting April 27. Two-bend gooseneck, three-tube parallel-offset bundle inside. The harvested valve body keeps Westbrass's UL/ASSE/NSF certifications intact through the appliance.

- **The reservoirs.** Custom printed food-grade PETG, shaped to nest the cold-core envelope rather than fit around an off-the-shelf bottle. Level sensing per reservoir via 4 reed switches outside + magnetic donut float on a welded 316L SS rod inside.

- **The compressor shroud.** Non-combustible sheet metal (G90 galvanized) enclosing the compressor's terminal block + PTC start relay — the only ignition-risk parts in the R-600a compartment. SF76E thermal fuse inside. MQ-6 hydrocarbon sensor on the rear interior enclosure wall.

- **The electronics shelf.** Full AC distribution + relays + Mean Well PSU + ESP32 + MCP23017 + ULN2803 + L298N + regulators on one assembly behind the C14 inlet.

- **The backflow vent monitoring.** Multiplex 19-0897 ASSE 1022 backflow preventer's atmospheric vent terminating over an internal drip pan with a moisture sensor wired to the ESP32. When vent #1 leaks, firmware fires the iOS app.

- **The CAD work itself.** A `cadlib` emerged: snap-fit primitives with deliberate vocabulary, a `WorldWorkplane` abstraction handling list-arg orientation, `revolve_rect` helpers, semantic constants, companion `.md` notes for the trickier modules. The AI design wall from April 5's doc is six weeks behind. The AI is still the typist; the typist works in proper engineering grammar now.

- **The assembly procedures.** Created in two batches — May 13 (cold-core, pressure-vessel, refrigerant-loop) and May 18 (electronics-shelf, faucet-and-umbilical, enclosure-mechanical, internal-plumbing, firmware-and-commissioning, wiring, acceptance-and-burn-in, finish-pack-ship). The per-unit production sequence is end-to-end. Each one a real procedure, not a sketch.

- **The regulatory work.** ASSE 1022 (the correct standard for beverage dispensing, not the more common 1024). UL 943 ground-fault protection via the front-flush GFCI. DOT recertification cadence on the customer's CO2 cylinder. NSF/FDA materials throughout — 316L SS wetted, food-grade PETG, NSF-rated tubing.

The bulk of the period went here. 1,425 commits across seven weeks, May 17 alone at 222. Most days were 20-50 commits. The work was sustained.

## The website, as enabler

A significant amount of effort went into the public website at homesodamachine.com. Honest framing: that effort was a side effect of needing live mobile interaction with artifacts. Being able to pull up the 3D viewer on a phone, see a CAD change an agent made, react to it from anywhere. The website is the surface that makes that possible — the same Node server hosts both the public landing and the parts viewer, dev-mode toggles via localStorage.

The website is not the work. It's an enabler.

There's a second job the website could do, not yet fully realized: the most likely path to a ring-1 buyer. The mechanism: in conversation, Derek pulls out a phone, shows a 3D view of an interesting part. The listener notices the URL is a public website. They visit later, on their own time, and discover depth — assembled subsystems, exploded views, harvested parts shown alongside the printed shells designed around them. The depth visible from a casual visit closes more of the trust-gap than any pitch could.

The future the website is reaching toward: complete navigation, drill-down into specific parts, assembled views with good-enough placeholders for the off-the-shelf components, not just the printed ones. The Touch-Flo with its custom shell is one example of where the model already shows harvested + printed together. The compressor with its custom shroud is the next subsystem to get the same treatment.

That's hope, not yet done. The direction.

## Where it is now [May 23, 2026]

Six welding videos shipped, the wire-stick technique landed. The integrated appliance's subsystem architecture is largely written. Most parts ordered or acquired. Cold-core CAD mature enough that the AI can be directed effectively. Pressure testing is the next gating event — hydro at 180 PSI on a real vessel, built with the technique developed across the welding videos. The marketing folder is pointing at the Founder Edition launch but ring-1 will be mediated through personal network with prices well below the public anchor.

The 316L plan-A production vessel hasn't been welded yet — practice welds have happened on 304L plan-B test vessels. The hydro test hasn't been run. The integrated appliance hasn't been assembled. The Founder Edition hasn't shipped.

But the longer-term vision is no longer just designed.
