# Carbonator weld filming plan

Film Derek welding a carbonator end cap while the rotator moves the seam under the hand-held welding head. Capture the work, the geometry that explains it, and the result. The main visual question is simple: **can the moving workpiece make this circular weld easier to control?** Let the actual attempt supply the answer.

The audience needs to understand where the cap meets the tube, what turns, what Derek controls, and what the weld looks like afterward. Those four ideas should each have a clear picture.

## The footage to collect

**B-roll** is the supporting footage shown alongside or under the main story and narration. It includes useful details, demonstrations, atmosphere, and results. Several more specific terms help when planning it:

| Type | Its job here | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Establishing shot | Show where the work happens and how the pieces relate. | Whole rotator, vertical tube, Derek's working position. |
| Insert or detail shot | Make one small thing legible. | The recessed cap meeting the tube bore. |
| Explanation shot | Provide a clear surface for arrows, lines, and labels. | A stationary joint, then the same joint with the head and wire in position, laser disabled. |
| Process coverage | Show an action and its consequence. | Pedal press, tube rotation, pedal release. |
| Cutaway | Give the edit another relevant view. | A close view of the belt while narration explains rotation. |
| Atmosphere | Establish pace, place, or a pause. | An empty, lit bench or the finished assembly resting on it. |
| Result shot | Let the viewer inspect what happened. | The start and finish of the actual bead. |
| Room tone | Supply continuous background sound beneath audio edits. | Thirty seconds of the shop without speech or handling. |

Give each shot a sentence it could accompany. A turning rotator can carry an explanation of consistent motion; an empty bench can carry a short pause. When the narration names a tiny joint feature, show that feature.

## What the reference screenshots teach

The supplied frames from This Old Tony's *Grinding HSS Tools*, around 6:56–7:13, keep the same close view while an arrow changes position and lines explain clearance. From those stills, the useful ingredients are a readable edge, stable composition, directional light, and space around the feature. The screenshots alone do not establish whether the underlying image is held video or a freeze frame; either can support this treatment.

For the carbonator, collect a small family of matching images:

1. **Overall:** the cap inside the tube, so the viewer understands the circular joint.
2. **Joint detail:** one short arc where both the tube's inner wall and the cap face are visible.
3. **Head and wire:** the same detail with the disabled head at the actual working pose.
4. **Motion:** a dry rotation from a fixed view, showing which side of the head the material approaches from.
5. **Result:** the corresponding completed bead, including its start and finish.

The edit can move from the overall view to the detail, trace the unwelded joint, label the two surfaces, add a direction arrow during the dry rotation, and return to the real weld. A cross-section drawing can explain the hidden recess; the photograph establishes where that drawing belongs.

## A short list for the bench

These are the highest priority captures if filming time is limited. Durations are recording targets, not proposed edit lengths.

- [ ] Whole setup, held still for 15–20 seconds.
- [ ] Unwelded joint detail, hands clear, held still for 20–30 seconds.
- [ ] Same detail with the disabled head and wire in working position, 15–20 seconds.
- [ ] Dry rotation, one complete revolution plus a few seconds at either end.
- [ ] Pedal press and release, filmed separately with the laser disabled.
- [ ] Actual weld attempt, uninterrupted from preparation to the immediate aftermath, from a camera position suitable for active laser work.
- [ ] Completed bead at the same viewing angle as the unwelded detail, plus the start and finish.
- [ ] Immediate spoken observation, then 30 seconds of room tone.

Get the unwelded joint before tacking or welding changes it. Capture the actual bead before dressing or cleaning changes its appearance, once access is safe. Record the interior while it remains accessible. These opportunities have more value than extra views of an idle room.

## Camera roles and settings

The available kit is a GoPro HERO13 Black with cap mount, an iPhone, and a DJI Mic Mini transmitter and USB-C receiver. Use the phone for stationary details and the GoPro for a wider record of the work. A camera on a fixed support also gives a useful alternative to the cap view.

The iPhone is both a camera and the current audio recorder. For live speech accompanying GoPro footage, record the DJI receiver into Voice Memos and clap once with both recordings running. For a phone insert, use Camera and record narration afterward. Do not depend on Voice Memos and Camera recording separate tracks simultaneously on that phone.

### GoPro

Use **4K, 30 fps, 16:9, Linear, standard SDR** for this horizontal video. Use HyperSmooth On for the cap view and Off when the camera is firmly mounted for explanation shots. Natural color and High bitrate are useful starting choices; leave shutter on Auto for the first ordinary bench-light test. Use 60 fps only for a specific motion intended to play at half speed.

Full-sensor 8:7 capture is available at 4K and 5.3K in supported frame rates. Choose it when the extra framing room matters, and use a lens mode offered by that preset. The 16:9 Linear profile above is the straightforward choice for showing mechanical geometry. See [GoPro's 8:7 mode information](https://community.gopro.com/s/article/HERO13-Black-8-7-Sensor-Information?language=en_US) and [Protune controls](https://community.gopro.com/s/article/HERO13-Black-Advanced-Protune-Controls-Explained?language=en_US).

The stock lens is a wide view of the work. Establish useful detail by examining a recorded sample at full size; moving the camera closer only helps while the feature remains sharply focused. If the joint is soft or too small, use the phone. A tighter digital view cannot recover missing focus.

### iPhone

Use the rear camera, landscape orientation, ordinary Video mode, and **4K at 30 fps** where supported. Turn HDR Video off where that setting is available to keep the clips convenient to combine with the GoPro's SDR footage. Start at 1×. Move the supported phone toward the work until the joint fills the frame while remaining sharp.

Touch and hold the joint until **AE/AF Lock** appears, then adjust brightness so the metal still shows surface detail. Check the lock after changing the lens, mode, or camera distance. On models with macro video, choose the macro view before the take and let the camera settle; inspect the recording for unwanted lens changes. Apple documents [focus and exposure locking](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/use-camera-tools-iph3dc593597/ios) and [macro photo and video support](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/take-macro-photos-and-videos-iphfaacf2eb0/ios).

If close focus is unavailable at the required distance, back up until the image is sharp and capture a wider detail plus a high-resolution still. Keep the original 4K files. A crop from 3840 × 2160 to a 1920 × 1080 region gives a 2× linear punch-in for a 1080p edit before upscaling is needed; practical sharpness still depends on the source.

## How to make the explanation closeups

Do these with the laser disabled. Reserve a quiet part of the bench where the phone and light can remain in place for several related shots.

1. **Support both the camera and the part.** Use a tripod or rigid mount. Keep the tube seated securely. Set the camera where touching the work does not shake it; a floor tripod isolates it from a vibrating bench better than a clamp attached to that bench.
2. **Find the view that explains the joint.** Start above and to one side of the open tube, looking obliquely into the recessed cap. Adjust until the near rim stops hiding the joint and both mating surfaces are visible. A top view explains the circular path; the oblique detail explains the recess and corner.
3. **Frame one idea.** Let the selected arc occupy roughly half to two-thirds of the picture width, with enough wall and plate around it to identify both surfaces. Leave about a quarter of the frame relatively plain for a label. Keep the main feature away from the bottom strip where subtitles may sit.
4. **Light across the surface.** Put the diffused light above and to one side, initially about 45° from the camera direction. Move it while watching the image. Aim for a bright-to-dark transition that separates the wall from the plate without washing the joint into white glare. The angle visible on the screen matters more than the starting number.
5. **Arrange reflections.** Stainless reflects the light and surroundings. With the laser disabled, a plain light or dark card outside the frame can create a broad reflection that defines an edge. Remove temporary cards and other filming props before active welding.
6. **Make both surfaces readable.** Focus on the joint, then check the nearby wall and plate. If only a narrow sliver is sharp, back the camera away slightly or choose a less oblique view. Technical explanation benefits from readable geometry throughout the relevant area.
7. **Record the clean view.** Start recording, move your hands away, allow five seconds to settle, hold everything still for 20–30 seconds, then leave another five seconds before touching the camera. This uninterrupted view is the clean plate for graphics.
8. **Record a pointing version separately.** Without changing the framing, point beside the feature with a suitable pointer, withdraw it, and hold the clear view again. Say what the pointer identifies. Keep the tip from covering the edge or contacting prepared joint surfaces.
9. **Add the disabled head and wire.** Hold them at the actual working pose and repeat a clear hold. Arrange the camera so the hand and nozzle do not hide the feature being explained. Capture a wider version if the tight view loses the relationship.
10. **Take a still as well.** Hands out, same lighting, full camera resolution. A photograph is useful for a long explanation, a crop, or a traced diagram. Preserve the unmarked original so all graphics can be added separately.

For a moving arrow like the reference, the camera and work can stay completely still while the arrow travels along the edge in the edit. If the work itself moves, an arrow tied to one material feature needs tracking or manual animation. A label pointing to the fixed welding location during rotation serves a different purpose. Describe which of those the shot is intended to show.

The existing [edit workflow](workflow.md) supports freezes, arrows, boxes, and magnified insets. A continuously moving edge arrow will need animation during editing; collecting a stable clean plate makes that practical.

## Coverage for each stage

For a repeatable, non-emitting action, collect a wide view showing the relationship, a medium view showing the hands, and a detail showing the consequence. Change position between takes. The real weld gets its uninterrupted recording; supplementary dry demonstrations can be filmed separately and identified as demonstrations when emission status matters.

The following durations include useful holds but allow additional time for handling and settling.

### Before tacking or closing the vessel

| Shot | Framing and action | Record | What it lets the edit explain |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Parts on the bench | Fixed oblique view of the actual tube and cap, then hands place them together. Hold clear views before and after. | 20–30 s | What is being joined and its scale. |
| Whole rotator | Three-quarter view showing the vertical tube, nest, drive, and room for Derek to work. | 20 s | The arrangement before moving into details. |
| Joint from above | Camera centered enough to see the cap and circular seam. Keep port features visible for orientation. | 20 s | The path around the cap. |
| Joint detail | Oblique closeup of the inner wall meeting the recessed plate; hands clear. Also take a still. | 30–40 s | Joint line, wall, cap, and the location of a cross-section diagram. |
| Seating the cap | Medium shot of the real seating operation, with a clear settled end position. Do not disturb a prepared fit for a second take. | Whole action | How the components locate together. |
| Internal features | While accessible, record the actual rod, ports, and any installed internal parts. | 15–20 s each | What will be enclosed; why access matters. |

### Setup and dry motion

| Shot | Framing and action | Record | What it lets the edit explain |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Real preparation | Hands performing cleaning and fit work, from a view where the action is visible. | 20–30 s per action | Preparation that leads to this attempt. |
| Indicator | Actual indicator contact and dial during an existing runout check. Include the mounting context once. | One useful sweep | What is being checked; any readable observation can be discussed accurately. |
| Copper shoe | Stationary shoe touching the tube, including where the work lead attaches. Laser disabled. | 20 s | How contact is maintained while the vessel turns. |
| Belt and nest | Fixed detail showing drive motion and tube support during a dry run. Stay clear of moving parts. | 20–30 s | How the rotator moves the work. |
| Pedal | Frame the foot and pedal. Show approach, press, a short hold, release, and foot withdrawn. Laser disabled. | 20 s | The pedal commands rotation. |
| Dry full revolution | Fixed medium view; include an existing index or recognizable port feature returning to its starting position. | Full lap + 10 s | Rotation direction, steadiness, and actual elapsed time. |
| Head and wire pose | Joint closeup with the disabled head and wire in their actual working positions. Hold, withdraw, hold clear. | 25–35 s | Where the wire arrives and where welding occurs. |
| Working stance | Wider dry demonstration with Derek at the head and foot at the pedal. | 20–30 s | The relationship between operator, head, and moving work. |
| Actual settings | Square-on view of welder settings; screen-record the actual rotator console if useful. Hold each legible display. | 10–15 s per view | Values used for this attempt. |
| Tack pattern | Still overall view and detail of the actual tacks after they have been made. | 20 s + still | How the plate is held and where the continuous pass starts. |

The [rotator description](../../hardware/assembly/weld-rotation-rig.md) gives 48.6 seconds per revolution at its nominal 8 mm/s travel setting. Use that only if it is the setting actually used. The [bench record](../../hardware/snapshots/weld-rotator-bench-2026-09-12.md) records counterclockwise motion viewed from above; verify the direction visible in this session before drawing arrows. The pedal operates rotation, and the head's trigger operates the laser. Show and label those controls accordingly.

### The actual attempt

Set and start the camera before taking the working position. Frame the joint, the hands, and enough of the tube to show motion. Use a disabled rehearsal to check that the helmet, hands, nozzle, and near rim do not hide the intended subject.

Keep the recording running through the approach, actual start, complete pass, finish, and immediate reaction. Leave roughly ten seconds at either end where the work permits. Do not interrupt a pass to change framing. With one suitable recording camera, prioritize a complete, readable account; collect the explanation details separately.

If something unexpected happens, keep recording while following the normal welding procedure. Once the equipment is safely stopped, describe what happened and film the visible condition. An ordinary pause or failed attempt can become the turning point of the story. Record the real observation before deciding what caused it.

### After the weld

| Shot | Framing and action | Record | What it lets the edit explain |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Immediate observation | Record a few sentences through the DJI mic once the work is safely stopped. | 30–60 s | What Derek noticed while it is fresh. |
| Whole bead | Match the unwelded overall view, then record a slow, safe inspection rotation with the laser disabled. | Full useful sweep | What was completed around the circumference. |
| Matched joint detail | Match the unwelded closeup; let the finished bead sit still in the same lighting. | 30 s + still | A direct comparison of the actual transformation. |
| Start and finish | Separate details showing where the pass began and ended, including the real overlap if visible. | 20 s each | How the pass closes and what happened at its endpoints. |
| Any trouble spot | Clear overall locator, then a tight detail before dressing or other alteration. | 20 s + still | The visible issue and its position on the part. |
| Accessible root | Record the real interior while it remains accessible, with enough light to distinguish surfaces. | 20–30 s | What can actually be seen inside. |
| Inspection | If inspection occurs during this session, record the real procedure and result under the governing fabrication instructions. | As performed | Evidence appropriate to the claim being made. |
| Finished assembly | Fixed three-quarter view; remove hands, hold, optionally make one slow presentation turn after safe handling is possible. | 20–30 s | A closing image and thumbnail source. |

Use [pressure-vessel.md](../../hardware/assembly/pressure-vessel.md) for fabrication and inspection. A bead closeup supports discussion of visible appearance. Describe a leak result, root condition, or qualification result only to the extent the actual inspection establishes it. If this session ends after welding, the video can end with that result and the next inspection step.

## Reusable B roll and sound

Record a small library of relevant footage while the setup already exists. **Ten good holds give more flexibility than many hurried camera moves.** For each, start, let the camera settle, and then leave the frame alone.

| Capture | How to shoot it | Useful duration |
|---|---|---:|
| Rotator running dry | Fixed medium shot showing obvious rotation; no hands entering midway. Record normal video so the editor can choose real time or acceleration. | 45–60 s, or one full lap |
| Belt running dry | Fixed detail, with the drive and table relationship legible. | 20–30 s |
| Tube waiting in the nest | Stable three-quarter view, deliberately composed and hands clear. | 20–30 s |
| Prepared tools | A restrained view of the actual head, wire, or measuring tool used in this session. | 15–20 s |
| Welder display | Square-on, readable values, no hand covering the screen. | 15–20 s |
| Empty lit bench | Fixed wide view. Keep any screen free of private messages, names, and notifications. | 20–30 s |
| Finished bead resting | Locked detail with good surface lighting. | 20–30 s |
| Entering and leaving | Fixed wide view; enter, settle into place, then later leave the bench. | 15–20 s each |

Let a stationary scene be stationary. The edit can use a gentle crop or push-in if it helps. A repeated machine process can carry a longer explanation, but showing the object named in the narration is usually the clearest choice. Avoid short loops with obvious repeated hand movements; a longer original hold gives cleaner choices.

Record **30 seconds of room tone** with the microphone in the normal recording position, no talking, walking, or touching equipment. Capture the normal idle sound of the shop. If the background changes substantially, such as extraction turning on, record that sound state separately while the equipment is in its normal safe configuration.

For useful natural sound, record a few seconds around individual pedal clicks, a dry motor start and stop, and ordinary tool handling. Let the sound decay before making another noise. The camera audio can supply these textures; the DJI track remains the source for speech. For a voiceover recorded later, keep the mic position consistent and leave a second of silence around each line.

## Active laser filming

The closeup recipes and repositioning instructions above apply to the **disabled laser**. During emission, every camera, light, support, cable, and viewing path must fit the established laser-controlled setup. Place a recording camera outside exposure or behind a viewing arrangement specified for that laser and use. Obtain the appropriate arrangement from the welder manufacturer or a qualified laser-safety provider before planning an exposed camera view of the process.

Do not treat a photographic ND filter, a dark image on the screen, or ordinary welding glass as qualification for this purpose. [XLaserlab identifies the X1 Pro as Class 4 and specifies laser-specific eye protection](https://www.xlaserlab.com/products/xlaserlab-x1-pro-laser-welder-cleaner-cutter). [Sony documents camera-sensor damage from direct and reflected laser exposure](https://www.sony.co.uk/electronics/support/cameras-camcorders-camcorders-and-video-cameras/articles/00266583). Camera exposure settings control the picture; they do not establish protection.

If a suitable active-process recording position is unavailable, collect the geometry and dry demonstrations, retain the real observations and inspection footage, and explain the weld through those images. Do not leave an unprotected camera aimed toward the weld merely because it is not recording. Restore normal guarding and clear filming props before emission, and make all camera adjustments with emission disabled. The project's separate [positioner observation camera](../../hardware/gun-positioner/README.md) is intended to withdraw before emission.

## Order of work for a solo session

1. **Set up the two useful views.** A wider GoPro position and a supported phone detail position. Put the light where it makes the joint readable. Charge devices, clear storage, clean lenses, and silence notifications.
2. **Record a ten-second disabled sample.** Perform a representative hand movement, then hold the joint clear. Play it back at full size. The decision is whether the viewer can distinguish the wall, cap, seam, and wire without focus hunting, glare, or hand obstruction. Adjust the camera or light only if that answer is no.
3. **Capture the unwelded geometry.** Overall, joint detail, clean plate, and still. Record internal features before they are enclosed.
4. **Film preparation and dry demonstrations.** Collect the actual fit work, settings, pedal, full lap, and disabled head pose. Reuse the already planned dry run for these views where practical.
5. **Prepare the active recording arrangement.** Remove temporary closeup props and restore the normal work area. Start suitable recording and the speech recorder, clap once if both tracks need syncing, then take the normal working position.
6. **Do the weld.** Let the full attempt run on camera without adjusting it during the pass.
7. **Record the consequence.** Safely stop, capture the immediate observation, then film the unchanged bead and accessible interior when safe to approach.
8. **Collect the quiet extras.** Finish with relevant B-roll, room tone, and a thumbnail still. Explain the observation separately from the suspected cause in the spoken notes.
9. **Preserve the originals.** Follow [workflow.md](workflow.md) to transfer the GoPro clips and external audio. Transfer the phone videos and original stills too. Keep the high-resolution sources for detail crops and freeze frames; make edit proxies separately.

Use clip notes such as `unwelded joint`, `dry rotation`, `actual attempt 1`, `bead before dressing`, and `room tone`. Say the label aloud at the beginning if writing it down is inconvenient, then pause before the usable shot. Distinguish another coupon, a dry demonstration, and the actual carbonator whenever clips could be confused.

## A possible edit

The final sequence follows what happens, but this is enough visual material for a compact story:

| Story beat | Pictures to have available |
|---|---|
| A few seconds of the real outcome | Active-process footage where available, or the actual finished bead and immediate observation. |
| What the job requires | Whole tube and cap, then the joint detail. |
| Why the rotator is useful | Dry lap, fixed head pose, pedal, and a direction arrow. |
| What this attempt uses | Real settings, preparation, fit, and tack pattern. |
| The attempt | Selected portions of the uninterrupted real recording, with honest elapsed-time treatment where relevant. |
| What happened | Matched detail, endpoints, trouble spots, and actual inspection observations. |
| What happens next | The next concrete fabrication or inspection step, over the assembly resting on the bench. |

For later narration, answer four prompts: What was difficult about the job? What did the rotator let you control? What did you actually observe? What remains to be done? Record those answers after reviewing the footage, when the pictures can determine the wording.
