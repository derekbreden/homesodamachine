# Video Workflow

Living procedure for the capture-to-publish loop. These steps are the minimum to ship at all — see [`principles.md`](/marketing/video/principles.md#the-pipeline-is-the-floor) for the frame they sit inside. Updates in place when a step changes; if a step changes meaningfully (CapCut → iMovie → Mac edit pipeline), update here. Snapshots in [`snapshots/`](/marketing/video/snapshots/) preserve the historical state at moments worth freezing.

## The Mac edit pipeline

Capture on the GoPro and the Canon R50 V, with narration on the DJI Mic Mini. Edit on the Mac with [native marketing commands](../../tools/hsm/marketing/README.md), driven from Claude Code. Run media commands through `tools/run`. Lossless waveform-based sync, Whisper transcription, segment-clustered cut decisions, scripted cross-dissolves, burned-in captions, generated title cards.

1. **Capture.** GoPro on cap mount for POV, R50 V on a tripod for fixed shots, DJI Mic Mini live to iPhone Voice Memos. See [`equipment.md`](/marketing/video/equipment.md) for camera and audio settings. *DJI Mic Mini failure mode: clip can fall off mid-session. Either camera's built-in mic is a usable fallback for narration; keep it as plan B.*
2. **Quik cloud (auto, GoPro).** GoPro reconnects to home wifi → footage auto-syncs to Quik cloud. No per-clip action needed once the cloud subscription is on.
3. **Quik → Photos (manual, GoPro).** Share each desired clip from Quik cloud to Photos. Once the file lands in Photos.app the rest of the pipeline can pull it via AppleScript (no Full Disk Access grant required).
4. **Voice Memos → Files (manual).** Share each desired audio clip from Voice Memos to iCloud Drive Downloads.
5. **Working dir.** `~/Developer/soda-edit/<YYYY-MM-DD-slug>/`. **Not** `~/Desktop/` — Desktop is iCloud-synced and aggressively evicts large files mid-encode, breaking ffmpeg with opaque "Operation timed out" errors. `~/Developer/` is non-iCloud on this machine; verify on yours before committing to a path.
6. **Pull video.** GoPro clips come from Photos: [`export-from-photos.sh`](export-from-photos.sh) wraps an `osascript` block that asks Photos.app to export the latest media item as the unmodified original. Photos.app does the file copy; we never touch the SQLite database, so no FDA grant is required. Use `with timeout of 1200 seconds` for big GoPro files (6 GB+ on 30-min clips). R50 V clips skip Photos: copy the original movie files from its SD card, through the Anker USB-C reader, straight into the working dir.
7. **Sync external audio to video.** [`hsm-marketing sync`](../../tools/hsm/marketing/src/video.rs) cross-correlates the lav audio against the camera audio around the shared transient. It reports the offset and confidence, writes a `.sync.json` sidecar, and with `-o` muxes the lav onto the video. Use `--search-seconds` to select the search window and listen to a synced sample when the result is ambiguous. The camera track supplies the sync reference; the lav supplies finished narration, following [the audio principle](principles.md#the-lav-is-the-audio-you-ship).
8. **Transcribe.** Run Whisper on the lav audio: `whisper <audio>.m4a --model medium.en --language en --word_timestamps True --output_format json`. Word timestamps are required for caption truncation in step 11. Use the project's `tools/video-venv/` for the external Whisper CLI.
9. **Cluster speech into scenes.** [`hsm-marketing segment`](../../tools/hsm/marketing/src/video.rs) groups Whisper segments using `--gap`, adds context with `--pad-before` and `--pad-after`, then merges overlaps. Tune those flags to the recording; command help owns the defaults.
10. **Visual analysis (interactive, with Claude).** Spend the tokens here. For each candidate scene, extract a frame every ~5 s within the scene window, plus a few from the gaps before/after that look interesting. Read them, decide refined cut points based on what's visible (the spoken word is rarely the whole story — interesting visual moments often start before or end after the speech). Output: a cut list JSON.
    - **Cold open.** When the footage contains a clear payoff — the success moment, the surprising reveal, the climax — extract a 3–4 s clip of it and place it as cut #1, before the title. The walkthrough becomes "here's how I got there" instead of "watch me adjust settings." Shipped on *One Out of Three* (the "clean break" reveal cross-faded into "all right, we are getting ready for another attempt"); refined on *Don't Let Go* — the payoff clip captures the active weld → torch lift, not a still framing of the result, so smoke + glow + the visible lift action carry the hook.
    - **Freeze + annotate.** Hold a source frame when an annotation needs a still target. The [cut-list contract](cuts.md) describes the `freeze` entry, and the [overlay schema](overlays.md) describes its arrows, text and boxes. Overlay times refer to the output freeze window. Inspect the selected source frame before placing its annotation.
11. **Generate captions.** [`hsm-marketing make-subtitles`](../../tools/hsm/marketing/src/video.rs) reads the cut list, per-source Whisper transcripts and sync offsets and emits output-timed ASS captions. It drops words truncated by a cut, filters short filler hallucinations and splits phrases at words and sentence endings. Match its fade and title settings to the [cut command](cuts.md).
12. **Render.** [`hsm-marketing cut`](../../tools/hsm/marketing/src/video/edit.rs) extracts ranges and freezes, applies per-cut labels and cross-dissolves, then burns captions. Supply `--overlays SIDECAR` or `--no-overlays`. A `title` overlay places a native-rendered hero panel over opening B-roll; `--title` prepends a separate slate. Encoding flags and defaults are documented by command help.
    - **Motion-graphics overlays.** Pass `--overlays SIDECAR` for native title and parameter panels, text, HUDs, lower thirds, stamps, boxes, arrows, picture-in-picture and magnifier insets. The [overlay schema](overlays.md) defines their fields. Times belong to the output picture, and supplied captions are burned in the same final pass.
    - **Sound design.** Add a `sounds` array to the overlays sidecar to mix synthesized SFX into the audio bed in the existing final pass. Each entry is `{file, t, volume, fade_in?, fade_out?}` with `t` in OUTPUT timeline (same convention as the visual overlays). The SFX library lives at [`sfx/`](/marketing/video/sfx/) — v1 placeholders (`stamp-thud`, `whoosh`, `ding`, `sting`, `click`) synthesized via [`sfx/synthesize.sh`](/marketing/video/sfx/synthesize.sh), each normalized to ~-3 dBFS peak so per-cue `volume=1.0` means "as loud as the others." Replace with curated / recorded SFX when a specific cue starts mattering. Shipped on *Don't Let Go* — a sting under the cold-open → title transition, five stamp-thuds under NO STICK × 3, WIRE FEEDS, and the final DON'T LET GO.
    - **4K → 1080p pre-scale.** Both cameras capture 4K at ~29.97 fps, the GoPro HERO13 in HEVC and the R50 V in H.264 (XF-AVC S); `hsm-marketing cut` preserves source resolution by default, but overlay coordinates in the sidecar are authored in 1080p pixel space. Pre-scale the synced source to 1920×1080 in a one-off ffmpeg pass before invoking `hsm-marketing cut` — `-vf scale=1920:1080:force_original_aspect_ratio=decrease,pad=1920:1080:...` keeps aspect-ratio honest and adds 4 GB → 400 MB of breathing room without a perceptible quality hit at YouTube's bitrate ceiling.
13. **Iterate on the cut list.** Drafts are cheap. Watch each draft, send adjustments back to Claude, re-render. Auto-delete the rejected draft to keep the working dir small (multi-GB H.264 files add up fast).
14. **Thumbnail.** [`/marketing/thumbnail/make.sh`](/marketing/thumbnail/make.sh) takes a source image + headline text and produces a 1280×720 PNG with white-on-black-outline text at the **top** of the frame. Top-positioned text avoids YouTube's bottom-right duration pill — particularly aggressive on mobile, where the pill takes a larger proportion of the cropped preview. Pre-crop the source frame in ImageMagick if the action isn't already centered or if the top portion is too busy for legible text — make.sh's center-crop assumes the action is in frame.
15. **Title and description.** Lead the title with the **broadest true topic, not the product brand** — per [`distribution.md`](distribution.md), broad-topic anchoring is what gets a video into the Browse recommendation pool (the channel's near-only reach at current scale), and brand anchoring shrank Browse eligibility ~365× in the one natural experiment we have. Keep the channel's `<hook> — <specifics>` shape, but make the specifics a broad topic and move the brand/model into the description body: e.g. *Stuck Less That Time — Laser Welding a Stainless Pressure Vessel*, **not** *— Stainless Welds on the Xlaserlab X1 Pro*. Where possible pick a broad anchor that overlaps the actual buyer ("home carbonation," "real soda on tap"), not just any large topic. Sentence- or title-cased, not all caps (all caps is algorithmically flagged as spam in titles). Description is 2–3 sentences front-loading the hook and personal stakes, then equipment names on final lines. Reference example for *I've Never Tapped — First NPT Threads in 316L Stainless*:
    > First time tapping NPT threads by hand. The plates are 1/4" 316 stainless from Send Cut Send — they'll be welded into a carbonation tank for a home soda machine I'm building. Forty threads to cut by hand. This is the first one.
    >
    > Tap: Drill America 1/4"-18 NPT
    > Drill press: WEN 4208T 8" benchtop
    > Cutting fluid: Tap Magic EP-Xtra
16. **Upload.** YouTube Studio. Two native file-picker steps are user-driven; everything else is Claude-driven via the Chrome MCP browser tools. **Path of least resistance** for an agent walking this end-to-end: user clicks the Studio upload icon and picks the rendered MP4 from the native dialog, then clicks "Upload file" in the Thumbnail section of the Details panel and picks the 1280×720 PNG. Claude does the rest — pastes title + description into the Details panel, selects "No, it's not made for kids," clicks Next through Video Elements (skip subtitles — we burn them in) and Checks, selects Public on Visibility, and clicks Publish. The `mcp__Claude_in_Chrome__file_upload` tool exists but rejects paths not on the session's shared-file allowlist; the 10 MB combined cap also blocks the rendered MP4 regardless of sharing — that's why the user is the right hand for the two file-picker steps. First exercised this split on *Don't Let Go*. Once published, the video *file* is immutable — you can edit metadata (title, description, thumbnail) and add audio from Studio → Editor → Audio's curated subset, but you cannot replace the file or add music from outside that subset without taking the video down and re-uploading (which creates a new URL, requires a follow-up post-URL edit, and forces a permanent delete of the old version). The Editor's audio subset is much smaller than the full Audio Library at `studio.youtube.com/channel/<id>/music`; for any track that's only in the full library, mux it into the local MP4 *before* this step. One-shot mux against the no-audio render: `ffmpeg -i out.mp4 -i music.mp3 -filter_complex "[1:a]afade=in:st=0:d=1,afade=out:st=<end-2>:d=2,volume=0.85[a]" -map 0:v -map "[a]" -c:v copy -c:a aac -b:a 192k -shortest out-with-music.mp4`.
17. **Verify the published video.** Open the public watch URL (`https://youtu.be/<id>`, copyable from the upload dialog) and confirm title, description, thumbnail, duration, and visibility all match. Studio's "Video published" confirmation dialog is not ground truth — the public watch URL is. HD transcoding runs in the background after publish, so the player may sit at 0:00 buffering for several minutes; that is not a verification failure, the upload is committed once the URL resolves. This step is mandatory: at least one prior agent skipped it and shipped a broken state.
18. **(Optional) Caption fix.** YouTube auto-captions still run in parallel to the burned-in captions; if YouTube's version has an error that crosses the brand-cringe threshold, fix it in Studio → Subtitles → click into the auto-caption editor. Burned-in captions are unaffected.

Steady-state target: ~60–90 minutes per clip including the visual-analysis pass. Render time scales with clip length; the 2026-05-08 first-tap clip rendered in ~10 min on M-series with `--preset fast`. Iteration cycle: ~5 min per draft once cut list is stable.

## On the iPhone + iMovie path

The on-phone workflow that shipped *First Weld* (2026-05-04) is preserved in [`snapshots/2026-05-04-first-execution.md`](/marketing/video/snapshots/2026-05-04-first-execution.md). It still works for very short clips that don't need cross-dissolves, captions, or precise sync. Below ~3 minutes of source material with one camera angle, it's still faster than the Mac pipeline. Anything past that, the Mac pipeline wins on every axis except setup cost.

## Open items

- **Live narration during shop work** — validated 2026-05-17 on the *One Out of Three* session: DJI Mic Mini live to Voice Memos, narration spoken in real time while operating the welder. Worked end-to-end with no on-camera ad-libbing required. The audio-first inversion is now the default for sessions where you can talk while you work; post-recorded narration is the fallback when you can't (loud machinery, two-handed delicate work, etc.).
- **Welder unboxing footage** — captured but not edited; planned as B-roll for the welding story, not standalone content.
- **Voiceover-from-script** — the v3 script in `~/Developer/soda-edit/2026-05-08-first-tap/script-v3.md` is what we'd use if recording on-camera audio fails entirely. Not yet exercised end-to-end (the GoPro audio for the 2026-05-08 first-tap was usable, narrowly).

## Why this shape

The original block was device-and-app context switching. Phone-only kept the Mac free for soda-machine work. But two videos in, the iMovie limitations bit hard: no waveform-based sync, no cross-dissolves, no caption tooling, no programmatic precision when iterating cut points. The Mac pipeline takes a one-time cost (write the scripts, learn them, set up the venv) and amortizes it across every future video. Claude Code does the orchestration, so the Mac stays mostly idle from the user's perspective during the heavy lifts (whisper transcription, ffmpeg encode passes).

The principles in [`principles.md`](/marketing/video/principles.md) didn't change. POV > talking head, voiceover > on-camera, build-in-public > tutorial. The Mac pipeline just makes it easier to honor those — especially voiceover-friendly editing.
