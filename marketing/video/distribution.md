# Distribution — what gets our videos seen

What we know about how this channel reaches viewers, plus the one natural experiment we have on it. Small-sample and provisional — a working theory, not settled fact. Sits under [`principles.md`](principles.md); informs the titling step in [`workflow.md`](workflow.md).

## Browse is the channel, not search

At current scale (low-double-digit subscribers) reach is almost entirely YouTube's recommendation feed — "Browse features" — not search. On the one video that broke out, *Stuck Less That Time* (599 lifetime views vs. single / low-double digits on every other video), Browse delivered 96.5% of views; search delivered 5 of 599. Plan for the recommendation feed, not for search keywords.

## The natural experiment: broad-topic title anchor vs. brand anchor

Across the welding videos, one content variable moved with reach: whether the **title anchors to a broad topic or to the narrow product brand.**

| Video | Title anchor | Lifetime Browse impressions |
|---|---|---|
| *Stuck Less That Time* | "Laser Welding A 304L Pressure Vessel" (broad topic) | ~10,200 |
| *One Out of Three* | "…on the Xlaserlab X1 Pro" (brand/model) | 28 |

~365×, and it is a **Browse-eligibility** gap (impressions), not a click-through or retention gap — the brand-anchored video was barely entered into Browse at all. Its scraps of traffic were mostly search, from people who already knew the brand name to type it.

Mechanism: YouTube classifies a video against topic/entity nodes drawn from its title, description, and transcript. "Laser welding" and "pressure vessel" are large nodes with big browse audiences; "Xlaserlab X1 Pro" is a tiny node (one product model). The broad-anchored video was swept into a large recommendation pool; the brand-anchored ones were filed into a niche and almost never surfaced.

Tags are not the lever — the breakout video's tag field is empty, and YouTube itself states tags "play a minimal role."

## The rule this implies

- **Anchor the title to the largest true topic; put the brand / model / project-jargon in the description body.** "Laser Welding a Stainless Pressure Vessel," not "…on the Xlaserlab X1 Pro." Both can appear — the broad topic leads.
- Generalizes past welding: anchor to nodes people actually browse — "home carbonation," "DIY soda machine," "SodaStream," "ice maker teardown," "kegerator" — not project-internal terms like "Touch-Flo faucet shell" or "cold core."
- **Mind the buyer mismatch.** The broad node that pulls Browse is not always the buyer. "Laser welding" reaches welders, not diet-soda drinkers. Prefer broad anchors that overlap the buyer where they exist — "home carbonation," "SodaStream upgrade," "real soda on tap."

## What this does not address

Retention. The breakout's push died flat in ~two weeks at ~15% average view-through and converted ~0 subscribers — getting into the pool is necessary, not sufficient. Retention (cold open, pacing, payoff) is a separate problem, addressed in [`workflow.md`](workflow.md) and [`principles.md`](principles.md).

## Caveat

One natural experiment (a single breakout) with secondary differences between videos (thumbnail, transcript, publish timing). The impression gap is large and specifically an *eligibility* gap, which rules out the obvious click-through / retention confounds — but it remains a theory. It is falsifiable: title coming videos by broad topic and watch whether Browse impressions rise.
