# Lincoln sales and installation

An exploratory local sales track for Home Soda Machine: reach households in Lincoln,
Nebraska, include installation with the purchase, and have Derek deliver, install and
support the machines himself. A branded car with decals and a roof topper makes that
work visible in the neighborhoods where the machines are used.

**Working recommendation:** develop this around three additional local households, with
one installation at a time and one paid advertising test at a time. Three is a proposed
learning batch, not a sales commitment. The offer, price, spending limit and service
boundary remain decisions to make before a campaign runs.

The [advertising research](advertising.md) holds sources, prices and contacts. The
[working materials](working-materials.md) hold draft copy, quote requests and the records
needed to run a pilot. Product positioning and existing prices remain in
[target market](../target-market.md); physical readiness remains in
[the build plan](../../future/README.md).

## What local changes

The local offer combines the appliance with access to its maker. A buyer can see a pour,
meet Derek, get their kitchen assessed and have the machine installed by someone who
knows it. That could remove substantial uncertainty from a purchase of an unfamiliar
appliance.

| Opportunity | What would establish its value |
|---|---|
| Service at the customer's kitchen | Actual visits resolve problems; record travel, diagnosis, parts and follow-up time alongside any avoided freight |
| Better engineering feedback | Observe the cabinet, installation, refill and cleaning habits that surround a failure |
| Installation as a reason to buy | Qualified prospects identify installation help as material to their decision and accept the complete quoted offer |
| A visible car outside a real installation | Neighbors mention the car or use its link; resulting inquiries become qualified conversations |
| Owners introducing other owners | Referrals produce additional installations within the service area |
| Local trust | Unconnected households buy and use the machine, with permission-based reviews or demonstrations available to subsequent prospects |

These are hypotheses to measure. A service visit can save freight while taking more of
Derek's time. A parked car's exposure depends on the street and time of day. The strongest
result is a cluster of satisfied daily users whose installations and support fit around
the work of building the next machine.

## Relationship to ring one

One nearby household is planned for the ring-one beta. The other currently identified
ring-one prospects are remote. The local track runs alongside those commitments and
uses the same production queue and founder hours.

Record relationship and geography separately. A nearby buyer found through an ad is a
local customer; proximity alone does not make them a ring-one personal connection.
A local friend-of-a-friend can belong to both groups, with their unit counted once.
Track unique installations across the project and retain the ring-one count defined in
[the internal plan](../target-market.md#the-internal-plan-rings-of-trust).

The nearby beta installation can establish a repeatable visit, handoff and support
process. Beta participation does not establish what a buyer will pay through a public
offer. The proposed three-household local batch therefore tracks buyers recruited
through local marketing separately from existing beta commitments.

## The buyer and the offer

Start with the existing buyer profile: a daily drinker of a supported soda flavor who
would value eliminating the can routine, has authority to alter the kitchen, and has a
compatible cabinet. Geography is another qualification. Suggested starting boundary:
Lincoln city limits, with any nearby exception assessed individually before an offer.
Record actual round-trip travel time; a ZIP code alone does not define the service area.

The message to explore is:

> Cold soda on tap in your kitchen. Delivered and installed by the person who built it.

The two price approaches answer different questions:

| Offer to consider | Price status | What it can teach |
|---|---|---|
| Small local beta cohort, installation included | Unset. The existing ring-one expectation of roughly $2,000–$3,000 is context, not an authorized local price | Whether local recruitment, daily use and hands-on support make early field learning workable |
| Public Founder Edition, installation included locally | The repo's public anchor is $7,500; the local installation inclusion is proposed | Whether unrelated local buyers will buy the complete offer at the public price |

Choose one offer for an acquisition test and record exactly what buyers saw. Discounted
beta sales do not establish demand at the Founder price. A beta offer needs a defined
number of places, the actual state of the product, expected feedback and agreed support
terms. Any relation to Founder Edition numbering must be settled before quoting a unit.

Make the price visible before a home visit. Until a price is selected, conversations
and interest signups provide discovery evidence rather than purchase commitments.

## What installation includes

The intended experience is that Derek arrives with a machine and leaves the customer
able to use it. The [install envelope](../install-envelope.md) and
[install guide](../../hardware/install-guide/README.md) provide the physical basis.

Proposed standard visit: delivery, placement, faucet mounting in a prepared compatible
opening, the approved water and CO₂ connections, commissioning, leak checks, a first
cold pour and an owner walkthrough. Allocate travel, setup, chill-down and cleanup as
part of the visit; an hour of chill-down is time that needs an explicit plan.

Resolve these parts of the offer before advertising free installation:

| Item | Proposed treatment to evaluate |
|---|---|
| Countertop opening | Survey the existing 1-3/8-inch opening or the drilling needed. Price a countertop professional into a complete-install option; otherwise state required preparation beside the free-install claim |
| Cold-water connection | Use the documented compatible connection where permitted; identify plumbing alterations during the survey |
| Power and cabinet fit | Confirm an appropriate existing outlet, ventilation, access and cylinder space; agree any preparation before accepting the job |
| CO₂ and first concentrate | Existing product scope makes these customer-supplied. Decide whether a local starter supply is included so first-pour readiness is unambiguous |
| Support | Publish a working contact, service area, scheduling expectations and the boundary between covered repairs and chargeable work |
| Unusual kitchens | Give a complete written scope and price before commitment; use the survey to identify work outside the standard visit |

Lincoln's [homeowner permit guidance](https://www.lincoln.ne.gov/City/Departments/PDS/Building-Safety/Homeowner-Building-Permits)
says plumbing permits may be issued to a licensed contractor or a homeowner for their
primary residence. It does not classify this appliance connection. Confirm the specific
tee, faucet and backflow arrangement with Building and Safety before promising that
Derek personally performs every part of every installation. Their published contact is
402-441-7882. Include any required trade work in the offer's costs and scope.

The install guide records unfinished commissioning and handoff work, including flavor
priming, CO₂ disconnection and a support contact. Those procedures need to be usable at
the first customer visit. Local service also needs a way to isolate a machine, arrange
a scheduled visit and bring it back to the shop when necessary. Promise only the
response and repair coverage Derek can actually provide.

## The car as part of the offer

The car's main job is to explain what is being installed. Use the existing
[faucet identity and palette](../../brand/README.md): a large faucet mark, a plain
description and a memorable website address.

- **Moving traffic:** “SODA ON TAP AT HOME” and the domain, readable in a glance.
- **Parked during a visit:** add the local installation offer and a QR code leading to
  the local inquiry page. The short address must also be readable without scanning.
- **Roof topper:** use the same short message. Select its size and mounting from the
  actual vehicle and the manufacturer's driving, wind and roof requirements.
- **Owner referrals:** leave a few small cards with the same message and link for
  visitors who ask about the faucet. Invite introductions after the owner is using it.

[Local graphics shops](advertising.md#vehicle-decals-and-roof-topper) can quote lettering,
decals or removable magnets. Roof-topper supply and fit are still unverified. Vehicle
make, model, roof material and available mounting area are needed before selecting one.
Have the shop show a proof on the actual car, with legibility from across the street.

Signage is reusable campaign equipment. Log inquiries attributed to it and the hours
spent at installations; do not turn a vendor's generic impression estimate into a
sales forecast. A customer's participation in photos, a demonstration or referrals is
optional and agreed separately from installation.

## A pilot that fits the build

Progress follows evidence and available machines, with no promised launch date.

| Step | Work | Evidence needed to move on |
|---|---|---|
| Define the offer | Choose beta or public price, proposed three-household limit, geography, installation scope and founder-time allowance | A complete offer Derek would be comfortable quoting |
| Prepare discovery | Obtain media and signage quotes; show an honestly identified prototype demonstration; talk with prospective buyers | People understand the product, flavor choices and likely price; questions are recorded |
| Establish installation | Complete the physical qualifications in the build plan and the nearby beta installation when ready | A measured visit, working commissioning and owner handoff, and usable support procedures |
| Recruit locally | Publish the local inquiry path and run one bounded test with the chosen offer | Qualified households, source attribution and actual acquisition spending |
| Install and observe | Admit one household at a time, within the capacity available after existing commitments | Daily use, actual service workload, and a first refill/cleaning check before expanding |
| Decide whether to repeat | Review the three households and unresolved inquiries together | A workable offer and support load, plus enough contribution to justify another batch |

For an initial paid test, **$300 in media spend is a proposed experiment limit**, not a
vendor quote or an approved budget. Pick one digital platform, one offer and one local
inquiry destination. Confirm available geography and spending controls before launch.
Signage, printing, demo materials and founder hours need separate limits so a $300 ad
test cannot quietly become a much larger project.

End paid recruitment when that spending limit is reached or the available pilot places
are committed. Then follow up the existing inquiries before deciding what the test
produced. If it yields no qualified conversations, inspect the message, price and
audience before repeating it. If conversations fail on kitchen fit, installation scope
or flavor, address that specific obstacle. If support consumes the time needed for
the next build, finish that work before accepting another installation.

## What to measure

A qualified inquiry is a household in the agreed area that wants a supported flavor,
understands the actual price, has authority over the kitchen and appears to have a
feasible installation. A completed sale means an installed, accepted unit; record
deposits, cancellations and refunds separately.

For each test, retain inquiries, qualified inquiries, demonstrations, kitchen surveys,
written offers, commitments and installed units. Capture the first source and any
later influence, including “saw your car,” a friend, a bus or a digital ad. Small samples
provide case evidence, not a reliable market-wide conversion rate.

Track local cash outlays and founder hours separately: advertising and creative,
travel, installation, trade work, starter supplies, follow-up and repairs. The
[parts](../../hardware/ledger/bom.md) and [build labor](../../hardware/ledger/labor.md)
ledgers remain the source for manufacturing costs. Local costs can become inputs to
the project's investment-recovery work once measured; this pilot carries no payback
forecast.

Media cost per installed customer is media spending divided by attributable completed
installations. Report reusable signage separately and also show total pilot cash.
With no installed customers, report the spend and zero installations rather than a
numerical acquisition cost. Evaluate the remaining contribution after local costs and
the value of founder time before expanding a paid channel.
