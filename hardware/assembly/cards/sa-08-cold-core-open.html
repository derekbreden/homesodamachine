Closed underneath, open at the top. This is what the pour goes into — 6 lines standing up out of the mouth and nothing on the crown.

Closed underneath

The bottom cap and its lid. Poured on the shell’s own face and screwed back on after cure. One shot finds every open cavity in parallel and leaves by any gap it can — the floor is what it must not.

Standing in the mouth

6 lines, dressed up and out. Beads squeeze out at the lane slots and at every tight tube exit as the foam rises, so each line stands clear of the rim before the shot is mixed.

Not on yet

The top cap, its lid, and everything SA-04 stands on them. The cap comes down over these lines at CC-15, and water-5 — the last of the 7 conduits’ lines, a jumper off the pump’s discharge chain — is made up with the crown, not here.

The shell is drawn as the printed piece it is. What stands in those cavities by now — the vessel with its coil, both reservoirs, the copper plugs — is in and is not a body this picture carries.

Done when the floor is sealed and every line stands clear of the rim, in one pour’s reach.
