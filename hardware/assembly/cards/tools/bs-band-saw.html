One cut, then edges. The saw runs three rods and stops; the rest of the bench is a swivel blade riding an edge under hand pressure. The plate perimeter is where that matters — one laser-cut circle, two faces, and only one of them is a weld surface.

CutPV-05 BA4555 horizontal, vise + length stop 1/8″ 316L rod, from 12″ sticks 131.1 mm ×1 · 174 mm ×2 square, then deburred both ends Every rod end is a seating face — a drilled register at the vessel, a printed boss at the reservoirs.
Plate edgePV-04 Noga around the laser-cut perimeter both plates — the two faces treated differently Inside face chamfered, outside face burr-broken the register marks the inside; mark the outside if it isn't obvious The inside edge is the lead-in that finds center in the ~0.005″ slip. The outside edge is the fillet root.
Tube endsPV-07 Noga, ID and OD both ends in one sitting, tube still loose Light — the 0.065″ wall has little to give the fillet sits 1/4″ below the cut edge Clearance, not fusion. A rolled-over burr catches a plate part-way down the bore and holds it off seated depth.
Collar seatEN-06 Noga, then a wipe the hopper opening in the enclosure-front-top print Flat and clean — no brim residue, no support nib nothing to fasten: the opening is cut at the funnel's collar The silicone funnel's brim rests on this face; a nib holds it proud and the basin reads crooked.

The outside-face edge is the fillet root — burr-break only. A chamfer there widens the gap the beam has to bridge.
