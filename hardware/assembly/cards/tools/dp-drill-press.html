Drill press WEN 4208T benchtop · both 316L end plates, before any welding

Tool station · 01/13Kitchen edition

Three operations, one clamp, and only one of them is a drilling job. The press drives a countersink, then holds a tap square while your wrench turns it, then drills the one blind hole on the vessel that must not go through. Both plates get all three before anything is welded — the register is the same drill-press setup as the tap, so the disc is clamped once and the chuck changes three times.

The press is a squareness fixture here, not a driver: the guide holds the tap perpendicular, the tap wrench turns it.
RegisterPV-03 9/64″ M35 cobalt twist 135° split point ~740 RPMthe slowest of the five Depth stop 0.10″ to the tip at (0, −2.007″), inside face Seats and squares the level-sensing rod — bottom plate for its tack weld, top plate to capture the rod tip at closure. Prove it on a scrap disc before a real plate.
⛔
The register is blind. 0.10″ deep leaves 0.15″ of the 1/4″ plate intact — this hole must not break through; it is part of the 90 PSI pressure boundary.
At the press. The disc is clamped by hand — the first-tap rig is a single-use Baltic-birch + MDF snapshot, and the per-vessel batch fixture is an open item. Tap Magic on every cut. Canned air through the threads after tapping.
Have in hand WEN 4208T · JNB Pro 82° countersink set · LingGan M35 NPT tap · B&S spring guide · Drill America tap wrench · 9/64″ M35 cobalt bits · Tap Magic EP-Xtra · depth stop · scrap disc · clamp · canned air for the chips
The plate as the press receives it — two ports cut; the third feature is the one you add. A laser cuts only through, so the register cannot be in the cut file without breaching the boundary it exists to preserve.
