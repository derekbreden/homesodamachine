EN-03

Bolt the compressor
down to the slab

Enclosure · 03/9Kitchen edition

The compressor is the one body the box fastens — four printed posts stand up out of the floor slab, one through each of its own feet.

⚠
120 V terminals + a PTC at 140–150 °C in normal use stand in the power box at the −Y end. Nothing lands on that block until WR.
  1. It comes in on the charged loop — every refrigerant line already brazed to it (RL-08). It is never separated from that assembly.
  2. Power box to the front. The bolt pattern is symmetric about the plate’s centre and the box is not, so the box is the only thing that tells the two ends apart. Read it before the plate goes down.
  3. Lower it onto the four posts. Each rises through one ⌀14 plate hole to the plate’s own crown, so the posts locate it before a screw is turned — if all four do not enter, it is on the wrong end.
  4. One M3 down into each post’s insert. The screw bears on the grommet’s steel bushing: the grommet is the isolation element and is never crushed.
  5. Check the loop is slack before and after — a plate pulled onto its posts must not drag a brazed joint.

Upright, plate down, power box forward — the oil sits in the can’s bottom and the pickup is gravity-fed, so upright is the compressor’s own constraint and the only freedom it has left is which way it is turned.
It is the only body in the box that is bolted down. The condenser and the cold core stand on the same slab and are held by it and by each other; the stations that would fasten them are not cut. So this plate is what the stratum is squared off — get it right and EN-04 mates against a body that cannot walk.
Done whenPower box forward, all four posts through their own feet, four M3 home on the bushings, no tension anywhere in the brazed loop.
