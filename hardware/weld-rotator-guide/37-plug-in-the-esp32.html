Controller · step 26

Plug the ESP32 into the laptop

The board's own Micro-USB, straight to the laptop. Its onboard CP2102 carries the upload, resets the board into the bootloader by itself, and powers it while it is connected.

  1. Unplug the 24 V brick. If it was ever plugged in, unplug it now. The motor must be dead for everything on this page and the next.
  2. Take the Micro-USB data cable — the one that has flashed this project's other DevKitC boards, not a charge-only cable.
  3. Plug it into the ESP32's own Micro-USB socket. Not the 5 V adapter's tip: that comes back on page 39.
  4. Plug the other end into the laptop.
  5. Tell me it is connected. I will build and upload firmware/src_weld_rotator/ to it.
Gate — the board is on the bus

A new serial device appears on the laptop. On this Mac it will be a /dev/cu.usbserial-* or /dev/cu.SLAB_USBtoUART. No device means a charge-only cable, or the wrong socket.

Nothing about the upload opens a motor

Building and flashing touches no motor terminal, and the driver has no power. The host's USB is what powers the board through this step and the next.

What lands on it

  • A controller that boots motionless and will not arm until it has seen the pedal released once.
  • lap mode: press and hold for one counted 380° move. Release early and it aborts.
  • jog mode: follows the pedal continuously, for indicating and setup.
  • Settings stored in NVS, so they survive being unplugged.
