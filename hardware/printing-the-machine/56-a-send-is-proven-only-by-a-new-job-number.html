Four ways a send has gone wrong

21 September 2026 · Mark2

It vanished

Two sends went out while the printer was loading filament, its nozzle held hot with no job. Each time the dialog closed and nothing started. The third, after the heat was off, ran. Three more vanished over the next two days, for no reason anyone found.

4 October 2026 · Mark2

Refused: still hot

Just after a spool was loaded, the printer’s heater targets already read zero. Its nozzle was still at 232 °C, and it refused the job. Twenty-seven minutes later it took the same file. That is the send drawn above.

12 October 2026 · Mark2

Refused: seen before

A file that had been started and cancelled was refused as invalid when it was sent again. The identical file under a new name was accepted.

20 September 2026

The other printer

The send dialog offered Mark 1 three times after Mark2’s page had been opened. The tool now reads the name in the dialog and changes it before it presses anything else.

How an agent sends a job

1

Read both printers. Finished and failed both mean idle. A heater still set above zero means wait.

2

Open the checked file in Bambu Connect by pressing its controls the way a screen reader would. The printer ignores a start from any program Bambu has not signed.

3

Check the dialog: the printer named, the spool on each nozzle by type and colour, timelapse and bed levelling on, both calibrations on auto.

4

Press Send once.

5

Listen to the printer. A new job number, the file’s name, preparing or running, no error. An unclear send is never simply repeated.
