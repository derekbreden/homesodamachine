The one idea

Every course lands on something.

A printer builds a part one course at a time, from the bed up. Each road of plastic comes out soft and stays put only if something is already underneath. There are five things it can land on.

Holds for any filament: it is geometry · each plastic’s own limits are in its part

1

Straight up

The course below, directly underneath. A plain wall. Everything else here is a way of getting back to this.

In PET-GF

Every wall of the enclosure.

2

Lean out

Step each course out a little, so most of every road still sits on the one below.

In PET-GF

The lifting handholds, the chamfer under a bed-facing round, the pointed roof of a teardrop hole.

3

Bridge

Pull the road tight between two landings. Both have to be there before the road arrives.

In PET-GF

The connector pockets, the receiver catches, the roofs of the insert pilots.

4

Fin

A thin wall we draw into the part under an edge, to be snapped off at the bench.

In PET-GF

The visible lower edge of the faucet base.

5

Support

Scaffold from the slicer, with a gap so it lets go. Only where a tool can get in to lever it out.

In PET-GF

The two rail catches of front-bottom. Nothing else on that part.

The first three leave nothing to remove.

A wall, a lean and a bridge are all part. A fin has to be snapped off and a support levered out, so each needs a way in. In PET-GF a bridge leaves the roughest underside of the five, so it goes where nobody looks or no tool can reach. Before any of them: can the part be turned so the roof becomes a floor?

Air is not on the list. A road laid over nothing does not hang there waiting for the next one. It droops, and everything printed onto it afterwards goes with it.
