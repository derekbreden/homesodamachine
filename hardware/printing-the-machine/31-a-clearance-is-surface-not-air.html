Fits · Printed face against printed face

A clearance is surface, not air.

A printed face is not the flat plane that was drawn. It is the sides of roads, the ridges between courses and a seam on every loop. Press two together and those touch first. The gap we draw between parts is the room they take.

Seen in PET-GF against PET-GF · Mark 1 and Mark2 · textured PEI · left 0.4 mmNot tried in: other pairs

How it gets readTwo flat faces and nothing between them. Read that way, the gap looks like room to spend somewhere else in a stack.

What it isBoth faces stand a little past where they were drawn. Seated under a screw or its own weight, a part still rests about where it was drawn.

“You do realize our clearances are not filled with free space but are actually filled with rough edges of materials? … if you put a 1mm object next to a 1mm object pressed with several lbs of fource you still don’t get a 2 mm object, you get a 2.15 mm one.”
DEREK · 22 OCTOBER 2026
“Our clearance rules are not "we need more free space", they are "this is the reality of what these surfaces are"”
DEREK · THE SAME DAY

What we do now

Every allowance stays on the face it describes. A face that seats, bears or stops a stroke gets one too. Derek, 16 October: “SEATING FACES ARE NOT EXEMPT FROM THE RULES!”

The slicer’s hole and contour corrections stay at {{petgf:/xy_hole_compensation}}, so every fit is in the drawing, where it can be read.

7 October 2026. Three quarters of the enclosure, the foam shell, the pump cradle and cap, a tee carrier and a funnel frame were on the bench: “And all those prints fit together right now”.

The pocket floor came first. On 23 September Derek said the bottom of a printed cavity “is almost guaranteed to narrow at the tip because of the layer transitions resulting in very slight rounding”.

Still openThe rail slice went together with “plenty of clearance” and “might be a little bit loose”. Derek left it alone until a full-length rail is tried. That result is not in the record.
