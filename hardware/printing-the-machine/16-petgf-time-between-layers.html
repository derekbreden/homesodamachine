Cooling · small parts

A small part needs time between layers.

Each layer is laid on the one before while that one is still giving up its heat. On a big part the nozzle is away for a minute or more. On a part the size of a thumb it is back in a second or two, laying hot plastic on plastic that has not set.

Seen in black and white PET-GF · Mark2 and Mark 1 · textured PEI · left 0.4 mm nozzleNot tried in: any other plastic. PET-GF has no fan to fall back on, so time is all it has

“So that last one came out pretty melty there. I stopped it partway, as you’ll see. So we definitely can’t do anything quite like that again without some major part fan cooling and time between layers.”
DEREK · 26 OCTOBER 2026

The file does not wait by itself. Many profiles slow down when a layer is short. This one will not slow a layer that takes more than {{petgf:/slow_down_layer_time/0}} s.

Three ways it gets the time

1

A batch

Other parts on the plate keep the nozzle busy somewhere else. On 7 November two regulator caps shared a plate with their test receivers, and the file gave each cap 19 seconds or more between layers with no tower. “Like that gives us the layer time already.”

2

A tower

Alone on the plate, the nozzle is sent away after every layer to print a tower, {{petgf-setup-pieces:/pieces/single-regulator-cap/settings/prime_tower_width/value}} mm wide at {{petgf-setup-pieces:/pieces/single-regulator-cap/settings/prime_tower_max_speed/value}} mm/s. The printer’s smooth timelapse move does the sending. 7 November 2026, one cap, Mark2: “The print turned out fine”.

3

A wait

11 November 2026, one white display cover, Mark 1: a tower on all 86 layers, and the nozzle parked above it for ten seconds before each. “Just the cover. With a prime tower and wait between layers so its not a melty blob.”

“But, sometimes I want to print just one.”
DEREK · 7 NOVEMBER 2026

Still openThe retry on the night of 26 October changed two things at once: three parts shared the plate with a 20-second floor on each layer, and the fan ran at 100 percent. The lone cap of 7 November printed fine on time alone, with the fan off except over air. No small part has been printed alone and unhelped since, so there is no control, and nobody has measured a temperature. Twenty seconds was a target borrowed from other people’s practice.
