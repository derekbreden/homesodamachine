The plastic

Choose hardness by the fit.

TPU is the rubbery one: the pump’s elbows, the faucet’s sealing sleeve, the gaskets. The first two must stretch over something rigid, and only the softest grade we own would.

Seen in Bambu TPU 85A, 90A, 95A and TPU for AMS · Siraya TPU 85A · Mark 1 and Mark2 · right 0.6 mm nozzle

68D

Too stiffBambu TPU for AMS · the pump elbow · 13 October 2026

“That was too stiff.” Its maker lets 12 mm³ a second through the nozzle, and the slicer gave the elbow 43.5 minutes. In 85A the same elbow was given 62.

95A

Locked upBambu TPU 95A · the faucet sleeve · 24 May 2026

It went over the tube alone and into the faucet’s port alone, “barely any force required”. “But attempting both at the same time? Nah, not happening.”

90A

Never stretchedBambu TPU 90A · flat gaskets · October 2026

Printed only as flat gaskets, which are squeezed and never stretched. How those fitted was not written down.

85A

FitsBambu and Siraya TPU 85A · sleeve, elbow · May, October 2026

The sleeve seated in the port with the tube in it “for the first time”. The elbow went onto the pump by hand: “nice firm friction stretchy fit”.

“85A is the only one that flexes enough here.”

Still openThe 85A sleeve that seated also had a thicker wall than the 95A one that locked, so that pair changed two things. 90A has never been tried on a stretch fit. Nothing compares the two plates: gaskets printed on textured PEI on 8 October “turned out fine”.

The flow limit is the speed

Each maker caps how much plastic may pass through the nozzle in a second: {{tpu-elbow:/filament_max_volumetric_speed/0}} mm³ for 85A. On a solid part that cap decides the speed. The gasket profile asks for {{tpu-gasket:/outer_wall_speed/0}} mm/s walls. The gasket printed on 23 October 2026 ran them at 21.

How it is fed

Through the right hotend, from a spool outside the printer. No AMS feeds TPU. The spool turns on an axle in a sealed box. To dry it, the Bambu 85A spool went into the AMS HT on 13 October 2026 at {{tpu-drying:/displayed_state/set_temperature_c}} °C for {{tpu-drying:/displayed_state/set_duration_hours}} hours, the cycle published for 90A.

Its bed is not PET-GF’s

A glued Engineering Plate at {{tpu-elbow:/eng_plate_temp/0}} °C, with a Z trim of {{tpu-accepted:/local_user_z_trim_mm}}. PET-GF prints on a {{tpu-petgf:/curr_bed_type}} at {{tpu-petgf:/textured_plate_temp/0}} °C, with a trim of its own on each printer. On 12 October a TPU liner printed at that trim fell over. The next evening Derek changed the plate: “So not our usual 0.18 offset, but with zero offset.”
