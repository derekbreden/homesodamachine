Supports · Inside a cavity

Support blocks do not come out of a cavity.

Where a part hangs over air, the slicer prints scaffold under it. In PET-GF that scaffold is the part’s own plastic, from the same nozzle, and nothing dissolves it. It must be levered out, and a block that fills a cavity leaves a tool nowhere to go.

Two full-size prints lost in a week

3 October 2026, Mark 1. Front-top, nearly 26 hours. Tall blocks, which the slicer calls normal supports, had fused with the trees beside them into masses bigger than the cavities they had to leave through. A cover holder buried in one snapped. Rejected the next day.

8 October 2026, Mark 1. Back-top, 27 hours. Every support had been switched to blocks, to keep the print inside the margin we hold at the plate’s edge. Thrown away the next day.

10 October 2026, Mark 1. Back-top again, on trees. For one groove they came up from far below, long and thin, and stuck in the walls on the way.

“lol, lmfao, it is *every* support here ... yeah, there’s no way I’m going to be able to do anything with this except throw it away”
DEREK · 9 OCTOBER 2026, ON THE SECOND PRINT
“"Auto" is fine ... I’ve yet to see bambu’s auto put a normal support in an impossible to remove place ... it’s when you choose to put normal somewhere that it introduces problems.”
DEREK · THE SAME DAY

What we do now

The shared recipe asks for {{petgf:/support_type}}. No cavity gets a rectangular support, whether a person asked for it or the slicer chose it. On 6 November 2026 Derek made it flat: “So we can’t have normal supports and cavities.”

A tree has to start in the cavity it serves, and allowing that does not make the slicer do it, so we read each start in the sliced file. In the front-bottom sent to print on 11 November 2026, {{front:/summary/model_rooted_bodies}} of {{front:/summary/support_bodies}} supports start inside their cavity, {{front:/summary/shortest_build_up_mm}} mm under the face they carry.
