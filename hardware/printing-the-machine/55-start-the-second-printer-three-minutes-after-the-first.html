Both printers plug into one household circuit. Started within a minute of each other, they tripped its breaker and both jobs were lost. Started four minutes apart, the same two jobs ran to the end.

What happened

Two display covers, one on each printer, were sent 54 seconds apart late on 27 September 2026. The breaker tripped. When the power came back both printers reported their job failed at layer 0, and neither picked it up again. Derek had seen the same thing when he started them by hand.

A cut mid-print is different

Two days earlier both printers had been unplugged by accident. Mark 1 was part of the way through a quarter of the enclosure. It carried on when the power returned, and Derek could see no mark where it had stopped.

What we do

After one printer accepts a job, wait at least three minutes before starting or resuming the other. A resume counts as a start. After a power cut, read both printers first: a job that is still running needs nothing sent.

Who keeps the time

The sending tool stops two sends colliding in the app. It does not keep this interval. That is done by hand, and both start times go in the record. The enclosure quarter of 11 November 2026 records that Mark 1 had started {{h2c-frontjob:/launch/shared_circuit/interval_s}} seconds before it.
