Print around a magnet

Pause. Seat the magnet. Print on.

The level float has a ring magnet closed inside it, with no lid and no seam. The printer builds the float up to the top of an open pocket and stops. The magnet is pushed in by hand. The printer starts again and lays the rest of the float over it.

What happened

The float went to Mark2 at about three in the afternoon. After layer {{aeroprint:/magnet_pause_review/completed_layers_before_pause}} of {{aeroprint:/layers}} the printer parked and waited. Derek pushed one ring into the pocket and pressed resume. The pocket was drawn one layer deeper the same day.

Push in, not drop in

The nozzle is steel and passes close over the magnet. Before the print: “…I think it might just work, especially because the magnet actually sits and grips quite well in the ASA Aero.” It did. The next day the same pause in PET-GF, in a pump cartridge, left a magnet that “rattles”: it had been “too easy to "drop the magnet in" instead of "push the magnet in" as had happened with the aero that worked even better.”

Still openThe deeper pocket went onto a plate of three floats on 8 October 2026, and nobody wrote down how it came out. No finished float has been cut open or held under water. The magnet’s maker rates it for {{aerodesign:/magnet/continuous_operating_limit_c}} °C in steady use, and foam at {{aero:/nozzle_temperature/0}} °C is laid straight onto it. What that costs the magnet has not been measured. What is on record: that evening the float worked a reed switch from 20 mm away.
