What it left behind

A plastic’s lessons stay with it.

From May to August 2026 the faucet was PET-CF. Then the machine moved to PET-GF, glass fibre in place of carbon. What PET-CF had taught about layers and about the printer went along. What it had taught about itself did not.

Seen in PET-CF, May and June 2026 · PET-GF from 15 August 2026 · left nozzle of an H2C · textured PEI

Stayed with PET-CF

Its settings. PET-GF’s profile began as a copy of PET-CF’s, so the first PET-GF print ran on PET-CF’s plate, chamber and flow limit. Ten days later the faucet’s copy was checked against the maker’s sheet. The plate and the chamber were both hotter than PET-GF is meant to have, and they came down. One thing never changed: the name.

Its fits. The push-fit joints settled at five hundredths of a millimetre a side on 21 May. On 10 June, in the second brand, the same joints were too tight and went to a quarter of a millimetre on the diameter. PET-GF’s fits were found again from nothing.

Carried on paper, and wrong

“No snaps.” On 21 May the snap fits came off the pump case, on the reasoning that PET-CF is too stiff and brittle to flex. No snap that broke is on the record. On 25 August the same reasoning was written down as a rule for PET-GF: a catch goes home “never by deflecting”. On 16 September the rule was deleted and the faucet’s display cover got two sprung wings. The next day: “They are tough though, and do spring back.”

“…those broad long thin (but not too thin!) walls provide the necessary give and spring, more so than anything else anyone else has tried, in PET-GF so far.”
DEREK · 20 SEPTEMBER 2026

Went along to PET-GF

The left nozzle, and a hard one. Carbon and glass both wear a nozzle away. Both print from the left hotend, the one that takes tungsten carbide and diamond tips.

Hands off a clog from below. The toolhead comes apart from the top, in order. Learning that broke one and bought the second printer.

A second brand is a second plastic. On 17 May the choice was written up as PET-CF from any maker. Nine days later the second maker’s spool failed on the first one’s settings.

The dryer, and its limit. Both dry in the same dryer. Drying did not fix what was not damp.

15 August 2026. The first PET-GF print was the top front quarter of the enclosure, and on 24 August the faucet’s material was changed to match. About six hours into that first print Derek wrote that it was “working a treat so far” and called what had printed “very beautiful wall surface.”

Still openNo PET-CF snap was ever printed and broken on the record, so “too brittle to snap” is still a reasoning, not a result. PET-GF’s snaps have been judged in the hand. None has a measured force or a count of cycles.
