Supports · Painted by hand

Paint supports by hand and every underside is yours.

Left alone, the slicer puts support under everything that overhangs. To keep supports out of pockets no tool reaches, back-top was switched to supports painted on by hand: none unless asked for. From then on, whatever nobody painted was printed over air.

Holds for any filament: it is method · seen here in PET-GF on Mark 1, textured PEI, left 0.4 mm

“… at least three spots here that are not supported and pretty obviously should be, and it’s printing an error and loops are falling down. Do you see what I’m talking about? What the hell happened here?”
DEREK · 10 NOVEMBER 2026, TWELVE HOURS INTO A 37-HOUR PRINT

What had been checked

Four regions were painted: the rails and the rear outside. Before the print was sent, the sliced file was read for supports in forbidden pockets, for room at the edge of the bed, for the right walls and fill in the right places. All of it passed. Nobody had asked the other question: does every underside have something to land on? He stopped the print.

Every underside gets an owner

The list made that afternoon has 84 downward-facing surfaces on this one piece, in 22 groups. Each group is given one of the five landings, and each support a way out.

Before you print with painted supports: walk the model for every face that points down, not for the ones you remember. For each, find its first course in the sliced file and follow it down to what it rests on. A review that passes has answered only the questions it asked.
