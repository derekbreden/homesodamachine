Orange is what changed from the print above it. Lap is how far the fill is pushed into the loops, °C is the nozzle on the first layer and after, and time is the slicer’s estimate for a body and its lid.

What did not matter

Going slow. September’s recipe asked for 30 mm/s everywhere and took 20 hours. It held on September’s solid floor and leaked in many places on the flat one. The recipe that holds asks for the slicer’s ordinary speeds and is done in under six.

Ten more degrees. Heat went up with three other things on 22 October. It came back down on the 24th, alone, and the body held. Derek had asked for that: the hotter print held, and he did not like the look of it.

What was never isolated

Flow. 0.97, then 1.02, then 1.05, each time with something else. No body has been printed at two flows with the rest left alone.

Loop count. Six became five by reading slices at two, three, four and five loops. Five was never water-tested against six.

Plate and nozzle. The glued plate and the left nozzle came in to stop lifting and fouling. 50% has only ever run on them.
