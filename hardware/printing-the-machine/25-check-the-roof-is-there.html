Supports · Tilted undersides

Before blaming the gap, check the roof is there.

The faucet’s base prints leaning over, so its underside is a slope that curves. Under a flat ceiling a support’s roof is one sheet. Under a slope it has to be built again, a step further out, on every layer.

Seen in PET-GF, support included · Mark 1 and Mark2 · textured PEI · left 0.4 mm · the faucet’s baseNot tried in: any other tilted part

A different problem than we thought

The first faucet printed with Support D looked as if its roof had welded on: the stems broke and something stayed behind. Read back, the sliced file had almost no roof on the slope. The default trees met the part there with bare tips.

What we do now. Read the sliced file for the roof before judging a support. Keep supports out of blind holes, and bridge each hole’s shoulder and roof instead. Derek on why the fifth print let go: “it’s the removal of the heat sets that really made that possible”.
