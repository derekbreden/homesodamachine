Stops and starts

One island at a time.

Each time the nozzle stops laying TPU and starts again elsewhere, a little leaks on the way and a little too much arrives. Two objects on a bed mean that on every layer.

Seen in Bambu and Siraya TPU 85A · Mark 1 and Mark2 · right 0.6 mm nozzleNot tried in: 90A, 95A

“With both ASA Aero and TPU I am finding that the distance between objects increases risk of problems on these "unclean start/stop" filaments.”
DEREK · 12 OCTOBER 2026

That day two liners printed 32 mm apart, and one fell over. The next evening two elbows on one plate failed: “you’re gonna make life much easier on both of us if you only try to print one at a time.”

A timelapse is a hop too. With it on, the head left for the camera after every layer, 184 times in the pair’s file. It is off for TPU.

What we do

One TPU object on a plate, or neighbours two millimetres apart. A pair is drawn as one piece, joined by fins. The fins are cut off with a blade: soft TPU stretches instead of snapping.

“Why didn’t you make the fin between the two objects a vertical fin at the center point of them?”
DEREK · 16 OCTOBER 2026

The first web had followed one leg. Joining alone was not enough: that pair still came out full of threads. The strings page says why.
