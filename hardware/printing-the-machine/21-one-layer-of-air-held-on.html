Supports · The gap

One layer of air held on. Two let go.

A support does not touch the part. The slicer leaves a little air between the support’s roof and the first course of the part, so that the two can be pulled apart, and that first course is laid across the air like a bridge. How much air is the whole question.

The edges changed nothing

The first reports had the support sticking at its edges. So twelve of the sixteen had the support’s roof pulled back 0.6 mm from the walls, from the open edge, or from both. The clean ones are the two-layer ones, whatever was done at their edges. Derek’s best three used three different edge treatments. The best of all had none.

What stayed stuck was the part

A second plate that afternoon had four coupons: a crossed roof, straight lines, straight lines cut apart at their ends, and no roof at all, only the tips of the trees. The same strands stayed under all four. Under the one with no roof there was nothing else they could be. In the sliced file they are the part’s own first course.

What the gap costs

Even where the support let go, that first course hung loose. On all eight clean coupons it looked and felt loose beside the course above it. A face printed over support is rougher and lower than it was drawn, so every fit beside one is given {{fits:/fits/supported_surface}} mm more room. The pages on fits show where that goes.
