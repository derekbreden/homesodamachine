PET-CFThe print log

Spring 2026 · the faucet

Twenty-one tries at one faucet.

PET-CF is PET with chopped carbon fibre in it: stiff, matte and hard on nozzles. This is the print log of its first job here, try by try, in Derek’s words.

Seen in Bambu PET-CF (tries 1 to 15), Polymaker Fiberon PET-CF17 (16 to 21) · H2C · left nozzle · textured PEI

Clogs and air

TRIES 1 TO 6
BY 2 MAY TO 4 MAY
0.4 MM NOZZLES

Two clogs above the hot end. A calibration that failed “over and over”. A print that “started printing air” near layer 15 with nothing blocked. The fifth finished, full of gaps. For the sixth the spool was dried twelve more hours and everything was slowed down, and how it came out was never written. On 4 May, clearing a clog with a wire pushed up from below snapped a pincer in the right-hand heating assembly of the toolhead and cut its cable. The spare was sold out. A second printer was ordered that night, and that is why there are two.

A wider nozzle

TRIES 7 TO 9
9 AND 10 MAY
0.6 MM TUNGSTEN CARBIDE

An off-brand 0.6 mm nozzle arrived on 9 May. With it the stock profile went back on and the supports were printed in PET-CF too. “The most recent PET-CF print worked beautifully.” Try eight, the shell in one piece, “failed in an interesting way, the support tower fell over” and fused into the top of the faucet. Try nine gave the tower a wide first layer and rooted every support on the bed. “The last print is beautiful! Actual success, and I was able to remove all the supports even those from inside of the tube.”

Three pieces

TRIES 10 TO 15
18 TO 22 MAY
TWO PUSH-FIT JOINTS

The gooseneck was cut into three pieces. Drawn with no clearance, the straight joint stopped “just 2 mm short” and the curved one jammed a third of the way in. With half a millimetre and more: “it definitely made a difference, but far too much of one, lol.” Three more prints closed in on five hundredths of a millimetre a side. “Pretty good. Or good enough at least for now.”

A second brand

TRIES 16 TO 18
26 AND 27 MAY
POLYMAKER FOR BAMBU

The Bambu spool ran out and Polymaker’s PET-CF went on with nothing else changed. A tall support came apart and the outer walls had small bare patches. Ten hours in the dryer changed nothing. A hotter nozzle with the fan off, then the first diamond-tipped nozzle and a flow calibration: “They do look better. I still see some, but far less noticeable…” The new brand also printed glossy where the old one was matte.

Screws, a slower top

TRIES 19 TO 21
10 TO 14 JUNE
THE SHELL REDRAWN

The plate under the faucet got three screws (next page) and the tip got a cradle for the display. Everything came out too tight. Try twenty opened the fits: “things fit as well as I need them to for now.” One fault was left, strands drooping at the top of the piece that prints standing on end. Try twenty-one slows its last few millimetres to 40 % speed and 20 % acceleration, with the fan on.

“the direction that things are falling are actually the direction they are being pulled, and it is the force of the movement that is pulling things apart. So my thought is 'less velocity' => 'less force'.”
DEREK · 14 JUNE 2026, ON TRY TWENTY-ONE

Still openTry twenty-one was sliced on 14 June and its result was never written down, so that thought is untested on the record. Try seven changed the nozzle, the profile and the support plastic together, and which of them ended the clogs was never separated.
