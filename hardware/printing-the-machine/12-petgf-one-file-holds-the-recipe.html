The recipe

One file holds the recipe.

A printer does not know what plastic is in it. How hot, how thick, how fast and when to blow are all saved in one project file, petgf.3mf. Every PET-GF job starts from that file, and what a print teaches goes back into it.

Seen in Polymaker Fiberon PET-GF15 · Mark 1 and Mark2 · textured PEI · hardened 0.4 mm nozzles

Where it started

On 15 August 2026 the first enclosure piece ran on a 0.8 mm nozzle in 0.4 mm layers: 290 °C, a plate at 100 °C, the chamber held at 50 °C, no fan. Ten days later the maker’s sheet was read beside it: nozzle 280 to 310, plate 70 to 80, room air, fan off. The nozzle had been in range. The plate and the chamber had not.

Fed and dried

Black PET-GF, nearly all of it, feeds the left hotend, the only side that takes carbide and diamond. White and coloured PET-GF has fed the right, through hardened steel. Polymaker gives one drying cycle, for a spool that has taken on moisture. It is hotter than either AMS reaches, so it runs in a separate dryer. A 3 kg spool turns too stiffly in that dryer to print from, and feeds from the sealed box.

The printer calls it PET-CF

The slot was copied from Bambu’s carbon-fibre preset. Its type still reads {{petgf:/filament_type/0}}, so the screen and the send dialog say PET-CF. Its density still reads {{petgf:/filament_density/0}} where this plastic is {{petgf-setup-pieces:/pieces/foam-shell/settings/filament_density/value/0}}, so the grams the slicer shows for a plate are not this spool’s.

“I don’t know why bambu defaults 0.24 layer height to a 0.20 first layer, but if I don’t have a reason to muck with a bambu default choice, I don’t muck with it.”
DEREK · 25 OCTOBER 2026

Why these numbers are read from the file. On 29 August 2026 an ordinary re-save in the slicer moved this file’s nozzle from 290 to 265 and changed its first-layer preset. Nobody had asked for it and nothing showed it. So this page reads the file each time the book is bound, and a sliced job is read back before it is sent.
