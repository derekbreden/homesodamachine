Supports · The way out

Plan a support from its way out.

Whether a support will come out is settled before the print starts, by the shape around it. A test piece is small and open on every side. The whole part puts the same pocket deep inside, behind its own walls.

Seen in PET-GF, support included · Mark 1 and Mark2 · textured PEI · left 0.4 mmNot tried in: any other plastic

1

A way in for a tool. A small flat screwdriver, and room to swing it.

2

A tree, not a block. Thin where it meets the part.

3

A root inside the cavity. A tree from far below arrives long, thin and stuck to the wall.

4

An open lane. One side of the cavity stays open until the support is out.

5

One piece. A roof that ties the trees together, so the first pry brings all of them.

6

The whole part’s own walls. The pocket is the same. The way to it is gone.

7

A bridge instead. Two full shoulders, a bar pulled across, nothing underneath.

Easy on the test piece

1 November 2026, both printers. Ten test pieces for the rear fittings, each small enough to hold in one hand. Their tiny cavity supports came out with a small flat screwdriver: “just an extra step”.

5 November 2026, Mark2. The whole back-top, 27 hours, with the same receivers inside it and the same supports inside them. Its own walls stood in the way.

“I can’t reach in that far to remove those tiny little supports.”
DEREK · 6 NOVEMBER 2026

Where no tool reaches

“But when there’s not a clear path for the screwdriver, like there’s not on the Pisco there and on the ASSE there, then I need those to be printed without supports entirely.”
DEREK · 6 NOVEMBER 2026

Take the overhang away. The receivers’ catches became bars bridged between two full shoulders, and bores across the rear wall grew pointed roofs. The back-top part study shows both.

Fill it. Derek, 25 October 2026, of pockets beside the display: “inner supported areas that could be filled entirely solid instead of being impossible to remove supports”. Saving plastic is not a goal.

Change the joint. 6 November 2026: “So I’d like four screws for this display cover instead of these impossible-to-remove supports.” The snap-in cover became a screwed one.

What stays supported. A face that has to be flat to do its job keeps its support, where all five needs are met. The front-bottom part study has exactly two such faces, its rail catches. A support that comes out cleanly is not a debt, and a working face is never sloped to be rid of one.

Still openNo full-size enclosure piece printed since these changes has a recorded cleanup. One was stopped, one result is pending and one has not been written down.
