Test pieces

A coupon has to print like the part.

A coupon is a small piece cut out of a big part, so a fit can be tried in an hour instead of a day. It tells the truth only if it keeps everything that decides how the feature prints.

Holds for any filament: it is method · every example on this page is PET-GF

What a coupon keeps

1

The pose. The same way up as the full part, so its layers run the same way and its overhangs face the same way.

2

The layers. The plastic’s own first layer and layer height, the same nozzle and that printer’s own trim.

3

The wall it sits in. A pocket cut free of its wall is a different pocket. Keep the wall and its full thickness.

4

Both landings. Every bridge in the coupon needs both of the landings it has in the part.

5

Every stop. Whatever the mating part touches in the machine, it should touch in the coupon.

What a coupon cannot tell you

Whether a tool can reach

Tiny supports came out of the coupons with a screwdriver. In the full rear quadrant the walls were in the way.

“I can’t reach in that far to remove those tiny little supports.”
DEREK · 5 NOVEMBER 2026

How it feels at full length

A screw hole that passed a screw freely in a short sample dragged in a longer one. Drag grows with the length of plastic the screw passes through.

“On the 12 mm sample it pushed through fine, on the 40 mm sample I got some resistance in some cases and had to screw to get past it.”
DEREK · 26 OCTOBER 2026

How long a layer takes

A small coupon printed alone finishes a layer in a few seconds, and the next goes down on plastic that is still soft. On 26 October 2026 a cable-clip coupon printed that way deformed and was cancelled. The full part gives every layer minutes.
