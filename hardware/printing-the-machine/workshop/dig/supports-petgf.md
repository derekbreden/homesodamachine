A. STORY
September: coupon plates on tree supports found only the larger gap released cleanly (0.48 mm emitted: 8 of 8, against 0 of 8 at 0.24); edge setbacks changed nothing, and the "stuck interface" proved to be the model's own first bridge layer, so Derek added 0.25 mm to supported mating gaps. October: two enclosure prints were lost to normal supports fused in cavities and 0.08 mm layers turning to spaghetti; eight fin plates in one day and the faucet coupons sorted fins from trees and fixed bridge flow, speed and cooling. November: a dense-roof screen (A, B, C) fell off its supports yet stayed stringy, so the gap was closed instead; D left the real rail cavity in one piece and became the shared Support D. D then failed on the faucet's tilted rim until missing interface paths, stems trapped in insert pockets and a stair-stepped first perimeter were fixed. The enclosure settled on short trees rooted inside cavities and no supports where no screwdriver reaches.

B. LESSONS
1. Plan a support from its exit; where a screwdriver cannot reach in the full-size part, print the feature support-free. Why: easy removal needed a tool path, a tree not a block, a root inside the cavity, an open lane and one-piece release; coupon access vanishes behind full walls. Evidence: P/enclosure/enclosure/prints/2026-11-05-back-top-smooth-corners-mark2/physical-result.json; contrast T/rear-snap-retention/physical-result/physical-result.json. Derek: "I can't reach in that far to remove those tiny little supports." Scope: any cavity. Stated.
2. Inside cavities use short trees rooted on the cavity floor, never rectangular supports. Why: local trees pry out together; blocks and tall trees from below fuse and jam. Evidence: P/drain-readiness/prints/2026-10-08-current-back-top-mark1/physical-result.json (27-hour part discarded); P/enclosure/enclosure/front-rail-support-review.json; H/concerns.md. Derek: "So we can't have normal supports and cavities." (398411128). Scope: enclosure; model roots allowed, branch angle limited. Stated.
3. Support D: solid three-layer interface, 0.24 mm requested gap, bridges at 125% flow and 15 mm/s. Why: a solid roof close under the bridge carries the first course and leaves joined. The losers: A, B, C (0.5, 0.15, 0 mm spacing; two layers; 0.48 gap) fell off and stayed stringy; E (0.12) fused a corner and broke up; F (0.06) was never reported. Read the emitted gap, not the request: organic trees round to whole layers (0.45 gives 0.48, or 0.60 over 0.12 mm layers) and a 125% bridge bead is 0.30 tall, so 0.24 prints as 0.18. Evidence: T/petgf-dense-roofs/rail-fit-30/physical-result.json; T/petgf-dense-roofs/physical-result.json; .../close-contact/verification.json; .../rail-cavity-30-E/physical-result.json; T/petgf-interface-edges/README.md; P/support-D-rollout.json. Derek: "It came off as a single piece and it pried out just as easily as the others." Scope: flat ceilings with access; Bambu Studio 2.8. Stated; mechanism inferred.
4. Judge the surface left behind, not the release. Why: the easiest releases shed or loosened the model's first layer. Evidence: P/faucet/finish-coupons/2026-10-10/physical-result/physical-result.json; T/petgf-interface-patterns/results.json. Derek: "So this lifting is actually "perfect removal" for the removal I was initially seeking, before I realized that was removing the first layer." Stated.
5. On tilted undersides, confirm interface paths exist, then put fins and trees under one shared interface and let the first perimeter follow the rim. Why: Default trees left bare tips on the curve; with a roof, tree contacts printed as waves. Evidence: T/petgf-dense-roofs/inclines/contact-reading.json; T/petgf-dense-roofs/inclines/prints/2026-11-10-faucet-{slim-mark1@1fe50e4ff,slim-gap048-mark1@416bf47e9,nonplanar-gap024-widebands-mark1@b06ab316f}/physical-result/physical-result.json; P/faucet/sculpted/prints/2026-11-11-support-and-fit-mark1/physical-result/physical-result.json@7ebe4a45b. Derek: "a different problem than we thought, very different." (3c10c46a0). Scope: faucet base, one Sculpted section printed. Stated.
6. Keep supports out of blind recesses; bridge their shoulders and roofs. Why: stems snap off inside and pin the whole mat. Evidence: T/petgf-dense-roofs/inclines/prints/2026-11-10-faucet-nonplanar-rim-fins-mark1/physical-result/physical-result.json@b388123d2. Derek: "it's the removal of the heat sets that really made that possible". Stated, from one stopped print.
7. No fine layers over supports or on outward-growing overhangs. Why: thin edges shrink back so the next bead prints in air, and the gap rounds wider. Evidence: P/enclosure/tee-readiness/full-enclosure-print/native-slice-reviews/2026-10-03-enclosure-front-top-current-h2c-v17/physical-result.json; P/faucet/finish-coupons/coarse-start-v1/combined/physical-result.json; P/enclosure/enclosure/print-log.md (tee carrier v11-v14). Derek: "0.24 separates every time I think, it just recovers better as more heavy layers are added." (58d4c667b). Scope: PET-GF; unsupported inward rounds keep 0.08 mm. Inferred.
8. Shape the overhang away first: 45-degree corbel, teardrop bore, gabled roof, catch bridged between shoulders, chamfer tangent to a print-down round. Evidence: P/enclosure/enclosure/README.md; P/enclosure/tee-carrier/physical-acceptance.json@d8d50f9a. Derek: "simply add a corbel to avoid the ugly overhang here. We don't need any fine layers." (3dc5416a8). Stated; visible in the parts.
9. Every bridge stroke needs deposited material under both ends. Why: fill landing on a fresh perimeter or thin teeth falls. Evidence: T/front-top-wago-fit/2026-11-12-mark2/physical-result/physical-result.json@a8f24b2b5; T/petgf-fins/short-spans-v2/landing-path-review.json. Derek: "The only reason a bridge works is because it *is* a bridge that actually has a column supporting both ends." Stated.
10. A fin is a thin wall directly under the roof with one empty layer; use it along a visible edge, trees for the field. Why: toothed side fins collapsed and fin fields looked worse than trees, but rim arcs bear under the first perimeter and snap off. Evidence: P/faucet/finish-coupons/2026-10-15-edge-supports/base-mark2/physical-result/physical-result.json; P/faucet/finish-coupons/2026-10-14-foot-fins-current-angle/README.md; T/petgf-fins/under-roof-walls-v8/mark1/physical-result.json. Derek: "this is the best faucet underside I have ever seen". Scope: faucet rim; handhold fins later became corbels. Stated.
11. Fan off except on bridges and overhangs (70%); keep that cooling and bridge speed on the courses just above a fresh bridge. Why: general cooling made PET-GF brittle; the slicer cuts cooling after the bridge and lays the next courses fast. Evidence: P/cooling/README.md; P/enclosure/enclosure/prints/2026-11-05-back-top-smooth-corners-mark2/rail-cooling.json; T/petgf-fins/README.md (125% flow and normal mode won; slopes from 35 degrees off the bed accepted, 30 sagged). Derek: "100% cooling if we want, but only on the places that actually need it" (9ae148e1d). Stated; recovery courses lack a physical test.

C. BAKED-IN BASICS
- Coupons keep the production pose, layer schedule and layer direction; no result transfers to another geometry.
- The G-code is read, not the settings: contact planes, roots, build-up, interface islands, unlabelled support paths.
- Hand-painted supports leave every other underside bare (P/enclosure/enclosure/prints/2026-11-10-back-top-D-mark1/physical-observation/physical-observation.json: "What the hell happened here?").
- Supported mating faces get 0.25 mm extra; a bore only at its supported crown.
- An accessible support is kept ("not a debt"); a working face is never sloped to avoid one.
- Support and interface are the same PET-GF as the part; nothing soluble.
- Faucet parts are tilted so show faces need no support; a toppling start gets a bonded disc on a hidden face; the tree-carried base has no brim.

E. SURPRISES
1. Parts that dropped off their supports unaided were a failure; the faucet's "perfect removal" was its own first layer.
2. With a solid roof the smaller gap removed more easily, in one piece; the larger gap had won earlier with a sparse roof.
3. The default tree style emitted 0.3 m of interface path on the curved faucet against 9 m with Tree Slim: density trials had been testing bare tips.
4. One stem snapped inside an insert pocket pinned the whole support mat.
5. Fan off made a fine-layer overhang worse ("Exploded basically"), and the slicer cuts cooling right after a bridge.
