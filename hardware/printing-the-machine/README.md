# Printing the Machine

What nine months of printing the Home Soda Machine taught us about FDM, one plastic at a time.
[Illustrated PDF](printing-the-machine.pdf) · [Website reader](https://homesodamachine.com/read/printing-the-machine/printing-the-machine.pdf)

Each page of the book is one HTML file in this folder, built on one real print. Its title is the rule that print taught.
Every page says which plastic, printer, plate and nozzle its evidence comes from. A lesson learned in one plastic is
not a rule for another, so read the part for the plastic you are printing.

## Every page, by part

### Front matter

- [What this book is](02-every-page-is-one-of-our-prints.html) — Nearly everything you can see or touch on the Home Soda Machine comes off two desktop printers. Getting there took nine months of prints that failed for reasons. This book is the reasons, each shown on the print that taught it.
- [Contents](03-contents.html)

### Before any filament

- [Every course lands on something](05-every-course-lands-on-something.html) — A printer builds a part one course at a time, from the bed up. Each road of plastic comes out soft and stays put only if something is already underneath. There are five things it can land on. *Holds for any filament: it is geometry · each plastic’s own limits are in its part.*
- [A bridge needs two landings](06-a-bridge-needs-two-landings.html) *Holds for any filament: it is geometry · shown here in PET-GF.*
- [Height moves one layer at a time](07-height-moves-one-layer-at-a-time.html) — The printer cannot lay half a layer. A height in the drawing is rounded to a whole number of layers, so a change smaller than a layer either vanishes or arrives as a whole layer. *Holds for any filament: it is arithmetic · the example is a PET-GF pocket, 12 November 2026.*
- [A coupon has to print like the part](08-a-coupon-has-to-print-like-the-part.html) — A coupon is a small piece cut out of a big part, so a fit can be tried in an hour instead of a day. It tells the truth only if it keeps everything that decides how the feature prints. *Holds for any filament: it is method · every example on this page is PET-GF.*
- [Change one thing](09-change-one-thing.html) — A print has dozens of settings and every one of them moves the result. A trial that changes two cannot say which one did it. *Holds for any filament: it is method · the quotes are from PET-GF, PETG and TPU trials.*
- [The same question, a different answer](10-the-same-question-a-different-answer.html) — Four recipes side by side, each read from the project file that owns it. Hardly a row agrees. Settings that are right for one plastic have failed prints in another, which is why the rest of this book is sorted by plastic. *Read from one part’s project file in each plastic · filled in each time the book is bound.*

### PET-GF

- [PET-GF: it hides its layers and breaks along them](11-petgf-the-plastic.html) — PET-GF is PET with short glass fibres mixed in. The glass makes a wall stiff and gives it a flat, grainy face on which the layers do not show. It also takes the stretch away: bent the wrong way, a thin wall splits between two layers. *Seen in Polymaker Fiberon PET-GF15 · Mark 1 and Mark2 · textured PEI · hardened 0.4 mm nozzles.*
- [PET-GF: one file holds the recipe](12-petgf-one-file-holds-the-recipe.html) — A printer does not know what plastic is in it. How hot, how thick, how fast and when to blow are all saved in one project file, petgf.3mf. Every PET-GF job starts from that file, and what a print teaches goes back into it. *Seen in Polymaker Fiberon PET-GF15 · Mark 1 and Mark2 · textured PEI · hardened 0.4 mm nozzles.*
- [PET-GF: a first-layer trim does not travel](13-petgf-a-trim-does-not-travel.html) — The printer finds the plate by touching it with its nozzle. On a textured plate it touches the tops of the bumps, so how hard a first layer is pressed in depends on that one plate. The trim is the small correction, found by printing. *Seen in PET-GF · Mark 1 and Mark2 · textured PEI · left 0.4 mm nozzle.*
- [PET-GF: season the edge of the plate, or stay off it](14-petgf-season-the-edge.html) — Small parts are printed in the middle of the plate, so the middle gets used and the last few centimetres at the edge do not. PET-GF held on the used part of a textured plate and let go where nothing had been printed. A part that fills the bed finds that out in its first layer. *Seen in black PET-GF · Mark2’s textured PEI · left 0.4 mm nozzle · 20 September, 4 October and 10 October 2026.*
- [PET-GF: fan only where the plastic is over air](15-petgf-fan-only-over-air.html) — The part fan blows room air at the plastic as it leaves the nozzle. A road laid over air needs that, to set before it sags. A plain wall of PET-GF does not. Printed with the fan on everywhere, thin covers cracked along their layers in the hand. *Seen in white and black PET-GF · Mark2 · textured PEI · right and left 0.4 mm nozzles.*
- [PET-GF: a small part needs time between layers](16-petgf-time-between-layers.html) — Each layer is laid on the one before while that one is still giving up its heat. On a big part the nozzle is away for a minute or more. On a part the size of a thumb it is back in a second or two, laying hot plastic on plastic that has not set. *Seen in black and white PET-GF · Mark2 and Mark 1 · textured PEI · left 0.4 mm nozzle.*
- [PET-GF: a matte wall hides the layers and nothing else](17-petgf-a-matte-wall.html) — On a flat PET-GF wall the layers vanish into the grain. That leaves a face so even that anything which breaks it stands out: the seam where every layer starts and stops, a patch of ripples, a flat in the model. Some of it shows only when the light comes from the side. *Seen in black PET-GF · Mark2 · textured PEI · left 0.4 mm nozzle.*
- [PET-GF: layer height is not a knob](18-layer-height-is-not-a-knob.html) — A print is a stack of courses, and the layer height is how thick each one is. It sets the step on every lean, the gap over every support, and how often the nozzle passes the same edge. Change it and all three change. *Seen in PET-GF · Mark 1 and Mark2 · textured PEI · left 0.4 mm.*
- [PET-GF: start a round with a chamfer](19-start-a-round-with-a-chamfer.html) — A round edge that begins on the bed grows outward as it rises: each course is wider than the last. Printed in fine layers, its edges drew back and the next course came down on air. The fan did not fix it. The shape did. *Seen in PET-GF · Mark2 and Mark 1, September 2026 · textured PEI · left 0.4 mm.*
- [PET-GF supports: blocks do not come out of a cavity](20-support-blocks-do-not-come-out.html) — Where a part hangs over air, the slicer prints scaffold under it. In PET-GF that scaffold is the part’s own plastic, from the same nozzle, and nothing dissolves it. It must be levered out, and a block that fills a cavity leaves a tool nowhere to go. *Seen in PET-GF, support included · Mark 1 · textured PEI · left 0.4 mm.*
- [PET-GF supports: one layer of air held on, two let go](21-one-layer-of-air-held-on.html) — A support does not touch the part. The slicer leaves a little air between the support’s roof and the first course of the part, so that the two can be pulled apart, and that first course is laid across the air like a bridge. How much air is the whole question. *Seen in PET-GF, support included · Mark2 · textured PEI · left 0.4 mm.*
- [PET-GF supports: the support that fell off was the failure](22-the-support-that-fell-off.html) — A support has two jobs that pull against each other. It has to hold up the first course of the part while that course is soft, and afterwards it has to let go. One that lets go with no effort at all has usually skipped the first job. *Seen in PET-GF, support included · Mark 1 and Mark2 · textured PEI · left 0.4 mm.*
- [PET-GF supports: Support D, a solid roof and a small gap](23-solid-roof-small-gap.html) — On the evening of 9 November 2026 Mark 1 printed six small ceilings, lettered A to F. Three kept the wide gap and made the support’s roof denser. Three made the roof solid and closed the gap up. Every PET-GF part now prints with the fourth. *Seen in PET-GF, support included · Mark 1 · textured PEI · left 0.4 mm · flat ceilings a tool can reach.*
- [PET-GF supports: plan a support from its way out](24-plan-a-support-from-its-way-out.html) — Whether a support will come out is settled before the print starts, by the shape around it. A test piece is small and open on every side. The whole part puts the same pocket deep inside, behind its own walls. *Seen in PET-GF, support included · Mark 1 and Mark2 · textured PEI · left 0.4 mm.*
- [PET-GF supports: on a tilted underside, check the roof is there](25-check-the-roof-is-there.html) — The faucet’s base prints leaning over, so its underside is a slope that curves. Under a flat ceiling a support’s roof is one sheet. Under a slope it has to be built again, a step further out, on every layer. *Seen in PET-GF, support included · Mark 1 and Mark2 · textured PEI · left 0.4 mm · the faucet’s base.*
- [PET-GF supports: paint supports by hand and every underside is yours](26-every-underside-is-yours.html) — Left alone, the slicer puts support under everything that overhangs. To keep supports out of pockets no tool reaches, back-top was switched to supports painted on by hand: none unless asked for. From then on, whatever nobody painted was printed over air. *Holds for any filament: it is method · seen here in PET-GF on Mark 1, textured PEI, left 0.4 mm.*
- [Front-bottom: one pair of supported faces](27-one-pair-of-supported-faces.html) — It takes about a day to print and it overhangs all over: handholds, vents, rails, seam knuckles. All but two of those places land on plastic. The two are flat because their job needs them flat. *Seen in PET-GF · the print sent to Mark2 on 11 November 2026 · textured PEI · left 0.4 mm.*
- [PET-GF: orientation comes first](28-orientation-comes-first.html) — Before a part has a hole in it, it has a face that will lie on the bed. That choice fixes which surface comes out flat, which way grooves and bores run, and where the undersides fall. *Seen in PET-GF · the four enclosure quadrants as prepared in November 2026 · textured PEI · left 0.4 mm.*
- [Back-top: the roof is the first layer](29-the-roof-is-the-first-layer.html) — Back-top is the lid over the plumbing and electronics. It prints upside down. Its outer roof lies on the plate, and what will hang from the ceiling grows up from it. *Seen in PET-GF · back-top as prepared for Mark2 on 11 November 2026 · textured PEI · left 0.4 mm.*
- [PET-GF: the skin is a printing decision](30-the-skin-is-a-printing-decision.html) — The outside of the box is fluted from floor to roof. A printer draws each layer’s outline in one pass, so a groove that runs straight up is simply part of that outline, layer after layer. Turned sideways, the same groove is a row of ledges. *Seen in PET-GF · every quadrant printed since September 2026 · Mark 1 and Mark2 · left 0.4 mm.*
- [PET-GF fits: a clearance is surface, not air](31-a-clearance-is-surface-not-air.html) — A printed face is not the flat plane that was drawn. It is the sides of roads, the ridges between courses and a seam on every loop. Press two together and those touch first. The gap we draw between parts is the room they take. *Seen in PET-GF against PET-GF · Mark 1 and Mark2 · textured PEI · left 0.4 mm.*
- [PET-GF fits: PET-GF does not stretch](32-pet-gf-does-not-stretch.html) — Glass-filled PET is stiff. A pocket wall gives a few tenths of a millimetre and then an edge lets go. A hard part held by friction gets a pocket drawn at the part’s own size, give or take a tenth of a millimetre. *Seen in PET-GF, black Fiberon PET-GF15 · Mark 1 · textured PEI · left 0.4 mm.*
- [PET-GF fits: a snap bends within its layers](33-a-snap-bends-within-its-layers.html) — A snap is a leaf that bends out of the way and springs back. Printed so that every road runs the length of the leaf, the plastic itself takes the bend. Printed in courses across the leaf, the bend pulls one course off the next. *Seen in PET-GF · Mark 1 and Mark2 · textured PEI · left 0.4 mm · caps printed solid.*
- [PET-GF fits: never load a peg across its layers](34-never-load-a-peg-across-its-layers.html) — Each layer is welded to the one below, and that weld is the weakest thing in a print. A peg standing up off a plate joins it along one such weld. Push the peg sideways and that weld takes the whole load. *Seen in PET-CF first: a faucet plate, May 2026, 0.6 mm nozzle · then PET-GF: a pump base, 2 November 2026, Mark 1.*

### PETG

- [PETG: plain PETG holds the flavoring](35-plain-petg-holds-the-flavoring.html) — The enclosure is stiffened with glass fibre. The two reservoirs are not. They and their lids are plain clear PETG, laid a little fat so that every road is pressed into its neighbours. *Seen in Bambu PETG Translucent Clear · Mark 1 · glued Engineering Plate · left 0.8 mm.*
- [PETG: it weeps where the floor meets the wall](36-it-weeps-where-the-floor-meets-the-wall.html) — A floor is printed in two parts: loops that follow its outline, then straight fill inside them. Unless the fill is pushed into the loops, the line where the two meet lets water through. *Seen in Bambu PETG Translucent Clear · Mark 1 · 0.8 mm nozzle · flat 3 mm floor.*
- [PETG: a floor made of loops leaks along its seams](37-a-floor-made-of-loops-leaks-along-its-seams.html) — Loops laid side by side run out of room where they turn a corner and where they meet in the middle. Each of those places is a small gap, and it comes back on every layer. *Seen in Bambu PETG Translucent Clear · Mark 1 · right 0.8 mm · textured PEI.*
- [PETG: slow and hot were not what sealed it](38-slow-and-hot-were-not-what-sealed-it.html) — Between the reservoir that leaked everywhere and the one that holds, the print got three times quicker and, for one body, ten degrees hotter. The heat came back out and it still held. Flow, loop count and the first lap step moved together. *Seen in Bambu PETG Translucent Clear · one reservoir · printer, nozzle and plate named in each row.*
- [PETG: five hundredths of a millimetre](39-five-hundredths-of-a-millimetre.html) — The first layer is pressed into the plate through a gap the printer sets for itself. A little too small and the plastic has nowhere to go. It tears and smears, and the printer stops to say its extruder is overloaded, which sounds like a different problem. *Seen in Bambu PETG Basic, white · Mark 1 · glued Engineering Plate · right 0.2 mm nozzle · 8 November 2026.*

### TPU

- [TPU: choose the hardness by the fit](40-choose-the-hardness-by-the-fit.html) — TPU is the rubbery one: the pump’s elbows, the faucet’s sealing sleeve, the gaskets. The first two must stretch over something rigid, and only the softest grade we own would. *Seen in Bambu TPU 85A, 90A, 95A and TPU for AMS · Siraya TPU 85A · Mark 1 and Mark2 · right 0.6 mm nozzle.*
- [TPU: the nozzle goes around, the string goes straight](41-the-string-goes-straight.html) — Soft TPU never quite stops coming out. When the nozzle stops printing and moves, a thread stays attached where it left off and follows. Sending the nozzle the long way round, over the walls, does nothing. The thread is not on that route. It is pulled tight between its two ends. *Seen in TPU 85A, both brands · Mark 1 and Mark2 · right 0.6 mm nozzle.*
- [TPU: less fan made it curl more](42-less-fan-made-it-curl-more.html) — Where a wall leans out, the newest ring of plastic has air under its outer edge. In soft TPU that edge curled upward while the fan was blowing at full. Derek halved the fan to see what would happen. Both lips curled at once. *Seen in Bambu TPU 85A, black · Mark 1 · glued Engineering Plate · right 0.6 mm nozzle.*
- [TPU: the setting said 10, the lip ran at 16](43-the-setting-said-10.html) — A slicer keeps more than one speed for the same wall. Where the wall leans out, Bambu Studio takes its speed from a separate overhang table. Two trials that were meant to print a curling lip slowly printed it faster than the trial before them. *Seen in Bambu TPU 85A · Mark 1 · right 0.6 mm nozzle · sliced in Bambu Studio.*
- [TPU: one island at a time](44-one-island-at-a-time.html) — Each time the nozzle stops laying TPU and starts again elsewhere, a little leaks on the way and a little too much arrives. Two objects on a bed mean that on every layer. *Seen in Bambu and Siraya TPU 85A · Mark 1 and Mark2 · right 0.6 mm nozzle.*
- [TPU: every underside at 45 degrees or steeper](45-every-underside-45-degrees-or-steeper.html) — The elbow prints standing on its corner like a V, both mouths up. Stood that way, no face looks down at the bed more shallowly than 45 degrees and no bore needs support. Its two sides are still not alike. *Seen in Bambu TPU 85A, black · Mark 1 · glued Engineering Plate · right 0.6 mm nozzle.*
- [TPU: a thin soft wall starts on a cap](46-a-thin-soft-wall-starts-on-a-cap.html) — The sleeve that seals the faucet’s tube is a cylinder of soft plastic one bead thick. A wall that thin has almost nothing to stand on, and the first version could not be sliced at all. It now begins with a flat cap, laid first, and the wall rises from that. *Seen in Bambu TPU 95A in May 2026, 85A since · the sleeve of 23 October 2026: Mark2, glued Engineering Plate, right 0.6 mm nozzle.*
- [TPU: two brands, one hardness](47-two-brands-one-hardness.html) — Two spools both say 85A. One curled its lips on three elbows in an evening. The other printed them clean at its first try, an hour after the last. Six things changed between those two prints, and nobody has gone back to find out which one mattered. *Seen in Bambu TPU 85A and Siraya TPU 85A · Mark 1 · glued Engineering Plate, no Z trim · right 0.6 mm nozzle.*

### ASA Aero

- [ASA Aero: it prints lighter than it feeds](48-it-prints-lighter-than-it-feeds.html) — ASA Aero foams when it is heated. It swells in the nozzle, so the printer feeds about half the plastic of an ordinary line and the foam makes up the rest. *Seen in Bambu ASA Aero · Mark2 · glued Engineering plate · right 0.4 mm hardened nozzle.*
- [ASA Aero: pause, seat the magnet, print on](49-pause-seat-the-magnet-print-on.html) — The level float has a ring magnet closed inside it, with no lid and no seam. The printer builds the float up to the top of an open pocket and stops. The magnet is pushed in by hand. The printer starts again and lays the rest of the float over it. *Seen in Bambu ASA Aero · Mark2 · glued Engineering plate · right 0.4 mm hardened nozzle.*
- [ASA Aero: every jump carries a bit along](50-every-jump-carries-a-bit-along.html) — When the nozzle moves without printing, ASA Aero keeps coming out of it. A bit goes along on every move and is left wherever the nozzle lands. So the float is printed with as few of those moves as its shape allows. *Seen in Bambu ASA Aero · Mark2 · glued Engineering plate · right 0.4 mm hardened nozzle.*

### PET-CF

- [PET-CF: twenty-one tries at one faucet](51-twenty-one-tries-at-one-faucet.html) — PET-CF is PET with chopped carbon fibre in it: stiff, matte and hard on nozzles. This is the print log of its first job here, try by try, in Derek’s words. *Seen in Bambu PET-CF (tries 1 to 15), Polymaker Fiberon PET-CF17 (16 to 21) · H2C · left nozzle · textured PEI.*
- [PET-CF: a plastic’s lessons stay with it](52-a-plastics-lessons-stay-with-it.html) — From May to August 2026 the faucet was PET-CF. Then the machine moved to PET-GF, glass fibre in place of carbon. What PET-CF had taught about layers and about the printer went along. What it had taught about itself did not. *Seen in PET-CF, May and June 2026 · PET-GF from 15 August 2026 · left nozzle of an H2C · textured PEI.*

### The H2C

- [The H2C: each plastic has its side, plate and feed](53-each-plastic-has-its-side-plate-and-feed.html) — Both printers are Bambu Lab H2Cs, with two hotends each. A print settled which side, plate and feed every plastic gets. *Seen in Mark 1 and Mark2 · every plastic the machine is made of · each row of the table is one dated print.*
- [The H2C: measure where the second nozzle lands](54-measure-where-the-second-nozzle-lands.html) — Two nozzles sharing a part must lay their lines side by side. The printer measures the gap between its own nozzles before every job. On Mark2 that still left white lettering out of its black pockets. *Seen in PET-GF · Mark2 · textured PEI · both 0.4 mm · 29 September 2026.*
- [The H2C: start the second printer three minutes after the first](55-start-the-second-printer-three-minutes-after-the-first.html) — Both printers plug into one household circuit. Started within a minute of each other, they tripped its breaker and both jobs were lost. Started four minutes apart, the same two jobs ran to the end. *Seen in Mark 1 and Mark2 on one circuit · both printing black PET-GF from the left 0.4 mm · the night of 27 September 2026.*
- [The H2C: a send is proven only by a new job number](56-a-send-is-proven-only-by-a-new-job-number.html) — The printer starts a job only when Bambu’s own program asks. So an agent presses that program’s buttons, then listens to the printer itself. Several times the press did nothing, and nothing on the screen said so. *Seen in Mark2 · PET-GF jobs · sent through Bambu Connect · autumn 2026.*
- [The H2C: one overload message, three causes](57-one-message-three-causes.html) — The printer feels how hard its motor has to push the filament, and pauses when that is too hard. The message is the same whatever is in the way. We have had three different things in the way. *Seen in recorded on Mark 1, three times · PET-GF on a left 0.4 mm, twice · white PETG on a right 0.2 mm, once.*

### At the back

- [Seven things that had to be said again](58-seven-things-that-had-to-be-said-again.html) — Since late September 2026, every change to the machine is saved with the words that asked for it. ## Keeping the book

- **Pages** are the `NN-title.html` files here, numbered in reading order. Text is plain HTML; drawings are inline SVG, each with a
  title and a description. `style.css` holds every style.
- **Numbers.** A current setting appears in a page as `{{source:/json/pointer}}` and is filled from its owning file when the book is
  bound. [`guide-inputs.json`](guide-inputs.json) names the sources. A number from a dated trial is typed, with its date.
- **Pictures.** [`art/manifest.json`](art/manifest.json) lists each photograph with the file and Git revision it was copied from, and
  each model picture with the render job that drew it.
- **Binding.** `tools/run hsm-manual-guides bind printing-the-machine` draws every page, stops on any whose content overflows, and
  writes `printing-the-machine.pdf`, its cover and its source receipt here.
- **Adding a page.** Start from one print: its date, printer, plastic, plate and nozzle, and a photograph. Say what happened, the rule
  it taught and how far that has been tested. Put it in its plastic's part and link the record.
- **Where it came from.** The generator that drew the first pages, the brief its writers worked from and their research
  are archived in Git at [`474d5e90c`](https://github.com/derekbreden/homesodamachine/tree/474d5e90c84781b467263a23998406cf1c72f61a/hardware/printing-the-machine/workshop).
  The pages here are edited directly.
