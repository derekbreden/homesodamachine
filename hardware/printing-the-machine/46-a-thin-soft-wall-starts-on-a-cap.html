First layer

A thin soft wall starts on a cap.

The sleeve that seals the faucet’s tube is a cylinder of soft plastic one bead thick. A wall that thin has almost nothing to stand on, and the first version could not be sliced at all. It now begins with a flat cap, laid first, and the wall rises from that.

Seen in Bambu TPU 95A in May 2026, 85A since · the sleeve of 23 October 2026: Mark2, glued Engineering Plate, right 0.6 mm nozzleNot tried in: Siraya 85A

22 May 2026: refused

The first sleeve was an open ring with a wall 0.375 mm thick. The slicer would not start it: “empty initial layer”. One first-layer line was wider than the wall.

24 May: cap down

A floor with a hole in it was added to one end and the part was turned to print on it. The first layer is now a flat ring several beads across. It sliced without a raft and printed without trouble, in 95A.

30 May: one bead, one spiral

The wall was thickened to one bead of the {{tpu-sleeve:/line_width}} mm profile and the softer 85A went in. After the cap the nozzle climbs in a single unbroken spiral. A spiral never stops, so the wall cannot string. The cap can: its flat layers had five places where a thread could cross the hole, until the 23 October file laid each of them as one tour.

Drawn thick, printed thin

In spiral mode only the bottom layers are solid. This recipe has {{tpu-sleeve-cap:/native_bottom_layers}}, which makes a cap {{tpu-sleeve-cap:/native_solid_cap_height_mm}} mm thick. The model draws it {{tpu-sleeve-cap:/modeled_cap_height_mm}} mm thick. Read the cap from the slice, not from the drawing.

Flat gaskets print solid

A gasket is squeezed between two faces, so it is laid flat and filled: {{tpu-gasket:/wall_loops}} walls round every edge and {{tpu-gasket:/sparse_infill_density}} fill between, in Bambu 85A on the stock profile. In June 2026: “gaskets are going well.” The plate of eight printed on Mark2 on 8 October “turned out fine”.

“…consider carefully if there’s any lessons we’ve learned from our avoiding threads other places that might apply here…”
DEREK · 23 OCTOBER 2026, ASKING FOR THE SLEEVE

Still open“Pretty well” is all that is recorded of the 23 October sleeve. Whether it strings less, how it seats, and whether a cap a third as thick as drawn seals as intended are not written down. Nor is the result of the gasket printed the same evening with every layer as one tour.
