Landing 3 · Bridge

A bridge needs two landings.

Holds for any filament: it is geometry · shown here in PET-GF

A bridge is the one time the printer lays plastic over air on purpose. It works because the road is fixed at one end, pulled across, and pressed down at the other while it is still soft. It hangs like a line pulled tight. Take away either end and it is just a road laid over nothing.

DEREK · ON SEEING THAT COUPON

How it gets in

Nobody draws a bridge to nowhere on purpose. It arrives as a side effect, when a relief, a chamfer or a cutout made for some other reason passes through the wall a bridge was resting on. On the rear receiver it was a service opening. Five days later, on these pockets, it was clearance for a lever.

Before you print

In the slice, find the first road across every opening and follow each of its ends straight down. If either end is over air it is not a bridge, whatever the slicer calls it. After any cut near an opening, look at the roofs beside it again.
