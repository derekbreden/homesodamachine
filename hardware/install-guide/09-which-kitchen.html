Quick Start 345

Which kitchen
do you have?

Two tees came in the kit. You will use one of them. Which one depends on what is already under your sink on the cold side.

Open the cabinet and look at the cold side

Is there a soft 1/4-inch plastic tube on a push fitting — the kind a fridge or an under-sink filter runs on?

Then use the black tee. The Quick Start draws the whole thing at 345, and there is nothing to turn. Come back here when it is in.

Or is the cold supply a braided hose on a shut-off valve, with no 1/4-inch line anywhere?

Then use the white tee, and turn the page. Two joints with a wrench, and the appliance's own line still just pushes in. Steps 12 and 6 on the sheet are unchanged.

Your long white run arrives pushed into the black tee's side port. Press that collet with the little printed tool and pull the run out of it — the black tee and its short jumper are not used on this path.

Close the right valve

Whichever path you are on, the valve you close is the one feeding the fitting you are about to open. Follow the line back with your hand, from the fitting to the first valve you meet, and turn that one a quarter turn. Then run the cold side of your kitchen tap: if it goes dry, you closed the right valve. Leave it open a moment to drop the pressure.

Mind this

A closed valve is not an empty line. There is still water standing in the pipe between the valve and the fitting, and it comes out when you open the joint. Put a towel and a cup under the work before you start.
