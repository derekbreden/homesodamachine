# Flute evidence

Two photographs of **full-size enclosure front assemblies** — front-top and front-bottom, stood
together — printed once in PETG and once in PET-GF and set side by side. They are what the
appearance claims in `flute_skin.py`, `enclosure.py` and the cold core's own prose are read off.

This is not the corner coupon and does not stand in for it. That coupon — two 50 mm standing
walls meeting at the box's rounded corner, at `c14bb2fff` under
`hardware/printed-parts/enclosure/texture-corner/` — settled the **figures**: the profile, the
depth, the sampling step, the ramp distance, and that a groove crosses the corner's quarter turn
by arc length without a seam. A sample that size can prove a groove cuts and prints clean. It
cannot show a whole show face at production size, with the openings, the 45° facet and the top
rails a finished part actually has, under the light a kitchen actually has. That is the only
thing these two photographs are for.

**Which unit is which material is not the same in the two frames.** They were shot at different
times from different sides. The filenames carry the reading and the sections below repeat it,
because nothing in either frame labels itself.

## 01 — flat faces, raking light · PET-GF left, PETG right

Interior flats under low sun. The left bay is one continuous matte surface — a fine granular
speckle with no direction in it. The right bay is the same geometry covered in horizontal
striations, worst on the sloped ceiling: the shallower the angle to the light, the more the
banding amplifies.

The small features — mounting rails, boss pads, screw pockets — read nearly identically on both.
Edges and small facets hide the process. **It is large uninterrupted planes that give the
material away**, and on those the difference is not subtle.

## 02 — flute against flat, diffuse light · PETG left, PET-GF right

Four cells in one exposure: fluted and flat, PETG and PET-GF.

The fluted halves are indistinguishable. The groove field lays its own highlight and shadow at a
spacing finer, and in far higher contrast, than a layer line, so the layer lines fall under it
and both faces simply read black.

The flat cells separate. On the left unit's inner flank — the vertical strip beside the display
pocket, the box's own left, the viewer's right — the light splashes at a grazing angle and the
surface blooms warm bronze, with the striations legible inside the highlight. The right unit's
corresponding flank, same height and same light, stays dark and reads only slightly granular.

**The light in this frame favours PETG.** It is diffuse, not the raking sun of 01, which is the
condition under which layer lines are most forgiving. PETG gives itself away anyway, on the one
flat flank the light grazes. That is what makes this frame evidence rather than illustration,
and why it is the one to reach for — 01 is more dramatic and far easier to dismiss as a chosen
condition.

## What they hold

**A fluted vertical wall does not disclose its material.** Both materials read the same fluted.
This is why the flute field is not styling: it is what makes the material choice invisible on
the exterior, and what leaves an imperfect print usable as a customer-facing surface. PET-GF is
difficult to print perfectly, so on a fluted wall the field is insurance as much as ornament.

**A flat show face does disclose it, and does so under ordinary light.** Not only under a raking
sun — 02 is diffuse and the flank still blooms.

**The two mechanisms cover complementary halves of the show surface, and neither covers both.**
The field is Z-invariant: a flute is the outer wall loop taking a wavier path, so every layer
traces the same toothed perimeter. Tip a face off vertical and the groove stops being a wall
feature and becomes top-shell geometry, rendered as stepped solid infill — it does not degrade,
it changes into a different mechanism. So the show surface splits in two. The standing walls are
fluted and material-invisible. The 45° display facet, the top rails and the recessed pockets
around the drop cutouts cannot be fluted at all, and on those the material is the whole of the
answer. Fluting handles the first set; PET-GF handles the second.

## What they do not hold

No scale, no calibrated light, no controlled exposure. Nothing here is a measurement and no
figure may be read off either frame. The figures the field is cut at are the corner coupon's.
