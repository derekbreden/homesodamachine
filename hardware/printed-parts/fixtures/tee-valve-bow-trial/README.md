# Tee–valve bow trial

This is the Step-3 bench coupon for one lifted fore valve, one anchor tee and the short
1/4-inch LLDPE tube between them. It is an experiment, not appliance geometry. The machine
must not depend on the bowed stub until a physical run here shows that the tee can make its
release stroke without losing either insertion, kinking or leaking.

The frame holds the two real purchased parts in the machine's relative axes:

- the Beduan valve is fixed in one ordinary four-post valve-tray socket;
- the PP0208E tee's branch collar runs in the same kind of radial journal as the tee wall, free
  to translate along the branch axis;
- the upper tee sleeve face and the valve's downward sleeve face are coaxial and **10.0 mm
  apart** at rest;
- pulling the tee away from its rest datum shears those two tube endpoints by the measured
  **3.15 mm release stroke**, with a **4.62 mm modeled ceiling** available as a separate check.

`tee-connector.step` remains the tree's McMaster stand-in for the PP0208E outline. The fixture
uses that committed solid and its checked branch-collar stations, but the trial itself must use
the production PP0208E in hand. The valve seat is generated by
`valve_tray.build_valve_tray`, from the Beduan reference's post pattern; it is not a second
fixture-only approximation of the socket.

The assembly STEP contains no modeled tube. A guessed arc would quietly turn a length trial
into a prescribed bend shape, while collet tilt and end compliance are exactly what the bench
must discover.

## The gauge

The rectangular window has two inside faces **12.0 mm apart**. Those faces gauge the distance
between the two sleeve-face marks on a fitted tube after it is removed and gently laid straight;
they do not specify a stock cut. The valve's insertion depth has not been measured, so no total
tube length belongs in this source before the trial.

The two tongues share one stop plane:

- **narrow tongue:** 3.15 mm, the required release travel;
- **wide tongue:** 4.62 mm, the modeled travel ceiling.

Both lengths lie in the print plane rather than in Z, so layer height does not quantize them.
Measure the printed window and tongues with calipers before treating them as gauges; record the
as-printed dimensions with the trial result.

The circular-arc figures printed by `selftest` are comparison witnesses only. A 12.0 mm arc over
the endpoints is not the mechanism's mandated shape and is not a bend-radius claim.

## Print and set up

Print the frame and gauge in PET-GF15. The exported frame is already laid on its broad rear face
with Z=0 on the bed; the valve sockets and tee journal open upward. The gauge prints flat. No
support is intended on either part.

1. Deburr only loose whiskers. Do not ream the valve sockets or the tee journal before recording
   the first fit.
2. Press one Beduan valve into the four sockets until its round body boss is on the fixture face.
3. Feed the production PP0208E's branch nose through the round guide from the component side.
   At rest, its branch sleeve face is flush with the broad rear datum. Confirm that it slides
   without yaw and still has journal engagement at the 4.62 mm station.
4. Verify the two opposed sleeve faces are 10.0 mm apart with depth jaws or gauge blocks. Record
   the actual separation; do not trim the fixture to make a failed reading pass.

## Fit the trial stub

Use production 1/4-inch OD LLDPE. The tee's measured depths are from the pressed sleeve face:
first resistance at 7.0 mm, grip at 8.5 mm and bottom at 10.0 mm. The corresponding depths from
the extended face are 8.65, 10.15 and 11.65 mm. The valve-side insertion remains a physical
reading for this trial.

1. Start long. Seat both ends fully, without holding either release sleeve down, and mark the
   tube exactly at both extended sleeve faces.
2. Remove the tube without stretching it and lay the marked exposed span gently straight across
   the gauge window. Trim and repeat until the two marks meet the two 12.0 mm inside faces while
   both ends still bottom when assembled. Record the final total cut length as a result, not as
   an input to this fixture.
3. Refit the stub. The two sleeve-face marks must return to the faces; a mark disappearing into
   a fitting or standing away from it means one end changed insertion.

## Exercise the release

Keep the valve fixed. Pull the tee along its branch axis; do not twist the tee or use the tube as
the handle.

1. Put the narrow tongue between the frame's rear datum and the tee branch sleeve face. Reach
   3.15 mm repeatedly, returning to the flush rest datum each time. Record cycle count, peak
   hand force or force-gauge reading if available, and any whitening, flattening or permanent
   set in the tube.
2. Confirm after the run that both sleeve-face marks remain at their faces and both ends still
   resist a firm axial tug.
3. Use the wide 4.62 mm tongue for one cautious ceiling excursion only after 3.15 mm succeeds.
   It is clearance evidence, not the operating target. Stop at a hard rise in force, a sleeve
   beginning to press, a kink, or either mark moving.
4. Leak-test the made-up valve–stub–tee chain at the pressure and method used for the plumbing
   bench, then repeat the 3.15 mm motion and leak-test again.

## Pass record

The Step-3 premise passes this coupon only when all of these are recorded on the same physical
stub:

- at least 3.15 mm of repeated tee travel with the valve fixed;
- both tube ends remain fully inserted and tug-resistant;
- no kink, crease, pullout, sleeve actuation or visible permanent damage;
- no leak before or after the motion run;
- as-printed gauge dimensions, final tube cut length, achieved travel and cycle count retained
  with the result.

A 4.62 mm excursion is margin evidence, not required operating travel. A failure is a result:
record where it occurred and change the mechanism dimensions rather than the coupon.
