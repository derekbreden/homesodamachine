# Reading The Pour

The Pour is a physical reference for the home soda machine's printed surfaces and
interfaces. Keep one assembled example per filament and print recipe. The marked
alternatives belong to this object; the machine's production dimensions and
settings remain in their own source files.

Use diffuse light to compare the overall finish, then light grazing the surface
to reveal layer steps and seams. Stroke the same region in the same direction on
each specimen. For a fit comparison, use the same mating piece and approach it
from the same direction.

Keep the printed identifiers with their pieces. Record the filament, nozzle,
printer, layer heights, orientation, support settings and any local overrides
beside the assembled object. The identifiers locate the samples; the saved
print projects establish how they were made.

The neck sections keep their original geometry without added lettering. Each
has a marked storage shoe and keeper bar: N08 uses the N08D and N08K files,
both bearing the N08 recipe mark.
Preserve the plate identities when loading the shoes. Slide a keeper out along
the shoe's front-to-back direction to lift its neck for close comparison, and
keep that neck with its marked shoe. Angle blades and support windows carry
their IDs; sliders carry their decimal clearance. G is the unique full-length
grip strip, and GR is its bridge receiver.

## Read the marked comparisons

| Mark | What it asks | Saved comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| N08, N12, N24 | How do the real tilted faucet neck and its ordinary seam look and feel at each layer height? | 0.08, 0.12 and 0.24 mm layers on identical neck sections. |
| A08, A12, A24 | How do changing slopes and curves expose those layer heights? | The same angle blade at 0.08, 0.12 and 0.24 mm layers. |
| NR00, NR05, NR15 | Does the regular seam leave a groove, a flat join or a ridge? | Regular seam gaps of 0%, 5% and 15%, with 0.12 mm layers. |
| NS00, NS10, NS20 | How does trimming the scarf overlap change the curved seam? | Contour scarf gaps of 0%, 10% and 20%, with 0.12 mm layers. |
| S15, S25, S35, S45 | How does lateral sliding clearance change binding, smoothness and play? | 0.15, 0.25, 0.35 and 0.45 mm X clearance per side. R-suffixed parts are their receivers. |
| R30, R45, R60 | How does support separation affect removal and the roof left behind? | Requested top support distances of 0.30, 0.45 and 0.60 mm; identical 3 mm ceilings at 0.24 mm layers. |
| G, GR | How does the complete grip strip bend into its end slots and recover? | Full-length strip at 0.08 mm layers and an adapted receiver with the grip's current wing section and fit allowances. |
| P front deck | How do the tube holes, signal slot and small lettering print in this material? | Ø6.65 and Ø4.40 mm holes and a 5.4 × 1.8 mm slot through the 6.6 mm deck, with raised/recessed markings. |

Every project uses a 0.20 mm first bed layer. The N and A layer heights apply to
the subsequent model layers. These pieces are printed separately so each is an
independent finish reference.

The regular and scarf gap percentages describe a distance relative to the
0.4 mm nozzle diameter: 5% is 0.02 mm, 15% is 0.06 mm and 20% is 0.08 mm.
They are not flow percentages. The NS specimens keep a 10 mm scarf length,
10% start height, ten steps, the smart 155° setting and inner-wall scarf;
entire-loop scarf is off. Their regular seam gap is held at 15%. Judge the rear
join under grazing light and by touch, then check the nearby wall for an open
gap, broad trough or disturbed finish.

The angle blades are representative surfaces constructed for this object. Their
marked outer ramps run from 0° to 90° above the print bed, in 15° increments.
Their upper R6 curves lie in the YZ print plane; the small X-marked R6 shoulder
lies in the orthogonal XZ plane. Compare both directions on each blade. The
nominal curved strip is 3 mm thick. These surfaces expose layer stepping; the
real faucet section in N carries the direct geometry comparison.

The S15 files bear the printed number 0.15, S25 bears 0.25, and so on. Those
numbers are per-side clearance, so S15 has 0.30 mm total lateral air
and S45 has 0.90 mm. Their floor clearance remains 0.25 mm and the clearance
above the wings remains 0.45 mm. The four short tongues have the same contact
shape; their labels differ. Clear each receiver's supports before sliding and
use the same tongue across the four lanes to compare the receiver gaps.

The R windows have the same 30 mm wide cantilever roof and fully open removal
access. A requested support distance is distinct from the separation emitted on
the slicer's layer grid and from the finished underside. Let each sample cool,
inspect it with its support still attached, then remove the support and compare
the remaining model edge, roof strands and continuity. Keep the removed support
with its identifier until the three surfaces have been compared.

The prepared native slices put the first ceiling layer at Z17.00–17.24 mm
in each specimen's bed frame. The last support-interface extrusion below that
layer gives these planned gaps:

| Window | Requested top distance | Last interface nozzle Z | Emitted planned gap |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| R30 | 0.30 mm | 16.76 mm | 0.24 mm |
| R45 | 0.45 mm | 16.52 mm | 0.48 mm |
| R60 | 0.60 mm | 16.28 mm | 0.72 mm |

These are commanded separations, measured from the layer's nominal underside
at Z17.00 mm. Real strand sag, attachment and cleanup remain physical results.

The referenced accepted prints establish particular reported appearances or fits.
Physical results for The Pour are unrecorded. Short sections and a plate of
several objects change cooling, layer return time, warping and support placement.
Use them to choose a promising comparison, then judge the affected complete part
before changing its production recipe. Surface finish and hand feel do not
measure load capacity or lifetime.

## Assemble the desk reference

Use the individual STL files at 100% scale in their saved print poses. The
assembled STEP shows where the pieces belong. With the carrier upright:

1. Seat the four R-suffixed sliding receivers on the front pairs of pegs. Feed
   their matching tongues into the open lanes with the numbered handles facing
   forward.
2. Seat R30, R45 and R60 on the middle pairs of pegs, and A08, A12 and A24 on
   the three long pairs behind them.
3. Seat GR on the two elevated front posts. Tuck one G wing into its slot,
   bow the strip downward enough to enter the second wing, and release it.
   This crown presents the finger face upward. Pull the centre downward to
   remove the strip.
4. Put each neck into its matching D shoe with the flat cut bottom on the
   shoe floor. Slide its matching K keeper through the upper side slots.
   The keeper clears the cut top by 0.40 mm. Place each completed holder on
   its marked carrier shelf, with its label facing forward.

The holders park by gravity on the shelves. Keep the assembled object upright;
lift a holder out before removing its keeper and examining the neck. The storage
fits are separate from the marked calibration clearances.

## The features behind the reference

The neck specimens retain the actual Industrial faucet section: a 27 mm round
exterior, its internal passages and the production −15° pose, cut horizontally
into an 18.2 mm tall specimen. The source is the
[frozen faucet neck swatch](../../faucet/finish-coupons/scarf-swatches-v1/README.md).
Compare the rear seam and a clean region away from it separately. The
[Industrial faucet acceptance](../../faucet/industrial/physical-acceptance.json)
establishes the identified complete faucet's shipping appearance. The swatch's
short-section heat exposure and bed start differ from the complete faucet.

The grip crown uses the complete
[flat-wing grip interface](../../enclosure/grip-cover/README.md). Its body is
79.7 × 11.558 × 3.36 mm, and its end wings extend the complete length to
84.5 mm. Each wing reaches 2.4 mm, spans 5 mm and is 1.68 mm thick. Keeping that
full flexible span matters: shortening the strip changes the feel. Its receiver
keeps 0.15 mm at each body end and wing side, 0.45 mm at the retaining face,
0.25 mm behind the strip and 12 mm of roof stock above the slots. The slots span
5.30 mm and exit through the 3 mm end walls. The
[accepted receiver trial](../../enclosure/grip-cover/physical-acceptance.json)
establishes easy support removal and assembled fit for the identified production
coupons and strip. The Pour's crown receiver is a new arrangement of those
features, presenting the finger face upward and using this object's own print
recipe.

The moving and supported samples draw their questions from the enclosure's
current fits. Ordinary assembly seats use 0.15 mm per face; moving service fits
use 0.25 mm per face. A supported mating face has an additional 0.25 mm allowance,
assigned once to the gap, in
[`fits.py`](../../cadlib/fits.py). The
[tee-carrier sliding fit](../../enclosure/tee-carrier/low-force-trial/physical-acceptance.json)
is accepted at an asymmetric 1.00 mm roof gap and 0.25 mm floor gap. That
low-force interface includes the printed roof roughness and available actuation
force; a small reference slider supplies a feel comparison, rather than a claim
that one gap works for every joint.

The support windows make removal and the resulting underside visible together.
The [PET-GF interface-edge experiment](../../../print-tests/petgf-interface-edges/README.md)
reports cleaner release with a requested 0.45 mm top distance, two interface
layers and 0.50 mm interface spacing; the measured planned separation was
0.48 mm in that slice. The
[interface-pattern experiment](../../../print-tests/petgf-interface-patterns/README.md)
identifies the loose horizontal underside strands as the first model bridge
layer. Easy removal can include lost model material; judge the remaining roof
as well as the removed support.

The front deck uses the organizer's current nominal passage sizes from the
[umbilical organizer](../../faucet/umbilical-organizer/README.md). Its 6.6 mm straight
holes are shorter than the organizer's 9.2 mm smooth contact length, and it has
no matching 0.4 mm entrance chamfers. A tube passing through the deck demonstrates
that sample's fit; it does not reproduce the accepted organizer contact. Its
raised and recessed letters provide a small-detail appearance reference.
