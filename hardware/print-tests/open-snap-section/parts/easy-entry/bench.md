# Compare the direct press

Start with the **one-dot pair**. Its working faces are also on the full Pisco pair. Stand the receiver on its flat back, lower the matching cover onto it with the wings relaxed, and press straight down on the middle of the cover. Watch the first contact through an open end. Both toes should start down the guides under that same press.

| Dots | What changed | What to compare |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One | A long receiver bevel starts inside the free toes. A 0.60 mm shorter retaining lip reduces how far both wings must spread. | Can one thumb start and seat it? Does it still resist rocking and pull off deliberately? |
| Two | A 0.96 mm longer, fully backed receiver bevel leads to the full-depth retaining lip. | If it starts as easily as one but takes a harder push farther down, the deeper catch accounts for the difference. |
| Three | The first 2.40 mm of the cover has a steeper lead-in like the older easy-starting coupon. Both leaves retain their 2.00 mm stock. | Does moving part of the lead-in onto the cover make it easier to line up? Compare the push over the full-depth catch with two. |

Try each once centered and once starting slightly off center. Distinguish a stop at the mouth from resistance partway down. After the click, release your thumb, press over one side and then the other, and check both feet and long guide faces. Record the dots, whether straight pressure alone started it, whether it rocked, and how release felt. Then try the full one-dot geometry with the Pisco installed. Its wings engage across a longer distance, so the full part is the test of thumb effort.
