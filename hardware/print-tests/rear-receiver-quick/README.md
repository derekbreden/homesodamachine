# Pisco roof contact and meter shoulder

The Pisco comparison fits the original rear enclosure corner. The side-by-side front collars share the outlet contour, spring roots, lead-in noses and loaded catch geometry of the three-diamond cover. The roof variant broadens and raises the top of each point to give the wing a flat bearing surface against the existing roof. The separate, common rear collar locates the other end of the real regulator. Short sections let the two fronts cool and print together. The comparison measures the effect at the outlet; the force and stiffness of a full-length cover also depend on the stock between the two sections.

Fit the real regulator in the original quadrant. Snap the **unmarked rear collar** at the back with its rigid heel toward the back wall. Leave it in place. At the outlet, try the **one-bar front** as the control and then the **two-bar front** with the raised tips. Seat each flush with the outlet end of the original cover. Press the exposed outlet as in the video and compare whether the cover can settle into another position. The [model checks](parts/pisco-fit.json) give the insertion path, and the [Mark1 plate](prints/2026-11-23-roof-mark1/print-job.json) records the exact articles and finished toolpaths.

The Mark2 specimen is the recessed aft shoulder of the flow-meter receiver, printed in the enclosure’s roof-down direction. A permanent slope from the flank carries its protruding edge. The taper near the step provides room for the meter cap. Compare the underside with the long, loose strands shown beside the installed Pisco in the four-notch coupon. Run a fingertip along the edge and look at how it meets the step; repeated handling reveals strands that lift off an apparently firm surface. [Geometry](parts/meter-geometry.json) · [Mark2 plate](prints/2026-11-23-meter-mark2/print-job.json)

Derek compared the same three-diamond cover in the relieved receiver and the original corner. The original held the outlet more firmly, despite a repeatable change between two resting positions. The relieved receiver also has a shorter, more flexible surrounding wall. On teardown, the strands in both segmented Pisco catches separated and shifted. The loose strands under the meter shoulder were visible independently while those parts were still fitted.
