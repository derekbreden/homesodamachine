# SNAP end-use inquiry

Draft for EPA's [SNAP program contact](https://www.epa.gov/snap/forms/contact-us-about-significant-new-alternatives-policy-snap). Not sent.

Subject: SNAP end-use and R-600a eligibility for a residential carbonated beverage dispenser

We are developing a soda machine exclusively for installation in home kitchens in the
United States. Please confirm its SNAP end-use classification and the applicable
refrigerant listing/use conditions.

The machine is an electrically powered, plumbed, under-counter unit, approximately
215 mm wide × 462 mm deep × 361 mm high. An entirely self-contained vapor-compression
refrigeration circuit chills a pressure vessel holding carbonated drinking water and
two flavor reservoirs. Water and flavor run through food-contact tubing to a separate
faucet; the refrigerant circuit does not extend to that faucet. The machine dispenses
chilled carbonated beverages into the user's glass. It does not dispense ice, store
packaged food for retail sale, or accept payment.

The prototype circuit uses R-600a (isobutane, A3). Compressor/condenser components are
harvested from a household countertop ice maker, with the original evaporator replaced
by a custom coil around the carbonated-water vessel. The expected finished charge is
under 40 g per circuit; a validated final charge specification is not yet established.
The finished product is a newly assembled dispenser, not an unchanged donor appliance.

1. Does this residential-only machine fall under household refrigerators/freezers
   (including small household beverage appliances), refrigerated food processing and
   dispensing equipment, water coolers, or another SNAP end-use?
2. If it falls under refrigerated food processing and dispensing equipment, is there
   an applicable listing allowing pure R-600a? We find an R-290 listing in Rule 26,
   but no pure R-600a entry in the current dispensing table. If no such listing
   applies, what SNAP submission/review route is needed for this use?
3. Which current use conditions and incorporated standard editions apply, including
   warning wording, letter height, symbols, charge/room-area markings and service-port
   color? Does the household UL 60335-2-24 route apply to this specific product?
4. Does the R-600a end-use exemption in 40 CFR 82.154(a)(1)(ix) cover service or disposal
   of the completed dispenser, as distinct from the household ice-maker donor?

We can provide a circuit diagram, enclosure drawing and intended installation
description. Please point us to the controlling listing and any classification
guidance for this boundary.
