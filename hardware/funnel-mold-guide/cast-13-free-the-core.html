Watch the rod, then lift it off

At 32 mm three things are true at once, and their order is the whole design: the rod is free of the core before the guides let go.

At 32 mm of liftWhat it means
Rod socket depth28.8 mm Already cleared. A stationary rod is out of the core.
Guide engagement left12 mm Still constrained square by all four blades.
Working lift32 mm The travel marks are at the bearing mouths.
  1. Look at the rod before you lift further. If silicone has gripped it, the rod may have come up with the core instead of staying put. Ease it back down and free it by hand.
  2. Then lift the core straight up, by hand, until the four blades clear their bearings. The screws come away in it, still in their nuts.
  3. Set it down on its back, plug upward, somewhere it cannot rock on a blade.
⛔ Do not rock it A blade is far weaker to an in-and-out rock than to a sideways one, and by this point it is standing 54 mm free. Rocking is how you snap a guide root, and a core with three guides never comes off square again. If it will not come, put it back down and jack it further.
Done when The core is off and set aside, the rod is still in the silicone, and all four blades are intact.
