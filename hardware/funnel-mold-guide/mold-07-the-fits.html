Check the three fits

Do these with the actual steel you will build with, not with a nominally identical box of it. Every one of them must go together by hand, without force and without rocking. A fit you have to persuade on a 60 mm coupon is a fit you cannot persuade on a 270 mm plate.

  1. The square nut into its slot. Slide one nut in from the block's outside edge. The slot is 8.4 × 4.4 mm on an 8 × 4 mm nut. Then thread an M5 screw through the 5.8 mm bore into it and back it out again. The slot is reproduced in the core's inverted print orientation, so what you are really checking is the thin retaining lip that bridges over it.
  2. The blade through the bearing. Slide the guide-blade coupon through the sleeve in the hardware witness — 12 × 40 mm blade in a 12.6 × 40.6 mm bore. It must slide freely and must not rock. This is the fit that decides whether the core comes off straight.
  3. The washer into its pocket. Drop one fender washer flat into the 25.4 mm pocket. It must sit on a flat, fully supported floor with no rock. Measure the washer's thickness now and write it down; the design accommodates 0.8–2.0 mm.
  4. The bridge roof on the finish witness. Look underneath at the 17.6 mm window. The roof over it must be attached at both ends. That same span carries the forming skin across every rib bay in the mold.
⛔ Correct it here or not at all A tight fit is fixed on this coupon — by clearing strings, adjusting flow, or re-slicing — and then re-printed as a 2-hour plate. Discovering it after 69 hours of printing costs you both large parts.

What this coupon cannot tell you: whether the four widely spaced guides on the real core line up with the four bearings on the real cavity. That check is the dry assembly on page 13.

Done when Nut, screw, blade and washer each go home by hand, and the bridge roof is sound at both ends. Washer thickness recorded.
