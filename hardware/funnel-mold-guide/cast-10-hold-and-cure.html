Wait10

Hold it seated, and wait

The clock stops here. The only job left is keeping the core down, evenly, until the silicone is properly cured — and keeping the mold breathing while it does.

  1. Put the holding arrangement on. Even pressure across the four arms, not a point load on the plate.
  2. Leave the backing-air exits uncovered. All of them, on both halves, for the whole cure.
  3. Leave the pour dish and the five vents open. Do not wipe them flush, do not cap them.
  4. Leave it alone for 5 hours. Level bench, no draughts, stable room temperature — the maker's 5 h is quoted at 23 °C, and a cold shop stretches it. Nudging a half-cured casting is how you get a witness line in the wall.
  5. Full strength comes at 24 h. Demold at 5 h; leave the trimming and the bake until it is properly up.
The guide's call, not spec No clamp, board, weight or force is specified anywhere in the source — only that something must hold the core seated, that the jacks do not, and that the air exits stay open. A flat board spanning the four arms with dead weight on it satisfies all three. Give the board a cut-out or a standoff over the pour dish so you can still see it.
⛔ Wait for a full cure, not a firm one A part that is firm but not through-cured tears at the spout when you lift the core, and it will not survive the post-cure either. If in doubt, give it longer — nothing is lost by waiting.
Done when Five hours have passed at a stable room temperature and the silicone in the pour dish is fully cured to the touch.
