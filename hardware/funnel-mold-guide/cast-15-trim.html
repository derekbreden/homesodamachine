Cut at the shoulder

The spout is cast 12 mm too long on purpose, and the last 2 mm of that is solid. One cut, at a step you can feel, opens the bore the rod already made.

  1. Find the shoulder by touch, then cut square to the axis with a fresh, thin, very sharp blade in one pass. A sawing cut leaves a lip that fouls the drain stub.
  2. Trim the six pips — one from the pour throat, five from the vents — flush with the brim, without gouging it.
  3. Check the bore. Round, clean, unmarked, all the way through. This is the surface a push-fit collet will grip through the drain stub.
Why the tip is there at all A spout that ended at its finished length would have to be cast against a free rod end in open silicone, and its last millimetre would be the worst-formed part of the funnel. Casting 12 mm past the exit puts that region in the scrap, and leaves the cut face square on material that formed against a mold wall.
Done when The spout ends square at the shoulder with an open 6.35 mm bore, and the brim carries no pips.
