Mask, then coat

Work through this list on both halves and tick it off. Anything on it that takes a film is a fit you cannot get back without stripping the part.

Mask before you spray

  • The rod socket. 0.1 mm of clearance. Plug it.
  • Four guide blades and four bearings. 0.6 mm total across a 12 mm width.
  • Four square-nut slots and their bores.
  • Four washer pockets. A film under a washer is a soft bearing.
  • The registration skirt and both parting lands.
  • Every backing bay, air channel and tower exit.
  • The pour throat and all five vent bores.

Then coat

  1. Apply the proven process to the two forming faces. The cavity's bowl and the core's plug, including the brim recess in the plate's underside.
  2. Work up to the forming-face edges, and stop. No bead on the parting land — a bead there holds the mold open and floods the brim.
  3. Do not round the trim shoulder. The 1 mm step at the spout exit is what your blade indexes on after the cast. Round it now and you will be cutting the funnel's bore freehand.
  4. Watch the spout pocket. Deep, narrow, and the one place a coating pools instead of levelling. Inspect it under light before you call the coat done.
  5. Cure fully and dry. Then sand and polish to the number the coupon gave you.
⛔ The coat is the seal Printed road-to-road porosity is what makes a printed mold leak gas into silicone. Both forming faces need a continuous film. A thin patch is not a cosmetic problem; it is where the bubbles come from.
Done when Both forming faces carry a continuous, fully cured, polished film, and every item in the mask list is still bare PETG.
