Measure the bare coupon

The finish witness is a gauge for one number: 0.20 mm of net growth. Its two end rails stand exactly that far above its measuring flat, so when the coated flat comes up level with the rails, the process is on target. Measure it before you coat anything.

  1. Mark stations along the flat and record the uncoated thickness at each with calipers. Several — one reading is not a measurement.
  2. Measure the actual printed rail step too. It is modelled at 0.20 mm, but layer quantisation and first-layer error move it. The number you write down is the gauge, not the number in the CAD.
  3. Keep the rails and the underside bare from here on. They are the datum and the bridge inspection window.
  4. Use the ramp as the honest half of the test. It is cut at the funnel's own floor grade, so it asks the question a flat cannot: does your process take the layer terraces off a shallow slope without rounding the transitions at the top and bottom of it, and without the coating pooling at the foot of the grade?
Two numbers, not one A spray builds differently on a floor than on a wall. Measure a vertical face separately from the flat and record both — the mold has far more vertical forming surface than horizontal.
Done when You have written down the bare flat thickness at each marked station and the real rail step. Nothing is coated yet.
