The part17

Inspect it, and read it

Four things to look for, and what each one is telling you about the run you just did.

What you seeWhat it meansNext time
Voids in the wall or the spout Air that never left — entrained in the mix, stalled in the spout, or coming out of the print through a thin patch of coating. Degas to a flat surface; pour the spout from below; check the coating for continuity.
Tacky or soft patches Off ratio, unmixed material off the cup wall, or something on the mold face inhibiting a platinum cure. Weigh to 0.1 g, scrape the cup, and re-run the coupon trial on the actual four materials.
Torn spout wall Thin release, a lift that started before the rod was free, or the core rocked. Re-release every pour; jack the full 32 mm before lifting by hand; never rock.
Short at the brim or a corner Underfilled — not topped up after the vacuum cycle, or a blocked vent that never let its corner fill. Top up while it still flows; clear all six ports before the next cast.
The first one is a test piece Measure this funnel against the nominal geometry — brim thickness, collar, the ramp transitions where the floor meets the walls. The first cast is the real dimensional and surface check on the tooling, and it is the only thing that can tell you the film came out even.
Done when The funnel is free of voids and tack, whole at the spout, full at the brim, and measures to the nominal geometry.
