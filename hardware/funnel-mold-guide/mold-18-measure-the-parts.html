Read the film on the real parts

The coupon measured your process. It cannot tell you the film is even over a 270 mm part. The before-and-after widths can, and they cost you nothing but writing numbers down.

StationBefore coatingAfter coatingExpected change
Cavity collar width, X—— internal, −0.40 mm
Cavity collar width, Y—— internal, −0.40 mm
Core plug width, X—— external, +0.40 mm
Core plug width, Y—— external, +0.40 mm
Brim recess depth—— −0.20 mm

Measure at matching stations on both halves, so a difference is a film and not two different places.

Then compare against the funnel

Put the finished faces against the nominal funnel geometry: the brim thickness, the ramp transitions where the floor meets the walls, and the collar. What you are looking for is not a single number in tolerance — it is the film being the same in the bowl, on the walls and in the spout pocket.

The last word belongs to the first cast No amount of measuring the tooling settles this. The first funnel out of the mold is the real dimensional and surface check — treat it as a test piece and expect to learn something from it.
Done when Before and after widths are recorded at matching stations on both halves, and the change at each is within ±0.05 mm of the expected 0.40.
