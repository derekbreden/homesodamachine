Dry-assemble, before any coating

The coupon proved four fits one at a time. It could not prove that four guides 238 mm apart line up with four bearings. Only the real parts can, and only while you can still fix them.

  1. Put the rod in the core. Seated, bottomed, 22 mm proud.
  2. Lower the core onto the cavity by hand. The chamfered blade tips enter their bearings before the forming plug enters the cavity — that ordering is the whole reason the blades are long. Let it find its own way down; do not steer it.
  3. Check the skirt seats flat. The core's skirt registers over the cavity for 10 mm. It should sit down all the way round with no gap and no rock.
  4. Run the full stroke by hand. Lift the core 32 mm and set it back down, twice. Nothing should bind at any point in the travel.
⛔ Never jack a tight fit The screw jacks are lifting devices for a cured casting. If a guide binds or the rod is stiff, the jacks will happily bend a printed ear before the obstruction gives. Fix the fit with abrasive and inspection, at zero load, now.
Write down what you learn If one corner sits proud, or one blade enters later than the others, note which. After coating, the same symptom will mean something different — and you will want to know whether it was there beforehand.
Done when The core drops onto the cavity under its own weight, the skirt seats flat all round, and the full 32 mm stroke runs free with the rod installed.
