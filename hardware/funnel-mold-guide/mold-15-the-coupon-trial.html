Prove the process on the coupon

You are not choosing a coating out of a catalogue. You are proving that this PETG, this coat, this release and this silicone work together, on a 60 mm coupon, before you commit two parts that took 69 hours to print.

  1. Abrade, clean, dry. Lightly — you are keying the surface, not removing the terraces yet.
  2. Coat the flat, the ramp and a vertical face. Use a sandable coat and let it cure fully. A soft film sands into a wave.
  3. Sand and polish back to the datum. Measure from the same stations as before. Target 0.20 mm ± 0.05 on the flat; record the vertical face separately; look along the ramp for pooling at its foot.
  4. Then cast against it. Release the coupon, put silicone on it, cure it, pull it off. Do that more than once on the same coupon.

What the cast half of the trial has to show

  • The silicone cures through, right down to the coated face — no tack, no soft skin.
  • It releases cleanly, without taking coating with it.
  • The coating survives repeated demolding, not just the first one.
  • Bubbles stop. Put a small coated patch under the silicone and watch for gas still coming out of the print. Ventilation does not make a printed mold airtight; the continuous coating is what does.
The guide's call, not spec The shop holds two candidate coats — Krylon Crystal Clear acrylic and Craft Resin epoxy. The acrylic is the one already chosen elsewhere in this machine for a silicone contact face, because it seals print porosity and does not inhibit a platinum cure. Start there. The trial is what decides it, and the trial is cheap.
⚠ Solvent and aerosol Spray coats and their thinners: ventilate, and keep them away from the silicone stock.
Done when One coupon reads 0.20 ± 0.05 mm on the flat and has released cured silicone cleanly at least twice.
