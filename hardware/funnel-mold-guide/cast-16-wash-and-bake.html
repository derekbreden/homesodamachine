Wash, then bake

This funnel takes flavour concentrate and goes in the dishwasher. The room-temperature cure makes it a part; the bake is what makes it a food-contact part, because that is what drives off the volatiles and finishes the platinum cure.

  1. Wash the release film off. Inside, outside, brim, spout and bore. Rinse and dry completely — water in the bore is water in the oven.
  2. Bake — and know this schedule is not the maker's. BBDINO publishes no post-cure for this silicone. What it does publish is the ceiling: the cured rubber takes 230 °C. The project's working figure is 4 h at ~200 °C, under that ceiling and a placeholder until you run the trial.
  3. Witness the temperature on the thermometer, not the dial. Put the oven thermometer in the cavity and read it. That is what the shop oven is paired with it for.
  4. Cool it slowly and inspect it again. A part that changed shape in the oven was not fully cured when it went in.
⛔ The tooling never goes in the oven The post-cure applies to the demolded silicone only. The PETG halves stay on the bench; they are unannealed, room-temperature tooling and heat will take their forming faces with it.
What the bake does and does not settle It is the gate the wetted-surface screen is written around — residual platinum catalyst and cyclic siloxanes, on a coupon prepared the way production would be. That screen is what this project has. It is not a certification, and a coating or release coupon does not qualify the funnel on its own.
Done when The funnel has been washed, dried, baked with the temperature witnessed on the thermometer rather than the dial, and cooled without distortion.
